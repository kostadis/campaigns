# Chapter 6 Vukradin, Rank Cheval

Date: 03-02 of Taraskh 1495

## Summary
The long walk belonged to Adabra, who used it to deliver the doctrine she had only sketched the night before. Harbin, bless his heart, wants the area cleared of monsters — and what is a monster to him? Anything that disrupts his idea of peace. The manticore and the orcs driven from their homes are "merely acting as nature intends," and "it is the meddling that truly upset the balance." Soma wanted room to think, but where her old master would have left her the space, Adabra always has the answer ready. She agreed anyway, and put it in her own terms: let nature take its course, wolves and deer are going to eat each other, and that is all right. Adabra seized on it, and pushed the argument to its edge — some believe every evil out of the outer planes must be fought, but where do you draw the line? At a spirit from the ethereal plane? A fiend from the abyss? At a god's design? "To fight against these is to deny the very nature of existence itself. And when you start denying the right of one thing to exist, you start calling people barbarians." Brewbarry, who had lost this argument once already, sighed that he *was* a barbarian, and was told again that he never was one — he was a nomad. Soma, fearing hours more of this, was rescued by Vukradin taking up his instrument, and this time the music was good.

Valphine asked to play alongside him. Vukradin's private assessment was that this might not work for him, though he had to admit it added a colour and texture that elevated his composition into art — so perhaps a collaboration was possible, provided she understood her role in the duo.

They arrived at the Townmaster's door to find Harbin once again pleading with a dragon to ignore him, and Vukradin once again preparing to explain that he is not a dragon. Adabra cut through it: "Harbin, get out here and pay this young man who has rescued me the money that is owed him." Vukradin, sensing room, coughed and noted the mission had been more involved than the note suggested and might be worth more. Harbin's counter revealed the local morality — why hadn't Vukradin simply paid himself out of the manticore's treasure, as the lawful agreement with Lord Neverember allowed? Before Vukradin could produce his outrage, Adabra produced hers, and it was better. Vukradin was no common sellsword of the kind Harbin and Lord Neverember insist on recruiting; he was "an idealist, respectful of life, and willing to sacrifice money for his principles," who wanted honest wages, fair trade for work done, and conflict-free gold. Vukradin, delighted, contributed "Well, said." She kept going: the town needs more than mercenaries, it needs champions whose principles, though perhaps untested, are fundamentally sound — "This isn't just an expense, it's an investment in the moral fabric of Phandalin's defense!" — because "those who protect us must do so with a deeper conviction than the possibility of tax-free plunder." Then the flourish: give this man more coin, and not just this man, but the tortle Soma who shares her concerns, and the nomad who has been so unjustly treated. Vukradin noticed what the list left out. Valphine was skipped over entirely, as if she did not exist, and he wondered whether something about her annoyed Adabra — whether it was because she is a drow.

Valphine noticed too, and filed it where she files such things: Lathander's light will guide her even if this woman chooses to ignore her, and had she still been a cleric of Lolth or still in her noble house, the woman would be dead. She has moved past that world. Harbin and Adabra talked on, and at some point both pointed at Corbin standing beside her. Harbin eventually admitted the real constraint — beyond monsters, the town has been under siege by bandits, and he is short of coin. Adabra's glare, ineffective on Valphine, worked perfectly on the Townmaster, who suddenly remembered a way to help and offered free room and board at the Stonehill Inn.

At the inn Vukradin went straight to the tavern keeper and offered to play, opening with a question: "Toblen, what do your friends call you?" The answer was "Spiderman," which briefly made Valphine wonder whether the man was one of her own — she does like spiders. The truth was better: as a child he fell into a spider's nest while the eggs were hatching and came out covered in baby spiders, unafraid, and the sight of him walking calmly under them earned him the name. Valphine's assessment was that in a different time and place her sisters would have recruited him, or killed him, or delightfully tortured him.

Vukradin sat to play and Valphine asked to join, receiving assent and a reminder that her role was to support him and his music. She nodded — and then heard the performance for what it was. The brilliance was there, but the piece was a discordant mess, far too complex for this audience. Listening, she realised that the fusion she had been developing, a weave of Lathanderian choral music and prayers to Lolth that she calls *Choralum Lux Maleficarum*, was exactly the counterweight it needed: rich layered choral harmonies drenched in bright resonant tones evoking the warmth and hope of Lathander's light, interwoven with sinuous melodic lines and subtle dissonances hinting at the enigmatic and unsettling nature of Lolth's influence. Vukradin was outraged at first — then saw the crowd enjoying it, and smiled. Valphine took a further chance and sang, her contralto weaving intricate, almost hypnotic lines that felt like a spell or an invocation. Vukradin raised an eyebrow and built on her vocals. The room went silent to watch them, and applauded at the end. His verdict was the chapter's first real alliance: "Perhaps we should play together."

Soma watched the two of them — the oddest of pairings — and thought about the forest and her children, travelling the wide world, and prayed they had avoided the horrors that felled the north. She half-heard another rumour about strange goings-on in the Neverwinter Wood, which reminds her of home and tempts her to go and look, or to talk the group into going. But Vukradin had already caught a different story, from a tall, lean half-elf of weathered but noble appearance whose once-dark hair was streaked generously with silver, with fine lines, a well-trimmed salt-and-pepper beard, and sharp emerald-green eyes still holding keen intelligence and quiet wisdom. He dressed practically, in earth-toned tunics and sturdy leather boots suited to tending an orchard and wandering the countryside. Soma learned his name later: Daran Edermath, known as Silverleaf.

Silverleaf's pitch was, on its own terms, excellent: he had been an adventurer once too, and west of Phandalin on the coast stands an old stone lighthouse. Ships are drawn to the gleaming tower like moths to a flame and doomed to crash on its rocks — and their wrecks must be filled with treasure. He had no way of knowing he was pitching salvage to Vukradin. "No! No! No! No! That's not treasure," the bard said, hands flopping down. "How many times do I have to explain this. You don't go and rob from the dead and call it treasure!" Silverleaf, shocked, apologised for the offence. Soma felt for the old elf while conceding that lessons need to be learned.

Vukradin was mid-sermon on why adventuring is wrong — when is it a monster, and when is it a poor farmer you plundered, and there is no blurry line that makes plunder right — when he noticed a man he had missed. Early thirties, steady and capable, carrying himself with a confidence that made him look larger; dark hair, clean-shaven, a scar above the right eyebrow, and chain mail of the sort no farmer buys. On him was an emblem of a gauntlet grasping a sword by the blade. Vukradin worked at the memory and produced a confident and entirely wrong answer: it must be the emblem of a legendary maestro who wields their instrument with such finesse that they grasp the blade — the sharp edge of sound — directly, mastering even the most dangerous notes. A fellow musician, then, or at least someone who appreciates his music. Something about a group of fanatics also stirred, but the details escaped him.

The man introduced himself as Ser Kaelen Thorn and asked to talk in private; Vukradin insisted on public, and Ser Kaelen shrugged, sat, and ordered drinks from Spiderman. He had heard stories around Phandalin about Vukradin and his goals. Vukradin, still committed to his reading, asked whether he had heard about the studio. "No." Awkward. What Ser Kaelen had heard about was the bard's unique perspective on treasure, refreshing in a world that measures worth only in plunder. Valphine, annoyed or mischievous, added "Or dead drow," and Ser Kaelen noticed her for the first time with a visible double-take — plainly his first drow cleric of Lathander. Vukradin vouched for her ("She's a good Drow. A cleric of Lathander"), and Valphine promptly undid it: "I find the sun deliciously painful." Vukradin worried the knight might leave, but had decided Valphine might be the missing link in his music and was not discarding her yet.

Over the drinks Ser Kaelen made his case. It is rare to find someone who grapples so earnestly with right and wrong in a place where sentients strip armour from the dead without a second thought — "We steal their armor in a final act of treachery on the living" — and he sensed a deeper conviction. "Pleasure and Pain," Valphine whispered, and he turned on her with the look of a man wanting to solve her. He asked her name, then asked whether she was a cleric of Lolth or any other underdark deity of horror or hell. Valphine, bored, said no: "They are very dull. Single-minded in their approach to things." Ser Kaelen agreed enthusiastically — they see only the path of death and destruction — and took it as confirmation he had found the right people. Vukradin noticed that Valphine was startled by his interpretation and was about to explain what she actually meant by *boring*, then thought better of it and said nothing.

Convinced the studio was about to be funded, Vukradin listened to the offer. The order is fighters, magicians, storytellers and clerics dedicated to making the north better. Vukradin tried steering — "It would be better if I had a music studio?" — and was told something else was on the table: with the order's backing, a voice that could motivate hundreds would be known to tens of thousands. Valphine recognised the type immediately. He cited the order's role in the War of the Dragon and bragged that one of their own, Milo Goodbarrel, led the strike force that reached the inner sanctum and stopped the ritual. Then he found the exact note — the order is about helping the underdog, "saving the world and not treating it as something to be plundered and taken," and it believes art embodies that mission. Through Vukradin's music and story, the message reaches a wide audience: "An artist who could make a fortune but turned away from it to do good? You would be a legend!" Vukradin was, to his credit, dubious about where the money came from, and was assured it was all conflict-free gold through donations. He would start as an associate member. Ser Kaelen extended the invitation to Valphine as well; she smiled and privately concluded they would not work well together. When Vukradin suggested Soma, the knight's face darkened: "She's a naturalist, and I don't think that our points of view would align." Valphine wondered about that. Vukradin was told he would receive a pin — which Valphine knew he would try to sell, and which is worth nothing — and instructed to keep being true to his mission and opposing senseless killing and plundering.

Brewbarry knew the Order of the Gauntlet from the other side. They fought his tribe and killed many, and they were proud, dangerous, ferocious warriors. His verdict was four words: "Order is bullies. They bully barbarians." Ser Kaelen did not deny the war, only reframed it — it fell to the order to defend their people from attacks by his, it was their kind or his, they took no joy in the slaughter, and he was sure some of Brewbarry's people took no joy in slaughtering the innocent either. Brewbarry answered with the accusation that actually mattered to him: "You stole." He wanted to hit the man. What he got instead was an offer of a drink, and an epitaph — the war is over, it was brutal, people died, and it was over nonsense. Brewbarry's assent was equally short: "Drink good." Spiderman brought four.

Soma watched the last stone drop. Vukradin had been waiting for a pebble to push him over the edge and Ser Kaelen supplied it: membership would carry his music and his message further, and the combination of his personal story with his celestial origins would be tremendous. Soma's instinct was the patience of her own kind — sit down, sip this tea, listen, there is more to life than rushing headlong into the tide — but he had already said yes. Ser Kaelen sealed it in the language of family: "Know this, you are a brother. And when we reach out for help, you must come to us as a brother. And as your brothers, we will come to your help!" Soma's read was colder than the moment looked. The shell-sprout was happy, part of a family — "A family he doesn't know that wants things he can't imagine."

What he wanted next was an adventure: "We need to destroy the evil tower!" Soma tried the obvious correction, that a lighthouse is meant to protect boats rather than destroy them, and got only "It's destroying them!" She explained that a lighthouse is placed where there is danger precisely to protect ships. He did not listen, and marched out of the Stonehill Inn toward the Townmaster's place of work. Harbin, who had not yet reached it, saw him coming and stopped. Confused at first, he offered what he knew: it is a fair distance, on the coast, he is very familiar with the tower, and he knows nothing of it being evil — "That's so much blah, blah, blah and rumors." Vukradin held to his solution and asked how to destroy it. Harbin shouted that Lord Neverember would be appalled, which Vukradin turned around on him: was he saying Lord Neverember would permit a cursed lighthouse to remain? "No! He would want his heroes to remove the curse!" That stopped him. Removing a curse is less work than destroying a lighthouse — so how does one remove a curse? Harbin's answer was that he is not an adventurer; they are. Vukradin thought of Ser Kaelen, and Harbin pointed them to Barthen's Provisions, where the knight was getting ready to leave town.

Ser Kaelen was delighted to see them, and delighted by the initiative — but candid that the order has very little intelligence about the curse. He offered three possibilities: the first requires a cleric; the second is a monster haunting the place; the third, and most common, is that the rumours are simply untrue. From what he had heard, he thought it was the second.
