# Chapter 1 Neverwinter Expansionism and the North, Excerpt from the Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th

Date: Pre-campaign (Lore Prologue)

## Summary

This chapter is not a session narrative but an in-world lore document — an excerpt from the collected writings of the Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th, chronicling the history of the North in the aftermath of the Second Sundering. It surveys the failed divine interventions that followed the gods' return to the mortal world, cataloguing successive crises that shaped the political and physical landscape of the Sword Coast.

The document traces a chain of calamities: Tiamat's attempted return through the Cult of the Dragon (revealed to have been orchestrated by Zariel rather than Tiamat herself), the All-Father's giant-centric ambitions that nearly destroyed the giant kingdoms, and the escape of the ancient blue dragon Iymrith. The Storm Giant King Hekaton's subsequent alliance between giants and the peoples of the realms is noted as a turning point. Lolth's machinations — using Gromph Baenre to loosen the bonds between planes — released demon lords into the Underdark, triggering chaos that rippled upward when Uthgardt barbarians, provoked by the Underdark's destabilization, launched devastating raids across the North, destroying Triboar, Calling Horn, and smaller settlements.

With the Ordning restored and the combined strength of the Silvery Marches and giant allies ending the barbarian invasion, Lord Neverember of Neverwinter turned his attention to re-establishing control over the hinterland. His plan: use adventurers as a low-cost pacification force, offering them tax-free plunder in exchange for clearing monsters and evildoers from the Phandalin area. One such adventurer was an avant-garde musician named Vukradin — whose work was, the sage notes, underappreciated.

This document establishes the post-war political context, the precedent-setting divine interference that frames the campaign's stakes, and the cynical economic logic that brings adventurers to Phandalin.

## Notes for the GM

- This document establishes the post-Second Sundering political landscape and explains why Neverwinter is using adventurer-settlers rather than military forces.
- The chain of divine interventions (Tiamat → giants → Lolth → demon lords → Uthgardt raids) creates a domino-effect cosmology that later naturalist/interventionist debates in the campaign will echo.
- The detail about Iymrith's escape is a hook that may connect to later Storm King's Thunder plot threads.
- The economic model of adventuring (tax-free plunder, no-questions-asked treasure rights) is the system Vukradin will reject on moral grounds — this sets up his core character conflict from the very beginning.
- The sage's dry, scholarly tone is a deliberate contrast to the first-person PC narratives that follow in subsequent chapters.
