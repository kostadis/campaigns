# Chapter 38 The Intervention

Date: Feb 21st, 2026

## Summary

The party returned to Phandalin from Dragon Barrow to find Harbin Wester waiting at the town boundary with banners, townsfolk arranged in festive semicircles, and a speech already drafted about his own foresight in establishing the Privy Council. Harbin praised the party warmly for ending the dragon threat that had hung over the region, tactfully lashing his name to the victory before it had time to acquire its own history. The festival atmosphere was interrupted when Ser Kaelen drew Soma aside with urgent news: Adabra had been busy. Using the events at the Whispering Wood — specifically Lyra's failed ritual and the treant's death — she had been moving to ostracize Lyra from the order and painting Soma as a dangerous interventionist who held Meril's Staff without the Enclave's blessing.

Thomas, one of Lyra's trusted agents, confirmed the worst of it on the road to the council: Adabra had called the meeting during Reidoth's absence, had already counted the votes, and intended to announce a verdict rather than discover one. The party marched toward the council knowing they walked into a room already arranged against them. Soma made it plain she would not apologize for interventionism — she had intervened because the situation required it, and if that was the argument, that was the argument.

At the druid council, Adabra laid out her case with devastating calm. She had walked to the sacred grove herself. The treant was dead. Roots collapsed, crown black, the grove ruined around it. Vukradin had given her a story that did not match what she found. Soma used the staff's Resonance to probe whether Adabra was controlled, deceived, or lying to herself — the answer came back clear: no, no, and no. Adabra was a true believer. Falcon, sitting beside her, rose to deliver a dramatic confession of his own past failures, positioning himself as a cautionary tale of interventionism — but managed to make himself the sympathetic protagonist of his own tragedy rather than a useful witness against the party.

Vukradin, stung by Falcon's personal attacks — "Trees are dead because of men like him" — walked out of the council in protest. The party stood their ground without him. Xanthopoulos, an ancient centaur of the high valleys, arrived to speak for Soma and the staff, declaring that Meril had not given it for sentiment — he had chosen her. He also delivered grim news: Corbin was dead, Sister Kaella had escaped, and Boney the skeletal horse had stood by and done nothing. Adabra seized on Corbin's death as evidence of the price of interventionism, but Xanthopoulos cut her off — "I am not on your side. I'm not on theirs."

The council deliberated and delivered their verdict: Adabra's case did not hold. The moderates sided with Soma and Lyra. Lyra was restored to full good standing. Adabra left seething — defeated for now but far from finished. Afterward, Valphine counseled Falcon, offering him a new path through Lathander's doctrine: pain as proof of contact with power, not proof that action corrupts. Falcon began to crack, admitting he did not know why he had supported Adabra, and asked for help apologizing to Vukradin.

## Scenes

### Return to Phandalin — Harbin's Festival

- Harbin Wester greets the party at the town boundary with a full festival: banners, arranged townsfolk, the particular smile of a man rehearsing modest pride.
- He praises the party's bravery and sacrifice, then tactfully credits his own Privy Council for bringing such capable heroes to Phandalin.
- Valphine observes this with clinical detachment — Harbin is doing exactly what any competent political creature would do, lashing his name to victory.
- Brewbarry beams. Vukradin accepts the praise with earnest dignity. Valphine smiles and performs graciousness while noting the cost to herself — Lathander's light is power that hurts to channel.
- Ser Kaelen draws Soma aside with quiet urgency: Adabra has been using Lyra's failure at the Whispering Wood as a lever, ostracizing Lyra from the order and targeting Soma as a dangerous interventionist.
- Valphine describes her temple — the Temple of Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice in Tresendar Manor — and Vukradin transfers unclaimed property to it, performing charity that also grows the church.
- Bryn and Giles arrive mid-conversation, scarred and sunburnt from a public self-flagellation parade fundraiser through town. They are smiling beatifically, having proven something to themselves. The townsfolk look unsettled.

### The Road to the Druid Council

- Vukradin walks with his flute in hand, thinking about Adabra — he likes her, which is inconvenient. She was always fair with him, paid him five gold pieces for information about the grove.
- Thomas materializes on the road with bad news: Adabra is moving to ostracize Lyra, not debate her. Ostracism is a verdict, not a discussion.
- Reidoth is absent — Adabra called the council during his departure. Vukradin recognizes the architecture of a predetermined conclusion.
- The party debates sending for Reidoth but keeps it as a held option. Vukradin notes the precision of the timing: Adabra had looked at the calendar, identified the window, and placed her move.
- Soma states plainly she will not apologize for interventionism. Vukradin silently begins drafting how to loosen Adabra's arrangement — to make her case sound weak before Soma has to carry the weight.
- **(DM note, not yet known to party):** Vukradin does not yet realize his attempt to help may make him the target instead.

### The Druid Council — Adabra's Accusation

- The glade is full: druids settled in grass, hands on knees, eyes forward. No hospitality, no preamble.
- Soma uses the staff's Resonance to probe Adabra — the answer is clear: she is not controlled, deceived, or lying. She is a true believer.
- Adabra speaks without raising her voice: she walked to the sacred grove herself three weeks ago. The treant was dead — roots collapsed, crown black, grove ruined.
- Vukradin tries cross-examination: "How do you know what you heard from me wasn't true, and the treant died within those two weeks?" A reasonable point, but Adabra has already considered it.
- Thomas and Lyra stand and give the plain account: Lyra asked them to deal with the metastasized treant, they failed, the party killed it. The killing stopped a planar infestation from spreading.
- Soma observes silently: the more she steps in, the more the staff wakes up. Power flows into her hands. Not owned. Borrowed. Maybe offered. Maybe expected.

### The Schism — Falcon's Betrayal

- Vukradin begins working the council — stepping toward the lean, trying to loosen Adabra's arrangement before Soma carries the weight.
- Falcon stands up and points at Vukradin: "Trees are dead because of men like *him*."
- Vukradin pulls Falcon aside, bewildered — asks what he did wrong. Falcon: "You intervened, and in your intervention, people died. Adabra sees it clearly."
- Vukradin challenges Falcon's hypocrisy: "Didn't you used to intervene? It got you a beautiful hunting lodge, and lots of fame, and glory… and a whole life built on a lie."
- Falcon calls Vukradin's bluff and confesses to the council — his adventuring days, the dragons he failed to fight, the lodge that burned while heroes stood by. He weeps. It makes him more sympathetic.
- Vukradin walks out: "I'm going back to Phandalin. You all can stay. I do not like the personal insults. I'm not interested in helping Lyra anymore."
- Brewbarry asks the simple question: "Do we kill all these people, or do we leave?" Soma pulls him back.
- Vukradin Sends to Reidoth from the road, telling the old ranger what happened.

### The Druid Council Debate — Soma Stands Alone

- With Vukradin gone, every eye in the circle turns to Soma. Old tortle, late lesson, friend's staff, too many people expecting answers.
- Aldric Stone Path stands with arms folded, jaw set — the weathered mountain man who has found someone who finally listened to him in Adabra.
- Soma asks: "What does Adabra say the solution is?" Aldric opens his mouth, closes it. Adabra answers: "The ecosystem finds equilibrium without help."
- Soma counters: "Equilibrium comes in many forms. One of these equilibriums could be the end of everything, of all life." Aldric sits down. Adabra notices.
- Xanthopoulos, the ancient centaur, arrives. He looks at the staff and declares: Meril chose her. "It's just a staff, isn't it?" Soma asks. No one answers.
- Xanthopoulos delivers the news: "Corbin is dead. Sister Kayla walked out. She's gone to the wind."
- Soma deflects: "And Boney stood by and did nothing?" — not the right thing to say.
- Adabra seizes on Corbin's death: "See, this is the price of interventionism. Dead bodies everywhere."
- Xanthopoulos cuts her off: "I am not on your side. I'm not on theirs. I'm telling you what my people saw."
- Soma refuses to sugarcoat: "The interplanar treant? Yeah, that's — I'm not going to cry for that. That treant was going to bring a lot more manifestation and horror with it."
- She points out Falcon's contradiction: he asked the party to intervene at his lodge, then turned anti-interventionist. The Talosians had taken his lodge from him anyway.

### The Verdict — Council Decision

- Lyra speaks carefully about the failing ritual, the planar damage, the treant that died. The party backs her up with evidence of Talosian blight from Axelholm and the mines.
- Someone points out that the Talosians took Falcon's own lodge — the cost of sitting idle, the philosophy Adabra defends demonstrated in action.
- Adabra gives a long, internally consistent, deeply felt speech about natural order, cycles, balance. Brewbarry recognizes the argument from his own tribe's elders.
- The presiding druid delivers the verdict: "Adabra, the case you've made just does not hold."
- Adabra's face does something complicated. The druid continues: "All you managed to do was offend the bard."
- The council finds that Adabra's personal attacks on Vukradin crossed the line. Her behavior was disgraceful.
- Lyra is restored to full good standing. The moderates side with Soma.
- Adabra leaves — seething. She does not accept the verdict; she has simply been outvoted. She walks out with the posture of someone choosing a different battlefield.
- Soma lifts her staff and brings it down once — a deliberate punctuation mark.
- **(DM note):** Adabra is not done. She lost a battle, not the war.

### Aftermath — Falco n and Valphine

- The others disperse. Valphine remains to watch Falcon. She identifies the specific stillness of a useful person who has concluded he is useless.
- Falcon asks how a drow became a cleric of Lathander. Valphine explains: after the demon invasion of Menzoberranzan, she reached the surface and the sun hurt — not as punishment, but as power made visible, radiant, inexhaustible. Pain without apology. She worshipped it.
- Falcon begins to crack: "I don't even know why I ever supported Adabra."
- He admits he owes the bard an apology but lacks the words. Valphine advises a gesture with substance — a musical instrument of real value, a room in Falcon's retreat for Vukradin to work.
- Falcon asks Valphine to mediate. She accepts. One correction at a time.

### Aftermath — The Grove

- Lyra offers to buy drinks at the Stonehill. Vukradin is not there, but some of the gratitude still feels aimed at him.
- The party discusses next steps: orcs, Phandalin's perimeter, Privy Council obligations, the road toward Neverwinter. Neverwinter wins as the immediate destination.
- Soma reflects on Falcon — lost hunter's look, the realization that he had been bent away from home for hours by trusting the wrong path.
- Soma also reflects on Vukradin: his silver tongue makes people feel managed even when he means well. Resentment grows slow, like rot under a floorboard. She does not tell him — not yet.
- Xanthopoulos says quietly: "Perhaps Meril made the right choice." Soma keeps the seven words like a smooth stone. Not good enough. Not useful enough. The right choice.

## NPCs

- **Adabra** — Druid of the Emerald Enclave, true believer in non-interventionism. Fair, direct, and meticulous — she walked to the grove herself, investigated for three weeks, and built a case from facts. Not controlled, not deceived, not lying. A conviction politician who called a council during Reidoth's absence, arranged the votes, and lost anyway. Left seething. **(DM note):** She lost a battle, not the war. She will find a new battlefield.

- **Falcon** — Retired adventurer, owner of Falcon Lodge (twice burned). Grieving, guilt-ridden, and manipulated by Adabra into becoming the council's cautionary tale. Confessed his past failures publicly in a way that made him the protagonist of his own tragedy. Began the session as Adabra's ally and ended it questioning his choices. Valphine is recruiting him for Lathander. Weakness: personal guilt makes him easy to weaponize. **(DM note):** Falcon has shifted allegiance — he now owes Vukradin an apology and may become a useful resource if handled properly.

- **Xanthopoulos** — Ancient centaur of the high valleys. Deliberate, considered, speaks with the weight of someone who does not waste words. Knew Meril. Declared that Meril chose Soma, not as sentiment but as judgment. Delivered news of Corbin's death with flat calm. Strictly neutral — "I am not on your side. I'm not on theirs." His testimony carried the council.

- **Lyra** — Emerald Enclave druid. Ostracized by Adabra after the failed ritual at the Whispering Wood. Stood and gave the plain account of events at the council — no shame, no defiance, just fact. Restored to full good standing by the verdict. Grateful to the party.

- **Harbin Wester** — Townmaster of Phandalin. Staged a full festival for the party's return and credited his own Privy Council with bringing them together. Polished, self-interested, politically competent. Valphine respects his maneuver while noting its predictability.

- **Ser Kaelen** — Delivered the warning about Adabra's machinations to the party. Quiet urgency, genuine concern.

- **Thomas** — Lyra's trusted agent. Materialized on the road to the council with the bad news that the meeting was called during Reidoth's absence. Delivered the news with the weariness of someone who has thought about it extensively.

- **Aldric Stone Path** — Weathered mountain druid. Supported Adabra — she was the first person who listened to him. Sat down during Soma's counter-argument. A moderate who could be swayed.

- **Sister Kaella** — Deadly assassin in priest's cloth. Escaped custody while Corbin was guarding her. At large. **(DM note):** She could reappear anywhere, anytime.

- **Corbin** — Former farmer, former bandit. Killed while guarding Sister Kaella. His death became evidence in Adabra's case against interventionism.

- **Boney** — Skeletal horse of uncertain moral standing. Stood by and did nothing while Corbin died. Status uncertain.

- **Reidoth** — Old ranger, nominal head of the local Emerald Enclave chapter. Conspicuously absent during Adabra's council. Vukradin Sended him the news of the schism. **(DM note):** Reidoth's return may shift the local power balance again.

- **Bryn and Giles** — Members of Valphine's congregation at the Temple of Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice. Conducted a public self-flagellation parade through Phandalin as a fundraiser. Scarred, sunburnt, beatifically smiling. The townsfolk are unsettled; Valphine is satisfied.

- **Brewbarry** — Goliath warrior, party muscle. Offered to solve the council problem with violence before anyone started talking. Stood at the edge and watched words do the work. Understood Adabra's argument from personal experience — his tribe's elders said the same thing about suffering being the mountain testing its children. Eventually, he stopped believing it.
