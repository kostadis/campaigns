# Chapter 7

Date: 2025-08-05

## Summary
The battle against the orc ambushers raged on with brutal intensity. An orc warrior landed a punishing blow on Valphine, shattering her concentration and extinguishing the glowing light she had conjured to outline their enemies. Vukradin attempted to bend the orc shaman's will with a powerful mental command, but the shaman resisted with uncanny fortitude, his mind holding firm against the magical intrusion. Soma, still in beast form, pressed the attack relentlessly, biting and clawing at the shaman until the creature was forced to abandon his boar transformation and revert to his true form, battered and desperate.

With the shaman exposed, the party pressed their advantage — but the tide nearly turned against them in a single, catastrophic moment. The shaman, cornered and furious, unleashed a searing bolt of lightning that tore through both Brewbarry and Valphine simultaneously, leaving them grievously wounded. Vukradin managed to magically halt the shaman in place, robbing him of any further action, while Soma continued to savage him with tooth and claw. One by one, the remaining orcs struck back, and the blows mounted — Brewbarry was finally felled by an orc's blade and crumpled to the ground, and moments later, Orc 1 screamed something about Talos and landed a devastating critical strike on Valphine, sending her down as well.

With two of their companions unconscious and bleeding, the fight fell to Soma and Vukradin. Soma tore into the shaman with savage fury and finished him off, ending the threat of the orc leader once and for all. Vukradin, daggers in hand, drove both blades into the last standing orc, landing a vicious strike that left the creature reeling. Soma delivered the killing blow, and the battlefield finally fell silent. One orc had attempted to flee, but was cut down before he could escape — a decision that sparked a brief but pointed moral debate among the survivors about the ethics of killing a retreating enemy.

Once the dust settled, the party searched the fallen orcs and made a troubling discovery. Among the shaman's belongings was a well-packaged set of high-quality rations, sealed with unusual care and bearing a distinctive "C" monogram. Corin, the former bandit now traveling with the group, went pale when he saw it — he recognized the mark immediately as belonging to the Carver, a local bandit chief. The implications were unsettling: someone had paid the Talos-worshipping orc cultists to ambush the party, and only two people had known of their travel plans — the town master and Ser Kaelen. The party also recovered a book detailing the dogma of Talos, the god of storms and destruction, whose priests were commanded to spread ruin wherever they walked. Vukradin, convinced that a pin he carried had been used to track the group, quietly broke it in two and scattered the pieces across the battlefield to make it look like an accident. Before moving on, Corin offered one final warning: the Carver had agents embedded within the local village itself.

After a long rest to recover from their wounds, the party took a circuitous and stealthy route toward their destination — the Tower of Storms, a lighthouse perched atop a rocky outcropping nearly surrounded by water and connected to the beach by a narrow natural causeway. As they arrived on the coast, they were greeted by an unexpected sight: a crab of monstrous size, lumbering along the shoreline. Soma approached to speak with it, but before she could even begin, the crab spoke first — in perfectly fluent Common. It introduced itself as a creature gifted with intelligence by a sea elf named Meral, to whom it remained fiercely loyal even in death. The crab explained that harpies nesting on the rocky cliffs were responsible for the shipwrecks scattered around the base of the lighthouse, and it offered to retrieve treasure from the sunken wrecks — including a powerful weapon from a ship called the Star-Crossed Lover — if the party would help lay Meral's tormented spirit to rest. The crab admitted, with something resembling grief, that it was too large and too frightened to enter the cave where Meral's ghost now dwelled, unwilling to see its beloved companion in such a state.

The party descended into a damp, lichen-encrusted cave carved into a natural rock pillar near the causeway. There, the ghost of Meral materialized before them — a sea elf with gills, webbed hands and feet, her spectral garments drifting as though she were still submerged beneath the waves. She told them her story in a haunted voice: an orc cleric of Talos named Moesko had invaded the tower, killed her, and stolen her opalescent conch — her most prized spellcasting focus — keeping it as a trophy. Her spirit had been bound to the cave ever since, cursed to demand a quest of all who passed through, and to destroy those who refused. Most chillingly, she described Moesko himself: he had a gaping hole in his chest where his heart should have been, and he had wielded a strange weapon of odd construction that pulsed with dark magic unlike anything she had ever seen. The party agreed to confront Moesko and retrieve the conch, and Meral wished them well with quiet, sorrowful gratitude.

The group climbed the narrow switchback path that wound up the rocky outcropping toward the lighthouse tower, the eerie green pulse of the beacon throbbing above them like a slow heartbeat. They had barely rounded the final bend when three harpies swept out from the cliffs, their eyes gleaming with hunger. Two of the creatures opened their mouths and began to sing — a haunting, irresistible melody that wound its way into the minds of the climbers. Valphine and Brewbarry, still weakened from the earlier battle, felt their wills dissolve under the weight of the enchanting song, their eyes going glassy as the charm took hold. Vukradin alone shook off the effect, gripping his daggers as the harpies closed in — and the party braced for yet another desperate fight, high above the crashing waves below.

## Locations
### Ambush Site
The location in the wilderness where the party was attacked by orcs and their shaman. After the battle, the party rested here and discovered clues linking the cultists to the bandit leader known as the Carver.

### Tower of Storms
A stone building surmounted by a lighthouse tower, situated atop a rocky outcropping nearly surrounded by water and connected to the beach by a natural causeway. An eerie green light pulses from the beacon westward out to sea, accompanied by a sound like a slow-beating heart. Several destroyed ships are scattered around the rocks below.

### Meral's Cave
A damp, eight-foot-high cave encrusted with lichen, located within a natural rock pillar near the causeway leading to the Tower of Storms. It is haunted by the ghost of the sea elf Meral, who demands a quest of all who pass through.

## NPCs
### Orc Shaman
A powerful spellcaster leading the orc ambush. He transformed into a boar to survive the party's initial assault, then unleashed a devastating lightning bolt that nearly killed Brewbarry and Valphine before being brought down by Soma.

### Corin
A former bandit now traveling with the party as a camp companion. He emerged from hiding after the battle and identified the Carver's monogram on a ration pack found among the orc supplies, revealing a connection between the cultists and the bandit leader. He also warned the party that the Carver has agents in the local village.

### Ser Kaelen
A figure suspected by the party of being in league with the orcs or the Carver. The party notes that only Ser Kaelen and the town master knew of their travel plans, making him a prime suspect in orchestrating the ambush.

### The Carver
A local bandit chief whose distinctive 'C' monogram was found on high-quality rations carried by the orc ambushers, suggesting he paid off the Talos cultists to attack the party. Corin confirms he has never actually seen the Carver in person.

### The Talking Crab
A monstrously sized, sentient crab on the beach near the Tower of Storms that speaks Common. It was gifted intelligence by a sea elf named Meral and remains deeply loyal to her memory. It asks the party to put Meral's ghost to rest in exchange for treasure salvaged from the nearby shipwrecks.

### Meral
The ghost of a sea elf with gills, webbed hands, and webbed feet, haunting a cave near the Tower of Storms. She was killed by an orc cleric of Talos named Moesko, who stole her opalescent conch spellcasting focus. She curses all who pass through her cave to undertake a quest to retrieve it, killing those who refuse.

### Moesko
An orc cleric of Talos who invaded the Tower of Storms and killed the sea elf Meral. He is described as having a hole in his chest where his heart should be and wielding a strange weapon of odd construction that pulsated with magic. He keeps Meral's opalescent conch as a trophy.

### Harpies
A trio of winged monstrosities nesting on the rocky outcroppings near the lighthouse. They use their enchanting song to charm prey, successfully charming Valphine and Brewbarry as the party climbed the switchback path toward the tower.

## Items
### Tracking Pin
A pin that Vukradin believes was used by their enemies to track the party's movements. After the battle, he breaks it in two and plants the pieces on the battlefield to make it appear it was destroyed during the fight rather than deliberately discarded.

### The Anthropology of Talos
A book recovered from the orc ambushers that details the dogma of Talos, the deity of storms and destruction. Valphine uses it to confirm that Talos's priests are instructed to spread destruction wherever they go.

### Monogrammed Ration Pack
A well-packaged set of high-quality rations found among the orc shaman's supplies, sealed in an unusually careful manner. It bears a distinctive 'C' monogram that Corin identifies as belonging to the Carver, suggesting it was payment for the ambush.

### Opalescent Conch
A beautiful, shimmering shell that served as the sea elf Meral's spellcasting focus. It was stolen by the orc cleric Moesko as a trophy after he killed her, and its theft has cursed Meral to haunt the cave near the Tower of Storms.

## Spells
### Faerie Fire
A spell previously cast by Valphine to outline enemies in glowing light, granting advantage on attacks against them. It was dispelled when Valphine took damage and lost her concentration.

### Command
Cast twice by Vukradin during the battle. The first attempt, enhanced by bardic inspiration to raise the difficulty of the saving throw, was resisted by the orc shaman with a natural 20. The second casting successfully halted the shaman, preventing him from taking any action for a critical round of combat.

### Lightning Bolt
A powerful electrical discharge unleashed by the orc shaman that struck both Brewbarry and Valphine simultaneously, dealing massive damage and nearly killing them both. Brewbarry's rage reduced the damage he took.

### Guiding Bolt
Cast by Valphine at the orc shaman, launching a bolt of radiant energy that granted advantage to the next ally who attacked him, helping the party press their assault.

### Speak with Animals
Soma prepared to cast this spell to communicate with the giant crab on the beach, but was caught off guard and never completed the casting when the crab began speaking Common on its own.

### Harpy Luring Song
A magical melody sung by the trio of harpies as the party climbed the switchback path toward the lighthouse. The enchanting song successfully charmed Valphine and Brewbarry, leaving them vulnerable as the session ended on a cliffhanger.
