# Chapter 31 Brewbarry's Bloody Axe and the Beer Blight

**Date:** Feb 18th, 2026

## Summary

The party began by recapping their tangled web of alliances and feuds. Having recently discovered that Don-Jon met his end at dwarven hands before being consumed by a carrion crawler, and having forged an uneasy truce between the Stonetallow shield dwarves and the wererats who both claimed the mine, they now faced the delicate work of making that truce stick. Vukradin, questioning the oxymoronic clan name "Stonetallow," inadvertently triggered a thorough dwarven etymology lesson from Horia: the name derived from "stone to allow," a reference to the clan's founder—a disgruntled Gauntlgrym engineer who was the only dwarf who knew how to build a proper archway. The revelation crystallized everything about the Stonetallow clan: they were obsessed with doing things correctly, and they viewed the wererats' slapdash modifications to the mine as a personal affront to the craft.

Negotiations with the wererats proved the session's dramatic centerpiece. Vukradin's insight revealed what the party had suspected—the wererats had never wanted to mine; they were muscle, not laborers. Brewbarry, still caked in orc and ogre blood from the previous battle, approached the negotiation with an elegant brutality. When Zeleen Varnaster demanded an upfront payment of one thousand gold for six months of protection, Brewbarry countered by reframing the entire arrangement: the wererats had been chased from their homes, and now they were being offered shelter, Petunia's legendary cheese, and protection from eviction—why would they demand payment for a deal this good? The implied threat, underscored by the blood-drenched axe, landed perfectly. Zeleen, impressed, agreed to protect the mine for free, viewing it as defending their home and family. He offered Vukradin a piece of Petunia's cheese—described as a gouda-adjacent delicacy unlike anything else in the region.

The party then charted their next course. Zeleen warned them that orcs, ogres, and a dragon were ravaging the countryside, and suggested the Shrine of Savras for intelligence on the dragon's whereabouts. More alarmingly for Brewbarry, the dragon's raids were disrupting farming so severely that there would be no beer brewed this year—a crisis that elevated dragon-hunting to the highest priority. After weighing their many outstanding quests, the party decided to head toward Neverwinter, stopping at Dragonbarrow en route to retrieve the legendary dragon-slaying sword known as the Dragonblade.

Travel brought an unexpected encounter with The Falcon on the Triboar Trail. He was distraught—orcs and ogres (the Anchorites of Talos) had overrun his hunting lodge, and he blamed the party for never clearing the Woodland Manse, whose fireballs and lightning had driven them away. He was returning to Phandalin to settle debts and refused to return to the lodge. However, he offered Vukradin a particular rare wood for his music studio and proposed turning the lodge into a venue for musical performances once reclaimed. Brewbarry enthusiastically endorsed the idea—everything, he reasoned, tastes better with ale, including music.

Arriving at Dragonbarrow, the party found a thirty-foot-high hill of bone-white rock resembling outstretched talons. A centaur named Xanth approached peacefully and warned them of will-o'-wisps that haunted the barrow at night. Vukradin, having never encountered a centaur before, was deeply unsettled, but Soma calmed him, explaining centaurs were harmless woodland creatures. Xanth also revealed that Neverwinter Wood had been overrun by orcs allied with half-orc Anchorites performing dark rites at a circle of standing stones. Brewbarry connected the dots: these were the same orcs driving farmers from their land, which meant the beer crisis—and the anchorites—were next on the list after the Dragonblade.

Within Dragonbarrow's tomb, the party encountered the will-o'-wisps directly. Soma's summoned rat familiar scouted the western passage, discovering four sarcophagi and a musical instrument in one. When Soma had the rat dance back and forth, triggering a mysterious light on and off, the light retaliated with a lightning strike, killing the familiar instantly. The party wisely decided to avoid the lights. Heading east instead, they found two sealed sarcophagi. Brewbarry opened one despite Valphine's trap check revealing nothing—and was engulfed in corrosive dust. His Stone Endurance turned what should have been fourteen points of acid damage into a mere three, his dwarven resilience on full display. A second sarcophagus, opened at distance with his halberd, released another dust cloud harmlessly. Both were empty. The session closed with the party standing in the dark tomb, surrounded by empty coffins and hostile lights, the Dragonblade still hidden deeper within.

## Scenes

### Recap and Dwarven Etymology

**The session opens with the party reviewing their recent history and Horia illuminating the Stonetallow clan's peculiar origin.**

- The GM recaps the party's return to the mine after defeating orcs and ogres allied with the Carver.
- The party recounts discovering Don-Jon's death (killed by dwarves, eaten by a carrion crawler).
- The Stonetallow clan of shield dwarves is introduced: intensely proud, tracing their lineage to a disgruntled Gauntlgrym engineer.
- The dwarves view the wererats' modifications to the mine as architectural vandalism.
- Vukradin questions the name "Stonetallow," pointing out the oxymoron of stone and tallow/wax.
- Horia delivers a detailed etymology: the name derives from "stone to allow" — referring to allowing arches to pass, the clan's signature architectural achievement.
- The party proposes a division of labor: dwarves mine, wererats guard.

### Negotiating with the Wererats

**Brewbarry turns a potential coin payment into a blood-and-cheese deal through intimidation and clever reframing.**

- Vukradin's insight check reveals the wererats never wanted to mine—they give off gangster vibes and prefer muscle work.
- The party approaches Zeleen Varnaster, who initially asks if the dwarves are dead.
- The party proposes: dwarves do labor, wererats act as muscle and protect the mine as their territory.
- Zeleen is intrigued and demands 1,000 gold upfront for six months of protection.
- Brewbarry counters sharply: the wererats were chased from their homes and are now getting shelter, Petunia's cheese, and protection from eviction. Why demand payment?
- Brewbarry's blood-soaked axe provides a silent but effective visual argument.
- Zeleen agrees to protect the mine for free, reframing it as protecting their home and family.
- Zeleen offers Vukradin Petunia's cheese—described as Gouda-adjacent but superior.
- Zeleen warns that orcs, ogres, and a dragon are still active, suggesting the Shrine of Savras for dragon intel.
- Vukradin attempts to trick Zeleen about dragons polymorphing; Zeleen rolls a natural 20, sees through it entirely, and laughs it off as a good joke.
- Brewbarry reveals his full name: Brewbarry Root Smasher Ogalakadu (family name, nickname, clan name).

**(DM note):** Zeleen's natural 20 insight here cements him as a shrewd leader, not easily fooled—his agreement to the deal was genuine, not gullible.

### Planning the Next Adventure and Mine Logistics

**The party reviews their quest log and commits to Dragonbarrow and the Dragonblade.**

- Outstanding quests reviewed: Lyra/Emerald Enclave, Ser Kaelen's statue in Neverwinter, the Carver, the Dragonblade, and the dragon.
- The Petunia cheese persuasion quest is confirmed completed.
- Ser Kaelen has intel on one of the party's magical objects.
- A statue from the lighthouse must be returned to an art collector before House Markaster attacks.
- Sister Kella never contacted the party about the Carver—a loose thread.
- Vukradin votes to visit Elara 'Seasong' Meliamne in Neverwinter to unlock loot.
- Brewbarry argues the dragon is the most worthy challenge.
- The party decides: Dragonbarrow for the dragon-slaying sword, then onward to Neverwinter.
- A long rest is taken in the mine, now guarded by wererats and worked by dwarves.
- Horia and the wererats formalize the agreement with contracts.
- Horia estimates weekly earnings around 100 gold, possibly more with investment.

### Encounter with The Falcon on the Triboar Trail

**The party meets a humbled Falcon whose stolen valor and lost lodge paint a portrait of a man at rock bottom.**

- The Falcon is encountered on the Triboar Trail, distraught.
- He blames the party for never clearing the Woodland Manse (fireballs and lightning drove them away).
- Orcs and ogres (Anchorites of Talos) have overrun Falcon Lodge.
- The Falcon is returning to Phandalin to settle debts, refusing to return to the lodge.
- He offers Vukradin a particular rare wood for his music studio if the party clears the lodge.
- The Falcon proposes turning the lodge into a music venue for Vukradin and Fina, with ale.
- Brewbarry enthusiastically agrees: ale improves everything, including music.

### Arrival at Dragonbarrow and Centaur Warning

**Dragonbarrow looms, a centaur delivers ominous warnings, and Vukradin has a crisis about mythological hybrids.**

- Dragonbarrow described: 30-foot hill, flat top, bone-white rocks like outstretched talons.
- Xanth the centaur approaches peacefully.
- Vukradin is freaked out by the centaur, calling it an abomination.
- Soma calms Vukradin, explaining centaurs are woodland creatures, not threats.
- Xanth warns of will-o'-wisps haunting Dragonbarrow at night—restless spirits of the dead.
- Soma (druid nature check) recognizes the description as will-o'-wisps, confirmed by Meryl's prior teachings.
- Xanth reports Neverwinter Wood overrun by orcs and half-orc Anchorites of Talos performing dark rites at a standing-stone circle on a cave-ridden hill.
- Xanth offers to guide the party to the anchorites after Dragonbarrow.
- Brewbarry connects the anchorites to the farmers fleeing (and thus the beer crisis).
- Xanth recognizes Valphine as a famous cleric of Lathander.

### Entering the Dragonbarrow Tomb

**The party finds and opens the tomb, stepping into a burial mound teeming with hostile lights.**

- Valphine's high perception finds a secret entrance: a rock plug embedded in earth at the base of the hill.
- Valphine is disappointed—the entrance is too obvious to feel like a real secret.
- Brewbarry insists on brute strength over magic. Soma offers Mold Earth "help" while letting Brewbarry think he did it alone.
- Brewbarry rolls a 22 on strength; the plug opens easily without help.
- Brewbarry gains inspiration from the confidence boost.
- The party enters and sees yellow lights from multiple directions within.
- The party discusses whether these are the will-o'-wisps Xanth warned about.

### Rat Familiar Scouts the Western Passage

**Soma's rat discovers sarcophagi, a musical instrument, and the lethal nature of the tomb's guardians.**

- Soma summons a rat familiar and establishes a telepathic connection (sight and sound).
- The rat scouts the western hallway, finding four sarcophagi in alcoves.
- Northwest sarcophagus contains loot (a musical instrument).
- Southeast sarcophagus is sealed too tightly for the rat to enter.
- Two other sarcophagi contain moldy bones of adventurers.
- A light in the area extinguishes, then reignites when the rat moves past it.
- Soma has the rat dance back and forth, triggering the light on/off repeatedly.
- The light attacks with lightning, dealing 9 damage and instantly killing the rat.
- The party resolves to avoid the lights.

### Eastern Passage and Trapped Sarcophagi

**The party explores eastward, opens trapped sarcophagi, and finds only dust and emptiness.**

- The party proceeds east to circumvent the will-o'-wisps.
- They find two sealed sarcophagi in alcoves and a narrow two-foot-wide passageway.
- Valphine checks for traps (perception 20) and finds nothing suspicious.
- Brewbarry opens the first sarcophagus; a corrosive dust cloud fills a 10-foot area.
- Brewbarry fails his Dexterity save and takes 14 acid damage.
- **Stone Endurance activates:** Brewbarry rolls 1d12+2, reducing damage by 11. Final damage: 3.
- The sarcophagus is completely empty—no corpse, no bones.
- Brewbarry uses his halberd to open the second sarcophagus from 10 feet away.
- Another dust cloud emerges but disperses harmlessly.
- The second sarcophagus is also empty.
- The session ends at 10:30 PM with the party in the dark tomb, two empty coffins behind them, dangerous lights ahead, and the Dragonblade still hidden within.
