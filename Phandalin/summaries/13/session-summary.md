# Chapter 13 Confronting Adabra and Learning of the Interventionists

Date: Early Taraskh 1495

## Summary

The party returns to Phandalin with Soma burning with righteous fury. She marches to confront Adabra, who has set up shop next to Garaele's house. Lyra and her Enclave agents follow, equally furious. Soma's opening is a raw, emotional accusation: "What the hell! What was that!"

Adabra turns cold. She speaks with patronizing condescension, accusing Soma of letting "interventionists" fill her head with noise. Soma stammers that they didn't help Lyra — the ritual failed — but she couldn't let them die. Adabra presses for details; the party realizes they must lie to avoid revealing they fought and killed the treant.

Vukradin steps in as the party's diplomat. His initial half-truths are shaky, so he pivots to charm and deflection — offering beer, changing the subject, softening the confrontation. When Adabra asks about the treant, Vukradin delivers his masterpiece: "He's in stable condition." A lie so perfectly ambiguous that Adabra assumes the treant survived. Lyra, knowing it's dead, nearly explodes — "You are insane!" — but Vukradin manages to de-escalate.

Adabra delivers another philosophical lecture, this time equating interventionism to musical censorship: "Is there bad music? No, there's only music." Vukradin, fresh from his realization that popular music has value, firmly disagrees: "There is music that is bad." Soma backs him up. Adabra, momentarily off-balance, hands Vukradin five conflict-free gold and leaves.

With Adabra defused, the party sells their dire wolf pelts. Linene at the Lionshield Coster pays double, excited about their planar origin — her network's interplanar shipping device has been nonfunctional, and she wants the materials studied. Vukradin trades information freely, to Valphine's private irritation.

That evening's performance at Stonehill Tavern is off. Vukradin's vibe is wrong — his people-pleasing has cost him his edge. Valphine shines, and Linene even praises her as the ensemble's highlight. Vukradin takes the compliment with forced grace.

The Emerald Enclave agents (Thomas, Marian) join the party for drinks. They explain Lyra acted without authorization — the Enclave was deadlocked over whether to intervene. She saw an opportunity, took it, and failed. Valphine casually describes Underdark torture rituals in response to Thomas's question about difficult rituals, horrifying the surface-dwelling agents. The agents politely decline Vukradin's offer to join the party, citing their oaths.

Ser Kaelen approaches and asks about the shipwreck treasures again — he's made little progress on identification. The conversation underscores that Vukradin's trust in him is eroding.

## Memorable Moments

> "What the hell! What was that!"
> — Soma, confronting Adabra

**Shell Sprout No More.** Soma's raw fury is the first time she's truly angry rather than disappointed or dismissive. Her worldview has been shattered, and Adabra is the one who handed her the hammer.

> "He's in stable condition."
> — Vukradin, lying about the treant's fate

**Masterpiece of Misdirection.** Vukradin's three-word lie perfectly threads the needle: "stable condition" is technically true (the treant is stable in death), and Adabra's assumptions do the rest. Lyra's fury at hearing the lie sells the moment.

> "If you cannot see the evil this woman represents and her fanaticism and its danger, then you are all fools."
> — Lyra, storming off

**The Interventionist's Wrath.** Lyra's exit speech crystallizes the escalating stakes: the Enclave is divided, the ritual failed, and Adabra is actively protecting the planar corruption. From Lyra's perspective, Adabra is complicit in the desecration of the natural world.

> "It's not a ritual? Is that not typical in the Overbright? Do you do this to yourself or to other people? To whoever wants it."
> — Valphine, casually discussing self-harm rituals

**Underdark Normal.** Valphine's nonchalant description of Menzoberranzan's torture rituals — and her confusion that surface-dwellers find it unusual — is the most revealing moment of her character so far. The Enclave agents are visibly horrified.

> "Yes, they do involve blood and death and torture."
> — Valphine, answering whether she's seen difficult rituals

**Matter-of-Fact Darkness.** Valphine's response to Thomas's question is delivered without malice or glee — just the flat reality of her upbringing. It's more unsettling than any villainous monologue.

## Scenes

### Confronting Adabra
- Soma marches to Adabra's location, furious.
- Lyra and Enclave agents follow, equally angry.
- Soma's opening accusation is raw and unfiltered.
- Adabra turns cold, speaking with patronizing condescension.
- **Dialogue exchange:** Adabra accuses Soma of being corrupted by "interventionist noise."
- Soma tries to claim the ritual wasn't completed (true) and they "interrupted" the interventionists (partial truth).
- Adabra presses for details about the treant's fate.
- Vukradin steps in as diplomat. His "stable condition" lie works perfectly on Adabra.
- Lyra nearly explodes: "You are insane!"

**(DM note):** Vukradin's successful deception here is a critical turning point. If Adabra knew the treant was dead, the confrontation would have escalated into open hostility. His social skills prevented a faction war in Phandalin's town square.

### De-escalation and Philosophy
- Vukradin offers beer, deflects with music.
- Adabra equates interventionism to musical censorship: "Is there bad music? There's only music."
- Vukradin disagrees: "There is music that is bad" — his new philosophy of popular appeal.
- Soma backs him: "Like, there are some planes that are bad for this one."
- Adabra is thrown off-balance by the united front.
- Hands Vukradin five conflict-free gold as a gesture of goodwill.
- Leaves, still believing the treant is alive.

**(DM note):** Adabra's "music is just music" analogy is philosophically consistent with her naturalism — she genuinely believes nothing is inherently bad, only different. Vukradin's rejection of this position marks his character development: he's choosing connection over purity.

### Selling the Pelts
- Corbin offers to find a fence; Vukradin declines.
- Barthen's Provisions offers a paltry sum.
- Lionshield Coster (Linene): pays more than double.
- Linene asks detailed questions about where/how the pelts were found.
- Vukradin answers truthfully — Valphine notes he's giving away valuable information for free.
- Linene explains: her shipping network uses an interplanar device that stopped working.
- Pelts may help her bosses understand the planar disruption.
- Vukradin accepts the price. Valphine thinks he'll never own a music studio at this rate.

**(DM note, not yet known to party):** The "interplanar device" the Lionshield Coster uses is connected to the same planar magic affecting the Whispering Wood. If their network is disrupted, the planar corruption may be affecting trade across the region, not just the forest.
