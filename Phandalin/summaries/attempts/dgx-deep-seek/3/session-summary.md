# Chapter 3 To Find a Shapeshifter

Date: 02-01 of Taraskh 1495

## Summary

The party's journey to Gnomengarde begins with a mountain pass ogre encounter that mirrors the last — Vukradin attempts negotiation (offering food), the ogre insists on eating Soma ("I want turtle!"), and a slog of a fight ensues where nobody can land a hit until Soma's poison and Valphine's ogre-knocking bullet turn the tide. The ogre is killed mid-retreat, prompting Soma's second protest against Vukradin's "killing fleeing enemies" policy.

Arriving at Gnomengarde, the party finds a stunning cave complex with a waterfall, mushroom islands, and multiple cliffside entrances 20–30 feet above a pool. Vukradin's inevitable search for a "secret treasure room behind the waterfall" yields nothing but wet clothes. Guards Pog and Ulla are paranoid about shapeshifters, almost attacking the party until Brewbarry's declaration ("I am Brewbarry, killer of Ogres!") and a bureaucratic debate about standing orders saves them. Soma sums it up: "Bureaucracy saved us."

Navigating deeper into the gnomish hold, the party bypasses a spinning blade death trap (by redirecting to a safer route) and encounters Facktore's automated crossbow turret — which they disable rather than destroy. Pushing on to the inventors' workshop, they meet Fibblestib and Dabbledob, who are arguing about how to cure King Korboz's madness. Valphine offers clerical healing; the gnomes reject it with prejudice ("We are gnomes, we invent our way out of illness"). Vukradin offers music therapy instead.

King Gnercli, the co-king, cuts through the bureaucracy: kill the shapeshifter creature terrorizing the caves, get the Hat of Wizardry. The party tracks the creature to the cellar, where a barrel transforms into a mimic. Brewbarry's halberd gets stuck (adhesion mechanic). The mimic is poison-immune, makes Vicious Mockery effective instead. A mid-battle theology debate erupts: Soma declares constructs and extraplanar creatures "abominations"; Vukradin (an aasimar) takes offense. Soma's conclusion — "You're just another creature born into this world" — leaves Vukradin spluttering about poetic license.

Brewbarry adapts to handaxes when his halberd is stuck, and Soma finishes the mimic. The inventors argue over the corpse's entrails. Vukradin collects the Hat of Wizardry and 50 gp from Harbin, learns about a legendary play and an actor named "Bob" from Triboar — and decides he wants the script.

## Scenes

### Mountain Pass Ogre — Combat 1
- **Opening conditions:** Narrow mountain valley, 5-foot-wide path with steep walls on either side. Single ogre blocking the route to Gnomengarde. Bright daylight.
- **Round-by-round highlights:** Vukradin offers food, ogre wants "turtle" (Soma). Soma shouts they killed its brother (the road ogre). Nobody can hit for several rounds — Valphine misses a prone ogre with a mace ("broad side of a barn" remark), Brewbarry's halberd clangs off stone. Soma's poison wears the ogre down. Ogre lands a blow on Brewbarry that does nothing through rage. Ogre breaks and runs. Valphine shoots it in the back. Vukradin delivers the killing blow from hiding.
- **(DM tactical note):** The party's collective inability to hit a prone ogre for multiple rounds highlights a tactical weakness: they have no method to gain advantage or force rerolls. This is a party without a dedicated martial archer or reliable save-based damage. Soma's Poison Spray is their most reliable damage source.
- **Key tactical moments:** Valphine's magic bullet again used to prone the ogre — excellent resource use. Brewbarry's rage demonstrating its value against single heavy hitters. The party's inability to finish a fleeing enemy faster shows they lack ranged finishing options.
- **Enemy behavior:** The ogre targets Brewbarry when he's the biggest threat. Runs when badly wounded — same morale break pattern as the road ogre. This suggests ogres have a "fight evaluation" instinct, not mindless aggression.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Poison Spray — effective but slow; Rage — negated the ogre's best hit; Magic bullet (knock-prone item) — continues to be the party's MVP.
- **PC resource decisions:** Vukradin withheld Starry Wisp until the killing moment. Brewbarry used rage when engaged in melee. Soma used poison each round.
- **Post-combat:** The ogre had a whistle, coins, and a key on its body. Soma protests again about killing a fleeing enemy.

### Gnomengarde Entrance
- The party arrives at the cave complex: waterfall, pool, mushroom islands (2 ft. tall, red/green/purple).
- Vukradin wades in to examine mushrooms: red ones are oily, green can be turned into flour, purple can be fermented into mushroom wine.
- Vukradin insists on finding a secret treasure room behind the waterfall. There isn't one. He taps, pokes, and prods solid rock.
- The party climbs a 20-foot cliff to reach the guard post entrance. Valphine and Brewbarry insist on a rope.

### Pog and Ulla's Guard Post
- Pog (male gnome) and Ulla (female gnome) are paranoid about shapeshifters.
- Soma: "Would we take these shapes if we were shapechangers?" Ulla: "That's what a shapechanger would say!"
- Vukradin argues: "Your standing orders are only to attack shapechangers; your interpretation would lead to attacking anyone. Is that the intent?"
- Brewbarry breaks the deadlock by declaring his identity and his ex-Uthgardt status.
- Debate about whether the "attack Uthgardtians" order was renewed. Soma: "Bureaucracy saved us."
- Vukradin plays gnomish music to build rapport.

### The Death Trap and Facktore's Crossbow Turret
- The direct route to the inventors' workshop has spinning blade turnstiles — a death trap.
- Vukradin backtracks instead of attempting it, taking the "long way" as Ulla instructs.
- Encounter with Facktore's automated rotating crossbow turret. Vukradin disables the device without killing Facktore.
- The party climbs down, wades the river, picks more mushrooms, and climbs back up to the sleeping quarters.

### The Inventors' Workshop
- Fibblestib and Dabbledob are arguing: sanity ray vs. straitjacket with no arm holes.
- Valphine offers clerical healing: "I am a cleric." Gnomes: "We are gnomes, we invent our way out of illness."
- The inventors explain: two gnomes (Warren and Orrin) went missing; King Korboz claims a shapechanger did it.
- Brewbarry suggests talking instead of torture. Valphine is disappointed.
- Vukradin offers music therapy. Fibblestib offers to build a piano.

### The Dual Monarchy
- Korboz is hysterical; Gnercli is exasperated.
- King Gnercli cuts a deal: kill the shapeshifter, get the Hat of Wizardry.
- Vukradin is disappointed — "Not exactly going to terrorize a dragon with that."
- Soma reminds him they get 50 gp from Harbin for bringing *something* back, not necessarily useful.
- Vukradin accepts the morally questionable deal.

### Cellar Mimic — Combat 2
- **Opening conditions:** Twelve 40-gallon barrels in wide alcoves with wooden braces. Two half-full barrels with spigots. The party knows to expect a shapeshifter but doesn't know what form. Valphine points a crossbow at the first barrel — it transforms into a mimic.
- **Round-by-round highlights:** Brewbarry charges with halberd, hits, weapon gets stuck (adhesion mechanic). Mimic attacks with pseudopod, misses Brewbarry. Soma casts Poison Spray — no effect (mimic is immune to poison). Valphine stays well clear of Soma's spell area after the Ice Knife incident. Vukradin uses Vicious Mockery — the psychic damage works. Mid-battle theology debate about constructs, extraplanar creatures, and aasimar origin. Brewbarry pulls out handaxes when halberd is stuck. Soma finishes the mimic.
- **Key tactical moments:** Brewbarry's weapon adhesion was a serious problem — he had to switch weapons mid-combat. The party learned nothing about the mimic's vulnerabilities before engaging, so the poison immunity was a nasty surprise. Vicious Mockery's psychic damage bypassing the mimic's natural resistances was key. The theology debate mid-combat was pure roleplay, but it cost action economy.
- **(DM tactical note):** The mimic's adhesion mechanic caught Brewbarry in a bad spot. If the party had scouted the cellar more carefully or set a trap to identify the creature's nature before engaging, they could have prepared appropriately. The players' mid-combat roleplay debate, while entertaining, was a tactical misstep — spending actions debating the nature of aasimar while a mimic is eating your weapon is suboptimal.
- **(DM tactical note):** Soma's Poison Spray being wasted on an immune target reinforces a knowledge gap: the party has no method to identify monster resistances before acting. A Knowledge check house rule or a Perception-based "you notice the barrel doesn't react to the gas" telegraph might help future encounters.
- **Enemy behavior:** The mimic attacked the nearest threat (Brewbarry) and did not reposition or retreat. No evidence of it communicating or coordinating.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Poison Spray — completely wasted (immunity); Vicious Mockery — excellent, bypassed natural resistances; Halberd damage — good but created an adhesion problem.
- **PC resource decisions:** Brewbarry wisely swapped to handaxes when his main weapon was compromised. Valphine positioned safely away from Soma's spells — lesson learned from chapter 2. Soma continued using Poison Spray despite the first round showing no effect — wasted action.
- **Post-combat:** Scouring the room yields nothing the gnomes forgot to claim. The inventors discuss building a construct from the mimic's entrails. Soma silently disapproves.

### Vukradin's Political Masterclass
- Vukradin interrogates the dual-monarchy system, exposing its weakness: no tiebreaking mechanism.
- He asks about seniority, king elections, and whether three kings would be "more difficult."
- Korboz seizes on the "senior king" argument — Gnercli is furious.
- Vukradin collects the Hat of Wizardry.
- Fibblestib proposes a bicameral legislature with a unitary executive. Dabbledob wants more kings for voting purposes.
- Vukradin suggests one of the inventors should be king — chaos ensues.

### Return to Phandalin
- Vukradin collects 50 gp from Harbin.
- At the Stonehill Inn, Toblen tells the tale of Bob — a demon-costume actor from Triboar whose play had a spectacular run.
- Vukradin becomes obsessed: "Perhaps we can do a revival!"
- The party heads toward Umbrage Hill next.

## NPCs
- **Pog** — Male gnome guard at Gnomengarde entrance. Paranoid about shapeshifters. Follows standing orders to the letter. Likes gnomish music.
- **Ulla** — Female gnome guard. Shares Pog's paranoia but is more politically aware of standing-order ambiguities. Decides the party can pass based on Brewbarry's ex-Uthgardt status.
- **Facktore** — Deranged gnomish inventor. Built an automated rotating crossbow turret. Tests it on the party. Fled when disabled. Left alive.
- **Fibblestib** — Gnomengarde inventor. Proposes a sanity ray for King Korboz. Favors bicameral legislature with a unitary executive. Overly enthusiastic about building things from monster parts. Offers to build a piano.
- **Dabbledob** — Gnomengarde inventor. Proposes a straitjacket with no arm holes for the mad king. Prefers more kings for voting. Enjoys one-upping Fibblestib.
- **King Korboz** — Co-king of Gnomengarde. Hysterical and paranoid about shapeshifters. Seizes on the "senior king" argument. Believes he should have preferential treatment over the "junior" co-king.
- **King Gnercli** — Co-king of Gnomengarde. Exasperated with Korboz's paranoia and the political dysfunction. Pragmatic. Strikes the deal with the party. Prefers to let adventurers solve problems.
- **Warren and Orrin** — Two gnomes who went missing. Presumed victims of the mimic.

## Items
