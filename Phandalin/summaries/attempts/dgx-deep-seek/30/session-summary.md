# Chapter 30 A Cheesy Compromise in the Mine

Date: Eleasis 1495 (late summer)

## Summary

The battle outside the mine continues as waves of orcs and ogres press the attack. Valphine's divine guardians devastate enemies who charge into her radiant aura — orc after orc falls to celestial fire. Brewbarry stands as an imposing figure, his red-glowing eyes and booming voice striking terror into enemies before his halberd cleaves through them.

A second wave of enemies emerges from around the hill. Soma rolls her Flaming Sphere into the enemy ranks while her bear claws and teeth tear through survivors. One orc lands a devastating blow on Brewbarry — but between his rage and the dread helm's protection, he barely flinches. Brewbarry turns, glares, and says "You're next" before killing the orc. The ogres are more resilient but fall to coordinated attacks, with Brewbarry's thrown axes delivering the killing blows.

Post-combat, the party searches the fallen. They find alchemist's fire, a pouch of coins and gems, and a symbol of Talos — confirming the ogres once served the Carver and have since shifted allegiances.

Zeleen Varnaster, the wererat leader, emerges and expresses relief. She reports that Don-Jon Raskin is dead — supposedly by carrion crawler. When pressed, Zeleen evasively says "kill is such a strong word," revealing Don-Jon was "conveniently lured to his death." The party negotiates for Don-Jon's share of the mine in exchange for dealing with the carrion crawler. Valphine presents the wererats with Petunia's cheese — a local delicacy that delights them.

Valphine investigates Don-Jon's death scene. The evidence is clear: Don-Jon died in combat with someone else — the carrion crawler merely ate his corpse. Dwarf footprints lead away from the scene, pointing to the true killers.

Soma speaks with the carrion crawler through magic. The creature speaks in rapid wet clicks: it has been feasting on "deep stone folk" (dwarves) and "false rats" (wererats) who fight each other. It simply waits for battles to end and enjoys the spoils. Soma convinces it to leave for better hunting — the mine will soon be noisy and devoid of easy food. The crawler departs. In its lair: goggles of darkvision and Don-Jon's chain.

Following the dwarf tracks, the party finds five dwarves led by Horia. Horia produces a contract — Don-Jon hired them to work the mine. Don-Jon told the dwarves he found "a sucker in some bard fellow" (Vukradin) who convinced the wererats to work for cheese. The argument over this cheap labor turned deadly. Horia warns that the wererats are incompetent miners — the operation will collapse in months. The party proposes a compromise: dwarves do skilled mining, wererats provide menial labor and security. Horia wants an apology from the wererats for killing dwarves. The party begins renegotiating the dwarves' original 20% share contract.

**From Beyond the Grave.** Don-Jon's last words to the dwarves reveal his true opinion of the party. He thought the bard (Vukradin) was a fool and the cheese deal was a joke. Vukradin may not find this flattering.

> "Wait, the cheese is REAL?"
> — A wererat, tasting Petunia's cheese

**Cheese Diplomacy.** The wererats are genuinely delighted by the cheese. Valphine's delivery solidifies the deal in a way no contract ever could.

> "Deep stone folk who smell of iron and sweat. False rats who smell of musk and bad blood. I wait. They fight. I eat."
> — The Carrion Crawler

**The Neutral Party.** The carrion crawler's philosophy is simple: stay out of dwarf-wererat conflicts, let them kill each other, feast on the leftovers. It's been living the dream.

> — Horia, offended by Valphine's statement

**Faith and Finance.** Valphine's blunt statement that "contracts exist only to cheat people" deeply offends the dwarven sense of honor and legality. Horia questions whether she truly serves a sun god.

## Scenes

### COMBAT — Mine Battle (Waves)
- **Opening conditions:** Outside Mountain's Toe Mine. Party vs. 2 ogres + 8 orcs (from previous session continuation). Open terrain. Wererats inside the mine. Second wave emerges from around the hill.
- **Wave 1 continuation:** Party has the AoE advantage.
- Valphine's Spirit Guardians — radiant aura devastates charging orcs. Orc after orc falls to celestial fire, unable to resist the divine damage.
- Brewbarry (Dread Helm, glowing red eyes) — booming voice terrifies enemies. Halberd cleaves through them.
- One orc lands a perfect devastating blow on Brewbarry — but between Rage damage resistance and Dread Helm protection, Brewbarry barely flinches.
- Brewbarry turns to the orc: "You're next." Then kills him.
- **Wave 2:** More enemies emerge from around the hill.
- Soma rolls Flaming Sphere into enemy ranks. Claws and teeth finish survivors.
- Ogres prove more resilient — they absorb multiple hits.
- Brewbarry's thrown axes deliver the killing blows to the last ogres.
- **Post-combat:** Party searches fallen.
- Loot: alchemist's fire, pouch of coins/gems, symbol of Talos.
- **(DM tactical note):** The party's AoE strategy (Spirit Guardians + Flaming Sphere + Slow from previous session) is highly effective against melee-focused orc and ogre enemies. This fight was decisively won by area denial.
- **(DM tactical note):** Brewbarry's Dread Helm appears to provide intimidation benefits — enemies failing morale checks against him. His "You're next" intimidation check on the orc was roleplayed, not a mechanical ability, but the DM may have applied fear effects from the helm.
- **(DM tactical note):** The ogres carried Talos symbols — confirming the Cult of Talos is expanding its reach beyond the Woodland Manse into ogre tribes. The Carver's influence may be waning.

### The Wererats and Don-Jon's Fate
- Zeleen Varnaster emerges — relieved the party arrived.
- Reports Don-Jon Raskin is dead. By carrion crawler.
- Party presses — Zeleen: "Kill is such a strong word."
- Reveals Don-Jon was "conveniently lured to his death."
- The wererats are not innocent in his demise.
- Party negotiates: they get Don-Jon's share of the mine in exchange for dealing with the carrion crawler.
- Valphine presents Petunia's cheese — the wererats are delighted.
- **(DM note):** The party now effectively owns Don-Jon's share of the mine. Combined with the dwarves' contract and the wererat arrangement, this is becoming a complex stakeholder situation.

### Valphine's Investigation
- Valphine examines the death scene.
- **Evidence:** Don-Jon died in combat — NOT eaten by the carrion crawler.
- The crawler merely feasted on the corpse afterward.
- Dwarf footprints lead away from the scene.
- **(DM note):** Valphine's investigation skills catch the deception. The wererats are lying about Don-Jon's death, but the party now knows the dwarves are the actual killers.

### Carrion Crawler Negotiation
- Soma speaks with the creature via magic.
- Carrion crawler speaks in rapid wet clicks.
- It's been feasting on "deep stone folk" (dwarves) and "false rats" (wererats) who fight each other.
- It doesn't attack — it waits for conflict to end and scavenges.
- Soma argues: the war will end soon. The mine will become noisy and devoid of easy food.
- The carrion crawler agrees to leave for better hunting grounds.
- **Lair search:** Goggles of Darkvision, Don-Jon's chain.
- **(DM tactical note):** Soma's diplomatic approach to the carrion crawler is an excellent example of non-combat problem-solving. The creature was not inherently hostile — it was just exploiting an opportunity. Talking was the right call.

### The Dwarves — Horia's Truth
- Following dwarf tracks, party finds five dwarves led by Horia.
- Horia produces a contract signed by Don-Jon.
- **The truth about Don-Jon's death:**
  - Don-Jon hired the dwarves to work the mine.
  - He told them he found "a sucker in some bard fellow" (Vukradin).
  - He bragged that the wererats were working for cheap cheese.
  - He planned to dismiss the dwarves as unnecessary.
  - The argument turned deadly.
- Horia: Don-Jon started the fight. The dwarves finished it.
- Horia warns: wererats are INCOMPETENT miners.
  - They'll cause the whole operation to collapse in months.
  - Unsafe practices, improper techniques.
- **Party proposes compromise:**
  - Dwarves do skilled mining work.
  - Wererats handle menial labor + security.
  - Each group plays to its strengths.
- Horia agrees in principle — but wants an apology from the wererats for killing dwarves in their initial conflicts.
- Valphine: "Contracts exist only to cheat people." — Deeply offends Horia.
- Party begins renegotiating the dwarves' original 20% share contract, hoping to reduce their cut.

**(DM note):** The mine now has three stakeholders: the party (via Don-Jon's share, acquired through negotiation), the dwarves (contractual 20%, under negotiation), and the wererats (cheese-based labor agreement). This is a complex three-way arrangement that could collapse if any party feels slighted.

**(DM note):** Don-Jon's death was largely his own fault — he tried to double-cross both the wererats and the dwarves. Vukradin was the "sucker" in his eyes, which may sting the bard's pride.

## NPCs

- **Zeleen Varnaster** — Wererat leader of the Whiskered Gang. Relieved the party saved the mine. Evasive about Don-Jon's death. Grateful for the cheese. **(GM note):** Zeleen will likely try to maintain the arrangement as long as cheese keeps coming. Her loyalty is to her gang, not the party.

Willing to compromise but wants acknowledgment from the wererats. **(GM note):** Horia is reasonable but proud. If the apology issue is resolved, he'll be a reliable partner. His warning about the wererats' incompetence is likely accurate.

- **Don-Jon Raskin (dead)** — Former mine overseer. Killed by dwarves after he tried to double-cross them. His body was eaten by the carrion crawler. His share of the mine now belongs to the party. His last words revealed he thought Vukradin was a fool and the cheese deal was a joke.
