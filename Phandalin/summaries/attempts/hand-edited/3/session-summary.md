# Chapter 3 To Find a Shapeshifter

**Date:** 02-01 of Taraskh 1495

---

## Summary

The party's journey to Gnomengarde begins with a mountain pass ogre encounter that mirrors the last — Vukradin attempts negotiation, the ogre insists on eating Soma ("I want turtle!"), and a comedy of misses unfolds where nobody can land a hit. Valphine, swinging at the prone ogre, is met with Soma's cutting remark: "A broad side of a barn would have been harder to miss." Brewbarry's halberd clangs off stone. Soma's poison wears the ogre down. Valphine's magic bullet knocks it prone. Despite a heavy ogre blow that would have felled a normal man, Brewbarry's rage renders it harmless — and the ogre, stunned, flees. Valphine shoots it in the back. Vukradin delivers the killing blow from hiding, prompting Soma's second protest against killing fleeing enemies.

Arriving at Gnomengarde, the party finds a stunning cave complex with a roaring waterfall, a shallow pool with mushroom islands, and multiple cave openings on ledges 20-30 feet above. The inevitable search for a "secret treasure room behind the waterfall" yields nothing but wet clothes. Guards Pog and Ulla are paranoid about shapeshifters, nearly attacking until Brewbarry's declaration ("I am Brewbarry, killer of Ogres!") triggers a bureaucratic debate about standing orders — whether the order to attack Uthgardtians on sight was renewed at the last meeting. Soma's verdict: "Bureaucracy saved us."

Navigating deeper, the party bypasses a spinning blade death trap (by redirecting to a safer route) and encounters Facktore's automated crossbow turret — which they disable rather than destroy. Pushing on to the inventors' workshop, they meet Fibblestib and Dabbledob, who are arguing about how to cure King Korboz's madness — a sanity ray versus a straitjacket with no arm holes. Valphine offers clerical healing; the gnomes reject it with prejudice ("We are gnomes, we invent our way out of illness"). Vukradin offers music therapy instead. Fibblestib offers to build a piano.

King Gnercli, the exasperated co-king, cuts through the political dysfunction: kill the shapeshifter creature, get the Hat of Wizardry. The party tracks the creature to the cellar, where a barrel transforms into a mimic. Brewbarry's halberd gets stuck (adhesion mechanic). The mimic is poison-immune, making Soma's Poison Spray useless and Vicious Mockery — psychic damage — the effective counter. A mid-battle theology debate erupts: Soma declares constructs and extraplanar creatures "abominations"; Vukradin (an aasimar) takes personal offense. Soma's flat conclusion — "You're just another creature born into this world. There is no celestial touching here" — leaves the bard spluttering about poetic license.

Brewbarry adapts to handaxes when his halberd is stuck, and Soma finishes the mimic. Back in the throne room, Vukradin's political dissection of the dual-monarchy system exposes its critical weakness — no tiebreaking mechanism — and sets the kings against each other. The inventors overhear and begin proposing rival governance models. Chaos is fully planted.

Vukradin collects the Hat of Wizardry and 50 gp from Harbin, learns at the Stonehill Inn about a legendary Triboar play featuring an actor named "Bob" — and becomes immediately obsessed with finding the script.

## Scenes

### Mountain Pass Ogre — Combat

**Opening conditions:** Narrow mountain valley, 5-foot-wide path with steep walls on either side. Single ogre blocking the route to Gnomengarde. Bright daylight. The ogre is whistling cheerfully — Valphine notes this distinguishes it from enslaved Menzoberranzan ogres.

- Vukradin offers food; the ogre wants "turtle" (Soma). Soma retaliates by shouting they killed its brother.
- A comedy of misses ensues. Valphine's mace swings wide ("broad side of a barn"). Brewbarry's halberd clangs off stone. Soma's poison is the only consistent damage source.
- The ogre lands a blow on Brewbarry that does nothing through rage. The ogre stares in disbelief.
- The ogre breaks and runs. Valphine shoots it in the back with a crossbow bolt — wounded but still running.
- Brewbarry cannot bring it down. Vukradin delivers the killing blow from hiding with Starry Wisp.
- **(DM tactical note):** The party's collective inability to hit a prone ogre for multiple rounds highlights a tactical weakness: they have no method to gain advantage or force rerolls. This is a party without a dedicated martial archer or reliable save-based damage. Soma's Poison Spray is their most consistent damage source.
- **Key tactical moments:** Valphine's magic bullet again used to prone the ogre — excellent resource management. Brewbarry's rage demonstrating its value against single heavy hitters. The party's inability to finish a fleeing enemy faster shows they lack ranged finishing options.
- **Enemy behavior:** The ogre targets Brewbarry when he's the biggest threat. Runs when badly wounded — same morale break pattern as the road ogre (chapter 2). This suggests ogres have a "fight evaluation" instinct, not mindless aggression.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Poison Spray — effective but slow; Rage — negated the ogre's best hit; Magic bullet — continues to be the party's MVP utility item.
- **Post-combat:** The ogre had a whistle, coins, and a key on its body. Soma protests again about killing a fleeing enemy. Vukradin's defense: "He hated music."

### Gnomengarde Entrance
- The party arrives at the cave complex: waterfall, pool, mushroom islands (2 ft. tall, red/green/purple).
- Vukradin wades in to examine mushrooms: red ones are oily, green can be turned into flour, purple fermented into mushroom wine. Soma is delighted at the prospect of mushroom wine; Vukradin assures her it "tastes as bad as it sounds."
- Vukradin insists on finding a secret treasure room behind the waterfall. There isn't one. He taps, pokes, and prods solid rock in soaking wet clothes.
- The party climbs a 20-foot cliff to reach the guard post entrance. Valphine and Brewbarry insist on a rope. Soma climbs down, takes the rope, and climbs back up with visible exasperation.

### Pog and Ulla's Guard Post
- Pog (male gnome) and Ulla (female gnome) are paranoid about shapeshifters. They shout challenges in Gnomish.
- Soma: "Would we take these shapes if we were shapechangers?" Ulla: "That's what a shapechanger would say!"
- Vukradin launches a careful argument: "Your standing orders are only to attack shapechangers; your interpretation would lead to attacking anyone. Is that the intent?" A clever bit of logic that gives the gnomes pause.
- Brewbarry breaks the deadlock: "I am Brewbarry, killer of Ogres! I am no shapeshifter!"
- Ulla recognizes him as Uthgardt — triggers a debate: does the standing order to attack Uthgardtians on sight still apply? Was it renewed? The gnomes decide it wasn't, and let the party pass. Soma: "Bureaucracy saved us."
- Vukradin builds rapport by playing gnomish music. The gnomes seem pleased.

### The Death Trap and Facktore's Crossbow Turret
- The direct route to the inventors' workshop has two spinning blade turnstiles (counterclockwise and clockwise) with blades spaced 1 foot apart — a death trap.
- Vukradin backtracks instead of attempting it, taking the "long way" Ulla described. He encounters Facktore's automated rotating crossbow turret.
- Vukradin disables the device without killing Facktore, who flees the scene.
- The party climbs down, wades the river, picks more mushrooms from the islands, and climbs back up to the sleeping quarters.

### The Inventors' Workshop
- Fibblestib and Dabbledob are locked in a heated argument: a sanity ray vs. a straitjacket with no arm holes for the mad king.
- Valphine offers clerical healing: "I am a cleric." Gnomes: "We are gnomes, we invent our way out of illness." The rejection is immediate and absolute.
- Gnomes explain: two gnomes (Warren and Orrin) went missing; King Korboz claims a shapechanger did it.
- Brewbarry suggests talking instead of torture. Valphine is disappointed — she was ready for interrogation.
- Vukradin offers music therapy. Fibblestib offers to build a piano — "It won't be a normal piano! But it will play music."

### The Dual Monarchy
- King Korboz is hysterical; King Gnercli is exasperated. Korboz uses the "senior king" argument.
- King Gnercli cuts a deal: kill the shapeshifter, get the Hat of Wizardry.
- Vukradin is disappointed with the reward. Soma reminds him they get 50 gp from Harbin for bringing something back, not necessarily something useful.
- Vukradin accepts the morally questionable deal, counting 50 gold in his head.

### Cellar Mimic — Combat

**Opening conditions:** Twelve 40-gallon barrels in wide alcoves with wooden braces. Two half-full barrels with spigots. The party knows a shapeshifter is hiding but doesn't know its form. Valphine points a crossbow at the first barrel — it transforms into a mimic.

- Brewbarry charges with halberd, hits — weapon gets stuck (adhesion mechanic). The mimic's pseudopod misses Brewbarry.
- Soma casts Poison Spray — no effect (mimic is poison-immune). This is the second wasted Poison Spray in as many chapters.
- Valphine stays well clear of Soma's spell area after the Ice Knife incident — lesson learned.
- Vukradin uses Vicious Mockery — psychic damage works where poison fails.
- Mid-battle: Soma mutters about "unnatural magical creations." Vukradin, mid-combat, turns to her: "Wait, you're a naturalist druid?" The theology debate about constructs, extraplanar creatures, and aasimar origins unfolds while the mimic is still attacking.
- Brewbarry pulls out handaxes when his halberd is stuck. Soma finishes the mimic.
- **(DM tactical note):** The mimic's adhesion mechanic caught Brewbarry in a bad spot — forced a weapon switch mid-combat. The party had no method to identify the mimic's resistances before engaging, so the poison immunity was a nasty surprise. Vicious Mockery's psychic damage bypassing natural resistances was key. The mid-combat roleplay debate, while entertaining, was a tactical misstep — spending actions debating theology while a mimic is eating your weapon is suboptimal.
- **(DM tactical note):** Soma's Poison Spray being wasted on an immune target reinforces a knowledge gap: the party has no method to identify monster resistances before acting. A Perception-based telegraph (e.g., "the barrel doesn't react to the gas") might help future encounters.
- **Enemy behavior:** The mimic attacked the nearest threat (Brewbarry) and did not reposition or retreat. No evidence of communication or coordination.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Poison Spray — completely wasted (immunity); Vicious Mockery — excellent, bypassed natural resistances; Halberd damage — good but created an adhesion problem.
- **PC resource decisions:** Brewbarry wisely swapped to handaxes when main weapon was compromised. Valphine positioned safely away from Soma's spells — lesson learned from chapter 2. Soma continued using Poison Spray despite the first round showing no effect — wasted action.
- **Post-combat:** Scouring the room yields nothing the gnomes forgot to claim. The inventors discuss building a construct from the mimic's entrails. Soma silently disapproves but keeps her mouth shut.

### Vukradin's Political Masterclass
- Vukradin interrogates the dual-monarchy system in front of both kings. How are decisions made? "We work together." And when you disagree? Silence.
- Valphine whispers about "clear approver structures" — drow wisdom from watching communes self-destruct.
- Vukradin suggests three kings to make things "more difficult." Gnercli is furious.
- Korboz seizes the "senior king" argument. Gnercli insists there's no seniority.
- Vukradin twists the knife: "So if I have been King for a decade, and this new guy becomes King, I have to treat him as an equal and take orders from him?"
- Korboz: "I told you as the senior king I should have preferential treatment!" The two kings are now set against each other.
- Fibblestib overhears and proposes a bicameral legislature with a unitary executive. Dabbledob wants more kings for voting purposes.
- Vukradin suggests one of the inventors should be king. Chaos ensues.
- **(DM note):** Vukradin's political dismantling of Gnomengarde is his first significant non-combat victory. It establishes his primary method of influencing the world: not through violence, but through social manipulation and finding the fault lines in existing power structures. This will be a defining pattern throughout the campaign.

### Return to Phandalin
- Vukradin collects the Hat of Wizardry and 50 gp from Harbin.
- At the Stonehill Inn, Toblen tells the tale of Bob — a demon-costume actor from Triboar whose play had a spectacular run. Vukradin becomes obsessed: "Perhaps we can do a revival!"
- **(DM note):** The Bob obsession is introduced here as a seemingly silly side quest. It will become a significant long-term plot thread driving Vukradin for sessions to come.
- The party heads toward Umbrage Hill next.

## NPCs
- **Pog** — Male gnome guard at Gnomengarde entrance. Paranoid about shapeshifters. Follows standing orders to the letter. Likes gnomish music.
- **Ulla** — Female gnome guard. Shares Pog's paranoia but is more politically aware of standing-order ambiguities. Her bureaucratic reasoning lets the party pass. Has noticed Pog looking "a little bit different" — a seed of doubt that nearly turns them against each other.
- **Facktore** — Deranged gnomish inventor. Built an automated rotating crossbow turret to test on intruders. Tests it on the party. Fled when disabled. Left alive.
- **Fibblestib** — Gnomengarde inventor. Proposes a sanity ray for King Korboz. Favors bicameral legislature with a unitary executive. Overly enthusiastic about building things from monster parts. Offers to build a piano for Vukradin.
- **Dabbledob** — Gnomengarde inventor. Proposes a straitjacket with no arm holes for the mad king. Prefers more kings for voting purposes. Enjoys one-upping Fibblestib.
- **King Korboz** — Co-king of Gnomengarde. Hysterical and paranoid about shapeshifters. Seizes on the "senior king" argument. Believes he should have preferential treatment over the "junior" co-king. His madness may or may not be related to the mimic.
- **King Gnercli** — Co-king of Gnomengarde. Exasperated with Korboz's paranoia and the political dysfunction. Pragmatic. Strikes the deal with the party. Prefers to let adventurers solve problems rather than deal with internal politics. Does not appreciate Vukradin's political meddling.
- **Warren and Orrin** — Two gnomes who went missing. Presumed victims of the mimic. Possibly more to this story? **(GM note: unresolved hook.)**

## Items

- **Mimic Remains** — The inventors take the corpse for research/construct-building. Soma disapproves but stays silent.

- **Poison Spray** — Soma. Wasted on the mimic (poison immunity). Reliable against ogres. Second chapter in a row where Soma's go-to spell hits an immunity — pattern emerging.
- **Clerical healing** — Valphine. Used after the mimic fight. No combat casting this chapter.
- **Starry Wisp** — Vukradin. Used for the killing blow on the fleeing ogre.
