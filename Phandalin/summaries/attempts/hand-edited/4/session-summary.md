# Chapter 4 The Bard, the Kings, and the Carver

**Date:** 02-01 to 03-01 of Taraskh 1495

---

## Summary

Fresh from the Gnomengarde mimic fight, the party returns to the inventors' workshop where Fibblestib and Dabbledob are already discussing what kind of construct to build from the mimic's entrails — a conversation Soma silently endures with growing unease. Vukradin collects his reward from the kings, but not before one final political twist: he observes that the dual-monarchy system has no tiebreaking mechanism, plants the "senior king" argument in Korboz's head, and walks away as the two kings turn on each other. Valphine watches with drow appreciation.

In Phandalin, Vukradin collects 50 gp from Harbin and learns a tavern tale that will consume him: a legendary Triboar play featuring an actor always dressed as a demon, with the ridiculous name "Bob." The play had a spectacular run before ending, and Vukradin decides he must find the script — "Perhaps we can do a revival!"

The party departs for Umbrage Hill to save Adabra the midwife. On the road, a bandit ambush interrupts their journey. Soma discovers her new Wild Shape ability and becomes a spider for the first time — an overwhelming sensory experience of eight eyes, taste-smelling, and alien perception. She kills one bandit with a spider bite. Brewbarry climbs a hill and beheads another with a critical halberd hit — the head flies off, the body falls and covers him in blood. The remaining bandits flee in terror.

One bandit, Corbin, is caught trying to run. Brewbarry — still covered in blood — drops his halberd and approaches. Instead of killing him, Brewbarry apologizes: "I am so sorry that the Uthgardts hurt you. They were mean to me, too." Corbin breaks down. He reveals that the bandit network is organized by a figure called "the Carver." Vukradin invites Corbin to join the party for dinner. Corbin asks, desperate and vulnerable: "Can I be with you guys?"

Soma finds a druidic scroll on one of the dead bandits. Vukradin's ethics lecture begins. Soma is genuinely conflicted — am I any better than a bandit if I keep this? The moral dilemma hangs unresolved as the party continues toward Umbrage Hill.

## Scenes

### Aftermath of the Mimic Fight
- Brewbarry struggles to free his halberd from the mimic's adhesion. Soma's poison has no effect. The mimic misses its pseudopod attack.
- Mid-battle theology debate: Soma declares constructs and extraplanars "abominations." Vukradin, personally offended as an aasimar, challenges her.
- Soma: "Aasimar are touched by Celestials, Tieflings by the Abyss." Soma: "What are those?" — genuine ignorance, not provocation.
- Soma's verdict: "You're just another creature born into this world. There is no celestial touching here."
- Vukradin, stung: "I am a poet, and I get to take whatever poetic license I want!"
- Brewbarry, oblivious to the debate, is about to punch the mimic when Vukradin asks if he has ranged weapons. Brewbarry thumps his head and pulls out handaxes.
- Soma finishes the mimic.
- **(DM note):** The mimic battle theology debate is more significant than it appears. Soma's naturalist philosophy — that all creatures, including aasimar and tieflings, are merely "creatures born into this world" — directly challenges Vukradin's sense of celestial exceptionalism. This is the first crack in his self-image, and it will echo later in the campaign's theological conflicts.
- Post-mimic: Vukradin questions Soma's desire to take treasure from the mimic's room. Soma pointedly ignores him, muttering about the impoverished life Vukradin seems to embrace.

### Gnomengarde Political Chaos
- Vukradin confronts the dual monarchy: "So how do you make decisions?" King Gnercli: "We work together."
- Vukradin: "And when you disagree? There is no decision?" Gnercli: "We argue until a decision is made."
- Valphine whispers to the party: "You need a clear approver structure." She has seen cultures like these and enjoyed tearing them apart.
- Vukradin suggests three kings to make things "more difficult." Gnercli is furious.
- King Korboz seizes on the "senior king" argument — he should have preferential treatment over this upstart.
- The inventors overhear and propose rival governance models: Fibblestib wants a bicameral legislature with unitary executive; Dabbledob prefers more kings for voting.
- Vukradin suggests one of the inventors should be king. Chaos follows.
- Valphine observes with quiet satisfaction: "I smile. The two kings will be at each other's throats for months if not years."
- **(DM note):** This sequence establishes Vukradin's primary mode of operation: find the fault line in existing power structures and exploit it — not through violence, but through social engineering. Valphine recognizes and appreciates the technique; Soma is indifferent or uncomfortable.

### Return to Phandalin — The Tale of Bob
- Vukradin collects 50 gp from Harbin after his standard debate about ethical treasure.
- At the Stonehill Inn, Toblen tells Vukradin about a legendary play from Triboar featuring an actor named Bob who always wore a demon costume. The play had a spectacular run before ending.
- Vukradin becomes fixated: "Perhaps we can do a revival!" He's been trying to get the script for years.
- The quest board still shows options. The party discusses which to pursue next — they've done two of three.

### Road to Umbrage Hill
- The party debates payment ethics. Vukradin wants to ask for more than 25 gp for the Umbrage Hill job — his studio is "going to take forever to build."
- Soma argues: "Sometimes the reward is knowing you did the right thing."
- Vukradin is unmoved. Brewbarry is indifferent. Soma clarifies: "I don't want a new studio. Just a nice new teapot."
- Valphine senses something on the road but dismisses her drow instincts — "I am not in the Dark Realms."

### Bandit Ambush — Combat

**Opening conditions:** Party traveling a narrow mountain trail through rocky hills. Ambushers are poorly hidden — Soma hears them before they spring because they make too much noise. Bandits are between the party and a steep slope. Five bandits visible initially. Fading light, cold wind.

- Soma uses Spider Wild Shape for the first time — overwhelming sensory overload. She thinks and feels as herself but is "wholly spider." Climbs up and strikes a bandit with a spider bite.
- Brewbarry climbs a hill and beheads a bandit with a critical halberd hit — the head flies off, the body falls on him, covering him in blood.
- A bandit shoots crossbow at Valphine and hits.
- Spider-Soma kills another with a poison bite. Her internal thought: "All life is precious, but not a bandit who is trying to kill me."
- Remaining two bandits flee in terror. One, seeing Soma's spider form, screams "Spiders!" — not realizing Soma is the spider.
- Vukradin shouts at the fleeing bandit: "Catch him! He's taking stolen treasure!"
- Valphine shoots one in the back with a crossbow. Vukradin sprints to kill the last.
- Soma watches, disillusioned: "We are assholes."
- **(DM tactical note):** Soma's first Wild Shape into a spider was handled well mechanically, but the overwhelming sensory description (eight eyes, taste-smelling, alien perception) creates an interesting limitation: in spider form, Soma can only communicate with other spiders. The GM should track what Soma can and cannot communicate in various Wild Shape forms going forward.
- **Key tactical moments:** Soma's climb-and-bite strategy was effective positioning. Brewbarry's critical hit created the blood-covered, terrifying image that made the remaining bandits rout. Valphine's spider-phobia callout from a fleeing bandit was a character beat that didn't land with the party.
- **Enemy behavior:** Bandits break and flee when outmatched — consistent with low-morality bandits. They do not fight to the death. The fleeing bandits represent a moral test: Valphine and Vukradin kill them anyway.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Spider Wild Shape — highly effective, created mobility and surprise. No other spells used this fight.
- **PC resource decisions:** Soma used her one Wild Shape on a spider — limited spellcasting but maximized mobility.
- **Post-combat:** Vukradin interrogates Corbin. Brewbarry's unexpected empathy disarms the situation.

### Corbin's Recruitment
- Brewbarry drops his halberd — an act of profound symbolic surrender from a barbarian covered in fresh blood.
- He approaches the terrified Corbin and apologizes for Uthgardt atrocities.
- Corbin breaks down: "They pillaged my village, destroyed everything. I had no money, nothing. I am not a bad person. I don't want to steal things. It's the Carver's fault."
- Brewbarry: "I am so sorry that the Uthgardts hurt you. They were mean to me, too."
- Corbin reveals: the Carver is the bandit network's leader. He's never met the Carver — just worked through an intermediary named Jax.
- Vukradin invites Corbin to join them for dinner.
- Corbin: "Can I be with you guys?"
- **(DM note):** This scene is a major character moment for Brewbarry. An Uthgardt barbarian covered in the blood of his enemies, apologizing to a victim of his tribe's violence, and finding common ground in shared trauma. This is the first glimpse of Brewbarry's depth beneath the simple warrior exterior.
- **(DM note):** Corbin is a potential source of intel on the bandit network. His trauma response to Brewbarry's Uthgardt tattoos is realistic — the party should handle him carefully.

### Soma's Druidic Scroll
- Soma finds a scroll with a druidic spell on one of the dead bandits.

- Soma is genuinely conflicted: Am I any better than a bandit if I keep this?

- **(DM note):** The scroll's contents and ownership history are unresolved hooks for future encounters.

## NPCs
- **Corbin** — Gaunt, late-20s human. Haunted, hollowed-out look. Matted brown hair, patchy beard, pale blue eyes that are often distant but can sharpen with desperate intensity. Wears ill-fitting leather armor scavenged from a dead man. Former farmer from Greenmeadow whose family (wife Elara, daughter Lily) was killed by Uthgardt Black Lion tribe raiders during the Ordning's collapse. Turned to banditry to survive. Deeply traumatized and desperate for belonging. Hired through a crew led by "Jax." Has never met the Carver personally. His recruitment by Brewbarry's unexpected empathy is the party's first real emotional victory.
- **The Carver** (first mentioned) — The bandit leader operating in the Phandalin area. Corbin has never met him, only worked through an intermediary named Jax. Described as "big." **(DM note): The Carver's identity and location are deliberately left as unresolved hooks — the party will encounter him later.**
- **Jax** (mentioned) — Intermediate figure in the bandit network. Corbin's direct contact. Possibly knows more than Corbin about the Carver. **(DM note: potential future contact/interrogation target.)**

## Items
- **Druidic Scroll** — Found on a dead bandit. Contains a druidic spell. Soma is conflicted about keeping it (ethics vs. utility). Vukradin argues it should be returned to its rightful owner. **(DM note): The scroll's contents and ownership history are hooks for future encounters — who did the bandit steal this from?**
- **Hat of Wizardry** (carried from chapter 3) — Collectible from Harbin for 50 gp bounty. Vukradin remains unimpressed with its dragon-fighting utility.
- **Sending Stones** (carried from chapter 2) — Vukradin discovers they only send one short message, not the musical application he imagined. Disappointed.

## Spells
- **Wild Shape (Spider)** — Soma's first use of this ability. Overwhelming sensory experience. Effective in combat (climb, bite, poison). Creates communication limitations (can only communicate with other spiders in this form). A major milestone in Soma's character development.
- **Poison Spray** — Soma. Wasted again on an immune target (mimic). Pattern: Soma needs to vary her spell selection when poison doesn't work.
- **Vicious Mockery** — Vukradin. Reliable psychic damage against the mimic.
- **Healing spells** — Not used in combat this chapter.
