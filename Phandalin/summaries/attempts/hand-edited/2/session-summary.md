# Chapter 2 Arrival in Phandalin

**Date:** 08-01 to 10-01 of Taraskh 1495

---

## Summary

Vukradin, an avant-garde aasimar bard whose experimental music finds no audience in Neverwinter, quits his gig at the First Flophouse in Heapside after a patron screams "PLAY MUSIC BARD!" one too many times. His only paying fan — Brewbarry, an Uthgardt barbarian — follows him out. Vukradin declares he'll make "honest coin" through adventure rather than participate in what he calls Neverwinter's "egregious money laundering scheme."

A flashback reveals the Wintershield recruitment pitch: Vukradin's pointed debate with a guard about the ethics of plunder (tax-free treasure from monsters and evildoers under Lord Neverember's authorization) left him deeply uncomfortable, but his empty coin purse leaves him little choice. The guard's cynical suggestion — turn found items into Wintershield for a 60-day holding period, claim what's unclaimed — does nothing to salve Vukradin's conscience.

He's followed out of the flophouse by Valphine Sortorra, a drow cleric of Lathander whose conversion from Lolth-worship is as pragmatic as it is sincere. She saw the Aasimar and sensed power. Just outside the city limits, a tortle druid of Mielikki named Soma offers to guide them to Phandalin — a frontier town of 40–50 log buildings nestled in the snow-capped Sword Mountains, with no walls and no garrison.

In Phandalin, Vukradin's music earns them a meal at the Stonehill Inn, where proprietor Toblen Stonehill convinces the party to check the quest board. Three jobs await: warning dwarven prospectors about a white dragon (50 gp), retrieving a magic device from Gnomengarde (50 gp), and convincing midwife Adabra Gwynn to return to town (25 gp). Vukradin picks the dwarven quest for its "honest wages."

The party travels to an ancient dwarven temple of Abbathor in a canyon southwest of Phandalin. Dwarven prospectors Dazlyn and Norbus offer sending stones as payment to investigate a disturbance in the temple depths. Inside, the party triggers a fierce orc ambush that nearly kills Vukradin twice and results in Soma's Ice Knife friendly-firing Valphine unconscious — the party's first taste of combat together, which they barely survive. The dwarves cook them a fine meal that night.

On the return journey, the party encounters a road ogre. Vukradin's attempt to befriend it as a "fellow musician" fails spectacularly — ogres hate music. The fight is a slog until Valphine uses a magical bullet to knock it prone and Brewbarry's rage tanks the damage. The ogre breaks morale and flees, but Vukradin kills it with a Starry Wisp from hiding — prompting Soma's first protest against killing a fleeing enemy.

Back in Phandalin, Vukradin collects 50 gp from Townmaster Harbin Wester (after his comedic "If you're a dragon…" routine), argues with him about dwarven treasure-hunting ethics (which Vukradin calls grave-robbing), and meets Linene Graywind of the Lionshield Coster, who hints at interdimensional supply chain disruption. The party sells orc loot and rests at the Stonehill Inn before Toblen convinces Vukradin to play for the evening crowd.

## Scenes

### Vukradin's Last Gig
- Vukradin plays to a sparse crowd at the First Flophouse in Heapside, Neverwinter; Brewbarry is his only listener. The clientele includes figures Vukradin suspects of darker dealings.
- Flashback to his Wintershield recruitment: Vukradin argues ethics about stolen treasure; the guard sighs and explains the 60-day holding period with an eyeroll. The guard suggests if Vukradin is so concerned, bring items to Wintershield headquarters — but the implication is clear.
- **(DM note):** This flashback is crucial for understanding Vukradin's character motivation. He enters the campaign not as a willing adventurer but as an artist who ran out of options. His moral qualms about plunder are not mere roleplay flavor — they are the central tension of his character arc, established before he takes a single step outside Neverwinter.
- An unnamed patron screams "PLAY MUSIC BARD!" Vukradin quits mid-set.
- Brewbarry follows him out, not really understanding what's happening, just wanting to stay near the bard.
- Valphine watches all of this from the flophouse, her drow instincts telling her that this aasimar is worth following.

### The Road Party Assembles
- Valphine, a drow Lathander cleric, follows Vukradin out of the flophouse and volunteers: "I can help. I am a cleric of Lathander, and I, too, wish to adventure." She is simultaneously drawn to Vukradin's celestial nature and viewing his naivety as something to observe — a tension that defines her early character.
- **(DM note):** Valphine's internal perspective reveals her conversion to Lathander is genuine but filtered through drow pragmatism. She initially dismisses surface-world ethics as "subterfuge" — she expects clerics to be scheming like Lolth's clergy, and is still adjusting to the discovery that Lathander's followers genuinely mean what they say. Her smile when told "monsters are what we call things we are allowed to kill" reveals a woman learning to code her violence in acceptable terms.
- Soma the tortle druid meets them outside Neverwinter: "I know this land very well, and we will not go hungry." She offers to guide them to Phandalin.
- The journey is uneventful. Soma knows the High Road is safe since the end of the barbarian incursion. The Triboar Trail is less secure, but looking tough and broke is usually enough protection. She has heard rumors of a white dragon — and keeps them to herself.
- **(DM note):** Soma's decision to withhold the white dragon rumor from her new companions is significant. It suggests either a survival instinct (don't scare off the paying clients) or a test (let's see how they handle surprises). Either way, it establishes Soma as someone who manages information strategically.

### Arrival in Phandalin
- The party enters Phandalin: 40–50 log buildings, crumbling stone ruins surrounding newer construction, no walls, no garrison. Snow-capped Sword Mountains loom overhead.
- At the Stonehill Inn, Vukradin meets Toblen Stonehill — a short, friendly human from Triboar who came to prospect but found he knew more about running an inn than mining.
- Vukradin has no coin. He convinces Toblen to accept music as payment for meals, but Toblen insists on "something popular from Waterdeep or Neverwinter." Vukradin sighs and plays what's asked.
- After the third or fourth song, Toblen lets Vukradin play his own music — and seems genuinely pleased, offering a complimentary beer.
- Toblen mentions past orc raids. Vukradin realizes uncomfortably that Brewbarry's tattoos match the Uthgardt tribes. Brewbarry: "I like Vukradin music. My tribe goes boom, boom, boom with a drum. I don't like drums. I like Vukradin." Toblen saves the moment: "Well, my friend, the wars of the past are the past."
- Toblen tells the party about the job board. He also quietly warns: "Be careful, there is a rumor of a white dragon in the neighborhood."
- Vukradin: "A WHITE DRAGON?"

### The Quest Board
- Three quests are posted at the Townmaster's Hall, all written in the same hand: Dwarven warning (50 gp), Gnomengarde device (50 gp), Umbrage Hill midwife (25 gp).
- Harbin's locked-door routine: a quavering voice from inside insists he's too thin and bony for a dragon.
- Vukradin picks the dwarven quest for honest wages. Valphine is disappointed — she wanted the Gnomengarde quest for plunder opportunities — but remembers Lathander's clerics telling her plundering is not the way. She hides her disappointment.
- The party learns of a local white dragon rumor — a running threat that colors every future decision.

### Temple of Abbathor — Combat (Orc Ambush)

**Opening conditions:** The party enters the dwarven temple of Abbathor, an evil dwarven god of greed. Two dwarven prospectors (Dazlyn, Norbus) have been excavating the ruins. The dungeon is dark — 10-foot ceilings with no light beyond what the party brings. The temple facade features life-sized granite statues of cloaked dwarves with evil grins. Orcs are hidden behind collapsed walls and on bluffs, using the terrain as defensive positions.

- Vukradin attempts parley, approaching the orcs with a clarinet: "The Orcs in Neverwinter loved this music." An orc rushes from the cliff and knocks him unconscious with one swing.
- Valphine watches him fall and is tempted to let him die — but Lathander wins. She heals the bard, muttering about fools.
- Soma uses Thorn Whip to drag a cliff-top orc 20 feet down, prone. The creature rolls but avoids most falling damage.
- Brewbarry charges with halberd, cleaving through orcs. Where one orc moves to attack Brewbarry, the backswing cuts it in half.
- Vukradin goes down a second time — the same orc targets him again. He looks "gallant and ineffectual" as his blades barely touch his enemies.
- Soma's Ice Knife misses the orcs and friendly-fires Valphine, knocking her unconscious. Soma's internal reaction: "Shit."
- Brewbarry rages when he sees Vukradin go down again, attacking in a frenzy of bloodlust. Orcs throw javelins but Brewbarry's rage protects him.
- Brewbarry finishes the last two orcs. Soma heals Valphine, who has "an ecstatic smile on her face" from the pain.
- **(DM tactical note):** Vukradin's orc parley attempt was tactically catastrophic. He had no leverage, no read on the enemies' disposition, and no escape plan. This establishes a pattern: Vukradin's idealism repeatedly clashes with tactical reality in the early chapters. Orcs in this scenario are guarding territory — the GM might telegraph this more clearly in future encounters to distinguish between parley-able and non-parley-able enemies.
- **(DM tactical note):** This combat exposes the party's lack of coordination. Soma's Ice Knife hitting Valphine establishes a recurring danger: area-effect spells in tight spaces. Brewbarry is the party's only reliable damage dealer. The party has no dedicated controller or battlefield manipulator at this stage.
- **Enemy behavior:** Orcs attacked from ambush positions (cliffs, collapsed walls). They focus-fired Vukradin (the weakest-looking target) — tactically sound. They did not pursue fleeing party members.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Ice Knife — effective AoE potential but dangerous in this party's formation; Thorn Whip — excellent repositioning; Valphine's healing spells — essential.
- **PC resource decisions:** Valphine chose to heal Vukradin despite temptation to let him die (faith over pragmatism — a key character moment). Soma cast Ice Knife with allies in the blast radius — questionable positioning awareness.
- **Post-combat:** The dwarves Dazlyn and Norbus are delighted the orcs are dead and cook a fine meal. Brewbarry carries orc axes as loot. Vukradin plays his own compositions, which the dwarves tolerate but clearly prefer popular songs.

### Road Ogre — Combat

**Opening conditions:** Single ogre encountered on the road back to Phandalin. Clear day, open terrain in a hilly area between the Sword Mountains and Phandalin. The ogre is whistling cheerfully — Valphine recognizes this as unusual, as ogres in Menzoberranzan were slaves and didn't whistle.

- Vukradin asks if the ogre is a "fellow musician." The ogre hates music and charges. Soma mutters, "The kid is going to get us killed."
- Brewbarry tanks with rage. The ogre's club blow lands but Brewbarry's rage absorbs the worst of it.
- Valphine uses a magical bullet (found on the first ogre's body) to knock it prone.
- Soma casts Poison Spray; the ogre chokes.
- Vukradin hides and uses Starry Wisp from stealth. His authorial omniscience intrudes: had Vukradin studied ogre ecology instead of music theory, he would have known that ogres' profound aversion to music and the arts made any negotiation doomed.
- The ogre breaks morale when seriously wounded and tries to flee. Valphine shoots it in the back with a crossbow bolt. It's wounded but still running.
- Vukradin delivers the killing blow from hiding with Starry Wisp.
- **(DM tactical note):** Vukradin's second non-combat approach in as many encounters — nearly as disastrous as the orc parley, but more justifiable since he genuinely didn't know about ogre ecology. The ogre's hatred of music is a good character beat but the GM should ensure the party learns from this pattern, or Vukradin will repeat it.
- **Key tactical moments:** Valphine's use of the magical ogre-knocking bullet was excellent resource management. The party's ranged finishing options are limited — they lack a dedicated martial archer.
- **Enemy behavior:** Ogre breaks morale when significantly wounded and tries to flee. Consistent with the road ogre pattern (then-unknown) — ogres fight until they evaluate the fight as lost, then retreat. This is the party's first encounter with a fleeing enemy, setting up the moral debate about killing retreating foes.
- **Spell/ability impact:** Starry Wisp — reliable ranged damage from hiding; Poison Spray — effective but slow; Rage — negated the ogre's best hit.
- **PC resource decisions:** Vukradin expended a spell slot on Starry Wisp to kill the fleeing ogre rather than let it escape — driven by roleplay (the ogre hated music) rather than tactical necessity.
- **Post-combat:** Soma searches the corpse and finds some items. Soma protests killing the fleeing ogre — the first significant moral disagreement in the party. Vukradin's defense: "He hated music. And he probably had some treasure he had stolen from someone else."

### Return to Phandalin
- Vukradin collects 50 gp from Harbin and immediately argues with him about dwarven grave-robbing ethics. Harbin insists Neverember guarantees tax-free plunder. Vukradin's diatribe about adventurers being "glorified thugs and goons stealing without regard to what is right and what is wrong" leaves Harbin upset.
- At the Lionshield Coster, Linene Graywind notes supply chain disruption — "strange magic pervades Neverwinter Wood, confounding navigators" — impacting the Coster's fabled internal network.
- Valphine is curious about what disrupted the Coster's interdimensional supply chain. **(DM note): This is a plot hook about interdimensional trade disruption that may connect to larger magical instabilities.**
- Vukradin plays at the Stonehill Inn for the evening. Toblen once again convinces him to play something popular first, then lets him play his own compositions.
- **The party's first night in Phandalin.** The campaign is properly underway.

## NPCs
- **Toblen Stonehill** — Human, proprietor of the Stonehill Inn. Native of Triboar. Runs a modest two-story roadhouse. Friendly but cautious, with good instincts for managing difficult customers. Accepts Vukradin's music as payment for meals. Hinted at orc/white dragon dangers. Quietly de-escalated the tension when Uthgardt tribal tattoos were noticed.
- **Harbin Wester** — Townmaster of Phandalin. Tremulous, thin-skinned, hides behind locked doors when frightened. Writes all job board notices in the same hand. Pragmatic about Neverember's adventurer policy. Pays promptly but is easily pressured. His "If you're a dragon..." routine is the party's first indicator of Phandalin's leadership quality.
- **Dazlyn Grayshard** — Shield dwarf prospector and business partner of Norbus. Excavating the Abbathor temple ruins for treasure. Offers sending stones as payment. Claims intent to return treasures to "rightful owners" — Vukradin is skeptical. Genuinely pleased when the orcs are dead.
- **Norbus Ironrune** — Shield dwarf prospector, Dazlyn's partner. Echoes Dazlyn's promises about returning treasure. Both dwarves are more treasure-hungry than they admit, but they do cook an excellent meal.
- **Linene Graywind** — Master of the Phandalin Lionshield Coster outpost. Businesslike, professional. Hints at interdimensional supply chain disruption — the Coster's normally unstoppable network is failing. Notable mention of "strange magic" in Neverwinter Wood.
- **Brewbarry** — Uthgardt barbarian of the Black Lion tribe. Vukradin's only paying fan. Deeply loyal to the bard, following him on instinct rather than understanding. Covers himself in orc blood during combat without concern. Emerges as the party's primary damage dealer. His simple declaration — "I like Vukradin music" — is the thread that holds the early party together.
- **Valphine Sortorra** — Drow cleric of Lathander. Recent convert from Lolth. Pragmatic, calculating, but genuinely drawn to Lathander's radiant power. Views Vukradin as an experiment in "good" behavior. Cruelty and kindness war within her — she saved Vukradin's life despite temptation to let him die. Her drow background gives her sharp insight into politics and power structures.
- **Soma** — Tortle druid of Mielikki. Guide from Neverwinter. Old, patient, wise. Withheld the white dragon rumor from the party — a strategic decision that hints at a pragmatic nature beneath her gentle exterior. Troubled by unnecessary killing. Becomes the party's moral anchor and voice of reason.

## Items
- **Sending Stones** — Offered by Dazlyn/Norbus as payment. Vukradin accepts them after Valphine convinces him they could be used for music (sending messages between locations for artistic effect). In truth, each stone can only send one short message — a disappointment Vukradin discovers later.
- **Orc Axes (broken) and Hide Armor** — Loot from the Temple of Abbathor. Sold to Lionshield Coster for coin. The orcs' gear is a "tax-free" source of income — the first iron filings against Vukradin's principles.
- **Magical Bullet (Ogre-knocking)** — Found on the first ogre's body. Used by Valphine to prone the second ogre. Excellent utility item against single targets. **(GM note: consider what type of magic item this is for tracking purposes.)**

## Spells
- **Thorn Whip** — Soma. Used to drag a cliff-top orc 20 feet down to the ground, prone. Excellent repositioning. First major tactical contribution in the campaign.
- **Ice Knife** — Soma's area-of-effect spell. Knocks Valphine unconscious in friendly fire. Pattern alert: Soma needs better ally-positioning awareness in tight quarters.
- **Starry Wisp** — Vukradin's ranged attack. Used to finish the fleeing ogre from hiding. Reliable, if low-damage, ranged option.
- **Poison Spray** — Soma's reliable single-target damage. Effective against ogres.
- **Healing Word / Cure Wounds** — Valphine. Used to keep Vukradin alive despite temptation to let him die. First concrete evidence that Valphine's Lathander faith is real, even as her instincts bargain against it.
- **Rage** — Brewbarry's barbarian ability. Absorbed the ogre's best hit. Proves essential for the party's survival against single heavy hitters.
