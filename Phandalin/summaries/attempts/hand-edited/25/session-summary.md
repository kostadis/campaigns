# Chapter 25 Where Reality Unravels — Breaching Axeholm

Date: Eleasis 1495 (late summer)

## Summary

The party arrives at Axeholm — the ancient dwarven fortress carved into the mountain. The entrance is sealed and the fortifications are intact but aged. The party finds a way inside and begins their exploration of the first level.

The spatial and temporal distortions become immediately apparent. Distances don't seem reliable. The fortress lighting is mathematically cold and unnatural — light that refuses to flicker like normal flames.

Combat erupts with out-of-phase dwarves. Their touch doesn't just wound — it triggers a terrifying paralysis that makes victims feel like objects being packaged for shipment. Brewbarry fights off the effect, and a disembodied voice speaks of "incorrect packaging." The implication is horrifying: the machine was preparing souls as cargo.

Valphine calls on Lathander's power, causing the dwarves to flee — despite them not being truly undead. The Chief Accountant later refers to her as "sister of the sun," suggesting her divine connection is uniquely potent in this corrupted space.

## Memorable Moments

**Incorrect Packaging.** The dwarves' paralysis tries to store Brewbarry like a package. He fights it off. The voice that speaks of "incorrect packaging" is an unsettling glimpse into the machine's logic — souls as cargo.

**Turn (Sort of) Undead.** Valphine's divine power causes the out-of-phase dwarves to flee, even though they aren't truly undead. The "sister of the sun" epithet from the Chief Accountant later hints at why.

**Packaged Souls.** The dwarves' touch doesn't just paralyze — it makes victims feel like objects being prepared for shipment. A horrifying insight into the machine's true purpose.

**Mathematically Cold Light.** The unnaturally still light in Axeholm refuses to flicker. Spatial distortions make distances unreliable. The fortress is fundamentally broken at a planar level.
