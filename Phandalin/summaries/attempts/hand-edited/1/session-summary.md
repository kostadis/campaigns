# Chapter 1 Neverwinter Expansionism and the North, Excerpt from the Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th

**Date:** Pre-campaign (Lore Prologue)

---

## Summary

This chapter is not a session narrative but an in-world lore document — an excerpt from the collected writings of the Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th, chronicling the history of the North in the aftermath of the Second Sundering. It surveys the chain of failed divine interventions that followed the gods' return to the mortal world, cataloguing successive crises that shaped the political and physical landscape of the Sword Coast.

The document traces the divine domino effect: Tiamat's attempted return through the Cult of the Dragon (revealed as Zariel's manipulation rather than Tiamat's own plan), Annam the All-Father's giant-centric ambitions that nearly destroyed the giant kingdoms when Iymrith exploited the chaos, and the escape of the ancient blue dragon Iymrith. The Storm Giant King Hekaton's subsequent alliance between giants and the peoples of the realms is noted as the first major recovery. Lolth's machinations — using Gromph Baenre to loosen the bonds between planes — released demon lords into the Underdark, triggering the fall of Menzoberranzan and widespread chaos. The Underdark's destabilization provoked the Uthgardt barbarians into devastating raids across the North, destroying Triboar, Calling Horn, and numerous smaller settlements on the outskirts of Silverymoon and Mirabar.

With the Ordning restored and the combined strength of the Silvery Marches and giant allies ending the barbarian invasion, Lord Neverember of Neverwinter turned his attention to re-establishing control over the hinterland. His plan: use adventurers as a low-cost pacification force, offering them tax-free, no-questions-asked plunder in exchange for clearing monsters and evildoers from the Phandalin area. One such adventurer was an avant-garde musician named Vukradin — whose work was, the sage notes dryly, underappreciated.

This document establishes the post-war political context, the precedent-setting divine interference that frames the campaign's stakes, and the cynical economic logic that brings adventurers to Phandalin. The "tax-free plunder" model directly sets up Vukradin's core character conflict: a principled artist entering a system built on morally compromised incentives.

## NPCs

- **Lord Dagult Neverember** — Open Lord of Neverwinter, architect of the adventurer-based hinterland pacification strategy. Pragmatic and expansionist, he uses tax-free plunder as incentive rather than military expenditure. His policy of "adventurer pacification" frames the entire campaign's economic and ethical landscape.

- **Vukradin** (mentioned) — An avant-garde musician whose music was "underappreciated," cited as one of the adventurers drawn to Phandalin by Neverember's offer. His entry into the campaign is framed not by choice but by economic desperation in a post-war world. The sage's detached mention of him as an afterthought foreshadows Vukradin's outsider status.

- **The Great Sage Konstantinious the 12th** (in-universe author) — An erudite scholar whose detached, academic tone deliberately stands in contrast to the visceral first-person narratives of later chapters. His analysis is historical rather than prophetic, but the patterns he documents (gods interfering, mortals suffering, power structures exploiting crisis) are thematic throughlines for the campaign.

- **Zariel** (mentioned) — Archduchess of Avernus, revealed to have manipulated the Cult of the Dragon as a scheme to remove Tiamat from her territory. The first example in the chain of divine deceptions.

- **Annam the All-Father** (mentioned) — God of giants, whose ambitions for giant rule nearly destroyed the giant kingdoms when Iymrith intervened.

- **Iymrith** (mentioned) — The ancient blue dragon escaped the collapse of Annam's plans. The sage's terse "she escaped" is a notable hook that may connect to later Storm King's Thunder plot threads.

- **King Hekaton** (mentioned) — Storm Giant King who created the alliance between giants and the peoples of the realms after the Ordning was restored, ending the giant crisis.

- **Gromph Baenre** (mentioned) — Archmage of the First House of Menzoberranzan, Lolth's pawn in the spell that loosened the bonds between planes, inadvertently releasing demon lords into the Underdark.

## Notes for the GM

- **Chain of causation:** The document establishes a domino-effect cosmology where each divine intervention triggers the next — Tiamat/Zariel → Annam/Iymrith → Lolth/Gromph → demon lords → Uthgardt raids → Neverember's adventurer policy. This chain is important because the naturalist/interventionist theological debates later in the campaign (Adabra's Emerald Enclave schism, Soma's worldview) echo this same question: are divine interventions part of the natural order, or violations of it?

- **Economic model of adventuring:** Neverember's "tax-free, no-questions-asked plunder" policy is the system Vukradin will reject on moral grounds. This sets up his core character conflict from the very beginning — he enters the campaign under an economic model he finds ethically repugnant, which drives his later embrace of "fair trade," "conflict-free gold," and his hostility toward treasure-taking.

- **Iymrith's escape:** The ancient blue dragon's survival is a significant loose thread. Given the campaign's escalating dragon focus (Cryovain, the dragon cult activity, the Ordning-related lore), Iymrith may be a candidate for connection to later events or as a deeper background element.

- **The sage's scholarly tone:** The dry, academic framing of this chapter is a deliberate contrast to the first-person PC narratives that follow in subsequent chapters. This document is meant to feel like research — a cold, historical overview of events that will become viscerally immediate once the party reaches Phandalin.

- **Silvery Marches alliance:** The revived alliance of the Silvery Marches (through agents from Everlund) is a notable faction mention that may connect to larger regional politics later, particularly if the party moves beyond the Phandalin area.
