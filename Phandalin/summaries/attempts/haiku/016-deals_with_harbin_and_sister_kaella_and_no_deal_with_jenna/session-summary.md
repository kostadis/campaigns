# Chapter 16 Deals with Harbin, and Sister Kaella, and no deal with Jenna

## Summary

The party pursues three separate agendas in Phandalin on the same evening, each fraught with political and moral complexity. Vukradin confronts Harbin over past accusations, winning support from Ser Kaelen of the Order of the Gauntlet, who insists that false accusations against an Order member are "scandalous" and suggests a trial to clear Vukradin's name. Harbin attempts to deflect by invoking Lord Neverember's authority to appoint and remove town officials, claiming that "only he has the privilege of replacing me." This invocation of authoritarianism triggers Vukradin's democratic instincts. He demands a town meeting to give townspeople voice in governance, threatening "Now or pitchforks. Do you want pitchforks?" when Harbin objects that such meetings require advance preparation. Vukradin takes matters into his own hands, marching to the town green to perform music and draw the community together. His concert attracts many local notables—Elmer and his clerks, Halia Thornton, Linene, and Adabra—along with dozens of farmers and miners. Among the crowd is an unfamiliar woman in her late thirties, dressed in fine wool and leather, who watches Vukradin with calculating intensity. Just as he prepares to give his speech about freedom and democracy, someone screams "DRAGON!" A white dragon dives toward the crowd. Rather than face it in combat, Vukradin casts phantasmal force to create an illusion of a peasant in the dragon's claws, deceiving it into departing with its illusory meal. After this display of resourcefulness, Harbin concedes, announcing a new privy council with Vukradin as a member. Ser Kaelen counsels pragmatism, explaining that forcibly removing Harbin would provoke Lord Neverember into sending troops, so compromise is wiser than revolution.

Valphine operates in shadows that same evening, staying behind while Vukradin ventures into town. She travels with Corbin and Locutus to an abandoned building at the town's edge. There, Sister Kaella emerges from the shadows—a woman with cold grey eyes, short hair, and a scar near her neck, bearing a rapier and bloodied dagger. Kaella questions whether Locutus killed Borg, and Valphine confirms it by presenting the severed head. Sister Kaella then kills Locutus with ruthless efficiency, her dagger cutting the orc down. Valphine finds this violence exhilarating, reminiscent of games played in Menzoberranzan. Sister Kaella removes her hood and reveals her true nature: she orchestrated the earlier plot to frame Valphine for theft from a merchant. The plan failed because Borg was supposed to kill Big Al and leave no witnesses, but he failed. Now Sister Kaella seeks a new alliance. She misreads Valphine as a fellow drow with underworld ambitions and proposes a partnership: Valphine will eliminate the Carver's lieutenants to force the Carver out of hiding. Sister Kaella explains that the Carver possesses a unique telepathic ability to communicate with his network and cannot be killed while hidden. Valphine is disturbed by the suggestion of dealing with an entity possessing telepathic powers and begins to refuse. But Sister Kaella presses the question: "Do we have an arrangement?" She adds that she needs to disappear because the Carver punishes failure with death. Valphine ultimately refuses the partnership but accepts a bag of coin from Sister Kaella as a "down payment," then watches Sister Kaella vanish into the night.

At the tavern, Brewbarry recognizes danger. The woman who watched Vukradin during the dragon attack is now smirking at him. Spiderman identifies her as Jenna from Neverwinter, working for the Lord's Alliance. The Lord's Alliance has a dark history with Brewbarry's people, and his hand goes to his halberd. Brewbarry approaches Jenna and learns that she admires Vukradin's music from Neverwinter performances. When Jenna reveals she sent the mysterious letter that drew Vukradin to the Order of the Gauntlet, Brewbarry becomes fully protective. He demands she hand over her weapons, and Jenna complies, placing a rapier, two daggers, and a hand-held crossbow on the table. Brewbarry then brings Vukradin to the table. Vukradin meets Jenna, who explains that she can book him in Waterdeep and Neverwinter's finest venues and potentially arrange an audience with Lord Neverember himself. Vukradin is intrigued but wary. When Brewbarry mispronounces her name as "GEEENA," Jenna responds with contempt, sarcastically mispronouncing his as "Blue beerrree." When Vukradin corrects her, Jenna responds with charm. She then presents her moral compromise: stolen goods taken from communities can be ethically redistributed through commerce and purchases, returning wealth to those from whom it was stolen. Vukradin firmly refuses. He tells Jenna plainly: "Yeah, no. This is not a line I'm gonna cross. I gotta go perform." He emphasizes that he believes in returning stolen items to their rightful owners, not in laundering stolen treasure through commerce. Jenna's negotiating position collapses. Though disappointed, she acknowledges that she has underestimated Vukradin's moral conviction and admits that she admires him.

## NPCs

### Harbin Wester
The Town Master faces pressure from multiple factions seeking influence over Phandalin's governance. After his accusations against Vukradin are undermined by Ser Kaelen, Harbin becomes increasingly isolated. He attempts to stall Vukradin's democratic initiatives with bureaucratic objections but ultimately surrenders to the inevitable shift in power. Recognizing Vukradin's growing authority—demonstrated by his ability to gather the town and his resourcefulness against the dragon—Harbin proposes a privy council structure that preserves his nominal position while ceding real decision-making authority to Vukradin and local merchants. His acquiescence reveals both pragmatism and fear.

### Ser Kaelen
A member of the Order of the Gauntlet, Ser Kaelen champions Vukradin's vindication and supports participatory governance in principle. However, he tempers Vukradin's revolutionary fervor with realpolitik advice: ousting Harbin would invite military retaliation from Lord Neverember, making the privy council compromise the wiser course. Ser Kaelen sees wisdom in informal power-sharing over overt conflict, advocating for a system where Vukradin and others shape decisions from within while Harbin remains nominally in charge.

### Sister Kaella
A woman of refined, unassuming elegance with grey eyes and a scar near her neck, Sister Kaella operates as an independent operative within Phandalin's criminal underworld. She reveals herself as the architect of the plot to frame Valphine for theft, a scheme that failed when Locutus's assassination of Borg went awry. Now she seeks to consolidate power by positioning herself between the Carver and his enemies. She misreads Valphine as a fellow Drow villain and makes a calculated offer of partnership, believing shared ruthlessness will cement loyalty. Her rapid assassination of Locutus, her command of martial skills, and her intimate knowledge of the Carver's network demonstrate both her competence and her isolation from legitimate authority.

### Jenna
A Lord's Alliance representative from Neverwinter, Jenna is sophisticated and manipulative, presenting herself as Vukradin's advocate while advancing her organization's interests. She sent the letter that drew Vukradin to the Order of the Gauntlet and now seeks to position herself as his agent, offering fame and access to nobility in exchange for compromising his ethical stance on treasure. When Vukradin refuses to accept that stolen goods can be morally "redistributed" through commerce, Jenna's proposal collapses, but she reveals genuine admiration for his integrity. Her contempt for Brewbarry and other "barbarians" surfaces despite her efforts at diplomatic restraint, suggesting the Lord's Alliance harbors prejudice against non-human cultures.
