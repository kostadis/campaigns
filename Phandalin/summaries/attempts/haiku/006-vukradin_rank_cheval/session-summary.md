# Chapter 6 Vukradin, Rank Cheval

## Summary

The party travels to the Townmaster's house to claim payment for rescuing Adabra. During the journey, Adabra engages Soma in philosophical debate about the nature of monsters, arguing that creatures like manticores and displaced orcs "are merely acting as nature intends" and that "it is the meddling that truly upset the balance." Soma agrees they should "let nature take its course." When Brewbarry mentions his past as a barbarian, Adabra assures him he was a nomad.

At the Townmaster's house, Vukradin negotiates for higher pay, explaining the mission was more involved than promised. The Townmaster suggests Vukradin should have taken payment from Manticore treasure as per his agreement with Lord Neverember. Adabra fiercely advocates for Vukradin, declaring he is "no common sellsword" but "an idealist, respectful of life, and willing to sacrifice money for his principles. He wants honest wages, fair trade for work done, and conflict-free gold." She argues that Phandalin "need[s] champions who care for the greater whole, whose principles, though perhaps untested, are fundamentally sound." The Townmaster, citing bandit siege and depleted funds, offers free room and board at the Stonehill Inn.

At the inn, Vukradin performs music. Valphine joins him, creating a collaboration that balances her composition (fusing Lathanderial and Lolth-inspired elements) with his complex style. After their performance, Vukradin suggests "perhaps we should play together." The innkeeper Toblen, nicknamed Spiderman for a childhood incident when baby spiders covered him unharmed, serves drinks throughout.

Daran Edermath, known as Silverleaf, approaches Vukradin with a story about a cursed lighthouse on the coast where ships are lured to crash like moths to a flame. Silverleaf pitches that wrecks "must be filled with treasure." Vukradin rejects the premise harshly: "How many times do I have to explain this. You don't go and rob from the dead and call it treasure\!"

A mysterious figure, Ser Kaelen Thorn, then approaches Vukradin and expresses admiration for his principles regarding treasure. When Valphine interjects with a comment about "dead drow," Ser Kaelen is visibly startled. After learning she is a cleric of Lathander, Ser Kaelen reveals he serves the Order of the Gauntlet, an organization dedicated to "making the north better" through fighters, magicians, storytellers, and clerics. He offers Vukradin membership, promising his voice and story would accomplish far more with their backing. Vukradin initially worries about funding sources but accepts after being assured it comes through conflict-free donations. When Vukradin suggests Soma join, Ser Kaelen says "she's a naturalist, and I don't think that our points of view would align." Ser Kaelen gives Vukradin a pin as a symbol of membership.

Brewbarry reacts poorly, revealing the Order killed many of his people during a past war. Ser Kaelen acknowledges the brutality: "We fought a war. A terrible war." He offers a drink in peace, and Brewbarry agrees "the war is over" and accepts it.

The next morning, Vukradin obsesses over the lighthouse and declares "we need to destroy the evil tower\!" Soma argues a lighthouse is meant to protect ships, but Vukradin insists "it's destroying them\!" The Townmaster clarifies the lighthouse is on the coast and dismisses the evil rumors as hearsay. When Vukradin demands to know how to remove a curse, the Townmaster directs him to Ser Kaelen, who explains there are three possible explanations: one requiring a cleric, a monster haunting the place, or simply untrue rumors. Based on their account, Ser Kaelen believes it most likely involves a monster.

## NPCs

### Harbin (Townmaster)
The Townmaster of Phandalin, embodying what Vukradin views as the town's sordid morality. Harbin initially balks at increased payment for Vukradin, suggesting he should have taken it from Manticore treasure. After Adabra's passionate advocacy, he offers the party free room and board at the Stonehill Inn, citing ongoing bandit siege and depleted funds. He later provides details about the lighthouse's coastal location and dismisses rumors of its evil nature as hearsay, directing Vukradin to seek Ser Kaelen's counsel on curse-removal.

### Adabra
A philosophical voice advocating natural balance and principled action. Adabra engages Soma in debate about monsters and meddling, arguing that creatures acting according to nature should not be called monsters. At the Townmaster's house, she becomes a fierce advocate for Vukradin, passionately defending him as an idealist seeking honest wages and conflict-free gold rather than plunder from the dead. She argues that Phandalin needs principled champions, not mercenaries, and uses her influence to negotiate better terms for the party, though she overlooks Valphine in her advocacy.

### Toblen (Spiderman)
The innkeeper of the Stonehill Inn, nicknamed Spiderman after a childhood incident. As a child, Toblen fell into a spider's nest, was covered in hatching baby spiders, and was not afraid—earning the nickname through his calm demeanor amid the spiders. He serves drinks during the musical performance.

### Daran Edermath (Silverleaf)
A tall, lean half-elf with silver-streaked dark hair, weathered features, and sharp emerald eyes reflecting quiet wisdom. Once an adventurer, Silverleaf now tends an orchard and wanders the countryside. He approaches Vukradin at the inn with a pitch about a cursed lighthouse on the coast where ships lure to crash, hoping to hire adventurers for treasure retrieval from wrecks. He is shocked by Vukradin's vehement refusal to rob the dead.

### Ser Kaelen Thorn
A man in his early thirties with dark hair, clean-shaven face, a scar above his right eyebrow, and chain mail armor. Ser Kaelen is an operative of the Order of the Gauntlet, an organization of fighters, magicians, storytellers, and clerics dedicated to improving the north. He recruits Vukradin based on admiration for his principles, offering associate membership and promising that the Order's backing would amplify his message to tens of thousands. He assures Vukradin the funding comes through conflict-free donations. He acknowledges his order's past war with Brewbarry's people as a necessary defense. He identifies the lighthouse curse as likely involving a monster rather than mere rumor.
