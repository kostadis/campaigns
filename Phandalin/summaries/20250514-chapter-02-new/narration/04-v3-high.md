"Meals sounds a little better than just running straight to work. To the inn."

Soma has chosen boiled leaves over profitable labor. Sensible, though she immediately undermines the calculation.

"Are the meals free?"

Vukradin repeats, "Are the meals free?"

"Where have you been living that meals are free?"

"I don't have any money for the meal. I'll just eat my rations outside. You guys go ahead."

"Starving artists."

"After traveling for a day, it's just getting off our feet for a while sounds better than getting a negotiation. But if the bard has his druthers, we won't."

"Maybe I could play for my meal."

"You could sit outside and play, and see if you can upturn your hat or whatever."

"Do some begging. Maybe people drop some coin. A performance for money. Yeah, I would do that."

Thus rest becomes commerce, and commerce becomes a performance. Vukradin can turn an empty purse into an ethical position before most people admit they are hungry.

The Stonehill Inn is a modest roadhouse with rooms above and a public room below. Toblen Stonehill is short, friendly, and far too pleased to see us, which tells me business has been poor or danger has been plentiful. He wipes down a table as we enter.

"Please sit. Rest your weary legs. How can I help you?"

"Absolutely. Tea."

"Tea? Just tea?"

"Just tea."

"You want me to boil water and give it to you?"

"Yes. I have the herbs myself, if you don't."

Toblen examines Soma, then the chair. His eyes linger on the joints beneath the seat.

"Oh, we're getting to the fat shaming now."

"I think you should sit right here, but if you don't mind, I'd like to get the sturdier chair."

He returns with a heavier chair and sets it beneath Soma with both hands. He charges ten copper for the boiled water. A remarkable profit margin. House Sotorra would approve, though we would have poisoned a rival before announcing it.

Vukradin places himself before Toblen.

"I want to play for your inn for a while. I have no money."

Toblen pauses. "Well, if you have something — one of the more fashionable tunes from Waterdeep, or from Neverwinter, I think. Or even Triboar."

"Yes, yes, one of those."

"I'd be delighted to hear that."

"Of course, that's what everybody wants to hear."

"One of those bangers. Play Freebird."

"Yeah, totally. Freebird."

The suggestion wounds Toblen. His mouth tightens, and his hand stops against the damp cloth.

"I'll play whatever you want. Whatever you want."

"I didn't realize that. My music — if you have some."

"I'm here to please."

"Well, I mean — if you're willing to have one of my creations, I think I could be even entertained by one of yours."

"What are your creations?"

"You seem to want to play a music piece of your own."

"Well, okay. So I can spend the evening playing music. And I don't know if he has a piano, but I have a couple instruments."

Vukradin begins. His performance is technically accomplished, and Toblen’s face loosens as the tune develops. I sit beside Soma’s scandalously expensive tea and endure the display. Music without pain is decoration. It enters the ear, occupies time, and leaves no mark.

Toblen pours a glass of ale.

"Well, that is definitely worth some ale."

"But did you love it?"

Toblen smiles. Vukradin studies him and discovers nothing useful.

"Well, if you didn't love it, I can't take your beer."

Vukradin looks prepared to reject the only compensation available because Toblen’s pleasure may not meet his moral standard. I intervene in the collapsing negotiation, though even I cannot decide how much certainty survives such heroic misreading. His confidence in Toblen’s adoration settles into place regardless.

"I believe it."

"All right. Well, then I will take the beer."

"Sometimes it's good to be an idiot."

"Ignorance is bliss."

"So Brewbarry liked it. He's my number one fan."

I am bored with the entire musical display. The ale, however, has served its purpose. Toblen has accepted us as customers without payment, which permits him to convert hospitality into questions.

"Once again, the orcs have come down from the mountains to prey on the lowlands. If Neverwinter doesn't send help soon, the orcs will overrun Phandalin and destroy everything we work so hard to rebuild."

Now the evening improves.

"Do you have anything to do with Phandalin? Are you part of the support Neverwinter is sending to us?"

"Yeah, that's right."

"If there's money in it."

Toblen’s relief falters. "Ah — money? I thought Neverwinter was going to pay you, not us. Oh. You're adventurers."

"What gave it away?"

"I don't really understand how we're going to get paid."

"Well, that's very easy. Outside the Townmaster's Hall, there is a quest board. The Townmaster — the city has some problems, and it's best if you go there and check them out."

"Are you dealing with these orcs on that list?"

"Well, you'd have to go check what the townmaster has decided is important enough for the city to collect. He is — in my mind, I'm not sure how he prioritizes things."

A townmaster who conceals his priorities behind a board is either incompetent or clever enough to make strangers absorb the first consequences. Both possibilities are useful.

"Do you know why the orcs are causing trouble lately?"

"Well, you must have heard of the War of the Dragons and the War of the Giants, followed by the barbarian hordes, followed by the disturbances in the Underdark."

"Oh, the world is always at war. What's special about these orcs today?"

"Well, usually during these orcs like to expand, and when there isn't a strong army to hold them back, they expand into territories that don't belong to them."

"So, wait, was there a strong army here previously that disbanded?"

"Well, the various wars depleted Neverwinter's resources."

"This place doesn't look like it ever held a lot of troops in the past — I don't know, 200 years."

"No, no, no. Phandalin has never been a place with a large army. It was overrun by orcs many years ago, but since then it's recovered."

Recovered is an optimistic term for rebuilding in the same vulnerable ground while relying upon an absent army. The Overbright provides light without limit, land without discipline, and settlements whose defenders are surprised each time an old enemy notices the walls are thin. Lathander’s radiance exposes every weakness and permits none of them to remain politely concealed. Power without apology.

Toblen turns his attention to Brewbarry.

"I, in fact, lived in Triboar, and managed to get word about the disaster that came there from the Uthgardt barbarians."

His gaze travels across Brewbarry’s height and arms.

"You wouldn't be one of those barbarians, would you? I'm so sorry."

"Are you apologizing for your ancestors?"

"I can vouch for him. He really loves music. He's not like the other barbarian."

"I've known him a week. He seems fine."

A perfect defense. Vukradin offers musical taste as evidence of innocence; Soma offers limited observation. Toblen accepts both because suspicion has become socially inconvenient.

"Look, my friends — whatever plans you might have, and whatever adventures you might have, and whatever stories you might have: all is forgiven between us. The past is the past. The future is the future."

"What are you forgiving me for?"

"I'm not forgiving you. I'm forgiving all barbarians."

"The barbarian."

"Yes, not all barbarians. Well said."

Toblen has now absolved Brewbarry of an offense he has not established, on behalf of victims who are not present, while granting himself authority over every barbarian. This is the finest entertainment the inn has provided.

"But I do recommend you go talk to you if you're looking for work. Well, if you're looking to spend some money or need a place to stay, I do have a room."

"I can't afford to live here."

"Yeah, I don't think we've got enough money for all of us."

"We'll have to go collect some coin."

"Yes — well, in that case, I do recommend you go check the quest board out."

We prepare to leave. Toblen remembers one final instrument of persuasion.

"Oh — one minor detail."

"Well, when you're outside, just be careful and aware that we do have a small dragon problem."

"The problem with the small, or the dragon?"

"Do we get paid if we kill the dragon?"

"I'm told the dragons have hoards."

*Dragons.* Vast, radiant destruction held inside scale and appetite, followed by the delicate pain of deciding who owns what remains. At last, a fashionable tune.

"People just don't understand."

"Yeah, you guys are all a little too materialistic, just robbing from each other. You know, boiled water is cheap."

The tea has cost more than the music and disclosed less than the ale. The orcs wait beyond Phandalin, a dragon waits somewhere above it, and the quest board holds the townmaster’s hierarchy of desperation.

That is sufficient reason to stand.
