“All right. So do you want to walk in? You walk in?”

Soma regards the ruined settlement without concern for whatever may be regarding us from within it. Prudence and fear are often confused in the Overbright, largely by those who possess too much of one and insufficient quantities of the other.

“Yes, let's go straight in.”

“Send me in first.”

“Yes, no. Who's going in? Oh, you're going. I'll be right behind you. Absolutely.”

“I'm the toughest. Also, I got nothing to lose. I'm late in life.”

The wall ahead has partially collapsed, leaving broken stone across the courtyard. Rubble hacked from the canyon lies in three heaps beneath a temple facade rising above us, its open doorway flanked by granite dwarves in cloaks. Weather has eaten their faces but spared their evil grins. Sensible masonry.

“So, does it look like it was recently destroyed or a long time ago?”

The exposed cuts and cleared stone answer me. This is an excavation, and not a fresh ruin.

“These are everywhere.”

“Grave robbers.”

Soma’s judgment is cleaner than archaeology’s preferred vocabulary.

“Is there statues? It's dwarves that are excavating a dwarven ruin. Is that correct?”

“I had no intention of being stealthy.”

“No, no. So, loud. It's an excavation.”

“There's bombs and stuff going off, right?”

“Very naive.”

“We're level one.”

Our approach announces itself across the stone. Two living dwarves appear among the dead ones, and Soma narrows the distinction.

“You said they were statues.”

Vukradin advances before caution can develop procedural authority.

“They're very excited. Okay, I'll rush up to talk to them. I have a high charisma.”

Dazlyn Grayshard is chewing when he confronts us. He guards the excavation before he asks why we have come, which tells me that possession has already preceded legitimacy.

“I'll just have you note that this is a private excavation, and everything that we're doing, we're not sharing.”

“So we're here from Townmaster Harbin. And he wanted us to give you a message.”

“Oh, how kind of him. What was the message?”

“There's a white dragon coming here to kill you.”

“Here there be dragons.”

“Well, okay, guys. We did it.”

“Let's go.”

“Let's go get our money. Right. I mean, that's all the quest said.”

“I'm walking.”

Vukradin turns. The dwarves have received the warning exactly as commissioned. Their subsequent dissatisfaction is not our contractual concern.

“Oh, hold on, hold on, hold on.”

“Did you need something?”

Norbus Ironrune introduces himself and his partner with sufficient detail to delay our departure.

“Well, you know — do you see the back? My name is Norbus Ironrune, and I'm a Shield Dwarf Prospector. And Dazlyn Grayshard over here is my partner. And yeah, we've been looking for mountains in the southwest of Phandalin. And we decided to explore this canyon, and we found startling evidence of an ancient dwarven settlement buried by an avalanche.”

“Are these your ancestors?”

“Well, not my ancestors per se, but they are somebody's ancestors.”

“Are you working on behalf of their ancestors?”

Norbus pauses. Vukradin’s questions have already located the weakness beneath the enterprise: the dwarves claim cultural authority while denying any specific mandate.

“No, no, but it's important to hear the rest of the story. Now, normally I would be very objected — I would object terribly to anyone excavating and digging into the Dwarven Dead, but this happens to be an old temple of Abbathor, the evil god of greed.”

“Do I know anything about Abbathor?”

The name catches against old lessons on divine appetite. Vukradin searches his memory first.

“Anybody do better than that?”

“Four.”

“I'm not very religious.”

“Any more roles, guys.”

“I said 14.”

“Oh, 14. Nice.”

The knowledge resolves with satisfying precision. Blood, gemstones, an eclipse, and a god displeased by an inadequate offering. I set the elements before the dwarves.

“Let me see if I can get this straight. Abbathor, during the solar eclipses, Abbathor is appeased with sacrifices of blood and gemstone.”

Dazlyn’s head begins moving before I finish.

“Yes, yes, yes, that's exactly right.”

“And on one occasion, the god found the sacrifices of this temple's priests lacking, so he caused an earthquake, and that avalanche collapsed the parts of the temple and buried the settlement.”

The violence is proportionate to the divine claim. A rationed sacrifice insults power by pretending tribute is negotiation.

“So is Abbathor a god I know from the drow cities?”

He is not. I have merely recognized the grammar of our gods: demand, deficiency, punishment. Menzoberranzan would consider the sequence elementary.

“Yeah. I mean, like, there's no news here.”

Dazlyn stiffens.

“Yes, but I'll have you note that Dwarven gods are not like your drow gods.”

His objection is cultural, not theological. Abbathor buried a settlement because its blood and gemstones failed to satisfy him, but apparently the distinction matters deeply if one is a dwarf.

Norbus notices the holy symbol of Lathander upon me. His attention moves from the emblem to my face, then back again. The contradiction is too complete for him: drow skin, the radiant disk, a cleric standing beneath the indifferent light of the Overbright.

“Unheard of. Unique.”

“There's like — I'm telling you, she's lying.”

There is a cost to becoming impossible. The ignorant merely hate what they recognize. Those who recognize every component separately must protect themselves by declaring the whole fraudulent.

Dazlyn studies the symbol.

“No, she's — that's that. That's definitely the symbol. Are you really a cleric of Lathander?”

The sunlight presses against my eyes and skin, inexhaustible power made visible through discomfort. Lolth hides below and rations favor through priestesses. Lathander crosses the world every morning without permission. My sisters never completed the calculation.

“Why, yes. I am.”

“Why, indeed. He's so gloriously bright.”

“Can I cast a light spell on you?”

Vukradin’s instinct is to answer disbelief with demonstration. Expert manipulation, though he persists in calling it sincerity.

The prospectors confer before returning to us.

“Let me say something. You know, we're just prospectors.”

“We're just messengers.”

“But I agree. We have a business proposition for you.”

“Okay.”

“We should at least hear them out. Talking is a free action, right?”

“Well, if you were willing to go in there and deal with any nasties and beasties, we would be willing to give you two sending stones.”

“Oh, those were, uh — messages, was it?”

“Are those valuable?”

“I'm not sure about that.”

“We also get the 50 gold pieces.”

“Well, we're already going to get the 50 gold pieces. All we have to do is go back to the town and we can get the 50 gold pieces.”

“Yeah. We just — our job here was to deliver the message.”

“The sending stones would allow you to project your music to two ends at one time. Just saying.”

“Does that mean I can listen to music all the time?”

“Well, at the other end of the matching stone or whatever has a musician on the other end. Yes.”

“Well, maybe we can sell them. They're probably worth something.”

The offer acquires value once Soma and Vukradin discover that the stones can serve either art or commerce. Both motives are more persuasive than danger, although neither dwarf has yet defined what waits inside.

“Do you have any idea what's in there?”

“You just said, take care of some nasties and beasties. What have you seen?”

“So what do you — what do you say more about what you're expecting us to do?”

“Well, I mean, just go inside, and if you see anything that is dangerous, kill it.”

“Do we look dangerous?”

“If you were bad people, yes.”

Norbus’s moral test is magnificently useless. I almost admire it.

“I just want to be clear. All of the treasure — you call it relics, all of that stuff — you're going to be returning that to the rightful owners, correct?”

“Yes, of course.”

“Okay, so we'll consider for you.”

“Make sure they don't consider themselves the rightful owners.”

“Oh. That's good, mate.”

Soma has found the precise defect in the agreement. Dazlyn’s first words concerned private ownership and refusal to share; Norbus’s answer therefore settles nothing.

“How green are you?”

“We're level one.”

“I mean, come on.”

“My whole life studying music and nothing else. What do you think?”

“You kids, you whipper snappers.”

“I've already made the day's journey.”

“We may as well have a look around and decide if it's worth our effort.”

We move toward the temple, but Norbus stops us before we proceed deeper. Fairness arrives late, after agreement, which is when disclosures are least likely to destroy a bargain. Still, we did ask.

He admits that they encountered an ochre jelly and fled before facing it.

“I mean, just very dangerous creatures. We ran out before we could face them.”

“Aren't those really bad?”

“Look, we are dwarven prospectors and explorers. I don't know whether what makes a dangerous monster or an easy monster. You've got swords, and you know how to cast spells, and you have a buff shell.”

“The shell doesn't help me eliminate the jelly.”

“Well, I mean, what I'm trying to say is that you seem to me as powerful, worthy adventurers.”

“We're all level one.”

“Stones are worth a lot of money, guys.”

“Come on.”

“Wait, how much?”

“I don't know. I think we might be able to get like a couple hundred gold pieces for them.”

“Well, that would be a big, big update. But we need to keep ourselves alive.”

“Oh, sure.”

“Jelly.”

“When your alternative is starving, you're willing to take some risks.”

Vukradin steps toward the open doorway. I follow, my holy symbol resting against my armor, still unmistakable and still, to Norbus, impossible.
