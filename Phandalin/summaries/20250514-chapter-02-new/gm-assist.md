# Chapter 2

Date: 2025-05-14

## Summary
In the city of Neverwinter, four unlikely souls found themselves drawn together by desperation, curiosity, and the promise of easy coin. Vukradin, a serious-minded Aasimar musician, had spent years playing his carefully composed pieces at a piano bar to patrons who simply did not care. Despite working seven days a week, he never managed to make ends meet, and his dream of opening a proper music studio felt further away than ever. When a flyer appeared on the streets of Neverwinter calling for adventurers to head south and help secure the hinterlands on behalf of Lord Neverember, Vukradin saw no other choice but to answer it.

Among the regulars at Vukradin's piano bar was Brewbarry, a large and boisterous Goliath barbarian who had been cast out of his Uthgardt tribe for his fondness for drink. Where the other patrons shrugged at Vukradin's music, Brewbarry was utterly transfixed by it, having grown up with nothing but drums his entire life. The sweeping melodies and complex harmonies struck him as the music of the ages, and he became Vukradin's most devoted — and only — fan. When Vukradin announced he was heading south to seek his fortune, Brewbarry followed without a second thought, eager for both the company and the adventure.

Traveling out of Neverwinter with them was Valphine Sotorra, a Drow cleric who had been driven from the Underdark after the great city of her people was devastated by demonic forces. Emerging into the sunlight for the first time, she had found herself inexplicably drawn to Lathander, the god of the dawn, and had taken up his faith with the fervor of a true convert — though she was equally drawn to the promise of danger and pain that the surface world's wilder places had to offer. Just outside the city, the three met Soma, an elderly Tortle druid in her sunset years who guided travelers through the surrounding wilderness. Soma offered her services as a guide, and the unlikely quartet set off along the High Road toward the south.

Before leaving Neverwinter, Vukradin had a memorable exchange with a city guard about the ethics of adventuring. He had raised pointed questions about whether looting monsters amounted to trafficking in stolen goods, and whether the treasure rightfully belonged to the heirs of the monsters' victims. The guard, increasingly exasperated, had drawn a very literal line: the laws of Neverwinter ended at the city wall, and everything beyond it was, as he put it, "the gray." He even cheekily suggested that any unclaimed treasure could be donated to the city guard retirement fund for safekeeping. The guard's parting shot — asking whether Vukradin was quite sure he wasn't a paladin — followed the bard all the way down the road.

After several days of travel, the party arrived in Phandalin, a quiet frontier town of forty or fifty simple log buildings nestled in the rocky foothills of the Sword Mountains. Crumbling stone ruins surrounded the newer structures, hinting at a much grander settlement that had existed in centuries past. The town had no walls and no garrison, and most of the adults kept weapons close at hand. It was a place built by hard-working people trying to carve out a life in the wilderness, and it showed.

The party made their way to the Stonehill Inn, a modest two-story roadhouse at the heart of town, where the friendly proprietor Toblen Stonehill welcomed them warmly. Vukradin, having no coin for a meal, offered to perform in exchange for food, and his music was impressive enough to earn him a glass of ale from Toblen. Whatever Toblen truly thought, Vukradin came away convinced that the innkeeper adored the performance. Over drinks, Toblen shared troubling news: orcs had been descending from the mountains to raid the lowlands, and if Neverwinter did not send help soon, Phandalin could be overrun. He also mentioned, almost as an afterthought, that there was a small dragon problem in the area. He directed the party to the quest board outside the Townmaster's Hall for any paying work.

The board outside the boarded-up Townmaster's Hall listed three tasks posted by Townmaster Harbin Wester, each offering a reward for services rendered. After some debate about ethics, rewards, and the general shadiness of the whole enterprise, the party settled on the Dwarven Excavation quest, which promised fifty gold pieces upon completion. The journey to the site took them roughly fifteen miles into the foothills, where a canyon with eighty-foot rocky walls opened up to reveal a ruined settlement at its end. A great wall of black stone with a broken gate marked the entrance, one door hanging by a single hinge and the other gone entirely.

Inside the canyon, the party found two Shield Dwarf prospectors named Dazlyn Grayshard and Norbus Ironrune, who were initially defensive about their private excavation before warming up once the party delivered Townmaster Harbin's warning about a white dragon spotted in the area. Norbus explained that they had found an ancient dwarven settlement buried by an avalanche and an old temple dedicated to Abbathor, the evil dwarven god of greed. After the party's Religion check supplied the lore that Abbathor had caused the earthquake and avalanche in displeasure at his priests' inadequate sacrifices, Dazlyn confirmed the account. Both dwarves were visibly startled to encounter a Drow cleric who worshipped Lathander, the god of light — a combination so unlikely that Norbus flatly declared she had to be lying, even as the holy symbol around her neck said otherwise. The prospectors offered the party a pair of sending stones — magical objects that allowed communication across great distances — as payment if they would venture inside and deal with whatever dangerous creatures had taken up residence in the ruins.

The party entered the temple with Soma at the front of the marching order. Valphine's passive Perception immediately revealed a secret door in the foyer, and Vukradin volunteered to enter the narrow passage beyond it first. Farther down the passage, the party discovered a second secret door before exploring a chamber to the east. The chamber was partially collapsed and filled with rubble, apparently shaken apart by the same ancient earthquake that had buried the settlement. There was nothing of value inside, and the unstable ceiling made further digging feel like a gamble not worth taking.

Turning their attention to the rest of the complex, the party found a side passage that appeared to loop back toward the entrance in a confusing arc, raising questions about the temple's original layout. A closer examination of the collapsed room revealed protruding stone blocks built into the walls — defensive positions designed to give fighters cover from an entrance that no longer existed. The room had once connected to another section of the temple to the northeast, but that passage had been sealed off entirely by the collapse. The party stood at the edge of the known, peering into the darkness of a ruin that clearly held more secrets than it had yet given up, with the prospectors' warning of an ochre jelly somewhere inside and reports of a white dragon somewhere in the region.

## NPCs
### Lord Neverember
The ruler of Neverwinter who is hiring adventurers as contractors to secure the hinterlands and re-establish trade to the south, offering them the right to keep whatever gold and treasure they find.

### Neverwinter City Guard
An official who explains the contractor nature of adventuring to the party, arguing that the laws of Neverwinter do not apply beyond the city walls and jokingly suggesting unclaimed treasure be donated to the city guard retirement fund.

### Toblen Stonehill
The short, friendly human proprietor of the Stonehill Inn in Phandalin. He welcomed the party, listened to Vukradin's music in exchange for ale, and shared local rumors about orc raids from the mountains and a dragon sighting in the area.

### Harbin Wester
The Townmaster of Phandalin who posted quests with varying rewards on the board outside the Townmaster's Hall. The selected Dwarven Excavation quest promised 50 gold pieces.

### Dazlyn Grayshard
A Shield Dwarf prospector partnering with Norbus Ironrune to excavate an ancient temple of Abbathor. Initially defensive about the private excavation, he warmed to the party after they delivered the dragon warning, confirmed the temple lore recalled by the party, and hired the party to deal with its dangerous creatures.

### Norbus Ironrune
A Shield Dwarf prospector excavating the ruins of a temple to Abbathor. He joined Dazlyn in discussing the site, warned the party about a dangerous ochre jelly they had seen somewhere inside the temple, and together with Dazlyn offered sending stones as payment for clearing the ruins.

## Locations
### Neverwinter
A major city on the Sword Coast that has recently survived wars with dragons, giants, and barbarian hordes. Lord Neverember rules here and is hiring adventurers as contractors to re-establish trade and civilization to the south. Vukradin worked at a piano bar here before taking up adventuring.

### The High Road
The main trade route between Neverwinter and the south, recently made safer by increased patrols following the recent wars.

### Phandalin
A frontier mining town of 40 to 50 simple log buildings nestled in the rocky foothills of the Sword Mountains. Crumbling stone ruins surround the newer structures. The town has no walls and no garrison, and is currently threatened by orc raids from the mountains and a dragon sighting nearby.

### Stonehill Inn
A modest two-story roadhouse at the center of Phandalin, run by the friendly proprietor Toblen Stonehill. Vukradin performed music here in exchange for a glass of ale.

### Townmaster's Hall
A boarded-up building in Phandalin featuring a quest board outside where adventurers can find work posted by Townmaster Harbin Wester.

### Lionshield Coster
A merchant outpost visible along the road in Phandalin, rumored to be experiencing supply chain disruptions.

### Dwarven Excavation Site
An ancient dwarven settlement and temple complex dedicated to Abbathor, buried by an avalanche in the canyon foothills of the Sword Mountains. The site features a broken black stone gate, heaps of rubble, a temple facade with statues of grinning dwarves, and partially collapsed interior chambers with defensive stone blocks.

## Items

A notice posted in Neverwinter on behalf of Lord Neverember, calling for adventurers and heroes to head south to secure the hinterlands and re-establish civilization. The promise of easy money lured Vukradin and others toward Phandalin.

### Sending Stones
A pair of magical stones that function as a communication device, similar to walkie-talkies. Offered by the dwarven prospectors Dazlyn and Norbus as payment if the party agrees to clear the ruined temple of dangerous creatures.
