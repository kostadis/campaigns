### Brewbarry

The hallway was narrow. Too narrow for a halberd. I knew it before I stepped in. But Vukradin said go, and I go where Vukradin says.

Soma called it an ochre jelly. I'd seen them before, in the northern caves near the tribe's winter grounds. You don't hit them with blades. Everyone knows you don't hit them with blades.

I swung my halberd anyway.

The blade passed through the thing like water, and the jelly split clean in two. One became two. Two blobs, both of them angry, both of them right next to me.

Vukradin said something about the AC. Eight. You don't see that every day. I wasn't listening. I was watching the two jellies pulse and shudder, reforming into independent shapes. My weapon felt stupid in my hands. I had made it worse.

The jellies moved faster than they should have. Jelly. Slow. That's what you think. These ones rolled a nineteen on their initiative. Minus two makes seventeen. Still faster than me. Faster than Vukradin. Faster than all of us.

The first pseudopod swung and missed. I heard Vukradin say it. "You missed." Good. One of them couldn't aim.

The second one found me.

It hit me in the chest, right where my scale mail doesn't cover the gap at the shoulder. The impact was enormous — the kind that doesn't just hurt, it reorganizes your understanding of what hurt means. Something wet and cold spread through my ribs. I felt my knees buckle. The world went gray at the edges.

*One hit point.*

I didn't say it. I felt it. The number sitting in my chest like a stone. One more hit and I was done. One more pseudopod and Brewbarry the legend becomes Brewbarry the corpse on a blood-stained altar in a hallway he chose to walk down.

The rage came up then. Hot and clean. It filled the space the pain had opened.

And then, later — after the second jelly dropped from the ceiling and we were still fighting — I remembered. Stone's Endurance. I'd forgotten to use it. The GM running me remembered for me. Rolled a d12 plus two. Eleven points absorbed.

I took one point of damage from a blow that should have broken me.

I stood there, halberd in hand, two jellies in front of me, and I thought: *My body saved me. My stupid, stubborn body. The thing that keeps getting hit and keeps getting back up.*

The blood-stained altar was still there at the end of the hall, waiting. But I was still standing in front of it.
