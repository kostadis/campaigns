The road back to town winds through low hills that catch the last of the day's light, and I savor the burn of it on my skin, that faint sting the Overbright offers so freely. The dwarves and their tedious gratitude are behind us. Ahead, I assume, lies nothing but walking.

I am wrong, and the wrongness announces itself the way it always does: a prickle at the base of my skull, the old House Sotorra instinct that kept me alive through three assassination attempts before my fortieth year. I stop. The others stop too. Even the goliath, who could sleep through a cave-in, has his head up and his grip shifted on the halberd. None of us are caught flat. My mother would call that competence. I call it a pleasant surprise.

Four orcs crest the hill ahead, weapons drawn, spread in a loose line. Ready to attack. I examine them the way I was taught to examine everything: axes, hide armor, no ranged weapons visible, no shaman among them. Crude. And yet I do the arithmetic I have been avoiding since the fungi caves, and the arithmetic is unkind. My connection to the fiery god is a thin warmth, nearly guttered. The Bard has sung himself hollow. The tortle has spent whatever green power she carries.

Soma says it aloud, flat as a stone dropped in a well. "All right. Oh, boy. Entering in with no spells on us, huh?"

*No.* Nothing but steel and tongues. How deliciously precarious.

Vukradin squints at the hilltop, genuinely baffled. "Why are they attacking—"

He does not finish the question, because the answer does not interest him. The next thing out of his mouth is entirely predictable and, I confess, the reason I have not yet abandoned this troupe.

"I'd like to talk to them."

Of course he would. He would talk to a cave-in. He would negotiate with a rockslide about its approver structure. I turn to look at him, and I let the pause stretch until it becomes its own small cruelty.

"I'm sure they would love to have that conversation with you. Do you speak Orcanese?"

He blinks. "What do I speak? That's a good question."

A good question. The aasimar has faced down two kings and a shapeshifter with nothing but his voice, and he does not know what languages live in it. I wait while he inventories himself.

"Uh, Goblin, but not Orc."

Goblin. Naturally. The tongue of the one people on this road who are not currently pointing axes at us.

Which leaves me. I feel the attention of the party swing my way, and I permit myself a small nod. Yes. I speak Orc. Fluently, with the accent of the noble districts, where the language was heard daily in the kitchens and the stables and the fighting pits below.

"She speaks Orc?" Vukradin sounds delighted, as if I have produced a rare instrument.

"We had servants," I say.

*Servants.* The word is a courtesy. In Menzoberranzan the orcs of House Sotorra wore collars, and I learned their tongue the way one learns the language of any tool one intends to use precisely. My sisters never bothered. They preferred the whip, which requires no vocabulary. I preferred to understand what the collared ones muttered when they thought no drow was listening. Knowledge is the sharper blade. It always was.

Soma makes a small sound in her throat. "Oh, you just made me feel bad."

How curious, that guilt. She did not hold the collar. She was not even there. The surface folk carry pain on behalf of strangers as if it were a virtue, and perhaps, in the theology I am still assembling, it is. Pain freely taken up. I file the thought away for my devotions.

"Excellent," Vukradin says, already recovered, already scheming. "Good choice." Then, brightening further: "I speak Undercommon. You, you speak Undercommon, don't you, Valphine? Of course. We should, uh, we should have our side conversations in Undercommon so the others can't understand us."

Of course I speak Undercommon. It is the tongue I dreamed in for a century. That the Bard proposes secret counsel with me, in my cradle language, while the tortle stands within earshot — there is a Lolthian elegance to it that I doubt he perceives. He proposes intrigue the way a child proposes a game.

Soma perceives it. "Like, I'm just, like, a hired tour guide. What are you even trying to hide from me? I st— I still need to, you know, get, uh, what's-his-face, Vukradin, wealthy enough to pay me for my services, right?"

"Uh, that might take a long time," Vukradin admits.

"It's gonna take the entire campaign. I think that's where we're at."

On the hill, the four orcs have not moved. Waiting. Measuring us as I measured them. I step forward, past Brewbarry's shoulder, out to where they can see me clearly: a drow, in mithral, walking toward them in the open light. Whatever they expected on this road, it was not that.

I have no fire left in me. Only words, in a tongue I learned from the collared. I take a breath and shape the first guttural syllable on my tongue.
