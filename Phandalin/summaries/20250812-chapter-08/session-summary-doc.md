# Chapter 10: The Tower of Storms

## Vukradin — Silence at the Tower of Storms

The harpy’s song still clings behind my eyes, sweet and coercive, turning affection into a weapon. Some of us have already felt it take hold. One singer is enough.

I answer with Silence.

The spell opens around us, a clean sphere where wingbeats, boots, breath, and song cease together. It does not merely muffle the harpy. It removes her argument.

"Like I said, it's not going to matter. Silence. That should eliminate the charm condition."

The others look toward me through the sudden absence. Soma tests the boundary and confirms what I already know.

"Everyone is deafened inside the circle."

"She can sing. She can sing all she wants. We can't hear her."

The compulsion releases. I feel it withdraw from the company, leaving no melody behind, only the memory of pressure. Precision matters in life-and-death situations, so I point to the condition that no longer applies.

"We can delete charmed. As I said, it wasn't going to matter."

The harpy beats backward and drops beyond the parapet. She has lost the only advantage she trusted, and instead of entering the sphere to fight us honestly, she abandons the exchange.

"Ran away. Okay. He ran away."

*Foolish!*

Soma studies the tower wall and the creatures circling above it. The silence protects us, but protection alone will not finish this. Our enemies remain airborne, outside the radius, and perfectly capable of waiting for us to make a mistake.

"Or I could go into spider mode. That'd probably be fine too."

She considers the creatures she knows well enough to become.

"Well, I think technically you're only supposed to have ones that you're familiar with, right?"

Soma chooses the giant wolf spider. Her limbs lengthen, her shell and flesh folding into a dark, many-legged body built for stone, height, and sudden violence.

Our immunity proves narrower than I first believe. Resisting one harpy’s song protects us from that singer for the rest of the day, but the other voices remain dangerous, and each song can travel across the tower and far down the coast.

"Oh, so I don't have to worry — am I immune to all harpy songs, or just the harpy song from that harpy?"

For one excellent moment, I understand that every harpy song has become irrelevant.

"Oh, wow."

Then the distinction becomes clear. Only the singer we have already resisted is harmless to us. The others can begin the same corruption afresh.

Fine. The Silence remains our center.

Soma points toward the wall beneath another harpy.

"I mean, in theory, I could probably scale this wall next to number two to help get into range, right?"

No stairs are necessary. The spider grips the exterior stone and climbs.

"I mean, the spider is climby crazy. So let's just say I climb the wall then. So wherever it is, I'm on top of it."

Her legs find mortar seams and wet projections while the sea wind presses against her. She checks herself before attacking, holding to the forms she has actually studied rather than pretending unlimited familiarity with every beast in existence.

"I don't know, hold on. We got some of the wolf spider. Trying to limit the shapes I use."

Then her fangs strike.

"That's a hit."

The bite lands hard enough to stagger the harpy in the air.

"Nice. 11 damage."

"And yep. On harpy number two."

The creature should be nearly finished. It is not. It beats its wings, wounded but entirely capable of continuing.

"Wow, these have a lot of hit points."

"Wow."

The tower’s upper ledge rises only a short distance above us, enough to matter to a halberd and not enough to discourage Brewbarry. I judge the height.

"10 feet. Okay."

Soma still clings within the Silence. Brewbarry advances beneath her, sets his hands on the halberd, and lets rage fill the spaces where sound ought to be.

"And then with rage. And rage, yep."

He swings. The blade passes beneath the harpy.

"Oh, does it 10 misses?"

"10 misses. Yeah. All right. So I guess that's it for me. Yikes."

It is a miss by the smallest possible margin, which remains a miss. Scandalous.

The wounded harpy disengages and flies away from Soma’s fangs and Brewbarry’s reach.

"Really?"

Of course. These creatures do not want a battle in which their song is useless. They want charmed victims walking willingly toward the edge. The Silence denies them that corruption, so they circle beyond our weapons and search for exposed minds.

Ranged attacks will decide the fight.

"Yeah, clearly we're going to need ranged."

Valphine searches through her magic.

"I believe I have a range spell. And let's see what this is."

I stop her before she wastes the attempt.

"You can't cast the spell with a verbal component."

"I know. Let's see if I can step outside and then come back in or something."

That is the answer, but stepping outside means accepting the song again unless the singer’s hold has already been broken. Valphine settles on her crossbow, which requires neither prayer nor audible command.

"So I can shoot it with an arrow, I guess."

She raises the weapon toward the harpy that tried to charm her.

"Yeah, you can definitely do that. Nice. Ah, crit."

The bolt strikes cleanly.

"Critical damage even. 4d6."

"Whoever. Can I see the one who was trying to charm me?"

The singer remains visible beyond the Silence.

"Number three. Yes. Number three would be the one I would attack."

The bolt hurts her, though not enough to break her control at once. Valphine and Brewbarry both force the remaining influence from their minds before the next song can pull them toward the parapet.

Now the rhythm is obvious.

"Which means you can step out of the silence, attack, and go back in. As long as another harpy doesn't start singing."

The sphere is no longer merely shelter. It is the center of the composition. We leave it for a single phrase, deliver the attack, and return before the harpies can answer. Their song controls the open air. I control when we enter it.

"So I can probably go out here, cast a spell against Harpy 3, and then run back inside, correct?"

The opening is there.

"Okay, so I'll do that."

I cross the boundary. Sound crashes back into me: surf against rock, wings above the parapet, the harpy drawing breath. She is close enough that range is no concern.

"Yeah, I mean, it's only 10 foot away, so yeah."

I release the spell. The blast catches her and shakes what remains of her defenses. Then I step backward into perfect silence before she can sing.

"It's down to 28. Run back inside."

Inside the sphere, I hear nothing.

Exactly.

## Soma — The Battle of the Parapet

The silence presses into my head until I can hear my own legs touch stone.

Eight legs. Better legs for this work.

I climb the outside of the tower, my spider body close against the wall. Salt has worked into the mortar. The stone crumbles beneath two of my feet, but six remain, and six are sufficient. The upper parapet waits ten feet above the plateau. The harpies wait above that.

Below me, Brewbarry swings his halberd and finds air. The blade carries him half a step before he catches his weight. A long weapon is useful when your enemy agrees to be near it.

One harpy does not. It tears away from the melee and circles beyond the silence, calling us after it. The song cannot enter Vukradin’s spell, so the creature tries to pull my bale out instead.

Vukradin takes the invitation halfway. He darts beyond the quiet, strikes the distant harpy with magic, and retreats before its song can settle into him. The blast is not much, but the creature stops howling.

Valphine fires at the other singer. Her bolt lands. The harpy jerks in the air, catches itself, and continues singing.

Of course it does.

I crest the parapet. The stone levels beneath my feet, and I turn toward the nearest harpy.

It folds its wings and drops at me. Claws scrape across my spider body, a shallow hit that shakes the borrowed flesh without threatening it. Then the harpy beats upward again.

It should not have turned its back.

I bite as it passes. My fangs sink deep enough to make the creature lurch in flight.

"It's 12 damage. Dude, Circle of the Moon druids start early. They don't really get better, but they start early."

The spider body is still strong. The hit costs me little. I can hold this form and keep climbing, biting, and occupying the attention of anything foolish enough to come close.

"Anyway, I'm just going to go whale on it anyway."

The harpy retreats along the parapet. Fine.

"I'm going to chase after it. I mean, it wants to play this game with me. I'll play with it."

My legs carry me over the stone, faster than my tortle joints would permit and with none of the complaint from my shell. I snap at the harpy and catch nothing.

"All right. That was a worthy effort."

The creature claws at me as I pass. I offer it the opening.

"Take the attack of opportunity. Come at me, bro."

Its talons cut through empty air.

"I see a miss."

It is not close. Not remotely.

"I see a hell of a miss."

Below, Brewbarry abandons the halberd and searches himself for something throwable.

"Well, I may have a throwing axe or something. Yeah, hand axe."

He studies the harpies, chooses the one I have already bitten, and moves until the distance suits his arm.

"All right. I guess hand axes are cheap, right?"

The axe turns once, twice, and passes wide.

"I heard that. Brewbarry's whiffing tonight. It's two whiffs."

Brewbarry finds the second axe.

"Okay. Sure. I guess I can throw the second one then."

"Hey, consistency, man. You can do it."

He throws. The second axe follows the first almost exactly.

"It's the exact same one."

Consistency achieved.

The second harpy draws breath and begins its own song, apparently convinced the first singer was the problem rather than the silence surrounding most of us.

"Good job."

Vukradin has reached his own conclusion about the pair of them.

"Oh, they really are dumb."

I give him the number.

"Intelligence 7."

Vukradin glances toward the boundary of his spell.

"So that'll be an issue if somebody wants to leave the sphere. Okay."

Valphine raises her crossbow toward the active singer.

"Two, you say. All right. Sure. I'll do number two."

The bolt punches into the harpy.

"Seven damage on two. I'll apply the damage."

The song wavers. Then it steadies.

"Ah, fiddlesticks."

The third harpy screeches and dives at Vukradin. Its claws miss, and he answers with magic. The blast catches it and breaks its song.

Vukradin does not wait for me.

"I'll do my attack."

"I hit — and another six damage on three. Okay, down to 22."

"Oh, that one stopped singing. It was singing, and it's no longer singing."

"Okay, but two is still singing."

The remaining voice carries across the open air with no sign of weakening.

"Oh. So it really doesn't matter if three stops singing while two is doing it."

Vukradin has already reached the same conclusion. The third harpy closes on him, expecting another spell.

It gets steel.

"So Vukradin is going to do his double daggers then."

I watch both blades come free.

"Thought you were a spell slinger."

"He's going to get two attacks. Let's see how that goes. That's one hit."

The first dagger lands hard.

"That's a hit and a half. Seven on three. That's down to 15."

The second does not connect.

"And then second attack. That's not a miss. All right. I'll take that."

It is a miss. Vukradin is allowed his own account of events.

The singing harpy drops toward Valphine next. Its claws scrape across her armor and find no purchase. Valphine already has her mace in hand. The harpy sees it too late.

As the creature climbs away, Valphine strikes it across the side. The blow is light, but it lands.

"All right. Yeah, and two."

I keep my eight feet planted on the upper stone and turn toward the harpy I chased. Below, my bale remains inside the silence, armed with daggers, a mace, an empty axe hand, and several increasingly firm opinions about flying creatures.

## Brewbarry — The Battle of the Harpies Continues

Vukradin stands inside the Silence. His mouth moves. Nothing comes out.

Then he touches his chest and points to himself. Magic gathers around him. His own music, held close.

He looks at the open stone beyond the Silence.

"And I will take the chance, because I don't want this combat to go on forever."

He runs out.

The harpy's song catches him at once. I see it in his step. His foot drags toward the singer. His face goes soft.

Then the magic he gave himself bites.

Vukradin stops. Shakes his head. Finds himself again.

He strikes the singer with magic. The spell lands. The harpy jerks in the air, and its song breaks.

"Wow. I'm having a good combat."

Good. Vukradin is still Vukradin.

Soma climbs past us in spider form. Legs catch stone where my boots would slip. She goes around the wall and comes up beneath the next harpy.

That one starts singing.

The sound presses into me even through the clash of wings and claws. Sweet. Empty. It promises that walking toward it will make everything easy.

Soma does not slow. She has heard Vukradin.

The spider climbs higher and bites. The harpy screams, but the song continues. Soma clings to the wall outside the Silence. The singer cannot pull her in again.

I need to reach them.

The battlement is rough stone. Easy enough. It only takes longer because I need both hands.

I put away my weapon and climb.

My fingers dig into the cracks. My boots push. The rage keeps me large and hot. The harpies are above me. Soma is fighting one alone.

Then the song finds me.

Everything stops.

The stone is still under my hands. My arms still hold my weight. But those things no longer matter. The harpy matters. I need to go to it.

I cannot.

It hangs beyond the battlement. Air between us.

My body strains forward. My hand will not reach for a weapon. My legs will not carry me through open sky. I can only cling to the wall and stare.

My rage consumes me. And yet, it has nowhere to go.

I try to tear my mind free. I know the song is wrong. I know Vukradin needs me. I know Soma is beside the creature.

The knowing does nothing.

The song stays.

Heat leaves my muscles. My reach shrinks. The rage dies because I have not struck anything, and nothing has struck me.

That hurts worse than a claw.

I have swung at air all night. My axes have missed. My hands have missed. Now even my anger misses.

The harpy swoops at Soma. It raises its claws, sees the spider beneath it, and pulls the blow short. The claws scrape stone instead. Soma shifts sideways and snaps at it. Her fangs close on air.

They stay there, missing each other.

Valphine does not miss.

Her crossbow cracks. The bolt catches the wounded harpy Vukradin struck. It drops from the air and hits the rock.

One dead.

The others keep moving. Down to strike. Up before we can answer. Wings beat grit into my face. I remain fixed to the wall, pulled toward a singer I cannot reach.

The third harpy dives at Vukradin.

Its claws hit him hard. He folds around the blow and nearly goes down. Nearly.

The Silence holds.

Vukradin looks thin on his feet. His shoulders sag. Blood darkens his clothes beneath the claw marks. There is almost nothing left in him, but his face tightens and the empty air remains empty.

He attacks. Misses.

He attacks again. Misses again.

His mouth moves inside the Silence.

"Yeah, I'm not feeling so well here, guys."

I cannot hear him. I know the words from his lips.

I pull against the song again. Nothing.

Soma keeps the other harpy busy against the wall. She turns her head toward Vukradin, then back to the claws in front of her.

"I mean, I'd like to come protect you, but I also have to help the other guy up here."

The harpies circle.

Vukradin stands.

I cling to the battlement without rage, without an axe in my hand, and without enough strength in my own mind to take one step away from the song.

## Valphine Sotorra — The Harpy's End

The harpy’s talons rake across Soma’s giant spider body and find the narrow weakness in her carapace. The impact drives her legs against the stone, hard enough to strip away the borrowed resilience of the form.

"16. Oh, yeah. That's just enough to hit. I'm at AC 16."

"All right. That takes me — that gets rid of my bonus hit points. Puts me down to 24. Or temporary."

She remains upright. Eight legs afford certain advantages in the disciplined management of pain, chiefly that several can tremble while the others continue the work.

Soma sinks her fangs into the last singer.

"And I mean, we're just going to try and break his concentration again. There's a hit. There's 12 damage on it. That takes him down to six."

The song stops.

Its absence strikes harder than the noise. The compulsion releases its grip upon Brewbarry, and his attention returns to the battlefield with all the subtlety of a portcullis falling.

"Brewbarry, you're free. Hooray. Brewbarry can actually attack. But can he hit?"

"Yeah, that's the key question."

There is Vukradin’s optimism again, presented as encouragement but functioning perfectly as provocation. He does not understand how expertly he manipulates Brewbarry. I see no reason to diminish the result by explaining it.

"I see no reason to move or disengage. I'm going to see this harpy to the end."

Soma anchors herself beside the wounded creature, her legs spread across the uneven rock. The harpy cannot withdraw without exposing itself, and Brewbarry now has the necessary angle.

"All right. So can I hit from here with my halberd?"

The weapon’s reach answers before anyone else needs to. Brewbarry plants his rear foot, sweeps the halberd through the open air, and catches the harpy cleanly.

"That is a hit. That is a kill."

The blade completes its course. The harpy falls.

The remaining creature turns in the air, wounded but still capable of resistance. I raise my hand crossbow and let the light, wind, and movement narrow to one precise point. The bolt strikes exactly where I intend, an immaculate shot whose elegance is somewhat undermined by the harpy’s stubborn refusal to die from it.

"Crit for four hit points."

Vukradin reads the total off before I can.

"Four damage."

"Nice. Well, you know, it's getting there."

Perfection and consequence are not always aligned. This is a basic principle of noble politics, divine service, and apparently hand crossbows.

Soma surges across the plateau toward the last harpy, the points of her spider legs scraping against the rock.

Soma measures the gap aloud.

"Yeah, I can clear that, right?"

"Yeah, no, I can get in there for sure. I got 40-range movement. Let's go."

Vukradin offers his encouragement.

"Chompy chompy."

She lunges. Her fangs close upon empty air.

"Roll. And I whiff. But at least I'm in the fray now."

The tactical merit remains. The harpy now faces a giant spider at close quarters, Brewbarry’s halberd within reach, and the others placed across every useful escape. Failure in the immediate act can still purchase position. Surface dwellers are inclined to call this consolation; in House Sotorra, we called it preparation.

Vukradin turns to the question of his own spell.

"You want me to drop the silence spell?"

Brewbarry, from inside the sphere:

"Well, I mean, I'm in melee now."

"Yeah, but would you be better off with a spell attack?"

He is asking me. I consider the arithmetic and find it favourable.

"Probably."

The silence has served its purpose. The song is broken, Brewbarry is free, and maintaining protection from a weapon no longer being used has become mere attachment to one’s own prior decision.

"All right, let's drop the silence. You can use a spell."

Sound returns all at once: surf below the plateau, wings beating, spider feet on stone, Brewbarry’s armor shifting beneath his grip. I invoke radiant power, inexhaustible and indifferent, and direct it against the harpy. Lathander’s judgment touches it with exquisite accuracy, although not with the annihilating force Vukradin evidently considers appropriate.

"That's a Dex save for four damage. Really? I could have."

Soma declines to let the contribution be diminished.

"Given the mace — hey, don't knock it."

"Four damage."

She puts the number in its proper proportion.

"It's half his hit points."

The light leaves a bright injury across the harpy’s defenses. It is not spectacular. It does not need to be. Pain is proof of contact, and divine contact has never required the approval of a bard conducting an arithmetic review.

"This is when we use Vicious Mockery."

Vukradin fixes his attention upon the harpy. His voice carries now, each syllable sharpened by the restored air, and the creature falters beneath an insult more lethal than either its content or dignity warrants.

Soma watches the method rather than the result.

"He's just taunting him on the way down."

"Pretty much."

She supplies the insult herself.

"Yeah, it smells of elderberry. 13 save for your life. Or four hit points. That's here."

The harpy’s wings lose their rhythm. Its body drops against the rock and does not rise.

"He is mocked to death."

"That Yo Mama joke really cut deep."

I lower my crossbow. There are many paths to divine judgment. Some are radiant. Some are deliciously painful. Some concern a harpy’s mother.

The battle’s end reveals the plateau beneath it: scattered feathers, thrown axes, harpy bodies, and several exhausted companions attempting to convert survival into procedure. I begin searching the corpses while Vukradin and Brewbarry address the more difficult question of where Brewbarry has thrown his weapons.

"I mean, I would like to retrieve my hand axes."

Vukradin suggests a larger supply.

"Might want to get more of those. Maybe, maybe. Yeah."

Brewbarry answers without hesitation. He has never counted them.

"I have a whole armory of hand axes available."

His armory is evidently subject to the limiting principle that its contents must exist. Brewbarry searches the nearby stone while I inspect claws, scraps of cloth, and whatever the harpies considered worth carrying.

"Oh. Oh, not like arrows?"

Ammunition, it appears, is finite, recoverable only in part, and best carried in quantities appropriate to the probable length of a battle. Vukradin receives this revelation with more surprise than he displayed when a flying creature sang Brewbarry out of his senses.

"Short rest? Yeah, and search the bodies."

"Well, then I will search for my hand axes."

The axes lie only a short distance from him. Their placement indicates that Brewbarry released them using much the same motion he gives his halberd, which is an admirable commitment to a successful technique and a poor understanding of differing weapons.

"Okay. They narrowly missed my own toes."

Soma asks after the hour.

"And his short rest is more than an hour, right?"

Vukradin rules it shorter.

"30 minutes."

We settle among the dead for half an hour. Wounds close as far as rest permits; breath steadies; weapons return to their owners. I continue my examination while the men recover from their contest against distance, inventory, and basic throwing technique.

"We'll no longer taunt them. We did a good thing here today."

Vukradin looks over the corpses and grants the defeated their final critical assessment.

"And also, I don't like harpies singing. Their music is — they're not joining the academy, that's for sure."

## Vukradin — Exploring the Tower of Storms

The rough-hewn stairs climb the eastern face of the rock, and the sea keeps striking below us as we ascend. At the top, a plateau hangs high above the water. Wooden doors stand in the tower wall, their iron hinges orange with rust, decorative lightning bolts carved across the boards.

"Rusty iron hinges. Okay. Can I go open the door?"

The harpies have stopped singing permanently.

"That's one way to put it."

I hear agreement behind me. "Yeah."

Soma and Brewbarry follow as I pull the door open. "Oh, yeah."

The first chamber is empty, its ceiling high enough to swallow the sound of our boots. Salt streaks the stone. A dirty window faces south.

"This window."

Confirmation comes back.

"Yeah."

"What do I see?"

Wrecks. Broken hulls lie among the rocks below, timbers rolling in the surf.

"Oh, yes. What the harpies have been doing to the local navy. Yes."

There are crates down there, chests, fittings, all the accumulated possessions of people who expected to reach another harbor. None of it became ownerless simply because its owners drowned.

"So many possessions that must be returned to their rightful owners."

The sailors are dead underwater. I understand this. Death does not erase ownership. It transfers it.

"It's — you know, what is it? They're heirs."

Someone groans behind me. I continue because the distinction is important.

"Their successors. Can't just take things from them. They died to these evil harpies."

The Order of the Gauntlet would apparently be pleased to have me on its side.

"I don't trust those guys."

Apparently that was not what was meant.

"You are going to be on your side."

No. They are pleased. That is somehow supposed to reassure me. Ser Kaelen can be helpful and still be managing me, and an institution can praise a principle while taking its funding from House Margaster. Compliments are not receipts.

The next room gives us frescoes: ships pitched sideways on stormy seas, sailors reaching from the decks, and a dark god looming above them. We all stare. I search my memory for hymns, forbidden verses, old performance pieces, anything that names the figure. Nothing answers.

"Anybody else?"

"Anybody else?" Soma echoes.

Valphine studies the walls and offers no revelation.

"Our cleric's not very religious, is she?"

Another attempt produces nothing.

"Wow. Nope. We got nothing tonight."

"Well, what you got is — it looks pretty. Pretty paintings."

That is true. The pigment has survived salt, damp, and whoever maintained a shrine devoted to drowning sailors. The workmanship is precise enough that the painted rigging remains visible.

"Is it frescoed onto the stone, or is it like a piece of art?"

Soma answers from the description.

"He said fresco, right? It's fresco."

"Very nice."

She is already imagining it somewhere else.

"I'd like something like this at the academy."

"You want to chisel out a section of wall and bring it along?"

"I could mold earth it out, right?"

I touch the stone beside the painted sea. It is tower wall, not loose soil, and removing it intact would require more than optimism.

"I don't think mold earth is going to be sufficient here."

"Ye have little faith."

The next chamber has another dirty window, this one crusted with salt. A stone altar stands against the southern wall, lightning bolts cut into its face. Above it, a metal rod descends from the ceiling, divides, and embeds both ends into the stone.

"That's like a metal lightning bolt or something. The lightning rod, yeah."

A lightning rod feeding an altar in a tower surrounded by wrecked ships. This is not subtle religious architecture. We may not know the god, but we know the arrangement is evil.

"Nice. Okay. Well, do we want to interact with the altar in any way? I mean, feel free, guys. Go right on."

Nobody rushes forward.

Beyond it, a ledge enclosed by a low stone wall holds the harpy nest. Feathers, bones, seaweed, and rotting scraps have been pressed together into a foul mat.

"Oh, the harpy nest?"

Yes.

"Oh, this is what you wanted to search, Valphine. Can we torch it?"

The smell alone argues for fire.

"I mean, it smells like nature."

Nature has many smells. This one is criminal.

"I think that's a great idea. I'll torch it right now. I'm not even going to search it."

"I'm just going to get out of there while you torch it. I think that's a fantastic idea."

Soma withdraws. Valphine searches through the nest first, turning aside clotted feathers until a potion of water breathing becomes impossible to miss. That creates another ownership problem. Consumable property is still property, and convenience is not provenance.

"Okay, well, we'll need to just determine — maybe we can use a legend lore spell to determine who owned this potion of water breathing. We'll just add that to blood money. Consumables."

"That's quite a moral code you got there."

I am accused of confusion.

"I'm confused."

No, everybody is confused.

"Everybody else is confused. I'm quite confused."

Apparently refusing to take property from dead sailors constitutes revolutionary behavior against the order of the realms.

"Yeah, this is a libertarian paradise, right? Like, just take what you want by force."

The more accurate description is colonial: civilization for those recognized as civilized, plunder for everyone else.

"Colonial. Mercantile. Yes."

There it is. A sailor dies outside the protected ledger, and suddenly every chest aboard the ship becomes a reward for whoever reaches it first.

"Well, more blood loot. Typical."

I put flame to the nest. The dry feathers catch first, then the seaweed begins to smoke. Heat pushes the rot-stink back through the opening.

"Let's lift the curse on this Tower of Storms."

"Oh, yeah. It might actually be something with that altar, huh?"

I watch the fire consume the place where the harpies slept and turn over what brought us here. Treasure was never the point.

"But the point of coming here was to liberate the castle, not just get the treasure, right? We're trying to liberate the castle. This is what is luring all the ships to be smashed. So we have a moral obligation to defeat the harpies."

"Sorry? We don't know that, do we?"

She is right. We know the harpies sang. We know ships broke below the tower. We do not know that one caused the other.

"I don't think Crabby McGee told us that."

Smoke crawls across the stone ledge. Behind it, the altar and lightning rod remain untouched.

## Soma — Showdown with Moesko

The door opens onto a long chamber with north-facing windows crusted in salt. Moesko sits against the south wall in a barnacle-covered chair. Giant octopus hide hangs across his body as armor, stiff where it has dried. The opalescent conch rests in his lap.

“Oh, yeah. I guess that doesn't look like a friendly, does it? Nope.”

The half-orc sees us. His hand closes around the conch.

“What are we facing?” Vukradin asks.

“Oh, geez. But he's just alone this time.”

“Oh, one of the orcs again?”

The same sort of anchorite we have already found among the Talosians. Another shell sprout who thinks a storm god needs help hurting people. This one stole the conch from Miraal.

“Oh, excellent. Okay.”

Vukradin finds the theft encouraging. Of course he does.

Moesko starts to rise. I send a thorn whip across the room, trying to drag him out of his chair before his feet settle. The hooked vine catches and pulls, but it will not put him on the floor. Fine. I have other small inconveniences.

A spray of poison follows. Moesko coughs and tightens his grip on the chair. It barely troubles him.

“I didn't feel like burning a wild shape in here.”

I look at the half-orc, alone in his large room.

“One lonely guy.”

Whatever Vukradin presses against Moesko’s mind meets a cleric’s stubbornness and stops there.

“He's a cleric, right? He's probably going to make that.”

“Oh, well — then he made it. Okay.”

Brewbarry rushes in, rage taking him again. His halberd cuts empty air. He plants his feet, swings once more, and misses by enough that even I cannot pretend otherwise.

“You know what? I'm sorry. It was the silence. The fact that you couldn't hear yourself scream — that was it.”

“That's right. That's definitely the problem.”

I offer the theory some support.

“I remember in martial arts, they teach you to call out with every punch. It makes you stronger.”

There. A sound tactical principle. Brewbarry has simply been deprived of yelling.

Valphine raises her crossbow. Her bolt punches into the octopus hide and makes Moesko turn. That is more attention than my poison earned.

Then lightning fills the room.

There is no warning beyond Moesko’s lifted hand and the smell of metal. The bolt passes through my bale in a white line. My muscles lock. Heat gets under my shell, along every joint and down into my legs. Vukradin stays upright, though his face has gone slack and pale. Valphine is still there. So am I.

Barely.

“Yep. Still standing.”

*One lonely guy.*

I reach for the borrowed life I had decided to conserve. The chamber is cramped, and a large body needs room. Brewbarry shifts aside. Sensible. I let the tortle body go and come down on four heavy paws as a brown bear.

The floor takes my weight. My breath comes hot through a wider mouth. Pain still flickers beneath the fur, but it belongs farther away now, behind thick muscle and another body’s endurance.

I close on Moesko before he can lift that hand again. One paw catches his armor and turns him. My other tears through the octopus hide. Then my jaws close. Both attacks land, hard enough to drive him back against the chair.

“Yay, bear. Rar.”

“Brown bear.”

Vukradin approves of the choice.

“The best bear. Best kind of bear.”

Correct.

Vukradin casts Cloud of Daggers around Moesko. Spinning blades fill the space between the half-orc, the chair, and me. Their edges catch the light from the windows. Moesko cannot remain there without being cut apart, but leaving means passing the bear.

He is still standing. That is becoming irritating.

“Because another lightning bolt is going to be deadly. Lightning will be an issue. Another lightning bolt will not go well. There will be three characters down.”

I tell Valphine what the arithmetic requires.

“Valphine, you got to do 12 damage or we're really screwed.”

“Or a silence spell, or something that's going to stop him from casting lightning bolt.”

Valphine keeps the crossbow.

Valphine considers her options.

“How about a crossbow bolt?”

It will not be.

“That's not going to be enough.”

“But sure. And we will go with that. Let's do five damage, and then I will step out of the room.”

Her bolt strikes. Then Valphine backs through the doorway, removing herself from any clear line Moesko might use.

“Good plan.”

Everyone else begins finding safer ground. I remain beside the spinning knives, broad enough to block most of the room and too stubborn to move without cause.

“Everyone's just abandoning the bear. The bear will be fine. Mama Bear go down. Brewbarry is fine.”

Moesko cannot call the lightning again. Good. Instead he throws both hands outward. Thunderwave bursts from him and hits my bear body as a wall of pressure. My paws leave the floor. I land farther back, claws scraping stone, ears ringing.

It hurts. I can continue.

Moesko abandons the dagger cloud before the blades can work through what remains of him.

“And if you leave that square, I'll get to attack you.”

Brewbarry charges. His halberd catches Moesko cleanly this time. The half-orc staggers, armor split, legs loose beneath him. He should be dead. He has neglected to fall.

There is no room for the bear to turn and finish this properly. I release the borrowed body. Fur draws inward. My shell returns around me, heavy and familiar, and the lightning pain comes close again.

Moesko is still upright.

Enough.

“All right. Can I attack to end the night? Please. Let's see if I can hit once on one of these two tries.”

I strike him.

Vukradin calls it before I can look.

“Dead.”

I check anyway.

“Dead?”

“Yes.”

Moesko collapses beside the barnacle-encrusted chair. The opalescent conch lies against the stone, still intact. My bale remains standing around it.

Barely is enough.
