The harpies’ music is finished. Permanently.

“That’s one way to put it.”

The tower waits above us, reached by rough-hewn stairs cut into the eastern face of the rock. At the top, a plateau stands eighty feet above the water, wet with salt and open to the wind. Wooden doors block the entrance. Lightning bolts have been carved into them, and their iron hinges are orange with corrosion.

“Rusty iron hinges.”

A neglected hinge is a small thing, but small neglects accumulate until doors fail, towers corrupt, and ships break on rocks.

“Okay.”

I put my hand to the wood.

“Can I go open the door?”

The hinges complain, but the door gives. Inside, the ceiling rises fifteen feet over an empty chamber. A dirty window in the south wall looks down toward the sea.

“This window.”

I cross to it, rubbing salt from the glass with my sleeve.

“What do I see?”

Wrecks. Broken masts, split hulls, and ribs of timber lie among the rocks below, washed by white water. Some sit deeper beneath the surface, dark lines visible between the waves.

“Oh, yes.”

I understand at once.

“The what the harpies have been doing to the local navy.”

The dead do not cease owning things merely because murder has made them inconvenient claimants. Their rings, tools, cargo, letters, and instruments remain theirs. Death does not convert private property into an adventurer’s reward.

“Yes.”

I press closer to the glass.

“So many possessions that must be returned to their rightful owners.”

Soma looks toward the water. The rightful owners are dead beneath it.

“It’s, you know, what is it?”

The answer is elementary, although the circumstances are admittedly damp.

“They’re heirs.”

No one greets this as the clean legal resolution it obviously is.

“Their successors. Can't just take things from them. Therefore, they died to these evil harpies.”

This is precisely the sort of position the Order of the Gauntlet would publicly celebrate, which does not improve it or diminish it.

“I don't trust those guys.”

We move into the next room. Frescoes cover the stone walls: ships thrown sideways on stormy seas, sailors clinging to rigging, and a dark, terrible god looming above them. The paint has endured the salt better than the hinges. Red, black, and deep green remain visible in the god’s robes and in the water beneath the ships.

I study the symbols. Nothing resolves. No remembered hymn, no theological refrain, no useful warning.

“Anybody else?”

Soma leans nearer. Valphine examines the work as well.

“Anybody else?”

The god remains anonymous.

“Our cleric's not very religious, is she?”

Another attempt produces nothing.

“Wow. Nope. We got nothing tonight.”

Soma steps back and considers the wall.

“Well, what you got is it looks pretty, it looks pretty. Pretty paintings.”

“Is it frescoed onto the stone, or is it like a piece of art?”

“He said fresco, right? It's fresco.”

“Very nice.”

The craftsmanship is excellent, even if the subject appears to endorse maritime slaughter.

“I'd like something like this at the academy.”

I look from the painted god to the masonry carrying it.

“You want to chisel out a section of wall and bring it along? Well, I don't think.”

“I could mold earth it out, right?”

“I don't think mold earth is going to be sufficient here.”

“Ye have little faith.”

My faith is abundant. My confidence in using a spell intended for loose soil to extract a painted stone wall from a tower is appropriately limited.

The shrine beyond has another dirty window, this one salt-encrusted and set into the west wall. A stone altar stands against the southern wall, lightning bolts carved into its face. Above it, a metal rod descends from the ceiling, splits, and embeds both branches into the stone.

“That's like a metal lightning bolt or something. The lightning rod, yeah.”

A lightning rod. Functional, deliberate, and connected directly to an altar devoted to a murderous storm god. Corruption often makes itself complicated so that sensible people hesitate to break it.

“Nice. Okay. Well, do we like want to interact the altar in any way? I mean, feel free, guys. Go right on.”

Before anyone touches it, we inspect the adjoining ledge. A retaining wall encloses a nest of filthy reeds, feathers, scraps, and compacted refuse. The smell hits the back of my throat.

“Oh, the harpy nest?”

It is.

“Oh, this is what you wanted to search, Valphine. Can we like torch it?”

Soma recoils from the fumes.

“I mean, it smells like a pitch fire.”

“I think that's a great idea. I'll torch it right now. I'm not even going to search it.”

The harpies murdered sailors and gathered debris above their graves. Whatever remains here is contaminated by both cruelty and odor. There is no ethical or artistic argument for preserving the nest.

Done.

“I'm just going to get out of there while you torch it. I think that's a fantastic idea.”

Valphine searches before the flame catches and finds a potion of water breathing sitting plainly among the refuse. Of course there is treasure. There is always treasure, placed directly atop death and waiting for someone to rename it salvage.

“Okay, well, we'll need to just determine. Maybe we can use like a legend lore spell to determine who owned this potion of water breathing. We'll just add that to blood money. Consumables.”

Soma looks at me.

“That's quite a moral code you got there.”

“I'm confused.”

No. The confusion belongs to the circumstances, although I am willing to acknowledge my share.

“Everybody else is confused. I'm quite confused.”

The world permits civilized people to claim the possessions of those it excludes from civilization, then calls the transfer lawful. Sailors die, harpies nest over their wrecks, adventurers kill the harpies, and suddenly everyone becomes vague about ownership.

“Yeah, this is a libertarian paradise, right?”

“Like, just take what you want by force.”

“Colonial. Mercantile. Yes.”

The potion goes with the other blood loot until its owner or lawful successor can be determined.

“Well, more blood loot. Typical.”

Smoke thickens over the retaining wall. Feathers curl black, oily refuse pops in the heat, and the wind drags the stench out over the sea. I turn back toward the altar and its divided rod.

“Let's lift the curse on this tower of storms.”

“Oh, yeah. It might actually be something with that altar, huh?”

The question of the wrecks still bothers me. We came to liberate this place, not to convert a massacre into studio capital.

“But the point of coming here was to liberate the castle, not just get the treasure, right? We're trying to liberate the castle. This is what is luring all the ships to be smashed. So we have a moral obligation to defeat the harpies.”

The harpies are already dead. Whether their song caused every wreck is less certain now that we stand inside the tower, beneath a lightning rod rooted in a storm altar.

“Sorry? We don't know that, do we?”

Soma is right to stop us there.

“I don't think Crabby McGee told us that.”

The nest burns behind us. The altar remains.
