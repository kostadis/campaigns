The rough-hewn stairs climb the eastern face of the rock, and the sea keeps striking below us as we ascend. At the top, a plateau hangs high above the water. Wooden doors stand in the tower wall, their iron hinges orange with rust, decorative lightning bolts carved across the boards.

"Rusty iron hinges. Okay. Can I go open the door?"

The harpies have stopped singing permanently.

"That's one way to put it."

I hear agreement behind me. "Yeah."

Soma and Brewbarry follow as I pull the door open. "Oh, yeah."

The first chamber is empty, its ceiling high enough to swallow the sound of our boots. Salt streaks the stone. A dirty window faces south.

"This window."

Confirmation comes back.

"Yeah."

"What do I see?"

Wrecks. Broken hulls lie among the rocks below, timbers rolling in the surf.

"Oh, yes. What the harpies have been doing to the local navy. Yes."

There are crates down there, chests, fittings, all the accumulated possessions of people who expected to reach another harbor. None of it became ownerless simply because its owners drowned.

"So many possessions that must be returned to their rightful owners."

The sailors are dead underwater. I understand this. Death does not erase ownership. It transfers it.

"It's — you know, what is it? They're heirs."

Someone groans behind me. I continue because the distinction is important.

"Their successors. Can't just take things from them. They died to these evil harpies."

The Order of the Gauntlet would apparently be pleased to have me on its side.

"I don't trust those guys."

Apparently that was not what was meant.

"You are going to be on your side."

No. They are pleased. That is somehow supposed to reassure me. Ser Kaelen can be helpful and still be managing me, and an institution can praise a principle while taking its funding from House Margaster. Compliments are not receipts.

The next room gives us frescoes: ships pitched sideways on stormy seas, sailors reaching from the decks, and a dark god looming above them. We all stare. I search my memory for hymns, forbidden verses, old performance pieces, anything that names the figure. Nothing answers.

"Anybody else?"

"Anybody else?" Soma echoes.

Valphine studies the walls and offers no revelation.

"Our cleric's not very religious, is she?"

Another attempt produces nothing.

"Wow. Nope. We got nothing tonight."

"Well, what you got is — it looks pretty. Pretty paintings."

That is true. The pigment has survived salt, damp, and whoever maintained a shrine devoted to drowning sailors. The workmanship is precise enough that the painted rigging remains visible.

"Is it frescoed onto the stone, or is it like a piece of art?"

Soma answers from the description.

"He said fresco, right? It's fresco."

"Very nice."

She is already imagining it somewhere else.

"I'd like something like this at the academy."

"You want to chisel out a section of wall and bring it along?"

"I could mold earth it out, right?"

I touch the stone beside the painted sea. It is tower wall, not loose soil, and removing it intact would require more than optimism.

"I don't think mold earth is going to be sufficient here."

"Ye have little faith."

The next chamber has another dirty window, this one crusted with salt. A stone altar stands against the southern wall, lightning bolts cut into its face. Above it, a metal rod descends from the ceiling, divides, and embeds both ends into the stone.

"That's like a metal lightning bolt or something. The lightning rod, yeah."

A lightning rod feeding an altar in a tower surrounded by wrecked ships. This is not subtle religious architecture. We may not know the god, but we know the arrangement is evil.

"Nice. Okay. Well, do we want to interact with the altar in any way? I mean, feel free, guys. Go right on."

Nobody rushes forward.

Beyond it, a ledge enclosed by a low stone wall holds the harpy nest. Feathers, bones, seaweed, and rotting scraps have been pressed together into a foul mat.

"Oh, the harpy nest?"

Yes.

"Oh, this is what you wanted to search, Valphine. Can we torch it?"

The smell alone argues for fire.

"I mean, it smells like nature."

Nature has many smells. This one is criminal.

"I think that's a great idea. I'll torch it right now. I'm not even going to search it."

"I'm just going to get out of there while you torch it. I think that's a fantastic idea."

Soma withdraws. Valphine searches through the nest first, turning aside clotted feathers until a potion of water breathing becomes impossible to miss. That creates another ownership problem. Consumable property is still property, and convenience is not provenance.

"Okay, well, we'll need to just determine — maybe we can use a legend lore spell to determine who owned this potion of water breathing. We'll just add that to blood money. Consumables."

"That's quite a moral code you got there."

I am accused of confusion.

"I'm confused."

No, everybody is confused.

"Everybody else is confused. I'm quite confused."

Apparently refusing to take property from dead sailors constitutes revolutionary behavior against the order of the realms.

"Yeah, this is a libertarian paradise, right? Like, just take what you want by force."

The more accurate description is colonial: civilization for those recognized as civilized, plunder for everyone else.

"Colonial. Mercantile. Yes."

There it is. A sailor dies outside the protected ledger, and suddenly every chest aboard the ship becomes a reward for whoever reaches it first.

"Well, more blood loot. Typical."

I put flame to the nest. The dry feathers catch first, then the seaweed begins to smoke. Heat pushes the rot-stink back through the opening.

"Let's lift the curse on this Tower of Storms."

"Oh, yeah. It might actually be something with that altar, huh?"

I watch the fire consume the place where the harpies slept and turn over what brought us here. Treasure was never the point.

"But the point of coming here was to liberate the castle, not just get the treasure, right? We're trying to liberate the castle. This is what is luring all the ships to be smashed. So we have a moral obligation to defeat the harpies."

"Sorry? We don't know that, do we?"

She is right. We know the harpies sang. We know ships broke below the tower. We do not know that one caused the other.

"I don't think Crabby McGee told us that."

Smoke crawls across the stone ledge. Behind it, the altar and lightning rod remain untouched.
