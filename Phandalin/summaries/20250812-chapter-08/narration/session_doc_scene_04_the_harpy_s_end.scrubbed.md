The harpy’s talons rake across Soma’s giant spider body and find the narrow weakness in her carapace. The impact drives her legs against the stone, hard enough to strip away the borrowed resilience of the form.

"16. Oh, yeah. That's just enough to hit. I'm at AC 16."

"All right. That takes me — that gets rid of my bonus hit points. Puts me down to 24. Or temporary."

She remains upright. Eight legs afford certain advantages in the disciplined management of pain, chiefly that several can tremble while the others continue the work.

Soma sinks her fangs into the last singer.

"And I mean, we're just going to try and break his concentration again. There's a hit. There's 12 damage on it. That takes him down to six."

The song stops.

Its absence strikes harder than the noise. The compulsion releases its grip upon Brewbarry, and his attention returns to the battlefield with all the subtlety of a portcullis falling.

"Brewbarry, you're free. Hooray. Brewbarry can actually attack. But can he hit?"

"Yeah, that's the key question."

There is Vukradin’s optimism again, presented as encouragement but functioning perfectly as provocation. He does not understand how expertly he manipulates Brewbarry. I see no reason to diminish the result by explaining it.

"I see no reason to move or disengage. I'm going to see this harpy to the end."

Soma anchors herself beside the wounded creature, her legs spread across the uneven rock. The harpy cannot withdraw without exposing itself, and Brewbarry now has the necessary angle.

"All right. So can I hit from here with my halberd?"

The weapon’s reach answers before anyone else needs to. Brewbarry plants his rear foot, sweeps the halberd through the open air, and catches the harpy cleanly.

"That is a hit. That is a kill."

The blade completes its course. The harpy falls.

The remaining creature turns in the air, wounded but still capable of resistance. I raise my hand crossbow and let the light, wind, and movement narrow to one precise point. The bolt strikes exactly where I intend, an immaculate shot whose elegance is somewhat undermined by the harpy’s stubborn refusal to die from it.

"Crit for four hit points."

Vukradin reads the total off before I can.

"Four damage."

"Nice. Well, you know, it's getting there."

Perfection and consequence are not always aligned. This is a basic principle of noble politics, divine service, and apparently hand crossbows.

Soma surges across the plateau toward the last harpy, the points of her spider legs scraping against the rock.

Soma measures the gap aloud.

"Yeah, I can clear that, right?"

"Yeah, no, I can get in there for sure. I got 40-range movement. Let's go."

Vukradin offers his encouragement.

"Chompy chompy."

She lunges. Her fangs close upon empty air.

"Roll. And I whiff. But at least I'm in the fray now."

The tactical merit remains. The harpy now faces a giant spider at close quarters, Brewbarry’s halberd within reach, and the others placed across every useful escape. Failure in the immediate act can still purchase position. Surface dwellers are inclined to call this consolation; in House Sotorra, we called it preparation.

Vukradin turns to the question of his own spell.

"You want me to drop the silence spell?"

Brewbarry, from inside the sphere:

"Well, I mean, I'm in melee now."

"Yeah, but would you be better off with a spell attack?"

He is asking me. I consider the arithmetic and find it favourable.

"Probably."

The silence has served its purpose. The song is broken, Brewbarry is free, and maintaining protection from a weapon no longer being used has become mere attachment to one’s own prior decision.

"All right, let's drop the silence. You can use a spell."

Sound returns all at once: surf below the plateau, wings beating, spider feet on stone, Brewbarry’s armor shifting beneath his grip. I invoke radiant power, inexhaustible and indifferent, and direct it against the harpy. Lathander’s judgment touches it with exquisite accuracy, although not with the annihilating force Vukradin evidently considers appropriate.

"That's a Dex save for four damage. Really? I could have."

Soma declines to let the contribution be diminished.

"Given the mace — hey, don't knock it."

"Four damage."

She puts the number in its proper proportion.

"It's half his hit points."

The light leaves a bright injury across the harpy’s defenses. It is not spectacular. It does not need to be. Pain is proof of contact, and divine contact has never required the approval of a bard conducting an arithmetic review.

"This is when we use Vicious Mockery."

Vukradin fixes his attention upon the harpy. His voice carries now, each syllable sharpened by the restored air, and the creature falters beneath an insult more lethal than either its content or dignity warrants.

Soma watches the method rather than the result.

"He's just taunting him on the way down."

"Pretty much."

She supplies the insult herself.

"Yeah, it smells of elderberry. 13 save for your life. Or four hit points. That's here."

The harpy’s wings lose their rhythm. Its body drops against the rock and does not rise.

"He is mocked to death."

"That Yo Mama joke really cut deep."

I lower my crossbow. There are many paths to divine judgment. Some are radiant. Some are deliciously painful. Some concern a harpy’s mother.

The battle’s end reveals the plateau beneath it: scattered feathers, thrown axes, harpy bodies, and several exhausted companions attempting to convert survival into procedure. I begin searching the corpses while Vukradin and Brewbarry address the more difficult question of where Brewbarry has thrown his weapons.

"I mean, I would like to retrieve my hand axes."

Vukradin suggests a larger supply.

"Might want to get more of those. Maybe, maybe. Yeah."

Brewbarry answers without hesitation. He has never counted them.

"I have a whole armory of hand axes available."

His armory is evidently subject to the limiting principle that its contents must exist. Brewbarry searches the nearby stone while I inspect claws, scraps of cloth, and whatever the harpies considered worth carrying.

"Oh. Oh, not like arrows?"

Ammunition, it appears, is finite, recoverable only in part, and best carried in quantities appropriate to the probable length of a battle. Vukradin receives this revelation with more surprise than he displayed when a flying creature sang Brewbarry out of his senses.

"Short rest? Yeah, and search the bodies."

"Well, then I will search for my hand axes."

The axes lie only a short distance from him. Their placement indicates that Brewbarry released them using much the same motion he gives his halberd, which is an admirable commitment to a successful technique and a poor understanding of differing weapons.

"Okay. They narrowly missed my own toes."

Soma asks after the hour.

"And his short rest is more than an hour, right?"

Vukradin rules it shorter.

"30 minutes."

We settle among the dead for half an hour. Wounds close as far as rest permits; breath steadies; weapons return to their owners. I continue my examination while the men recover from their contest against distance, inventory, and basic throwing technique.

"We'll no longer taunt them. We did a good thing here today."

Vukradin looks over the corpses and grants the defeated their final critical assessment.

"And also, I don't like harpies singing. Their music is — they're not joining the academy, that's for sure."
