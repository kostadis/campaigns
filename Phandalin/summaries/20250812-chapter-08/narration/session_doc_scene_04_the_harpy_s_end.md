The harpy’s talons rake across Soma’s spider body and find the narrow weakness between chitin plates. The impact strips away the borrowed resilience of her transformation, leaving the giant spider crouched low against the rock, legs flexing under the cost of remaining in the fight. Soma steadies herself. The creature has hurt her substantially, but not decisively.

She lunges at the singer.

The bite closes hard enough to break more than flesh. The harpy’s song fractures in its throat, one sustained note collapsing into a wet gasp, and the compulsion releases Brewbarry so abruptly that I see his shoulders jerk beneath the weight of his halberd.

“And the singing is over.”

“Brewbarry, you're free.”

“Hooray.”

Vukradin looks toward him, his relief immediately becoming provocation. “Brewbarry can actually attack.”

A pause. The goliath tightens both hands on the halberd.

“But can he hit?”

“Yeah, that's the key question.”

The exchange would be cruel from anyone else. From Vukradin, it is apparently encouragement.

Soma remains beside the wounded harpy, eight legs planted among loose stones. “I see no reason to move or disengage.”

The harpy claws at the air, trying to recover the advantage its song provided. Soma lowers her body.

“I'm going to see this harpy to the end.”

Brewbarry steps into reach. His earlier attempts at violence have lacked the discipline the situation deserves, but the charm is gone now, and his halberd does not negotiate. The blade cuts through the harpy’s defense and carries onward. The creature drops beside Soma’s forelegs.

One clean stroke. There is beauty in violence once hesitation has been removed.

My own opening comes against the remaining harpy. I bring divine force and practiced precision together, striking exactly where wing joins shoulder. The blow is perfect. The result is less so. The harpy reels, injured but still upright, and I am forced to confront the insulting distinction between precision and power.

“Nice.”

I watch it struggle to rebalance itself. “Well, you know, like it's getting there.”

A critical wound should possess greater procedural authority. Lathander’s radiance does not apologize for its magnitude; it burns according to its nature, inexhaustible and indifferent. My strike, by contrast, has delivered a very exact inconvenience.

Soma rushes the harpy in spider form, her legs carrying her across the plateau while the creature tries to gain height.

“Let's go.”

“Chompy chompy.”

The bite closes on air. Soma’s momentum brings her into the struggle, at least, placing another body beneath the harpy and another threat between it and escape.

Vukradin glances toward the boundary of his magical Silence. “You want me to drop the silent spell?”

“Well, I mean, I'm in mealy now.”

“Yeah, but would you be better off with a spell attack?”

He looks to me. I consider the harpy, the distance, and the indignity of trying to batter it down by increments.

“Probably.”

“All right, let's drop the silence.”

The pressure vanishes from my ears. Wind scrapes across the plateau. Wings beat. Soma’s claws click against stone.

“You can use a spell.”

The harpy has regained its voice, but not enough time to use it. Vukradin gives the creature his full attention, which is often more destructive than steel. His magic enters through ridicule rather than radiance, an inelegant method that nevertheless possesses exquisite utility.

“Yeah, it smells of elderberry.”

The insult lands somewhere no talon can protect. The harpy falters in the air, its wings losing agreement with one another, and drops among the other bodies.

“He is mocked to death.”

“That Yo Mama joke really cut deep.”

I kneel beside the corpses and begin searching them while the men assess the battlefield through the more urgent question of misplaced equipment. Harpy feathers cling to my gloves. Their bodies carry the sour smell of salt, old meat, and wet hide. Whatever power their music held is absent now. There is only anatomy.

“I mean, I would like to retrieve my hand axes.”

“Might want to get more of those.”

“Maybe, maybe.”

“Yeah.”

“I have a whole armory of hand axes available.”

Vukradin’s answer comes with sudden concern. “Oh.”

He looks across the plateau, where Brewbarry’s thrown weapons have landed embarrassingly close to their point of origin.

“Oh, not like arrows?”

Brewbarry searches among the stones while I continue with the bodies. His hand axes are only a few feet away, their blades lodged at angles suggesting that the harpies were never in serious danger from them.

“Well, then I will search for my hand axes.”

He recovers one near his boots and examines the mark it left in the rock.

“They narrowly missed my own toes.”

There are tactical errors, and then there are private rebellions by one’s own weaponry.

“Short rest?”

“Yeah, and search the bodies.”

That, at least, is already being done.

Brewbarry gathers the remaining axes. Soma settles her wounded spider body against the stone. Vukradin decides the rest will last “30 minutes,” lending temporal precision to exhaustion as if the body respects administration.

“We'll no longer taunt them.”

“We did a good thing here today.”

Vukradin looks down at the dead harpies.

“And also, I don't like harpies singing.”

He gives their silent throats a final appraisal.

“Their music is just they're not joining the academy, that's for sure.”
