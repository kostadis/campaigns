The stone is wet under my feet. All eight of them.

Silence covers the plateau below, keeping the harpies’ song outside my bale’s heads. It also leaves us fighting creatures that can fly while most of us cannot. Brewbarry swings his halberd at one passing overhead and cuts nothing but salt air. The harpy pulls away from him, screeching beyond the spell’s protection. It wants us to follow.

I take the practical route.

My spider body climbs the tower wall without effort. The parapet is above me, but height means little when every foot grips stone. My belly passes over cracks crusted with salt. Wind tugs at the coarse hairs along my legs. By the time I reach the upper level, the harpies no longer have the tower to themselves.

Below, Vukradin leaves the Silence long enough to strike at the distant singer with magic, then ducks back inside. Its howl stops. Valphine’s bolt hits another harpy, but the song continues. The creature’s chest keeps working around the wound.

Of course it does.

A shadow crosses the stone. Wings beat once, hard enough to push grit across the parapet, and claws rake through my spider body. The blow gets through, but not deeply. Borrowed flesh absorbs it. The harpy turns away at once, expecting to pass beyond my reach.

I bite.

My fangs catch under feathers and sink into muscle. The impact jerks the creature sideways in the air. Venom follows. The harpy’s wingbeat breaks apart, then catches again before it falls.

This is why I chose the spider. Circle of the Moon training gives me strong borrowed bodies early. It does not promise that the advantage will keep growing. That is a concern for another day. Today, the bite works.

The harpy flees across the parapet. I follow. If it wants to pull me away from the others, it has chosen the member of my bale who can walk down the outside wall whenever the arrangement becomes inconvenient.

I lunge and miss. A worthy effort. Heroic, even, if one has poor standards.

I leave my flank open as I move. The harpy claws at me and misses so badly that I hear talons scrape stone behind my abdomen. Not a near miss. Not even a respectable one.

Below, Brewbarry abandons the halberd and reaches for a hand axe. He watches the harpies’ height, measures the distance, and moves until his shoulder has room. The first axe turns in the air and passes wide.

Vukradin calls attention to it. Naturally.

Brewbarry checks his other hand and finds another axe. He throws that one too. Same release. Same turn. Same empty air.

Consistency has value. Usually.

Another harpy decides the failed song was the singer’s fault and begins its own. The notes reach across the open air far beyond the tower. Distance will not protect us. The Silence will, provided nobody lets feathers, claws, or bad judgment draw them out of it.

Valphine raises her crossbow and fires at the active singer. The bolt lands. The harpy jolts, loses altitude for a breath, then keeps singing.

*Fiddlesticks.*

A screech cuts across the parapet as another harpy dives at Vukradin. Its claws miss him. He answers with magic, striking hard enough to silence its howl, but the other song continues without pause. Stopping one singer accomplishes very little while another fills the same air.

Then the harpy closes on him.

It expects more spellcraft. Instead, Vukradin draws a dagger in each hand.

The creature hesitates. Only for a breath, but that is enough. It thought he was only a spell slinger. One dagger strikes cleanly. The other does not, but Vukradin has already made his point, and the harpy has lost more than feathers for the mistake.

The active singer sweeps down at Valphine next. Claws strike armor and slide away. Valphine has her mace ready before the creature can climb again. She catches it as it retreats, the mace landing against its side with a dull crack.

The harpy escapes her reach, but not untouched. Above them, I settle my weight across the wet stones and turn toward the next pair of wings.
