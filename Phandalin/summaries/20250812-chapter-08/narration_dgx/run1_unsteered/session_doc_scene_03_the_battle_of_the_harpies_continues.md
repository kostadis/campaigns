The wall was right there. Stone in front of me, the harpies circling above it like flies over something rotten. I am not good at waiting. I am good at climbing. So I asked, "Is Brewbarry's option here to scale the wall?"

Soma said yes. The GM said it was not a slippery surface, just half my movement.

"What?" I said. "You mean without a check or just half? Double movement."

"Double movement," Vukradin said.

So I climbed. Hands on the battlement stones, hauling myself up rung by rung, the Dread Helm warm on my skull. The harpies sang above me, their voices coiling down like smoke. I told myself it was fine. I was not going to do anything else up there but stand and be a threat.

"Well, you know, like, I'm not going to do anything else, right?"

"Yeah, you may as well," Soma said. "I mean, your wisdom's probably not great, but."

The die came up a two. Minus one. A net of a one. Soma's voice came through, half apologetic: "Okay, when I said not great, I didn't mean roll a one."

Vukradin read it like he was reading a tombstone. "Well, it's dismally time."

I felt the song hook into me. Not like a wound. Worse. Like a hand closing around the back of my neck, warm and insistent, telling me to come closer. I could hear my own heartbeat and the song and nothing else.

"You just got charmed," the GM said. "You just got charmed."

"And I'm charmed by which one?"

"Number one. One."

"Charmed one. Got it. Okay. Good times."

Good times. There was nothing good about it. I stood on that battlement, unable to lift my halberd, unable to turn my head away from the singer. The song said move, but Harpy 1 was up in the air, and I am not a bird. I do not walk on air. So I did nothing. I stood there, caught between the command in my head and the wall in front of me.

"Okay," I said. "Make a saving throw. So I get a save. And we'll see if I can roll better."

"A two or a three this time," Soma predicted.

Four. Or a five, really. Either way, it failed. Either way, the song still held me.

"Have you made any attacks or saves in this?" the GM asked.

"The entire day," Vukradin said. "Valphine's hit with a couple of crossbow bolts, but a universal whiff. Brewbarry has whiffed everything."

And then the worst of it. The thing that made my stomach drop through the stone. "We're no longer raging, by the way."

"Oh, no," the GM said. "Yes, we're no longer raging."

My rage was gone. It had slipped away sometime in the whiffing, tired of being unused. I stood on that wall, charmed and soft and useless, a legend of Phandalin in a bathrobe with nothing to show for it.

Up above, Harpy 1 was still singing and still attacking the spider. It flinched as it swung. Missed. The GM gave it a history I did not ask for and did not want: "It has a certain degree of arachnophobia... It had this whole incident with spiders."

"We're just whiffing at each other," Soma said. "As if this combat weren't long enough without the missings."

Then Valphine's crossbow sang its own smaller, truer song. "I'll shoot her with a crossbow and then finish her off. All right. Dead."

"One third of the way down," Soma said.

One down. Two to go. And I could not even move to help.

Harpy 3 came back for Vukradin. I heard the impact. I heard him say, "Okay. Hit for six. I'm feeling weak here, guys. And I got to do a concentration save."

He made it. Somehow, bleeding on the stones, he made it. I could not make a single save all night, and Vukradin, who has no business in a melee, held the silence that was keeping us all sane.

"That's for the silence," he said. "We are still silenced. All right. Vukradin with three hit points and silence concentration. And it's facing a harpy."

Three hit points. He was holding the fight together with three hit points and thin air. And I stood there on my wall, charmed, unable to lift a finger to protect the person I would die for.

"I am pissed off," Vukradin said. "There's a miss. Let's try another one. Yeah, I'm not feeling so well here, guys."

I wanted to tell him I would come. I wanted to move. But the song had my feet, and my rage had left me, and all I could do was stand there and be the biggest, most useless thing on the field.
