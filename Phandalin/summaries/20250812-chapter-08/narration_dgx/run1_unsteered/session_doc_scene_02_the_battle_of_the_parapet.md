Up the wall I went. Eight legs and a patience born of forty-some years of watching things scuttle — I'm a wolf spider now, and the exterior of the tower is just another web to cross. The stone was rough and cold against my palps, the wind tugging at my bristles, and ten feet below me the silence bubble held the rest of my bale like a lantern held its light.

I liked it up there. The plateau, the shouting, the *singing* — it all got quieter with altitude. One harpy on the upper level, thinking it was safe because it was high. They always think that. Flying things always think the ground can't reach them.

Brewbarry's halberd swung through empty air. I heard the *whiff* of it from the wall. He's a good man with a good reach, but harpies bob and weave like gulls on a fish-gut tide, and tonight they weren't landing.

Then she came for me.

The GM's voice, flat as a ledger entry: "flies by." "Strikes the spider." "The spider." A pause — even *he* couldn't believe I'd climbed that far for a fight. "Hold on." "I don't know if you saw that, but I got 20." Another pause. "I rolled a 22 and six points of damage." I felt the claws rake across my carapace — six points of sting, nothing a spider of my constitution can't shrug off. "And the harpy flies away again." Beckoning. Always beckoning. "So you get your attack of opportunity."

"Excellent," I muttered, and meant it. "I mean, it's something."

She thought she could peck and run. Thought the spider on the wall was prey.

"Public roll," I announced, because I'm old enough to know that when you're about to do something satisfying, you say it out loud so the universe can witness. "Harpies are 11. That's a hit."

The damage was pronounced with the same calm I'd use to note the tide coming in. "It's 12 damage." And because the GM was staring at his notes like I'd broken something, I explained it to him the way you'd explain a coastal rule to a landsman: "Dude, Circle of the Moon Druids start early. They don't really get better, but they start early."

I've been doing this less than two years. Meril gave me the staff and left before I could ask why, and now I'm biting harpies out of the sky at the top of a haunted tower. The bite-connected fangs to the harpy's flank and I felt it *give* — a wet tear that said *that's what you get for swooping.*

"That was Harpy 1 gets 12 damage. So 26." I tallied it the way I tally mended nets — neat, countable, done. "Yeah." Then, patient as a grandmother explaining arithmetic: "And you said, sorry, how much damage you say you did to me six?"

"Yeah," he confirmed, a little deflated.

"All right, back down to 30." I'm fine. Thirty's fine. She flew off dripping, and I watched her go, and I decided the chase was worth it.

"Anyway, I'm just going to go whale on it anyway."

Brewbarry threw his axes — both of them, action and bonus action, a cascade of steel — and both sailed wide. David's verdict landed before the second one did: "Brewbarry's whiffing tonight." Two whiffs, and I felt the old maternal reflex stir. "Hey, consistency, man," I called down. "You can do it." He rolled the same miss again, and I thought: *some nights the sky doesn't want to be touched.* I know that feeling.

I was still chasing Harpy 1 when Harpy 3 screeched down and closed with Vukradin. The bard braced — "All right." — and the claws missed, and instantly he was striking back, and the GM sputtered, "Oh my, you hit." Six more damage, and Harpy 3 stopped singing.

"Oh, that one stopped singing," Vukradin said, bright with discovery. "Oh, it was singing, and it's no longer singing."

"Yeah," the GM confirmed, but that only raised the obvious problem. The other harpy was *still* going.

"300 feet."

"Oh," I said, working it out from the wall. "So it really doesn't matter if three stops singing while two is doing it."

"Yeah."

Intelligence 7. That was the thing about these creatures. Each one thought the *other* had been the problem. So they just kept singing, one after another, and the silence bubble was the only thing between my bale and the ledge-hurl of charming lies. The moment anyone left it, the world went wrong.

Harpy 2 flew at Valphine, claws out. The drow didn't even flinch — "AC says miss." And when the creature saw her mace and thought better of it, tried to beat a retreat, the GM gave her the parting gift: "You can use your mace." Three points of damage, applied to Harpy 2. I watched it land and thought: good. Someone's staying in the fight.

Vukradin, meanwhile, had decided the harpy in his face was a *melee* problem. "So Vukradin is going to do his double daggers then." The GM, stunned: "Double daggers. Well, the harpy is not very intelligent. He thought you could only cast spells." I finished it for him, mid-scuttle: "Thought you were a spell slinger."

He got two attacks out. One crit, seven damage, and Harpy 3 was down to 15 and bleeding its song out on the stones. The second attack missed, but the point was made. The bard who hid in the silence could fight.

I'm chasing after her still. Harpy 1, who thought a spider on a wall was an easy snack. "I'm going to chase after it. I mean, it wants to play this game with me. I'll play with it."

I lunged and missed. "All right. That was a worthy effort."

"Heroic," the GM said dryly, and I could hear the smirk.

"Take the attack of opportunity. Come at me, bro." I offered her the opening deliberately — let her swing at me while she flees, let her think she's winning. "I see a miss," I announced, and then, because I'd actually *seen* it — her claws raking nothing but wind — "I see a hell of a miss."

And I kept coming up the wall, eight legs and the patience of something that knows the other creature will eventually run out of places to fly. She'll land eventually. They always do. And when she does, my bite will still be 12 damage.
