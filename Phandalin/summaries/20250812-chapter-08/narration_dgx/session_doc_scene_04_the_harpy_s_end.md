The song breaks. I feel it go — that dissonant, insistent pull on the mind, the one that had Brewbarry standing slack-jawed and useless — and it snaps like a plucked lute string. Soma's fangs are in Harpy 1's throat, twelve points of damage tearing the last notes from its chest. The singing is over. The plateau goes quiet except for the wind and the wet sound of a spider eating a singer.

Vukradin announces the obvious: "Brewbarry, you're free. Hooray. Brewbarry can actually attack." A pause. "But can he hit?"

"That's the key question," Brewbarry admits.

*At least he knows himself.* I watch the Goliath recover his grip on his halberd, the red clearing from his eyes now that the charm has released him. He's been stood in a stupor while the rest of us traded blows with these creatures. The efficiency of it irritates me. A barbarian who cannot be trusted to swing because a song got under his skin is a barbarian I have to plan around.

Soma says she sees no reason to move or disengage. "I'm going to see this harpy to the end." She stays in melee, the giant spider form bristling, mandibles still wet. Loyal to a fault, that tortle. She treats combat like a promise.

Brewbarry asks if he can hit from here with his halberd, and the answer is yes. There is a sound of metal finding flesh — the halberd head punching through feathers and bone in a single stroke. Vukradin reads the result like a ledger: "That is a hit. That is a kill."

*One down. Efficient.*

I turn my attention to Harpy 3, the one scuttling along the rock's edge. There is a beat of time where I have her — my mace comes around, and Lathander's light follows the arc. The strike lands true. Crit for four hit points, which sounds pathetic until I watch the creature reel from it. Pain is pain regardless of its arithmetic. Every injury is a prayer answered, a proof of contact.

"Four damage," Vukradin confirms.

"Nice." I take the incremental progress. "Well, you know, like it's getting there."

Soma is already moving — the spider form climbing, legs finding purchase on the broken rock. "Yeah, I can clear that, right?" She confirms her movement, forty feet of range, and goes. "Let's go."

Vukradin eggs her on: "Chompy chompy." The spider lunges. And whiffs — the mandibles close on air.

"But at least I'm in the fray now," Soma says.

Vukradin offers, "You want me to drop the silent spell?" The zone he's been holding has been our shield against the song, but the last singer is wounded and the risk has changed.

Brewbarry notes he's in melee now.

"Yeah, but would you be better off with a spell attack?" Vukradin asks.

"Probably," I admit. My mace is a blunt instrument of faith; what I carry in my blood is sharper.

"All right, let's drop the silence. You can use a spell."

The zone of magical silence dissolves. The plateau opens back up to sound — the wind, the sea, the ragged breathing of the dying harpy. Vukradin reacts to my four damage with a note of dawning complaint: "That's a Dex save for four damage. Really? I could have—"

"Given the mace that hey don't knock it," Soma cuts in. She defends my contribution like a mother defending a child's drawing.

"It's half his hit points," she adds.

Vukradin decides, "This is when we use Vicious Mockery."

Soma describes the shape of it: "He's just taunting him on the way down." And supplies the insult herself, her spider voice flat and dry: "Yeah, it smells of elderberry."

The harpy's eyes go wide. Whatever it hears in that moment — the insult striking something it cannot defend against, psychic damage carving through its last reserves — it reels. Vukradin pronounces the verdict: "He is mocked to death."

The last harpy folds. Feathers and ruin on the stone. Soma identifies the killing blow: "That Yo Mama joke really cut deep."

I survey the plateau. Three bodies. Silence where there was song. The worst of the fight was never the claws — it was the compulsion, the way their music reached past my faith and pulled at something older. I spent the first part of the battle on my back, a mace on the ground beside me, staring up at sky I did not choose to watch. There is a delicious humiliation in being laid flat by a bird's lullaby. It reminds me I am still flesh, still reachable, still capable of being unmade by the right note in the wrong key.

Brewbarry asks about retrieving his hand axes. Vukradin suggests he might want to get more. "Maybe, maybe," Brewbarry allows, "I have a whole armory of hand axes available."

The GM's ruling descends: "You do not have infinite hand axes."

Vukradin is genuinely surprised. "Oh. Oh, not like arrows?"

The explanation surfaces — arrow recovery, a forty-arrow cap, the assumption that combats rarely exceed twenty rounds. Brewbarry's axes, thrown badly enough to miss my own toes, sit a few feet from where he threw them. "You're so used to using the halberd action that you kind of threw them like you were using halberds," the voice of the world observes.

*He throws an axe like it is a polearm.* Of course he does. He has spent so long becoming competent with the halberd that he has forgotten the hand axes entirely.

"Short rest?" Brewbarry asks. "And search the bodies."

Thirty minutes, Vukradin rules — shorter than Soma expects, but enough. Enough to bind a wound, to find the blades, to let the adrenaline drain. I retrieve my mace and wipe the blood from the haft. The light off the plateau has shifted. We came up here chasing song; we leave with silence and the knowledge that the ships below will sail unmolested.

"We'll no longer taunt them," someone says. "We did a good thing here today."

Vukradin's eulogy is merciless: "And also, I don't like harpies singing. Their music is just they're not joining the academy, that's for sure."

*The academy.* He is still thinking about his studio, his fund, his charitable gala pitched to a Lord Protector who wants to discuss the circulation of wealth. Even standing over a dead harpy he is counting the cost of beauty. It is almost endearing. The bard believes the world should pay for music, and he has just watched three creatures sing their enemies to death and judged them wanting on artistic merit.

I kneel beside the bodies and begin to search them. The men flounder around me — Brewbarry retrieving his wayward axes, Vukradin theorizing about ammunition, Soma shifting back into a shape with thumbs. I work in silence, turning feathers and leather, cataloging what the dead carried. There is a rhythm to looting that I find almost devotional. The world gives up its secrets to whoever is willing to touch the dead.
