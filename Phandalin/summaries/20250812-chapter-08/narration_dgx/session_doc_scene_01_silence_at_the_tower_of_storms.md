### Vukradin

The harpy sang. That was the whole problem, laid out on that parapet like a bad chord that wouldn't resolve. One of them, up there on the ruined tower wall, wailing something that got inside your head and pulled at the levers behind your eyes. Soma had put it plainly enough — a couple of people had gotten charmed before we even knew what was happening. But the GM — no, the world, the world had only put one harpy on the board so far. Harpy one. The trouble was, Harpy one wasn't the only voice up there.

*Silence.* The word came out of me clean and certain, a note held true, and the sphere bloomed outward from my lute in a twenty-foot radius. I set it down on the battlefield with all the care a conductor gives the downbeat. "I put the 20-foot radius on the screen there for you," I said, because precision matters when lives are on the line and I wanted everyone to see exactly where the quiet lived.

"Everyone is deafened inside the circle," Soma confirmed, and I felt the shape of it take hold — the world going soft-edged and muffled beyond the boundary, all of us standing in a pocket of stillness.

"She can sing," I said. "She can sing all she wants." The words came out lighter than I meant, almost giddy, because this was the whole argument — my entire philosophy, rendered as a tactical fact. "We can't hear her."

And it worked. The charm fell off us like a shed skin. "All right," the world conceded, in that voice it uses when it's been outmaneuvered. "So you are no longer affected by the charm."

"We can delete charmed," I said. "As I said, it wasn't going to matter."

One of the harpies — the singer, the one who'd started all this — hung there for a long beat, its head cocked, listening to the nothing it was getting back. Then it turned and drifted over the parapet and was gone. "Ran away," I said, watching it go. "Okay. He ran away."

"Yeah, he ran away," the world agreed. "Into over the parapet. Frustrated."

Of course it was frustrated. I'd taken its voice. That's what you do to a performer who's abusing the stage.

Soma was already thinking in angles. "Or I could go into spider mode," she said, rolling the idea around in that unhurried way of hers. "That'd probably be fine too." She asked for a spider icon and I watched her shell sprout into something eight-legged and patient, the familiar shapes she trusts. "Well, I think technically you're only supposed to have like ones that you're familiar with, right?" Of course. Of course she limits herself. The woman could probably turn into a storm if she wanted, but she sticks to what she knows, and there's a kind of honor in that.

Then the ruling came down and I had to recalibrate. "So just to be clear, the folks who made the saving through originally are immune to the harpy song for 24 hours," the world said. "The other folks are not."

"Oh, so I don't have to worry about hiding in," I asked, "the immune to all harpy songs or just the harpy song from Harpy?"

"Immune to all harpy songs."

Oh, that would have been too good to be true. "Oh, wow."

"No, no, no, sorry, to this harpies, not all, not all."

Right. Of course. "So if the other harpy sings," the world went on, "they might, you have to re-roll."

That was the real shape of it. We'd silenced one voice but two more were still up there, waiting for their turn at the song. The sphere was a shelter, not a solution. But it was a shelter we could fight from.

Soma had already found the answer. "I mean, in theory, I could probably scale this wall next to number two to help get into range, right?"

"Yes."

"The spider is climby crazy," she said, and I watched her go up the stone like it was a branch she'd known her whole life. "So let's just say I climb the wall then. So wherever it is, I'm on top of it."

She bit down on harpy number two. "That's a hit," she said, and I read the damage off her strike. "Nice. 11 damage."

And then the number landed and I felt the whole table flinch. "Wow, these have a lot of hit points."

"Wow," Soma said.

You're not kidding. Eleven points of bite and the thing was barely dented. I looked at the upper level — ten feet up, the world confirmed — and Soma, still inside the sphere, still deafened, still safe. Good. The shape of this fight was forming.

Brewbarry came up with the halberd. He measured the distance, that big body of his finding the exact spot where the reach would close. "So Brewbarry can make it to right here," he said, "and then it's just enough range for the halberd." And then rage. The familiar heat. "And rage, yep."

The halberd came around in a great arc and hit nothing. "11," he said.

"Oh, does it — 10 misses?" I asked, watching the numbers shake out.

"10 misses. Yeah. All right. So I guess that's it for me. Yikes."

Ten. Against these things that could take eleven points of bite and shrug. I felt the ledger in my head start to run — what we had, what they had, and how thin the margin was getting.

Harpy two pulled back, disengaging, sliding out of reach like it had all the time in the world. "Really?" I said, surprised.

"Yes. Well, I mean, the harpies, harpies like to charm their enemies, not they literally know they cannot do it. Yeah. I think that's it's kind of like, you know, silent spells are very effective at dissuading harpies from attacking you."

*Silent spells are very effective at dissuading harpies from attacking you.* I tucked that one away. That was going in the song — the night the silence beat the song, and all it took was one musician who understood the difference between a performance and an attack.

"This is where ranged attacks would be useful," the world said, and for once I didn't argue, because it was right.

"Yeah, clearly we're going to need ranged," Soma said.

Valphine was already going through her options, that tactical mind of hers clicking over the field. "I believe I have a range spell," she said. "And let's see what this is."

"You can't cast the spell with a verbal component," I said, because the silence cuts both ways — it's a wall, and walls have doors only for the ones who brought the keys.

"I know. Let's see if I can step outside and then come back in or something."

"No, no, hold on, hold on," the world said. "At the end of each of its turns, right, at the end. Okay."

Valphine settled. "So I can shoot it with an arrow, I guess."

"Yeah, you can definitely do that." Her hand crossbow came up, steady. "Nice. Ah, crit."

"Critical damage even," she said, and the bolt went home.

"Who did you do this against?"

"Whoever. Can I see the one who was trying to charm me?"

"Yes, this is number three."

"Number three. Yes. Number three would be the one I would attack."

And that was the beautiful thing about Valphine — she held the grudge in perfect focus. Not the one that hurt us, not the one that ran. The one that tried to get inside her head. That one got the bolt.

The world called for the saves at the end of the turn — Valphine's, Brewbarry's, both of them rolling against the tail end of the charm that had tried to take them. "Yes, so you're both now no longer charmed," came the ruling. "You're now no longer affected by the charm of that of Harpy number three."

And there it was. The pattern resolved itself into a phrase I could hear like a melody I'd been chasing all fight. "Which means you can step out of the silence, attack, and go back in," I said. "As long as another harpy doesn't start singing."

The peek-and-shoot. It was the cleanest thing this fight had offered — a rhythm, a tempo, something I could actually play. "So I can probably go like out here, cast a spell against Harpy 3, and then run back inside, correct?"

"Yep."

"Okay, so I'll do that."

I stepped to the edge of the sphere, felt the wall of quiet at my back like a held breath, and looked at the harpy — barely ten feet away, the world confirmed, well inside my range. I reached for the magic and flung it. Four points of damage. Not much. But every note counts in a fight like this.

"It's down to 28," I said, and I ran back inside.

Back into the quiet. Back where the songs couldn't reach me. Harpy three was hurt, harpy two was keeping its distance, harpy one was gone over the parapet — and we had a rhythm now. Step out, strike, step in. The silence was the loudest thing on that battlefield, and I was the one who'd called it into being.

*That's what you do to a performer who's abusing the stage.* You take away their voice, and you keep playing.
