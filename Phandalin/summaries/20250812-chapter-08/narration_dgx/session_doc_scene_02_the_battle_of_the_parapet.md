### Soma

I am a giant wolf spider, and I am having the time of my life.

The wall is rough under my eight feet — old stone, wind-scoured, full of holds a two-legged climber would weep over. I skitter up it the way I used to climb the rocks behind my cabin, except now I've got eight legs doing the work instead of two tired arms and a shell that wants to stay put. The harpy on the upper ledge hasn't seen me yet. She's too busy singing her heart out, trying to charm my bale into walking off the edge.

The silence sphere sits below us like a bubble of held breath. Vukradin's doing. Take away the song and the singer is just a bird with ugly opinions. Clever shell sprout.

I haul myself onto the ledge and she finally notices me. Her eyes go wide — eight of mine, two of hers, and hers are definitely the more alarmed pair.

She swoops. Claws rake across my carapace, and I feel it — six points of sting through the chitin, nothing that'll slow a spider. She's already banking away, thinking she's made her point.

"Public roll," I announce, to nobody in particular. The table knows what I mean. "Harpies are 11."

There's a pause while the dice settle.

"That's a hit."

My fangs sink into her. The venom works fast — I feel her thrash, the strength going out of her wings. "It's 12 damage." I pull back, chittering with satisfaction. "Dude, Circle of the Moon Druids start early. They don't really get better, but they start early."

I do the arithmetic in my head. "That was Harpy 1 gets 12 damage. So 26." The number sits there, heavy and good. Harpy 2 took a crossbow bolt from Valphine. Harpy 3 took a blast from Vukradin. We're whittling them down.

"And you said, sorry, how much damage you said you did to me six?" I'm not really asking. I just like hearing the GM confirm that her bird took more from me than she gave. It's the natural order of things. Bite beats claw.

"All right, back down to 30." My spider form is still well within what's comfortable. I'm a sack of hit points wearing eight legs and an attitude.

The harpy is fleeing — heading back out over the water, trying to draw me away from the silence, away from my bale. It's so transparent. Leave the safety of the sphere, chase the retreating bird, wander into song range with nobody to protect me.

"Anyway, I'm just going to go whale on it anyway." I'm already moving, all eight legs eating up the stone. "I mean, it wants to play this game with me. I'll play with it."

I catch up to her at the edge of the tower. She's turned, waiting, thinking she's lured me into striking distance of something. I leap.

The bite misses. Claws catch stone where she was a heartbeat ago.

"All right." I give credit where it's due. "That was a worthy effort."

The GM sounds almost impressed. "Heroic."

I laugh — a dry clatter of mandibles. "Take the attack of opportunity. Come at me, bro." I plant myself, legs wide, inviting her to try again. Give me a reason to bite harder next time.

"I see a miss."

Her claws slash air a foot from my face.

"I see a hell of a miss."

*Dumb bird.* Intelligence 7 — and it shows. She's fighting a spider on a wall. I've been climbing walls since before the sea remembered her name.

Down below, I hear the party working their own problems. Brewbarry's cursing — I catch the tail end of it. "Well, I may have a throwing axe or something. Yeah, hand axe."

Two thuds. Two misses. Vukradin's voice drifts up: "Brewbarry's whiffing tonight. It's two whiffs."

"Hey, consistency, man," I call down, though I'm not sure he can hear me over the wind. "You can do it."

"It's the exact same one," Brewbarry reports, and I can hear the shrug in his voice. Some nights the axe just doesn't want to fly.

The other harpy starts singing. I don't need to see her to know what's happening — the sound cuts through the air, a second voice joining the first, trying to find a way around the silence.

"Good job," I mutter. Great. Another one. They're learning — or at least, they're doing the one thing their tiny brains can manage besides eating and screeching.

"Oh, they really are dumb," Vukradin says.

"Intelligence 7." I say it like it's a fact of nature, because it is. A wolf can't learn to sing. A harpy can't learn to shut up. The universe has a sense of humor.

"So that'll be an issue if somebody wants to leave the sphere," Vukradin says, and I can hear him thinking, *we're fine as long as we stay put.*

Harpy 3 screeches down at Vukradin. I hear the whoosh of wings, the clatter of claws on stone — a miss. And then Vukradin's voice, bright with surprise: "Oh my, you hit."

"I hit and another six damage on three. Okay, down to 22."

The singing stops. One voice gone. The silence sphere does what it does.

"Oh, that one stopped singing," Vukradin says. "It was singing, and it's no longer singing."

"But two is still singing," I add. I can hear the song from here, even at this distance. The range on it is terrifying — "300 feet," the GM confirms, and I do the math without wanting to.

"So it really doesn't matter if three stops singing while two is doing it." I state it flat. Running from the song doesn't work when the song can reach you from halfway across the tower. You have to kill the singer.

There's a pause, and then Vukradin's voice, patient and surprising. "So Vukradin is going to do his double daggers then."

"Double daggers," the GM says, and I can hear the amusement in his voice. "Well, the harpy is not very intelligent. He thought you could only cast spells."

I almost laugh. "Thought you were a spell slinger."

The bard's a man of hidden depths. One dagger lands — a crit, from the sound of it — and a second follows. "Seven on three. That's down to 15."

Whatever the harpy expected from a man who stands in a silence bubble waving a lute, it wasn't *this*.

Harpy 2, the still-singing one, dives at Valphine. Claws flash. There's a clang of metal on chitin — the drow's armor turning the blow.

"AC says miss," Valphine says, calm as still water.

"She did see you as a mace," the GM says, and I can picture it — the harpy looking at a drow in plate armor, deciding the crossbow was the bigger threat, and then realizing too late that the mace was coming. "One, two, three, four. He flies away here."

The whack of a mace connecting. "Three points of damage."

Three points. Not much. But every little bit helps when the creature's already carrying crossbow bolt, a spell, and my fangs.

I'm still on the wall, still facing my own harpy. She's circling, trying to decide if I'm worth another pass. I flex my legs, testing the grip of my feet on the stone, and think about Meril for a moment — standing on a cliff somewhere, watching the sea, nodding once as if to say, *you've got this, old friend.*

I've got this. Eight legs, a shell that's shrugged off worse, and a bite that's already put one harpy on her back foot.

She wants to play this game with me?

I'll play with it.
