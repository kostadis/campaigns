### Vukradin

Rusty iron hinges. The words stick in my head as I follow the others up those rough-hewn stairs, eighty feet above the churning water. Rusty iron hinges and decorative lightning bolts carved into the wood. Some craftsman put time into those bolts. Someone who believed in what they were carving.

I open the door.

The room is empty, fifteen feet high, and there's a dirty window in the south wall. I cross to it and look out. Wrecks. Ships broken on the rocks below, their ribs showing through the surf. "Oh, yes," I say. "The what the harpies have been doing to the local navy." And then I see them, scattered across the decks and tangled in the rigging: chests, crates, barrels, the glint of what might be coin or glass or steel. "So many possessions that must be returned to their rightful owners."

The reply comes back to me, amused: "Who are dead underwater?"

"It's, you know, what is it? They're heirs. Their successors. Can't just take things from them. Therefore, they died to these evil harpies."

"The Order of the Gauntlet is going to be so pleased to have you on their side."

I don't hesitate. "I don't trust those guys."

"Oh, I didn't say it. I didn't say that."

"You are going to be on your side."

"I said they are very pleased."

I let it go. The distinction matters to me, even if it doesn't land with everyone. I've met Ser Kaelen. I know what the Order says it stands for. I also know what happens when institutions talk about you like you're useful.

The next room is taller, the same fifteen feet, and the walls are covered in frescoes. Ships tossed on stormy seas, and looming above them a dark and terrible god. I study it, looking for a name, a symbol, a clue I can set to music. Nothing. The discipline isn't there tonight; every attempt to place this god comes up empty.

"Anybody else?"

"Anybody else?" Soma echoes me.

Soma shifts on her shell, looking at Valphine with what I have come to recognize as her patient, amused disappointment. "Our cleric's not very religious, is she?"

"Wow. Nope. We got nothing tonight."

"Well, what you got is it looks pretty, it looks pretty. Pretty paintings."

"Good art." I can appreciate that much. Whoever painted this knew their craft, even if their subject is everything I stand against.

"Is it frescoed onto the stone," I ask, "or is it like a piece of art?"

"He said fresco, right? It's fresco."

"Very nice."

"I'd like something like this at the academy."

"You want to chisel out a section of wall and bring it along?"

"I could mold earth it out, right?"

I picture the stone coming away in crumbling chunks, the painted surface shearing off. "I don't think mold earth is going to be sufficient here."

"Ye have little faith."

Faith. I have faith. I have faith in harmony, in resolution, in the clean note that ends a discordant passage. What I don't have is faith in a druid's cantrip to preserve a mural that's probably older than the town below us.

The west wall holds a dirty, salt-encrusted window. Against the south wall stands a stone altar carved with lightning bolts, and above it a metal rod descends from the ceiling, splitting in two before it embeds itself into the stone.

Soma sees it first. "That's like a metal lightning bolt or something. The lightning rod, yeah."

*Lightning rod.* I file that away. Bad things happen in this tower, and that rod is part of the curse. "Nice. Okay. Well, do we like want to interact the altar in any way? I mean, feel free, guys. Go right on."

Gary's voice cuts across the table — not Valphine, not anyone in the fiction. "Let's check the landing and make sure there's nothing on the landing."

"A ledge enclosed by three-foot-high stone retaining wall serves as a nest."

"Oh, the harpy nest?"

"Yes."

Oh, this is what Valphine wanted to search. I've seen what harpies leave in their nests. Feathers, bone, the gleam of whatever caught their eye before they killed its owner. Every time we touch something like that, I have to ask myself the same question: is this salvage, or is it stolen? "Can we like torch it?"

"That's a good idea. I mean, it stinks. It does stay. It does smell."

Soma pulls back, nostrils flaring. "I mean, it smells like napalm."

"I think that's a great idea. I'll torch it right now. I'm not even going to search it." Some things don't need a second look. A harpy nest is one of them. Whatever's in there, the owners are dead, and their heirs are scattered across the bottom of the sea.

"I'm just going to get out of there while you torch it. I think that's a fantastic idea."

"Do you look through anything or do you just torch it?"

Valphine is already crouched at the ledge, turning over feathers and debris. "I am blowing all my rolls to search it."

"You're just blowing all your rules tonight."

"The good news is you didn't have to blow them very hard because the potion of water breathing was quite obvious."

A potion of water breathing. Someone carried that down to the rocks with them, planning to swim. Planning to live. I look at it in Valphine's hand and my ledger opens in my head.

"Okay, well, we'll need to just determine. Maybe we can use like a legend lore spell to determine who owned this potion of water breathing. We'll just add that to blood money. Consumables."

"That's quite a moral code you got there."

But it's very confused about it, I hear, and I catch it wrong. "I'm confused."

"No, I said everybody."

"Everybody else is confused. I'm quite confused."

"You have to understand," comes the answer, "you are engaged in some very revolutionary behavior against the overall world order of this realms."

"Yeah," Soma agrees, "this is a libertarian paradise, right? Like, just take what you want by force."

"No, I wouldn't say it's a libertarian paradise. It's a liberal, it's not, no, it's, I would say a colonial paradise."

"Colonial. Mercantile. Yes." The words land in me like a chord I've been hearing for years without being able to name. Mercantile. Colonial. There are people considered part of civilized society, and people who are not, and the line between them determines whose possessions stay possessions and whose become plunder.

"Well, more blood loot. Typical."

The fresco looms over us, the dark god still reaching for the ships. I don't know his name. But I know his work. I've seen it in every dead sailor on those rocks, in every potion pried from a corpse, in the careful way the world sorts the worthy from the unworthy and calls it civilization.

"Let's lift the curse on this tower of storms."

"Oh, yeah. It might actually be something with that altar, huh?"

The altar. The rod. The green light I know is coming. "But the point of coming here was to liberate the castle, not just get the treasure, right? We're trying to liberate the castle. This is what is luring all the ships to be smashed. So we have a moral obligation to defeat the harpies."

"Actually, it's the green light, not the harpies."

Soma's head comes up. "Sorry? We don't know that, do we?"

"Yeah, you do, because what's his name? That's like the giant crab tool. Well, I guess you don't know."

Soma is already shaking her head. "I don't think Crabby McGee told us that."

"I don't know. You're right."

They concede. But I heard it. The green light, not the harpies. Something in this tower is luring the ships to their doom, and it isn't the birds. I look at the lightning rod, at the altar, at the fresco of the dark god with his hand stretched over the waves. The tower is singing a song I can't hear yet, and I intend to learn its key.
