### Soma

We go through the door.

The room is fifty feet across, two windows looking north at the water. A barnacle-encrusted chair stands against the south wall. Sitting in it is Moesko.

One of those weird clerics. The half-orc kind, armor stitched from giant octopus hide, and there in his lap, held loose like it belongs to him — the opalescent conch. The one he took from Miraal.

"Oh, yeah," Vukradin says behind me.

"I guess that doesn't look like a friendly, does it?"

"Nope."

He gets up out of the chair. He sees us. Of course he sees us — we're a tortle, an aasimar, a drow, and a goliath standing in his doorway. "Usual," the room seems to say. Another fight. Another cleric with a god I don't want to know.

But he's alone this time. "Oh, geez. But he's just alone this time."

I look at him. Seated back down now, alert. "Is he sitting? Is he, you know, any sort of..." Disadvantaged. Off balance. Anything I can use.

No. He is not.

"Then I'm just going to try to thorn whip him, knock him to his feet." Pull him down, get him on the ground, end this before it starts. "Oh, no, that just pulls him. That doesn't knock him over, does it?"

It doesn't.

"Hardly seems worth doing anything serious to him." One lonely guy in a chair with a stolen shell. I could burn a wild shape here, spend real power. "I didn't feel like burning a wild shape in here."

So I poison spray him instead. Two damage. Not a poison condition — poison damage, there's a difference. Small, but real. A clerical type will probably shake off anything Vukradin throws. "He's a cleric, right? He's probably going to make that."

He makes it.

Then Brewbarry runs in. Second rage of the day, halberd swinging, and it misses. The dice are cold. Vukradin tries to console him: "You know what? I'm sorry. It was the silence. The fact that you couldn't hear yourself scream, that was."

"That's right. That's definitely the problem."

And now Brewbarry is going to carry that with him — that silence makes him weak. I actually know that one. "I remember in martial arts, they teach you to, you know, call out with every punch. It makes you stronger."

Valphine puts a crossbow bolt in him. Six damage.

Then the orc is up. The anchorite. The word comes to me like a bad taste.

He casts lightning bolt.

"That's probably not going to be good. That seems bad. Yeah. TPK here."

The line cuts through nearly all of us. Three of the four — everyone but Brewbarry, who is somehow off the angle. I see Vukradin do the math on his face. The DC is twelve. The damage is twenty-five. Half of that is twelve. "Okay, take 12. Ooh, I'm down to four. Soma, you're down to two."

Still standing. Barely. The air smells of ozone and burnt shell.

Now I do want to burn the wild shape charge. "Yeah, that's that. Now I do want to burn the wild shape charge coming out as a spider."

Spider form. Eleven hit points. Small, hard to hit, and I can climb. Except — "I can't fit bear in here. Bear is large."

Every instinct I have is screaming bear. Brown bear, multi-attack, claws and teeth, the weight of a predator behind every swipe. But the room is cramped and the bear is large and I told Vukradin I was coming out as a spider, and I don't take it back.

Brewbarry looks at my square, at the ceiling, at the mess we're in. "Want me to move here and then you can get the bear in there?"

"Well, I mean, it was my turn earlier, but I mean, if you could, yeah, a bear would be a little more appropriate. Yes. Give me a bear icon quickly."

The spider form shifts and thickens and grows — fur, mass, the weight of a predator behind every swipe. Brown bear. "Yay, bear. Rar."

Both attacks land. Twenty damage. The bear is the best kind of bear, and right now it's the best kind of decision.

Vukradin conjures a cloud of spinning daggers in a five-foot cube around the anchorite. Nine damage the instant it forms — and he can't end his turn in the cube without taking it again. The blades sing between me and the half-orc. He's boxed in: the bear in front, the blades on top, nowhere to run.

And then the DM's internet dies.

"Losing you, Kostadis. The tubes are clogged. It's a series of tubes. Switch to the truck, man."

The bear token vanishes off the board. Just gone. I am standing in a room with a dead demon, a dead acolyte, and no way to reach the enemy. "I see a whisper of a bear and cannot get a bear. I should need to figure out how to do this without you."

The DM comes back. An internet flux. His house on Santorini has eight rooms and too many relatives with phones, and he had to buy an industrial router to handle fifty-plus connections. "Not the DHL connections, the VPN? No, no. The word was DHCP."

I have no idea what a DHCP is. I just know my bear is back on the board and the anchorite is bleeding.

"Valphine, you got to do 12 damage or we're really screwed."

She takes another crossbow shot. Five damage. Not enough — but then she steps out of the room, out of the line of fire, and I can't blame her. "Another lightning bolt will not go well. There will be three characters down."

The anchorite has no second lightning bolt. He releases a burst of thunder instead — 2d8, ten-foot push. I braced for it, held my ground: "I can survive this. Maybe." The wave hits. I'm pushed back, but I'm still standing. Barely. And he flees the dagger cube — he has to, or the blades will shred him — and Brewbarry gets the opportunity.

"Can I attack to end the night? Please."

The rage bonus carries it. Moesko is down to one.

"Dead."

I check. "Dead?"

Vukradin confirms it. "Yes."

He needed one hit point. I did thirteen.

Everyone's just abandoning the bear. Mama Bear go down.

I drop the form and pick up the loot. We'll figure out what that conch is and everything next week.
