## Summary
The morning after a tense night at the Falcon Hunter's Lodge, the party gathered their wits and their supplies, piecing together the events that had unfolded while Valfine and Brewberry slept. Vukredin explained his covert investigation into the lodge's origins — the Falcon Hunter had built his entire reputation on treasure scavenged from the hoard of Imrith, the fearsome dragon known as the Doom of the Desert. Among the relics he had kept was a mysterious magic ring, its surface etched with strange writing, and Vukredin was determined to trace it back to its rightful owners. The group debated the morality of the Falcon Hunter's claims, with Vukredin insisting that deception in service of justice was a perfectly reasonable tool, even as his companions raised an eyebrow at the contradiction. With little more to gain at the lodge, the party hitched up their supply cart — Brewberry keeping a particularly watchful eye over his wine — and set off through the ancient depths of Neverwinter Wood toward the logger's camp.

The forest was dense and quiet as the group pressed forward, but the silence was broken when sharp eyes caught the shape of an orc scout crouched ahead, poorly concealed among the undergrowth. Soma recognized immediately that the scout was no lone wanderer — he was a lookout, and somewhere further up the trail, an ambush was waiting. Before the party could fully form a plan, Valfine melted away into the form of a great bear and lumbered forward through the trees. The scout, startled by the enormous creature, scrambled up the nearest tree and began frantically signaling to his companions to the north. A second orc, hidden nearby, spotted Brewberry moving through the brush and let out a shout, shattering any hope of a quiet approach and bringing the full ambush party crashing into the open.

What followed was a chaotic and surprisingly comedic battle. Valfine, still in bear form, hauled herself up the tree with terrifying ease and sank her teeth into the scout, who had clearly not thought through the implications of his chosen refuge. Meanwhile, Vukredin worked his mental craft on one of the orc raiders, bending the warrior's mind until he turned on his own scout with furious, shouted insults about mothers, hamsters, and elderberries. The orcs, already a fractious group seething with family grievances and petty grudges, descended into infighting as raiders screamed at each other across the battlefield. Brewberry, for his part, caught a hurled javelin mid-flight, snapped it in two with a growl, and made it abundantly clear that no one was getting near the wine.

Soma leapt from the trees onto an orc brigand, shoving him into position before calling down a pillar of radiant moonlight that scorched the clustered enemies below. Vukredin conjured a terrifying illusion around one of the raiders, making the warrior believe he was surrounded by a duplicate of his own ally, leaving him swinging wildly at empty air until he finally fell. The brigand, overwhelmed by the chaos and what he could only describe as drow trickery, turned and fled into the forest. The scout, equally demoralized, vanished into the undergrowth shortly after, leaving only one raider standing — until Vukredin lulled him into a deep magical slumber, and the battle was over.

The party bound the sleeping orc with rope and waited for him to wake. When he did, he found himself surrounded by an Aasimar, a Drow, a towering barbarian, and a druid who had recently been a bear — and he was understandably nervous. He introduced himself, though the party quickly renamed him Shrimpy, and he revealed that his tribe had been driven from their home in the Sword Mountains by a white dragon, scattering the orcs into the valley below and fracturing them between the Cult of Talos and desperate independent raiding parties. When Shrimpy, with admirable audacity, asked whether he would be getting paid for his troubles, Brewberry informed him flatly that his wages were his continued existence. Shrimpy was bound to the handles of the supply cart and set to work pulling it down the road, while the party looted fifteen gold pieces from the fallen raider and pressed on.

By the time the logging camp came into view along the south shore of the river, the mood had shifted. A dozen tents stood on the sandy beach, a cabin with logs stacked beneath its awning sat near the dock, and the crumbling stone chimneys of older, torn-down structures jutted from the earth like broken teeth. But there was no sound, no movement, and no sign of Tibor Wester — the man they had come so far to find. A grim silence hung over the entire camp, and the party stood at its edge, supplies delivered to no one, their reward unsigned, and the unsettling feeling that something had gone very wrong here.

### Neverwinter Wood
A dense and ancient forest through which the party travels to reach the logger's camp. The party was ambushed here by a disorganized band of orc raiders among the thick trees and foliage.

### Sword Mountains
The ancestral home of the orc raiding party, mentioned by the prisoner as having been taken over by a white dragon, forcing the orcs down into the valley.

### Logger's Camp
A settlement on the south shore of a river featuring a sandy beach lined with tents and a cabin with an awning for stacked logs. Older cabins have been torn down, leaving only stone chimneys and foundations. The camp was found in an unsettling, grim silence with no one around.
