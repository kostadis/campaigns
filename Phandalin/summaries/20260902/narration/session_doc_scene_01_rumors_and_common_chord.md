### Vukradin

Petra’s payment is still a promise. My likeness, past and future, for five hundred gold pieces, payable when we perform in two days. I keep that amount apart from the studio’s donations. Money someone intends to give us cannot yet buy a door, or a chair, or a room where a young musician can make a terrible noise until it becomes a good one.

Aldus Hern has not supplied a time for dinner with Dagult Neverember. We cannot be late for an appointment that does not exist.

“Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor.”

“Great, isn't that Valphine?” Soma asks.

“No.”

“Ow.”

I look toward Valphine.

“Oh, right,” Soma adds.

“rumor, and I suspect… Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see.”

“I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I?”

“Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming.”

I return to the important part.

“new drow advisor that we will.”

“Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact.”

“That's my… that is my guess, too, yes.”

“Everyone's related to everyone,” Soma says.

I would prefer a dinner where we discuss the studio and nobody discovers a hostile relative behind the soup. Still, we need information, and an invitation is an invitation.

“Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?”

“I believe it's in progress.”

The money is secured, though. The difficulty is getting goods here.

“We have. Oh, okay.”

I turn the problem over.

“But, oh, but the cost of import is very high.”

“Right, because their shipping lanes are still…”

Soma stops and corrects herself.

“Shipping portals, whatever, are still hosed.”

House Margaster controls the remaining logistics.

“Of course they are.”

They also deliver on time and on schedule. I acknowledge that. A house can provide a useful service and still deserve scrutiny over what it charges people who have nowhere else to go. Brewbarry wants to bring softness to people. He should not need to purchase permission from House Margaster at every stage.

Valphine regards the house as competition. I hear considerably less indignation in her assessment.

“Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by 3 AM.”

There is time before then.

“And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready.”

At the Common Chord, someone within easy earshot is blaming me for the prices.

I stand still long enough to hear the complaint. The place is unaffordable now. Getting a ticket is an achievement. Apparently this is all Vukradin’s fault.

I look at the polished tables. I know what it costs to play in a room where nobody listens. I do not want success to mean the people who did listen can no longer come inside.

So I explain the independent studio. A charitable fund, a place of our own, affordable access to the music. In Phandalin we can make this work.

“That will speed the…”

The patron turns toward me.

“opening of a… of a pre… a low-cost, non-profit way to hear, Lathander's death on a regular basis.”

I want him to understand that he would be helping open the doors.

“And spread the word.”

While we talk, another conversation travels along the bar. The sewer water follows the river, and the river follows the sea.

“Hide in the sewers?”

I turn toward my companions.

“There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?”

Neverwinter sits beside the Sea of Swords. Yes. Fine. That makes the water’s behavior relevant to our evening.

“How many moons does this planet have?”

“That's an easy question to answer,” Valphine says.

“Just the one.”

“It just rotates It revolves really fast,” Soma says.

“It has one moon named Selûne.”

“Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah.”

The Tears of Selûne complicate the discussion briefly.

“So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons.”

Except the Tears are smaller bodies, not another moon.

“They're not sufficiently gravitational.”

“Yeah, they're… they're just comets,” Soma says.

“They're asteroids. So the one moon, then.”

By the time the surrounding conversation settles the matter, we have two high tides and two low tides each day.

“There you go, see? Not 1 and 1, there you go. Too high and too low, yeah.”

“That's murder on trade.”

“I should take effect underground, too, right?” Valphine asks.

“So it's a 6-hour cycle.”

I work through it aloud, because being precise now is preferable to discovering an error while sewage rises around our necks.

“Yeah, so it's a 6-hour cycle.”

Then I briefly improve the calculation into something wrong.

“High to low is 3 hours.”

No.

“Yeah, no, high to low. High to low is 6 hours, 6…”

I have it.

“High to low is 6 hours.”

The operational requirement remains simple.

“We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide.”

“3 AM,” Soma supplies.

“We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable.”

“I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely.”

I look at her shell. This is useful for Soma. It does considerably less for the rest of us.

The patron returns my attention to the studio by handing me ten gold pieces.

“Wow, that's pretty generous, actually.”

“That's a hell of a donation,” Soma agrees.

The coins have weight. Enough that I want to make absolutely certain they reach the right place.

“random nobody. We need to put that in the correct… that goes in the studio fund, so that does not go in the regular coffers, but thank you very much for contributing to the studio fund, that's awesome.”

He hesitates before releasing the last coin. He wants to know whether contributors receive recognition. A sign, perhaps. Something bearing his name.

“Absolutely. Yes.”

I can already picture the program.

“There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. 10 gold pieces puts you at the,”

“Premier or the Plus, which one?”

“At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet.”

I do not want that to diminish what he has done.

“You're at plus level. 10 gold is a lot for a rando.”

His face brightens. Good. He should be pleased. This is his studio too, in the sense that his gift helps make it possible, and I want him to see his name and know that.

“Yeah, we need his name, we need to write his name down, because we'll have to put it in the program notes, and…”

“It'd be the…”

“Per year.”

“Good section.”

“A year. I mean, then, if you want to be in next year, you'll have to donate again, but for one year, that's a long time.”

Soma considers the arrangement.

“This is where it comes from, patronage.”

Exactly. People supporting music because they want music to exist.

“But before we go, should I go around and, like, try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?”

Enough.

“Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here.”

I approach another handful of patrons. The words come cleanly. I explain what the studio will do, where the money goes, why affordable access matters. Faces turn toward me. Questions become practical. I can hear the proposal reaching people.

One man recognizes me immediately, but his charitable giving for the year is already complete.

“Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody.”

I mean it. A gift extracted through discomfort would spoil what I am trying to build.

“The benefits are all on you, if you can be at the, you know, plus or premier level, like, I'm cool. I just like fans.”

He assures me that he is my biggest fan.

“Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?”

We both look toward Brewbarry. The man concedes the point.

“It's alright. Nobody's as big as Brewbarry, that's just how it.”

The others contribute. Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, Ondrelle Kass. I get the names down carefully.

“Wow.”

There is something deeply satisfying about a list of people who voluntarily want this to happen.

“That's great. Well, let's add them to the program notes, and”

I look over the names again.

“That's awesome.”

Wick has never heard of Lathander’s Death. He does not seem convinced that I am Vukradin either. What excites him is the music school.

“School?” Soma asks.

“school, studio, they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people, that's just how it goes.”

Wick nods. We understand one another well enough. A studio ought to help people learn. Why would I build a place for music and then refuse to let anyone grow there?

Sabbatha’s attention rests mostly on Brewbarry. She admires the commitment to his appearance. Her gaze travels over his arms and stays there.

“Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?”

“Rippling,” Soma says.

“Exactly.”

Old Hesp recognizes me too. He remembers the empty room.

“Yeah?”

He remembers coming when I promise people their money back unless they love the performance completely.

“Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back at the end of.”

Yes. He remembers that part particularly well.

“I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?”

Now, he thinks the Common Chord costs too much. Everyone has to look fashionable. He shifts his attention from the room to the leather finish on Brewbarry’s table, then to its plaque.

“Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running.”

He wants the old roughness back. The room where people come to listen without first deciding whether their clothes deserve admission.

“We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back.”

He calls the place too commercial.

“Yeah, yeah, I agree.”

Petra has made a money-making operation of it, he says, and he respects the accomplishment. He remembers the table without leather or polish. He remembers when there is no fancy food.

I look where he points. My friend has a plaque. My listeners need more money to sit near it.

“A little bit more money.”

The later contributions bring the evening’s total to twenty gold pieces. I put it with the studio funds. Petra’s payment stays separate, still pending.

“I'll take it. Sounds good.”

I close the list of names and gather my things.

“Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking.”
