### Vukradin

I know why I enter the sewer. The construct takes the crate, and I follow it. None of that is missing.

I simply cannot make the route matter anymore.

My feet take another passage. Water darkens the lower stones. The air sits cold in my throat. I should return to my friends. That seems useful. Continuing after the crate does not.

Then several men block the passage, and their leader wants to know how the hell I get over here.

“I don't know.”

I look past his shoulder. More tunnel. No helpful sign.

“Where am I?”

He studies me. I study him. This produces very little understanding on either side.

He wants to know why I am here. I explain that I am not over there.

The answer does not satisfy him.

I cannot read these men. I cannot tell what arrangement I have interrupted, or why my location is a problem, and standing here searching their faces is not going to improve anything. Talking might.

I need to get back to my companions. I also need to avoid announcing that I enter the sewers to pursue a clandestine delivery.

“Yeah, there you go. I'm lost.”

This is true enough to say without difficulty. I direct all the weight of my explanation toward that part.

“I don't know what… I don't know how I got here, I just…”

His scrutiny eases. Good. We have found the actual problem. A lost musician, a tunnel, someone who knows the way out. These facts ought to cooperate.

He licks a finger, then informs me that this is his territory.

“This is your territory.”

I take that in.

“Okay.”

“Did you want me to go?”

He still wants an explanation.

“Oh, I'm sorry that I got lost. I'm happy to leave. Could you point me in the direction of Lim's?”

I clarify.

“Cafe, that's… that's where I want to be headed.”

Lim’s name changes his face. His attention fixes on me again.

“Yeah.”

“We're friends.”

Apparently this makes my presence more interesting rather than less.

“Okay.”

I wait. There is a pause long enough that I become aware of water moving somewhere behind me.

Then recognition reaches him. He asks whether I am that singer.

“Exactly. And Lim's a fan.”

“What are you doing down here, man?”

“I told you, I got lost!”

One of his associates is smiling now, openly delighted.

“This is… is this really Vukradin?”

“Do you guys want autographs?”

They do. Of course they do. We could have started here and saved a considerable amount of unease.

“Sure, we can do some autographs, but then I gotta be going.”

I gesture along the passage.

“Point me in the right direction.”

“You know what, we're gonna do one better, we're gonna take you there.”

“Alright, sounds good.”

“We don't want you to get lost again, Mr. Vukradin.”

“Yeah, exactly.”

“Lim will be thrilled.”

The leader, Rsolk, pauses to warn me about the tunnels.

“Is it?”

He considers them dangerous.

“Yeah, it's dangerous in your territory?”

I look at the men around him. They know where they stand. They know which passage belongs to whom. This seems like the beginning of competent local management.

“I would think you would keep a very… you seem like a very, you know, safe kind of individual. I'm sure you.”

I gesture at the tunnel.

“place ShipShape.”

“But I have to tell you that the reason I was out here, and I was a little bit concerned when I saw you, and I have to say, I'm very glad that I was able to recognize you from… you know, I… one of my guys over here tells me that you have a deal with a friend of mine.”

“At the Common Chord?”

“No, no, at the Toe mine.”

“Mountain's Toe Mine. Oh, yeah…”

“Zeleen…”

“Oh, I didn't know you knew him.”

Zeleen Varnaster. Our acquaintances keep reaching into places I do not expect.

“Yeah, we spent some time in Phandalin, yeah, yeah.”

“Yeah, he said that he had a business relationship with you, I said that he was full of shit.”

“No, no, that's true, that's true. Yeah, they haven't produced very much yet, but they say they're working hard.”

Rsolk laughs.

“Yeah.”

“That's how I feel about it, too.”

He laughs again, then explains his work. He manages these tunnels.

“Oh, you're the tunnel manager.”

“Yeah, a tunnel manager.”

“So this is your territory, and you have all the tunnels.”

“Not all the tunnels, but the tunnels in this area.”

“The tunnels in this area, interesting, okay.”

A specific responsibility. That helps.

“Two months ago, things started to come up the floodline.”

“Floodline.”

He describes a passage that floods, then the places his people no longer use.

“I don't know anything about tunnels, man, I'm a musician.”

He has lost men there. That part requires no knowledge of drainage. People go down a passage to do their work, something attacks them, and now everyone else has to avoid it.

I straighten.

“Did you need some help with that? Because we're looking… we're trying to look for, like, ways to make money. Like, we have some abilities, me and my friends. Like, if you need some help, maybe we can help you.”

Rsolk needs no further encouragement. He has a survivor’s account, and he gives it to me at once.

“Oh.”

“Creature stuff.”

“It looked like a spider, but it had all the wrong colors.”

“Okay.”

The webs are wrong too. Visibly wrong, beyond ordinary silk or a large spider’s work. I remember the unnatural webbing from our earlier encounter. My skin tightens beneath my sleeves.

“Yeah.”

“experience with that.”

Brewbarry can deal with bodies that need stopping. Soma knows when something living has become something else. Valphine brings light where it is needed, and I can keep everyone working together. We have useful abilities. Here is someone who needs them.

Then Rsolk asks for something I do not expect.

“Mr. Vukradin, I know this is too much to ask, but could you get me some tickets to the Common Chord?”

“I can ask, you know, I…”

I must be precise about what I can promise.

“we have kind of an arm's length relationship with the Common Chord, but I will ask for you.”

“That's all I could ask for.”

No fee is settled yet. We have the job, the danger, and enough directions to find Rsolk and the affected passage again. That is a beginning. A real opportunity to help people and earn money without picking through anyone’s possessions.

“Alright, let's do some autographs, and then we gotta be on our way.”

I sign what they offer. The pleased associate watches the movement of my hand. Rsolk’s people gather around, then open a path and lead me toward ground I recognize.

I keep the directions. I keep the promise about the tickets too.
