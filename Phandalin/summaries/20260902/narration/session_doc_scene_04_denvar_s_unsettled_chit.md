### Soma

Vukradin has a route back. Good. I can stop watching the Sending Stone and watch our carrier.

The man finishes his meal alone. Valphine’s assessment is plain enough: a delivery man. No concealed commander. Just a man who knows where to put a box.

“And dinner at 4 a.m.”

Breakfast, apparently. It depends which end of sleep you approach it from.

“It's just a night shift, so what part of town does he live in?”

We follow him into the Bluelake District. He enters a building full of small rented rooms.

“Blue Lake, okay.”

I keep near enough for the rat. My knees object to another wait. Meril’s Staff takes some of the weight.

“I mean, we sent the rat in.”

“Just see if I can find any, you know.”

I let the small eyes guide me past the room’s edges.

“Any odd signs, any, any receipts, any, any sort of pay stubs, anything to get a, a sense.”

The man sleeps. His belongings are better than I expect. So is his habit of paying someone else to cook before dawn.

“I mean, that's something to say, right? Has he got nice clothes? Does he have, you know…”

There is enough here to make the question worth asking.

“Yeah, he's a little overcompensated for his job.”

“No signs, no,”

I look again.

“No marks, no insignias, nothing, nothing for the rat to spot?”

Nothing obvious. No badge tying him to a house. No useful name laid out for visitors. A quiet room and a good living from carrying boxes.

“Oh shit, I want that gig.”

Valphine examines the building.

“So, is he in a house, or is he in an apartment?”

Rooms share a landlord and a roof. Each tenant gets little enough space beneath it.

“Okay, so he has a landlord.”

“Who we could potentially ask questions about him of.”

She looks at the quiet doors.

“But it's middle of the night, huh?”

“High efficiency flat,” I say.

We wait until approaching the landlord seems ordinary enough. The carrier stays asleep. I shift my shell and put my weight on the other foot.

“Yeah, no, I mean, we're… we can't spook them indirectly, so, just talk.”

I approach the man who runs the place.

“landlord. Alright. Okay, we just had some questions. We wanted to know,”

“What's it cost to live here? I think is the first one we need to know.”

He looks at my shell. He does not see many tortles.

“That's…”

I wait for the price.

“what I asked.”

Instead, I get an explanation of why he assumes I do not need a room. Turtles carry their houses on their shoulders.

“I see what you think of us. I can't live in this thing. It merely protects me.”

“Oh!”

I tap the shell. Solid. No hearth inside. No kettle.

“Well, outside of it. I was not in Neverwinter proper.”

My cabin is in the woods outside the city. An actual home. He seems to learn something from this.

“I guess you're not like… I guess you're not like turtles.”

“No, no, we have… Very… that's kind of slanderous to us.”

His eyes move past me. Valphine. Brewbarry. He takes a better look at the company I keep and begins to defend the questions.

“Oh, oh, I'm just asking questions, I see.”

“Just asking questions.”

“Look.”

I leave my staff planted on the floor.

“I'll pretend I didn't hear some of that. I just… I want to know what it costs to live here, and, you know, what kind of people normally stay in a place like this.”

A possible reason for asking comes easily enough. There are always young ones who need somewhere to go.

“You could say I'm looking for a place for my, you know, my shell sprouts in the future.”

He assures me that the establishment takes anyone. Equal opportunity.

“It's so progressive.”

His explanation is financial. He wants money. He does not care who brings it.

Then he notices that Valphine is a drow.

“Very astute.”

“I thought Drow didn't like the sunlight.”

“Oh, I love the sunlight.”

I look at her eyes. Even here, the gold is plain.

“I love the sunlight.”

“It's, it's not… it's not our duty to educate him.”

I return to the room prices.

“He needs to learn on his own. He needs to put in the work.”

“So, yeah, you're just… you just take money, you don't ask questions, so you don't know anything about your tenants, you don't know about that,”

“How do you deal with the KYC laws in here?” Valphine asks.

“No problem.”

The price requires cash up front. A good amount of it. More than casual day labor should comfortably cover.

“Yeah, it seems odd. You never, you never do any sort of background check, you never, you know, make sure that people have the gold that they, you know, claim they're gonna have after months, you know?”

He says he does not scry on customers.

“I don't believe that. Given the racism you presume, I'm sure you scry on certain ones of them, don't you?”

His attention goes back to Brewbarry.

“Jiggling about.”

The man wants no trouble. His gaze has reached the halberd.

“If you want a place to stay here, I'll give you a discount.”

“Really, we just want to know about one of your tenants.”

I describe the carrier we follow here. He supplies a name.

“Denvar. Yeah. That's more than we came with, we got a name.”

“Denvar!”

I settle back to the task.

“Yes, yes, we mean Denvar.”

“We, he dropped something, you know, last night.”

A small lost object. Easy to understand. No reason to run upstairs and warn him.

“We saw him skulking about in the, the alleyway super late at night, and he dropped a little pouch. We'd like to return it to him.”

“Making a delivery,” Valphine adds.

Through the rat’s eyes, I find the paper trail at last. Small delivery chits. I have to look closer.

“Yeah, see, this is what the rat was going in looking for.”

Enough records to cover a little more than a week. The same instructions recur: nine crates, the third hour, eight up, one down.

“Third up, one down.”

I catch my own mistake. Eight up. One down. The stack for Lim and the crate on the ledge.

“Yeah, but yeah.”

The chits carry a client reference and a house mark. No personal name. Settlement is weekly.

“And the… all his deliveries… 100% of his delivery chits are the same. They're all this one thing. He literally has one job.”

“Yeah. It is. Wow. Yeah, no, that's not suspicious.”

I move the rat’s head closer to the marks.

“The, yeah, the checks on them, whatever.”

“And stamp of payment.”

Paid. Paid. Paid.

“But no, nothing identifiable on them other than just…”

“They clearly seem to be related to this one contract.”

Then the latest chit. No settlement mark. The delivery we watch has not yet been paid.

“I, okay,”

“And so that was the one that he just, presumably delivered. Yeah, so I guess we have reason to follow him once he wakes up, don't we?”

I ease my grip on the staff.

“Yeah, that's…”

“I assume he… I assume he needs to, you know, be able to afford a place like this. Yeah, he needs the money pretty quick.”

The landlord is still here. Useful.

“In fact, we could ask the landlord, you know, is Denvar settled up on his bills?”

He owes money today.

“Yeah?”

“Yeah, so he's… he's motivated.”

A sleeping man with rent due and an unpaid job. We can let those things do the pulling.

“You know, he's just sleeping in his room right now, right? Like…”

“Yeah, well, I mean, he's got till the end of the day, and he's a good customer. He's quiet, he doesn't bother anybody.”

“I'll bet.”

“Good guy, huh?” Valphine asks.

“Yeah, totally good guy.”

“You don't think he's a serial killer or anything?”

The landlord looks at Meril’s Staff, Valphine’s white armor and golden eyes, then Brewbarry’s halberd. His gaze lingers on the blade.

“Should I be worried?”

“You never know with these types. You never know.”

“I'd be worried about him,” Valphine says.

We have what we need. I prepare to leave him to his rooms. But he returns to the question of tortle housing. This time he wants to know what would actually make a suitable room.

“Oh, a nice hearth, you know, plenty of water. Usually near, you know, near a body of water is nice.”

I mean it. Dry joints appreciate a hearth. Water ought to be close. A room should be fit for the body living in it.

I move aside to let my bale pass. There is still a sleeping carrier upstairs, and I need somewhere to rest my knees until he wakes.
