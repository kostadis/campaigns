### Valphine Sotorra

The stones below Lim’s delivery entrance are wet enough to betray careless feet. I descend the aqueduct steps without haste. The Overbright is tolerable at this hour; its inhabitants are mostly indoors, and the darkness offers fewer distractions than their daylight.

“I have a very high stealth, if we need me to…” Vukradin begins.

“We need someone to do something stealthily.”

“I mean, you could tag along with a familiar…” Soma says.

Vukradin draws attention to his Boots of Elvenkind. I consider my own footing, the weight of my armor, the smooth tread of his boots. The assignment is apparent before the discussion reaches it.

“And it's just because I have boots of elvenkind.”

“Hell yeah.”

Soma calls a rat into being through Find Familiar. Small ribs move beneath its fur. Its nose tests the damp.

“Yeah, we just have to stay within 100 feet. I'm going to, create a little rat. I see and hear everything the rat hears.”

“100 feet is… Pretty close.”

“Yeah, but, you know, we… we can be, like, on the other side of walls. It's not, like, line of sight 100 feet.”

“Hmm, okay…”

“There's… there's corners and everywhere, you know, we… we can be… we can make it work.”

I position myself beside the grill. The stone presses cold against my hand. Beyond it, the sewer gives off the smell of standing water and old refuse.

The carriers arrive.

“All 9 of them?” Soma asks.

All nine. Eight for Lim, another kept apart. I look for the Commission Harbor mark on the larger stack and find none. Someone has brought these goods into Neverwinter without passing through the official channel. The absence interests me less than the care taken with the remaining crate.

“So we… so we want one person to track down the gnome, right, crate 9.”

Vukradin watches the carriers work.

“We want other… also, we want to track the people who dropped them, so we want to do two things.”

“What, you said you got your high stealth.”

“Yeah.”

“If you want, I can track the gnome. You guys can go do the… the sewers.”

“Well, I'm tracking the crate.”

“Which crate?”

“The ninth crate, the one that doesn't go into Lim, because I'm more concerned about my friend's shady business.”

The arrangement begins to consume more attention than the delivery itself.

“We're not gonna follow… Okay, so you want me to follow them back into the sewers?”

Vukradin looks down.

“That seems pretty dangerous. One person in the… one party member in the sewers.”

“I see. Alright, we can send the rat that way, then. You can follow the crate.”

The carriers leave the boxes beside the grill. The one who sets the separate crate on the ledge pauses and peers below, searching the dark before he withdraws.

“Like, down at the grates, or the…” Soma murmurs.

I keep my gaze on his face. The other carrier gives the stack his attention. This one checks the place from which a collector must emerge.

“The rat's gonna go whichever way leads to the sewer.”

“So, look, the… The crates got dropped off. Lim's gonna come out and take 8 of the crates.”

“Right.”

“And she's not gonna take a 9th crate.”

Vukradin settles into waiting beside the separate delivery.

“So everyone other than Vukradin is gonna…”

I indicate the departing men.

“trail these guys, and then Vukradin's gonna trail the crate.”

“Is that right?”

“Yes,” Vukradin says.

“Yes,” Soma agrees.

“They come out of the sewers, they drop the crates, and then they just walk on through town, which way?”

In different directions. Neither acknowledges the other. The avoidance is conspicuous enough to be useful.

“That nonchalant whistling,” Soma says.

“So if they split up, that's gonna make them hard to follow. You're gonna have to choose one or split again.”

I examine their departure, looking for the smaller concessions that reveal rank: who waits, who checks, who merely completes a task. At first I have only a pair of retreating backs.

“relative to each other. Now, who's the more senior member?”

“Hold on.”

I catch the remnant of Vukradin’s inspiration, the phrase that sharpens my attention, and spend it on the moment before it slips away. The pause at the ledge returns to me with its proper weight.

“Alright.”

The man who checks below knows there is something to check for. I indicate him.

“I'd follow the guy with the… who paid attention to the ledge,” Soma says.

“He knows something. He knows something's coming out of that thing.”

Precisely. The other man stacks boxes. This one anticipates an arrival.

“I could handle this myself, guys, you don't trust me?”

Before I move, brass catches the scant light beneath the grill.

The collector is small enough to pass for a cat at a glance, until leather flexes between brass parts and its movement resolves into repeated, exact motions. It takes the waiting crate and retreats below. A servant with no appetite to interrupt its instructions.

Vukradin slips after it.

“This is my concern. Vukradin ends up going into the sewers now, and…”

“I'll be fine.”

“One person in the sewers? Alright.”

“got advantage.”

“Tell me the odds.”

“Only a 1 in 400 chance of disaster. It'll be fine.”

I watch his boots disappear into the dark. His calculation concerns whether someone will hear him. There are other ways to be found.

Soma carries one Sending Stone; Vukradin has its mate. At least the separation has a voice.

“Good to know.”

“So we could have little walkie-talkies over. Yeah.”

“We have walkie-talkies,” comes his reply.

I follow the carrier with Soma and Brewbarry. At an open cook shop, our quarry sits alone over breakfast.

“Can't be a lot of those,” Soma says.

Most nearby doors remain shut. The fourth hour belongs to laborers, people avoiding laborers, and whatever else has reason to move before dawn.

“He's not eating turtle soup or anything at this hour, is he?”

Soma examines the shop.

“It's not the Board Laid Bare he's at, of course, he's somewhere else.”

We wait. He eats. No visitor approaches the table; no signal passes that I can see. The tide continues without requiring our attention, which makes attending to it rather necessary.

“Oh, right, right, right,” Soma says.

“He's just eating alone, he's not talking to anybody, not, not,”

Vukradin’s voice reaches us through the stone.

“Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections.”

Then the refinement.

“Or you promised them.”

“Thank you so much.”

Soma keeps her distance.

“I mean, I'm not trying to get her, you know, out of business. I don't know, I kind of want to see where he goes next. I mean, just stopping off for a snack, that's… that's… doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem like, you know, kosher.”

I leave the carrier to his meal. A frightened intermediary closes routes that an untroubled one continues to use.

Then Vukradin’s account of his progress changes. The tunnel no longer interests him. He still knows why he enters it; he simply ceases to consider the pursuit worth continuing.

“I mean, okay, there's some passive charm effect or something.”

I bring my attention to Soma’s stone.

“Well, it can't have been cast directly on me.”

The collector has not needed to hear his boots.

“This is, like, an area effect.”

I look back at the man eating his breakfast. The carrier’s instructions end at a ledge. Something beyond it can make an intruder supply the reason for his own departure.

I keep watch over the spoon, the bowl, the ordinary hand lifting them.
