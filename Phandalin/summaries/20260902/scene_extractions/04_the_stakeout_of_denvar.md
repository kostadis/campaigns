# The Stakeout of Denvar

## Scene summary (from gm-assist, verbatim)

#### The rest of the party follows a delivery man named Denvar to his residence to uncover his connections.

- Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry watch Denvar eat a solitary early breakfast at a cook shop.
- Valphine’s intuition suggests that Denvar really is a delivery man rather than a disguised leader or mastermind.
- They follow him to a high-efficiency, single-room-occupancy-style building in the Bluelake District.
- Soma’s familiar scouts Denvar’s room while he sleeps.
- The familiar initially finds no factional insignia, ordinary identifying marks, receipts, or conventional pay stubs; the GM subsequently adds delivery chits bearing a client reference, house mark, and settlement stamps.
- Denvar nevertheless appears somewhat overcompensated: his belongings and ability to dine out at four in the morning exceed what the party expects from an ordinary laborer.
- The landlord initially focuses on Soma’s ancestry and says, “I thought turtles carried their house on their shoulders, so they didn’t need a place to stay.”
- Soma explains, “I can’t live in this thing. It merely protects me,” and notes that she owns a cabin outside Neverwinter.
- Before the Drow exchange, he notices Valphine and Brewbarry, insists he is “just asking questions,” and grows worried that he may have offended dangerous visitors; his alarm returns later when he inventories the party’s weapons.
- He claims to run an equal-opportunity establishment, clarifying that his indifference is financial: “money’s money, man, I don’t care who’s got it.”
- The landlord then tells Valphine, “You’re a drow,” and asks whether Drow dislike sunlight. Valphine replies that she loves it.
- The building requires significant cash up front, making Denvar’s ability to stay there even less consistent with ordinary day-labor wages.
- The landlord says that he does not scry on his customers and offers the party a discounted room before learning they are investigating a tenant.
- The party invents a story that Denvar dropped a pouch while making a late-night delivery and that they wish to return it.
- The landlord supplies Denvar’s name and describes him as quiet, reliable, and unobtrusive.
- Denvar owes money that day, although the landlord considers him a good customer and gives him until the end of the day to settle.
- Among Denvar’s possessions are seven to ten days of identical delivery chits reading, “9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down.”
- The chits contain a client reference and a house mark but no personal name and are reconciled through weekly settlements.
- Every chit appears paid except the seventh and newest one, corresponding to the delivery the party just witnessed.
- The party concludes that Denvar is likely to seek payment after waking because he needs to meet his rent obligation.
- Before they leave, the landlord asks Soma what would make a suitable Tortle room.
- Soma sincerely recommends a hearth, plenty of water, and proximity to a body of water instead of using the opportunity to mislead him.
