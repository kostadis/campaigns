The magical wrongness in these tunnels does not seize me or shove me. It merely makes the path I intend to follow seem unworthy of pursuit, which is much more insulting. One turn becomes another, the sewer walls narrow, and soon the sound of my friends has vanished behind wet stone and running water. I continue until the passage opens enough to hold a group of men.

One stands in front of the others. He studies me while they spread across the tunnel behind him, occupying the dry ground as if it belongs to them.

"How the hell did you get over here?"

"I don't know."

This is true, but not yet sufficient.

"Where am I?"

He keeps staring. I consider the immediate evidence: I am here, my friends are not, and none of these men appear eager to explain the distinction.

"Because I'm not over there."

Nothing in their faces tells me what they want or whether I have offended them. I only understand that their leader is confused by my arrival, which makes two of us.

"But I don't know what I'm doing here."

I remember the pursuit that brought me into the sewers. I do not know why I am standing in this tunnel, facing these particular men, after the magic persuaded me that every useful direction was artistically inferior.

"Yeah, there you go. I'm lost."

The explanation settles over them. I commit to it because it is also the truth.

"I don't know what… I don't know how I got here, I just…"

I gesture back toward the turns that failed to remain sensible.

"Yeah. Wrong turn."

There are wrong turns, and then there are wrong turns that remove an entire adventuring party.

"Should have taken that left at Leilon."

The leader licks his finger, though I cannot determine what this contributes.

"Well, this is my territory."

"This is your territory."

"Yes, this is my territory. Wow."

"Okay."

Territory is a serious word for a wet tunnel under Neverwinter, but if he maintains it, protects it, and has people who answer to him, then the word may be justified. Ownership and stewardship are not the same thing, of course. That distinction has caused considerable trouble everywhere from Mountain’s Toe Mine to the treasury of Phandalin.

"Did you want me to go?"

He wants to know why I am here. I have explained this, but sometimes a conversation requires repetition before the parties locate the same rhythm.

"Oh, I'm sorry that I got lost. I'm happy to leave. Could you point me in the direction of Lim's?"

I clarify the destination before he can decide I mean a person rather than a business.

"Cafe, that's… that's where I want to be headed."

He repeats Lim’s Cafe as a question and examines me more closely.

"Yeah."

His suspicion sharpens when I explain the connection.

"We're friends."

That changes something. He looks me over, no longer merely confused, and appears to consider whether my friendship with Lim requires him to act against me. This is unfortunate. Friendship should generally reduce the number of territorial confrontations in a sewer.

"Okay."

Then recognition interrupts whatever he is considering. He looks directly at my face.

"Are you that singer?"

*Finally.*

"Exactly. And Lim's a fan."

"What are you doing down here, man?"

"I told you, I got lost!"

He confirms my name, and one of the men beside him breaks into a broad smile.

"This is… is this really Vukradin?"

"Do you guys want autographs?"

The answer is immediate and enthusiastic. Fame, properly used, is not simply applause or a crowded hall. It is the transformation of a dangerous misunderstanding into people searching themselves for something suitable to sign.

"Sure, we can do some autographs, but then I gotta be going."

The leader agrees and begins to suggest a solution.

"Point me in the right direction."

"You know what, we're gonna do one better, we're gonna take you there."

"Alright, sounds good."

"We don't want you to get lost again, Mr. Vukradin."

"Yeah, exactly."

"There you go."

"Lim will be thrilled."

The confrontation is resolved. Autographs, safe passage, and no one has needed Brewbarry to kill anybody. Harmony.

Before we leave, the leader pauses and warns me that the tunnels are dangerous.

"Is it?"

"Yeah, it's, it's, it's,"

"Yeah, it's dangerous in your territory?"

This deserves clarification. A man who introduces a tunnel as his territory also accepts a degree of responsibility for its condition. Otherwise, it is not management. It is branding.

"I would think you'd keep it very safe. You seem like a safe individual. I'm sure you keep the place shipshape."

"Mr. Vukradin, I agree with you."

He explains that this danger is why he reacted so strongly when he found me. He is also glad he recognized me, because one of his men has heard that my friends and I have a business arrangement with one of his friends.

"At the Common Chord?"

"No, no, at the Toe mine."

"Mountain's Toe Mine. Oh, yeah…"

"Zeleen…"

"Oh, I didn't know you knew him."

"Oh, yeah, he… well, you know, knows that."

"Yeah, we spent some time in Phandalin, yeah, yeah."

He admits that Zeleen Varnaster claimed to have a business relationship with us, and that he had dismissed the claim.

"Yeah, he said that he had a business relationship with you, I said that he was full of shit."

"No, no, that's true, that's true. Yeah, they haven't produced very much yet, but they say they're working hard."

He laughs.

"Yeah."

The mine’s production has not inspired confidence in me either. There is a difference between recognizing an honest business relationship and pretending it has generated meaningful returns.

"That's how I feel about it, too."

He laughs again. At least the account is balanced: relationship confirmed, output disappointing, no fraudulent claims of prosperity.

Still laughing, he says, "Look, I manage the tunnels here."

"Oh, you're the tunnel manager."

"Yeah, a tunnel manager."

"So this is your territory, and you have all the tunnels."

"Not all the tunnels, but the tunnels in this area."

"The tunnels in this area, interesting, okay."

The limitation matters. He is not claiming every sewer beneath Neverwinter, only those around us. That is a more credible jurisdiction, and Rsolk becomes much easier to understand once I know his work. He is not simply a territorial man underground. He has associates, boundaries, dangerous infrastructure, and apparently no municipal assistance.

"Two months ago, things started coming up the floodline."

"Floodline."

He describes a nearby tunnel that floods regularly. The trouble began there around two months ago.

"I don't know anything about tunnels, man, I'm a musician."

"I've been having this problem with the tunnels. I've lost men, so we don't go there anymore."

That closes the moral question. People have died, the route has been abandoned, and Rsolk cannot safely resolve the problem with those available to him. My friends and I have fought creatures in mines, towers, woods, ranches, fortresses, and places where reality itself has behaved dishonestly. We also need legitimate work. This is exactly how fair-trade, conflict-free gold should begin: a person has a dangerous problem, we knowingly agree to solve it, and payment follows from useful labor rather than plunder.

"Do you need help with that? My friends and I have some abilities, and we're looking for ways to make money. Maybe we can help you."

Rsolk’s interest is immediate. He tells me that one of the men who escaped saw a creature unlike anything they had encountered.

"Oh."

"It looked like…"

"Creature stuff."

"It looked like a spider, but it had all the wrong colors."

"Okay."

The survivor also described its webs. They were visibly unnatural, wrong in the same disturbing fashion as webbing we have encountered before. I remember enough to understand that this is not an ordinary infestation that Rsolk’s men could clear with torches and shovels.

"Yeah, we have experience with that."

He does not demand proof, negotiate a price, or test whether I am exaggerating. Recognition has already established trust, and our connection to Zeleen confirms that we do this sort of work. Instead of naming a conventional reward, he asks for something he actually wants.

"Mr. Vukradin, I know this is too much to ask, but could you get me some tickets to the Common Chord?"

"I can ask."

I must be precise. Our relationship with the Common Chord exists, but it is not the sort of relationship where I can promise their seats as if I control the venue.

"We have kind of an arm's-length relationship with the Common Chord, but I will ask for you."

"That's all I could ask for."

Fair. No false promise, no invented influence, only an honest effort on behalf of a new source of honest work. We establish where the floodline tunnel lies and how to find Rsolk again. Then paper and writing implements emerge, passed between the men, and I sign my name for each of them while they prepare to escort me back toward familiar ground.

"Alright, let's do some autographs, and then we gotta be on our way."
