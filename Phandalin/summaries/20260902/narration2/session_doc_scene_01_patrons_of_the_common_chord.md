### Vukradin

Aldus Hern has still not given us an exact time for dinner. I am perfectly willing to speak to Dagult Neverember, but a summons without an hour cannot occupy every hour we have. The lord can wait.

"Is it Neverember or Neverwinter? I'm kidding."

I try the name again. "Neverember."

Then again. "Neverember."

"It is never, like, a fire ember."

Soma supplies an interpretation. "He's never flaming. He keeps it on the DL."

"It's Lord…"

I stop. "Yeah, I think Soma has it exactly right."

"He's never flaming, he keeps it on the DL."

"Yeah, never flaming."

The name is considerably less troubling than the rumor attached to it.

"Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor."

"Great, isn't that Valphine?"

"No."

"Ow." Soma pauses. "Oh, right."

"It's a rumor, and I suspect Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see."

Valphine looks back toward us. "I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I?"

"Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming."

I struggle to recover the sentence rather than the name.

"new drow advisor that we will."

"Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact."

"That is my guess too, yes."

"Everyone's related to everyone," Soma says.

I would appreciate meeting a powerful stranger who remains a stranger for the length of an introduction. Meanwhile, Brewbarry has an actual business to establish, and I turn to something we can settle.

"Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?"

"I believe it's in progress."

"Securing money for the."

But the money is secured. The loan is real.

"We have? Oh, okay."

I turn that over. Capital, cotton, shipping. Softness for people who have had quite enough of being hurt. Brewbarry is doing something good, and the next obstacle has a familiar name.

"But, oh, but the cost of import is very high."

"Right, because their shipping lanes are still…"

Soma corrects herself. "Shipping portals, whatever, are still hosed."

House Margaster controls the available routes.

"Of course they are."

Their deliveries arrive on time and on schedule. I grant them that. I do not grant that punctuality makes the price less of an obstacle to everyone else trying to make something.

Valphine regards House Margaster as competition. I find this assessment considerably less reassuring than she appears to.

"Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by 3 AM."

"And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready."

We still have time at the Common Chord. Time, apparently, for me to hear that I have ruined it.

A patron near me is complaining about the cost of the place, and my name is the explanation. He does not recognize me standing within earshot. I look past him at the room, at the seats people now compete to occupy, and remember performing toward empty tables. The difficulty then is getting anyone through the door. The difficulty now is that the people who want to come cannot afford it.

Well. We can do something about that.

"I could tell him I'm planning on opening my own independent music studio, and if he'd like to contribute to the charitable fund."

I bring the proposal to him. An independent studio in Phandalin, with a door people can afford to walk through.

"That will speed the opening of a low-cost, nonprofit way to hear Lathander's Death on a regular basis."

"And spread the word."

While he considers it, talk along the bar turns to the sewers. The river carries the sea's tide into them.

"Hide in the sewers?"

I look toward my companions.

"There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?"

Neverwinter is beside the sea. Yes. That does complicate hiding below it.

"How many moons does this planet have?"

"That's an easy question to answer," Valphine says.

"Just the one."

"It just rotates—it revolves really fast," Soma says.

"It has one moon named Selûne."

"Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah."

There are also the Tears of Selûne. I begin to account for those.

"So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons."

But the Tears are smaller bodies, a cluster rather than another moon.

"They're not sufficiently gravitational."

I reconsider.

"You're telling me?"

"They're not really moons."

"Yeah, they're… they're just comets," Soma says.

"They're asteroids. So the one moon, then."

The useful information resolves out of the discussion: the sewers rise and fall twice each day. I work through the interval, correct myself, and arrive at the important part.

"We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide."

"3 AM," Soma supplies.

"We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable."

"I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely."

I cannot. I keep that distinction firmly in the plan.

The patron brings me back to the studio by putting ten gold pieces into my hand. His speech is loose with drink, but the coins are solid and surprisingly heavy.

"Wow, that's pretty generous, actually."

"That's a hell of a donation," Soma says.

"Random nobody. We need to put that in the correct… That goes in the studio fund, not the regular coffers. Thank you very much for contributing. That's awesome."

He pauses before withdrawing his hand.

"Do I get on… Is there some kind of signage for people who contributed?"

"Absolutely. Yes."

Of course there should be recognition. These are the people making the room possible.

"There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. 10 gold pieces puts you at the,"

"Premier or the Plus, which one?"

"At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet."

I do not want the distinction to diminish what he has given.

"You're at plus level. 10 gold is a lot for a rando."

His face brightens. Good.

"We need his name. We need to write it down for the program notes, and…"

"It'd be the…"

"Per year."

"Good section."

"A year. If you want to be in next year's, you'll have to donate again, but one year is a long time."

Soma considers the arrangement.

"This is where it comes from, patronage."

I look around the room. There are people here who remember the music, people who cannot afford it anymore, people who might want to help someone else learn to make it. I do not have to persuade them that music matters. They are already here.

"Before we go, should I try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?"

Enough.

"Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here."

I approach another patron, and the words come cleanly, without the usual effort of finding where an introduction becomes an appeal. He recognizes me. I see the recognition settle before I finish speaking. He also explains that his charitable giving for the year is already complete.

"Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody."

"The benefits are all on you. If you can be at the Plus or Premier level, I'm cool. I just like fans."

He assures me that he is my biggest fan.

"Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?"

I glance toward my companion.

"It's alright. Nobody's as big as Brewbarry, that's just how it."

The next conversations come just as readily. I speak about a place to hear music, and people begin leaning toward me instead of away. Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, Ondrelle Kass. Names for the program.

"Yeah."

"Wow."

I look at what we have gathered.

"That's great. Well, let's add them to the program notes, and"

"That's awesome."

Wick has little interest in establishing whether I am really Vukradin. He does not know Lathander's Death. He is delighted by the prospect of a music school.

"School?" Soma asks.

"School, studio—they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people. That's just how it goes."

A room with instruments should have room for people learning to use them.

"Yeah."

Sabbatha contributes while looking Brewbarry over. Her admiration rests especially on his commitment to the Brewbarry appearance.

"Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?"

"Rippling," Soma adds.

"Exactly."

Then Old Hesp looks straight at me.

"I remember when you used to play to an empty room."

"Yeah?"

The crowded room becomes briefly difficult to look at.

"Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back at the end of."

He remembers the guarantee. If the performance does not deserve the highest praise, I will not take the listener's money. He remembers receiving his refund.

"I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?"

He pauses.

"Now this is a friggin' expensive place to be. Everybody's gotta look cool."

"Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running."

He misses the roughness of the old place. I know what he means before he finishes describing it. Bare table, ordinary food, a room where nobody first examines your clothes.

"We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back."

"This is too commercial."

"Yeah, yeah, I agree."

He gives Petra credit for making money. He points toward Brewbarry's table, its leather finish and its plaque, and remembers it without either.

"Was, you know, that was not leather and shiny and with a plaque."

The fancy food troubles him too.

I look at the table. I am glad Brewbarry has a place here. I want Old Hesp to have one as well.

The evening's donations come to twenty gold pieces. I keep them for the studio. Petra's separate payment for our likenesses is still pending until the performance in two days; it does not belong in tonight's collection, and I do not count it as money already received.

I put away the names for the program.

"Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking."
