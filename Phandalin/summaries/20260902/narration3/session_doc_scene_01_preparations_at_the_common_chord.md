The Common Chord used to throw me out unpaid. Now it is the hardest ticket in Neverwinter, and we are sitting inside it waiting on a lord who cannot commit to a dinner hour. Aldus has not confirmed the time with Lord Neverember, which is fine. The lord can wait. We have a sewer to enter at three in the morning, and I have a fund to grow.

First, the rumor. "Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor," I say, because Soma has already ruined the man's name for me forever, and honestly the joke landed. He's never flaming; he keeps it on the down-low. I endorsed it at the time. I stand by it.

"Great, isn't that Valphine?" Soma asks.

"No."

"Ow. Oh, right."

"It's a rumor, and I suspect Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see."

Valphine has lost the thread. "I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I?"

"Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming," I remind her. The "new drow advisor that we will."

"Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact."

"That is my guess too, yes." A Sotorra at the Lord Protector's elbow. That would be a discordant chord if I ever heard one. And when someone asks Valphine where she stands on House Margaster, her answer is precise: "I think Valphine is looking at House Margaster as the competition." Good. Competition I understand. Extortion I do not.

I turn the ledger over in my head. "Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?"

Soma believes it's in progress; she is wrong, which delights me. The money is secured. "We have? Oh, okay. But, oh, but the cost of import is very high." That part still stings. The shipping portals are, in Soma's word, hosed, and House Margaster controls the lanes.

"Of course they are," Soma says.

They deliver on time and on schedule, I'll grant them that. So did every tollkeeper who ever squeezed a road. Reliability is not virtue.

"Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by 3 AM," I say. "And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready."

That is when I hear him. A local, three tables over, complaining loudly that the Chord is unaffordable now, and that it is all due to Vukradin. He is talking about me without realizing I am standing within earshot. This is a man with a legitimate grievance and no idea he is about to meet its remedy.

"I could tell him I'm planning on opening my own independent music studio, and if he'd like to contribute to the charitable fund," I say. "That will speed the opening of a low-cost, nonprofit way to hear Lathander's Death on a regular basis." I make the offer. "And spread the word."

While he considers, the bar around us keeps talking, and the bar teaches. The tide, someone says, runs through the sewers.

"There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?" I ask. The town sits on the sea, apparently, and the tides come in. Fine. But precision matters when your plan is timed to water. "How many moons does this planet have?"

"That's an easy question to answer," Valphine says.

"Just the one," I guess.

"It just rotates—it revolves really fast," Soma offers, unhelpfully.

"It has one moon named Selûne," Valphine says.

"Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah." But Selûne travels with her Tears, so: "So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons." Except the Tears are asteroids, a cluster of meteors and rock. "They're not sufficiently gravitational. You're telling me? They're not really moons."

"Yeah, they're… they're just comets," Soma says.

"They're asteroids. So the one moon, then." And yet the correction comes: not one high and one low. "There you go. Not one and one: two high and two low."

"Jesus," Soma says. "That's murder on trade."

"It should take effect underground, too, right?" Valphine asks, which is the whole point.

I run it out loud. "So it's a 6-hour cycle. High to low is 6 hours. We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide." Which happens, conveniently, at three in the morning. "We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable."

"I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely," Soma says. Of course she can. She has a shell. I have boots.

Then my complaining local comes back around, on the drunk side and thoroughly charmed, and hands over ten gold pieces. Ten. From a man who told me he doesn't have a lot of money.

"Wow, that's pretty generous, actually," I say.

"That's a hell of a donation," Soma agrees.

"Random nobody. We need to put that in the correct… That goes in the studio fund, not the regular coffers. Thank you very much for contributing. That's awesome." The wall between funds is the whole point of the funds.

He pauses. "Do I get on… Is there some kind of signage for people who contributed?"

"Absolutely. Yes. There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. 10 gold pieces puts you at the,"

"Premier or the Plus, which one?" Soma prompts.

"At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet. You're at plus level. 10 gold is a lot for a rando." He is happy to hear it. "We need his name. We need to write it down for the program notes." One year of credit. "A year. If you want to be in next year's, you'll have to donate again, but one year is a long time."

"Yeah, we call this Patreon," Valphine says.

"It's my Patreon, exactly."

"This is where it comes from, patronage," Soma says, and she is right, and that is the honest version of what House Margaster does with shipping lanes.

There is time before three. "Before we go, should I try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?" Plenty. "Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here."

Five patrons. Four say yes. The one who declines actually recognizes me, the real Vukradin, and says he has already done his charitable giving for the year. "Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody. The benefits are all on you. If you can be at the Plus or Premier level, I'm cool. I just like fans." He insists he is my biggest fan.

"Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?"

"Brewbarry's very large. In fact, he's huge in Japan sometimes," Valphine says.

"It's alright. Nobody's as big as Brewbarry, that's just how it."

The names go down for the program credits: Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, and Ondrelle Kass. Wick is a marvel. He has no idea who Lathander's Death is, does not believe I am Vukradin, and does not care. He is simply thrilled that somebody is putting together a music school.

"School?" Soma says.

"School, studio—they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people. That's just how it goes." I do believe this. A studio that trains no one is just a vault with good acoustics.

Sabbatha barely hears my pitch because she is staring at Brewbarry across the room, cataloguing his commitment to the Brewbarry look. "Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?"

"Rippling," Soma adds.

"Exactly."

And then Old Hesp. He recognizes me before I recognize him. "I remember when you used to play to an empty room," he says.

"Yeah? Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back at the end of." He confirms it, I did. If he didn't give me a ten out of ten, I wouldn't take his coin. That guarantee was real. It still is.

"I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?"

He pauses, and what he says lands harder than any of the four donations. "Now this is a friggin' expensive place to be. Everybody's gotta look cool."

"Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running."

It doesn't have the look, he says, that feel, that gritty something. "We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back."

"This is too commercial," he says. "Yeah, yeah, I agree." And I do agree, that is the terrible part. Petra turned this place into a money-making operation, and Old Hesp respects her for it even as it prices him out of his own bar. He remembers when the Brewbarry table was not leather and shiny and with a plaque. He remembers when the food was not fancy. I remember playing to that empty room, and I remember what the ticket cost then: nothing, refundable. Twenty gold goes into the studio fund tonight, and Petra still owes the five hundred for my likeness after the performance in two days, and none of it will mean anything unless the studio stays what the Chord stopped being. Low-cost. Nonprofit. For the Old Hesps.

"Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking."
