# Session 2026-09-01

Date: 2026-09-01

## Summary
Earlier that night, while waiting for the third-hour stakeout, Vukradin worked the crowd at the Common Chord with characteristic charm, pitching his vision for an independent music studio to anyone who would listen. He found a handful of willing patrons — Wick, a passionate arts supporter who didn't much care whether Vukradin was who he claimed to be; Sabbatha, who spent most of her time stealing glances at Brewbarry's imposing frame; and Old Hesp, a grizzled regular who remembered when Vukradin played to empty rooms and lamented that the Common Chord had become too polished, too expensive, too commercial. Each of them contributed to the studio fund, and Vukradin dutifully noted their names for the program credits. Old Hesp's parting words lingered — a reminder of where they'd come from, and why the new studio mattered.

By the third hour of the night, the party had positioned themselves near a sewer grate at the edge of the city, waiting. Two delivery men emerged from below, hauling nine crates, eight of them without an official Commission Harbor mark — smuggled goods, plain and simple. Eight of the crates were stacked neatly for Lim's collection, but the ninth was set apart on a ledge, and when one of the carriers paused to peer down into the dark before walking away, it was clear he was expecting something. What emerged was not a person. It was small, cat-sized, built of brass and leather, moving with the quiet, unhurried precision of a thing that had done this a thousand times before. It took the ninth crate and disappeared back into the dark.

The party split. Vukradin slipped into the sewers to follow the construct, while Valphine, Soma, and Brewbarry trailed one of the delivery men back into the city. Deep in the tunnels, something strange washed over Vukradin — a creeping disinterest, as though the path ahead simply wasn't worth his time. He found himself wandering into an unfamiliar stretch of sewer, where a group of hard-eyed men materialized from the shadows and demanded to know what he was doing in their territory. Vukradin, never one to panic, played the part of a hopelessly lost musician looking for Lim's Cafe, and the deception held just long enough for one of the men to squint at him and ask, with dawning excitement, whether he was really Vukradin.

Rsolk's entire demeanor shifted the moment he recognized the bard. He explained that his men had been disappearing in certain sections of the tunnels — driven out by creatures that looked like spiders but moved wrong, with webs that were the wrong color and texture entirely. The party recognized the creatures and wrong-looking webs from an earlier encounter. Vukradin offered the party's services to clear out the infestation. Rsolk supplied its location and, thrilled beyond measure, asked Vukradin to try to secure tickets to the Common Chord in return. Autographs were exchanged, and Vukradin was escorted back toward familiar ground.

Meanwhile, the others had followed Denvar — the delivery man who had paused at the ledge — to a late-night cook shop, where he ate a quiet breakfast alone before heading to a high-efficiency flat in the Bluelake District. The building's landlord, a man called Uncle Tom, greeted the unusual group with a string of bewildered observations about Tortles and Drow, insisting all the while that he ran an equal-opportunity establishment. Soma answered his questions about Tortle housing preferences with earnest patience, while Valphine and Brewbarry looked on. What the party learned was that Denvar was a quiet, unremarkable tenant — except that his lifestyle was noticeably more comfortable than a simple laborer's wages could explain.

Soma's familiar rat slipped through Denvar's belongings and found a collection of delivery chits, each one identical: nine crates, third hour, eight up and one down, with a client reference and a house mark but no names. The chits recorded a weekly settlement, and one remained unsettled — the most recent delivery, for which Denvar had not yet been paid. The party waited, and sure enough, Denvar woke and made his way to a stone wall on the edge of a building, where he pressed a hidden mechanism, retrieved a bag of coin, and walked away satisfied. The wall, it turned out, was a two-way dead drop — and the building behind it was a tavern called the House of a Thousand Faces.

The party sent the familiar through the opened mechanism to scout the interior, and the rat found its way to a windowless back office filled with papers, supplies, and one unmistakable symbol: the mark of the Harpers. The revelation was unsettling. The Harpers in Neverwinter were not the noble organization some might have hoped — they were players in a shadow game. The dead drop and the back office are Harper-run — but who is paying for that ninth crate, and why, the party has not established. Brewbarry, who had heard of the House of a Thousand Faces for years, walked through the front door with the quiet satisfaction of a man who had finally arrived. Inside, Brewbarry settled in among mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, and lush couches; the others remained gathered at the establishment, with a great deal still left to unravel.

## NPCs
### Petra
The half-elf owner of the Common Chord who negotiated a 500 gold piece likeness-rights deal with the party, still pending the upcoming performance. Old Hesp notes she has turned the venue into a profitable commercial operation.

### Lord Neverember
The Lord of Neverwinter, who reportedly has a new Drow advisor. He has an upcoming dinner engagement with the party, though the exact timing has not yet been confirmed.

### Wick
A tavern patron who becomes an enthusiastic supporter of Vukradin's music studio project. He does not know Lathander's Death but is passionate about patronage of the arts.

### Sabbatha
A tavern patron who contributes to Vukradin's studio fund and is notably impressed by Brewbarry's physique.

### Old Hesp
A long-time local who remembers Vukradin's humble beginnings playing to empty rooms. He dislikes the new, expensive and commercial atmosphere of the Common Chord and is enthusiastic about Vukradin's plans for a new studio.

### Ondrelle Kass
One of the locals at the Common Chord who was persuaded to donate to Vukradin's music studio fund.

### Lim
A Tortle chef who runs the Board Laid Bare restaurant and is the intended recipient of eight of the nine smuggled crates delivered through the sewers each night.

### Denvar
A delivery man involved in a smuggling operation, delivering nine crates via the sewers each night. He lives in a modest flat in the Bluelake District but appears to live beyond the means of a typical laborer. He collects payment via a hidden dead drop in a stone wall.

### Rsolk
A man who oversees a section of the Neverwinter sewers and is a fan of Vukradin's music. He is seeking help to deal with a spider infestation after several of his men disappeared, describing the creatures as having all the wrong colors and producing strange webs. He supplies the infestation's location and asks Vukradin to try to secure Common Chord tickets in return for the party clearing it.

### Uncle Tom
The landlord of the flats where Denvar resides. He is primarily motivated by timely rent payments and asks few questions of his tenants. He holds several misconceptions about non-human races such as Tortles and Drow, though he claims to run an equal-opportunity establishment.

## Locations
### The Common Chord
A popular performance venue in Neverwinter where the party negotiated a 500 gold piece likeness-rights deal, still pending the upcoming performance. Vukradin uses it as a base to solicit donations for his music studio fund from patrons.

### Neverwinter Sewers
A damp, dark network of tunnels beneath the city, subject to tidal flooding twice a day. Currently used for smuggling operations and managed in sections by territorial factions. Rsolk reported that several of his men had disappeared around strange, colorful spider creatures.

### Bluelake District
A generally lower-end district in Neverwinter where Denvar resides and where the House of a Thousand Faces is located.

### Uncle Tom's Flats
A high-efficiency apartment building where Denvar resides. The landlord, Uncle Tom, maintains a cash-up-front policy and asks few questions of his tenants.

### House of a Thousand Faces
A high-end tavern in the Bluelake District decorated with mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, and lush couches. It serves as a front for a Harper headquarters located in its basement, and was the site of a hidden dead drop used to pay Denvar.
