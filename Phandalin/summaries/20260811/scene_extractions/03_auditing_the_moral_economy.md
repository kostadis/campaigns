# Auditing the Moral Economy

## Scene summary (from gm-assist, verbatim)

#### At the Counting House, the party encounters Rimardo and Corrin, who audit their recent financial gains and approve a dividend from the Mountain's Toe Gold Mine.
- Aurelan Vance directs the party to a different department to handle their letter of credit, where they find Rimardo and Corrin acting as 'divine accountants' at a small folding table with a velvet rope — and a queue of zero. The sign reads "Rimardo and Mr. Corrin, Divine Accountants and Occasional Entertainers."
- Vukradin is delighted at the reunion: "Oh, hey guys! We know you. We have a letter for you! We were actually looking for you." — the party hands over the letter they have been carrying from the Falcon. Rimardo replies, "Yes, we've been waiting for you for some time."
- Corrin produces receipts from every place the party has ever been — Valphine spots one from the Bronze Sun, which is immediately disclaimed by its owner. As Soma observes: "He literally brought receipts."
- Rimardo audits the party's 'moral economy,' accusing them of laundering dirty money through better penmanship, staring pointedly at Vukradin — his gaze lingering on the UBT ledger. Vukradin objects: "It was drunk money."
- Corrin stamps the party's subcommittee charter as 'comedically compliant,' then stamps the receipt as 'approved,' and the pair promptly get up and leave — Brewbarry's review: "What the hell was that?" Soma's: "These guys are weird."
- The party receives a credit receipt for 200 gold pieces as a dividend from the Mountain's Toe Gold Mine, paid by the Neverwinter Commission — to Vukradin's surprise, who learns for the first time that the Commission owns the mine and the party merely holds a share ("Somehow I did not know that").
- Vukradin and the party debate the ethics of the mine gold, eventually labeling it 'fair trade gold' — the mine was worked legally and above board, the employees were paid, no coercion was involved, and the party contributed management labor ("the hardest and most valuable type of labor"). Soma settles the philosophy: "If you eat a potato, is that an ill-gotten potato because it came out of the ground?"
- Brewbarry magnanimously offers to give some of the gold to charity — his own share, it is clarified — since "Aurelan and Linene are gonna make me rich" with the bathrobe business anyway.
