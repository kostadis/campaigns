# Chapter 48: The Only Viable Shipper

Date: 2026-08-11

## Summary
The party made their way to the Counting House, Neverwinter's premier banking establishment, with Brewbarry leading the charge. He had spent the previous evening rehearsing a grand speech in his bathrobe, and he delivered it with full barbarian conviction — only to find himself addressing an empty room. When the banker Aurelan Vance finally appeared and mistook Brewbarry's authentic Uthgardtian hides for an elaborate costume, the barbarian's patience was immediately tested. Brewbarry presented Linene's letter of introduction and made his intentions clear: he needed funding and shipping arrangements for his bathrobe business, and he was not interested in lengthy financial deliberations.

Aurelan attempted to steer the conversation toward profit margins and alternative distributors, suggesting that House Margaster offered far cheaper shipping rates than the Lionshield Coster. Brewbarry was having none of it. He cut off every tangent with a pointed tap of his halberd and a glare that made the banker's calculations feel suddenly less important. By the end of the exchange, Aurelan had signed the loan paperwork, securing the capital needed to launch Brewbarry's bathrobe venture with Linene as the shipping agent. A wealthy client in the bank, dressed in silk imitations of Uthgardtian barbarian clothing, attempted to compliment Brewbarry on his "commitment to the bit," and was thoroughly ignored.

Aurelan then directed Vukradin to a separate department to collect a letter of credit from the Neverwinter Commission, and the party found themselves face to face with a familiar comedy duo: Rimardo and Corrin, now operating as "divine accountants and occasional entertainers" behind a small folding table with a velvet rope. Rimardo solemnly declared that he was there to audit the party's moral economy, while Corrin produced receipts from every establishment the party had ever visited. After a pointed accusation that Vukradin had merely laundered dirty money through better penmanship, Corrin stamped the party's subcommittee charter as "comedically compliant," then stamped the credit receipt as "approved," and the two promptly got up and left. The receipt turned out to be worth two hundred gold pieces — a dividend from the Mountain's Toe Gold Mine, paid by the Neverwinter Commission.

As the party debated the moral provenance of mine gold on their way out, they turned their attention to the growing shadow of House Margaster. They had already witnessed the house's agents engage in extortion and forgery, and now the same organization had been pointed to as the cheapest shipping network in the city — conveniently so, given that the Neverwinter Commission's interplanar displacement manifold — on which the Lionshield Coster's cheap distribution depended — had recently and mysteriously stopped working. Brewbarry, loyal to his tribe and to Linene, had no interest in switching distributors. He had every interest in paying House Margaster a visit and making his feelings known in the most direct way possible.

Before the party could set off, a man in his thirties wearing unfashionable clothes stepped forward and introduced himself as Perrin Alagondar. He explained, with barely contained anxiety, that his family had lost a precious heirloom to grave robbers a generation ago, and that word had reached him that the party was in possession of it — the Necklace of Fireballs. He spoke of the necklace as more than jewelry; it was a symbol of the true heirs of Neverwinter, a rallying point for those who opposed Lord Neverember's rule. Almost immediately, a shadowy figure emerged and produced notarized paperwork sealed by House Margaster, vouching for Perrin's lineage and suggesting the necklace be handed over at once.

Boney, the skeletal horse who had once served Lady Alagondar herself, was deeply skeptical. He noted that the necklace had only recently been returned to the world by Vukradin — recovered from Lady Alagondar's own barrow — meaning it lay in her tomb, not in any family's hands, when Perrin's family supposedly lost it to grave robbers. Perrin's account of how it had been lost contained troubling inconsistencies. The party refused to surrender the necklace on the spot, and Brewbarry settled the matter by grabbing the House Margaster fixer firmly by the scruff and announcing that they would all be heading to House Margaster's offices together to sort this out properly. The fixer, after a brief and unsuccessful attempt to wriggle free, accepted his fate.

On the way to House Margaster, the party passed a building near the docks bearing a civic notice nailed to its door. It declared, by order of Lord Neverember, that the establishment's services for instantaneous shipping had been closed until further notice. The party recognized it immediately as the former home of the interplanar displacement device. Perrin launched into a tirade suggesting the Neverwinter Commission had deliberately shut it down to tighten their grip on trade, while the party quietly noted how conveniently the closure had benefited House Margaster's traditional caravan business.

The party arrived at the House Margaster Notary, a clean and well-organized establishment that served as the legal face of the house's operations. The notary presented a thick tome titled the Genealogy of the Alagondars, claiming it had been verified against forgeries, doppelgangers and miscellaneous magic through the "threefold rule" — coordinating between three different sources of conflicting evidence with different provenance — and that Perrin was indeed a rightful descendant of Lady Alagondar. House Margaster's agent then offered to hold the necklace in their warehouses for safekeeping until the matter could be fully resolved — a form of "title insurance," as Vukradin named it on the spot, to the notary's visible and acquisitive interest. The party saw through the scheme immediately and flatly refused.

At the notary the party declared that Perrin would need to produce three independent witnesses — none of them affiliated with House Margaster — before any necklace changed hands.

The party then made their way to the Margaster Logistics Operations at the Eastern Quay, a bustling and impressively well-run shipping hub near the docks. Valphine quickly observed that the facility was stocked with Lionshield Coster goods — House Margaster had effectively become the only game in town for moving cargo, charging whatever rates they pleased now that the displacement device was gone. Cullen Sharpe, the smooth-talking house representative the party had encountered before, greeted them warmly and with practiced charm. He denied any involvement in the device's failure but let slip that a gnome had been hired to work on it — and that the gnome's tinkering had preceded the device's final breakdown.

Vukradin pressed Cullen for the gnome's name, and after some persuasion, Cullen agreed to look into it and share the information the following day. Brewbarry, for his part, attempted to make his displeasure known in no uncertain terms, but Cullen Sharpe remained thoroughly unintimidated, treating the barbarian's barely veiled threats as the charming eccentricities of a colorful new business contact. When House Margaster renewed its offer to take the necklace into warehouse safekeeping, Brewbarry made the party's position crystal clear by crumpling the notarized paperwork into a ball and tossing it over his shoulder; the fixer's hand moved briefly toward his weapon before he thought better of it. Perrin agreed to the three-witness condition, and a meeting was arranged for after the party's upcoming benefit concert at the Spire of the Morninglord, a venue that Valphine noted with some satisfaction would require Perrin to set foot inside a temple. The party extended an invitation to that concert, which Cullen graciously accepted as a representative of his house. As the evening drew to a close, the party found themselves with a growing list of appointments, a pocketful of mine dividends, and the unsettling realization that in Neverwinter, House Margaster seemed to have its fingers in nearly everything — and that finding out who did not answer to them might be the most important task of all.

## NPCs
### Aurelan Vance
A banker at the Counting House who handles loans. He is meticulous with calculations and profit margins, initially mistakes Brewbarry's authentic barbarian attire for a costume, and is easily cowed by Brewbarry's aggressive negotiation style. He suggests House Margaster as a cheaper shipping alternative but ultimately signs the loan paperwork for Brewbarry's business.

### Linene Graywind
Brewbarry's wealth management consultant and Lionshield Coster franchise representative, whose letter of introduction to the Counting House helped secure funding for Brewbarry's bathrobe business. Her shipping operation has been severely disrupted by the failure of the interplanar displacement device.

### Rimardo
A self-styled 'divine accountant' and occasional entertainer who audits the party's moral economy at the Counting House, reviewing their financial receipts with a critical eye and accusing them of laundering dirty money through better penmanship.

### Corrin
Rimardo's partner, an accountant and occasional entertainer who produces receipts from every location the party has visited and stamps official documents, including stamping the party's subcommittee charter as 'comedically compliant.'

### Perrin Alagondar
A man in his 30s wearing unfashionable clothes who claims to be a descendant of Lady Alagondar. He views the Necklace of Fireballs as a political symbol to rally opposition against Lord Neverember's rule, and is closely assisted by House Margaster agents. Boney notes holes in his story about how the necklace was lost.

### The Fixer
An agent of House Margaster who emerges with notarized paperwork to vouch for Perrin's lineage and attempts to steer the party toward surrendering the necklace to House Margaster for safekeeping. He briefly reaches for his weapon when Brewbarry crumples his paperwork but thinks better of it.

### Boney
A skeletal horse formerly associated with Lady Alagondar who travels with the party. He is obsessed with royal lineages and successions, having studied them from gravestones while interred in a tomb. He expresses skepticism about Perrin's claim, noting that the necklace was only recently returned to the world — recovered by Vukradin from Lady Alagondar's own barrow, not from any family's keeping — and that Perrin's story therefore has holes.

### The Notary
An official at the House Margaster-controlled notary house who uses the 'Genealogy of the Alagondars' and the 'threefold rule' to validate Perrin's lineage claim. He is enthusiastic about the concept of 'title insurance' as a potential future product.

### Cullen Sharpe
A smooth-talking, well-dressed agent of House Margaster who acts as a business representative and fixer. He greets the party warmly at the Eastern Quay logistics hub, denies Margaster involvement in the broken shipping device, but admits a gnome was hired to work on it. He agrees to attend the party's benefit concert and to look into the gnome's identity.

A clean and well-organized establishment in Neverwinter that serves as a front for House Margaster, used to notarize documents and legitimize their business ventures through official-looking paperwork.

A highly organized and efficient shipping hub near the Neverwinter docks. It serves as the primary base for House Margaster's caravan-based distribution network and is stocked with Lionshield Coster goods.
