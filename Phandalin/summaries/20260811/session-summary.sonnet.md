# Chapter 48: The Only Viable Shipper

Date: 2026-08-11

## Summary
The party made their way to the Counting House, Neverwinter's premier banking establishment, with Brewbarry leading the charge. He had spent the previous evening rehearsing a grand speech in his bathrobe for "like 15" minutes, and he delivered it with full barbarian conviction — only to find himself addressing an empty room, prompting Vukradin to needle him with an offer of persuasion tips ("I've got a silvery tongue"). When the banker Aurelan Vance finally appeared and mistook Brewbarry's authentic Uthgardtian hides for an elaborate cosplay costume ("What a genuine costume!"), the barbarian's patience was immediately tested. Brewbarry presented Linene's letter of introduction and made his intentions clear: he needed funding and shipping arrangements for his bathrobe business, framing it grandly as bringing "roughness to all the good people of the Forgotten Realms" through bathrobes — and he was not interested in lengthy financial deliberations.

Aurelan attempted to steer the conversation toward profit margins and alternative distributors, suggesting that House Margaster offered far cheaper shipping rates than the Lionshield Coster. Brewbarry was having none of it, repeatedly cutting him off ("Let's not get sidetracked here" / "How remiss you're gonna be if you continue down this path, my friend") and tapping his halberd meaningfully in his other hand. By the end of the exchange — helped along by a successful intimidation roll that David noted "clearly has advantage" given Brewbarry towers over the man — Aurelan had signed the loan paperwork, securing significant capital to launch Brewbarry's bathrobe venture with Linene as the shipping agent. A wealthy client in the bank, dressed in silk imitations of Uthgardtian barbarian clothing, tried to touch Brewbarry's actual hides and attempted to compliment him on his "commitment to the bit" ("You are really into the bit. I am very impressed"), even mimicking his posture awkwardly; Brewbarry simply ignored him.

Aurelan then directed Vukradin to a separate department to collect a letter of credit from the Neverwinter Commission — apologizing that loans and fulfillment are handled by different departments, prompting a round of jokes about "Chinese walls," "planar barriers," and "firewalls" between them — and the party found themselves face to face with a familiar comedy duo: Rimardo and Corrin, now operating as "Divine Accountants and Occasional Entertainers" behind a small folding table with a velvet rope and a queue sign. Rimardo solemnly declared "I'm here to audit your moral economy," while Corrin produced receipts from every establishment the party had ever visited, including one Valphine noted was from the Bronze Sun (promptly disclaimed). After the pointed line — "You have not refused dirty money. You have merely laundered it through paperwork with better penmanship" — delivered while staring at the UBT, Corrin stamped the party's subcommittee charter as "comedically compliant," then stamped the credit receipt "approved," and the two promptly got up and left without further explanation ("These guys are weird" / "I'm so glad that we ran into them"). The receipt turned out to be worth two hundred gold pieces — a dividend from the Mountain's Toe Gold Mine, paid out by the Neverwinter Commission, which the party clarified actually owns the mine (the party merely holds a share).

As the party debated the moral provenance of mine gold on their way out — settling, after a lengthy back-and-forth about labor, management, and potatoes pulled from the ground, on recording it as "Fair Trade Gold" — they turned their attention to the growing shadow of House Margaster. They had already witnessed the house's agents engage in extortion and forgery (against Lord Cassian, regarding the returned statue/brooch), and now the same organization had been pointed to as the cheapest shipping network in the city — conveniently so, given that the Neverwinter Commission's interplanar displacement manifold, on which the Lionshield Coster's cheap distribution depended, had recently and mysteriously stopped working. Brewbarry, loyal to his tribe and to Linene, had no interest in switching distributors ("They're only loyal to money. I'm not"). He had every interest in paying House Margaster a visit and making his feelings known directly: "I'm a barbarian, not a thief... I let my enemy know that I am there. And that they will suffer my wrath." Before setting off, Gary had Brewbarry add elven chainmail to his inventory in anticipation of trouble.

Before the party could set off, a man in his thirties in decent but unfashionable clothes — visibly anxious, having "read the letter about 40 times" — stepped forward and introduced himself as Perrin Alagondar. He explained that his family had lost a precious heirloom to grave robbers a generation ago, that they'd "never stopped looking," and that word had reached him that the party was in possession of it — the Necklace of Fireballs. He spoke of the necklace as more than jewelry, growing visibly animated ("you notice a fire in his eye") as he explained it was a symbol of the true, rightful heirs of Neverwinter against "imposter Neverember," insisting, when pressed, that he wasn't trying to start a revolution — "this is just a symbol." Almost immediately, a shadowy figure emerged and produced notarized paperwork sealed by House Margaster, vouching for Perrin's lineage and suggesting the necklace be handed over at once — prompting Vukradin's dry observation, "either he just walks around with this, or..."

Boney, the skeletal horse who had once served Lady Alagondar herself, was deeply skeptical, cross-examining Perrin directly ("who exactly did this necklace belong to?"). Boney noted that he himself had "spent some time in a tomb" and knew the necklace's provenance — and that Perrin's account contained troubling holes, since the necklace had only recently been recovered by Vukradin and returned to circulation. Boney's obsession with genealogy, gleaned from studying gravestones during his centuries "recently unalived" in the barrow, made him uniquely qualified to spot the gaps. The party refused to surrender the necklace on the spot, and Brewbarry settled the matter by grabbing the House Margaster fixer by the neck ("No, you're coming") and announcing they would all be heading to House Margaster's offices together — Vukradin insisting, tongue-in-cheek, "it's not a kidnapping!... we're just supporting him." The fixer, after a brief and unsuccessful attempt to wriggle free, accepted his fate, while onlookers mistook the whole barbarian ensemble for committed cosplay.

On the way to House Margaster, the party passed a shuttered building near the docks bearing a civic notice nailed to the door, declaring — by order of Lord Neverember — that the services for instantaneous shipping had been closed until further notice. The party recognized it immediately as the former home of the interplanar displacement device. Perrin launched into a tirade suggesting the Neverwinter Commission had deliberately shut it down to tighten its grip on trade, while the party quietly noted (Wade: "How convenient for House Margaster, isn't it?") how conveniently the closure had benefited House Margaster's traditional caravan business — a coincidence sharpened by the reminder that the party is dining with Lord Neverember himself and had just been paid by the Commission.

The party arrived at the House Margaster Notary, a clean and well-organized establishment that served as the legal face of the house's operations — Valphine's investigation (rolling a 21) confirmed it lacked any overt "Margaster demonic" trappings; the forgeries, evidently, all happen off-site. The notary presented a thick tome titled the *Genealogy of the Alagondars*, claiming it had been verified against forgeries, doppelgangers, and miscellaneous magic through the "threefold rule" — coordinating between three different sources of conflicting evidence with different provenance — and that Perrin was indeed a rightful descendant of Lady Alagondar. When Vukradin pressed on how they could verify Perrin was really who he claimed, the notary's agent floated the idea of "title insurance" — offering to hold the necklace in layaway at House Margaster's warehouses for safekeeping, an idea so novel the agent's eyes visibly lit up at the scam potential, and Vukradin openly egged him on about extended warranties. The party saw through the scheme immediately and flatly refused: "Not happening... until you can produce 3 independent, independent from House Margaster, witnesses as to who you really are."

Brewbarry made his position crystal clear by crumpling the notarized paperwork into a ball and tossing it over his shoulder ("Is that making it crystal clear for you, Perrin? Whoever you are"). The fixer's hand moved briefly toward his weapon before Cullen Sharpe's look stopped him — "Wise decision," Brewbarry noted, "now don't make me... you eat that paper." The party declared that Perrin would need to produce three independent witnesses, none affiliated with House Margaster, before any necklace changed hands. Perrin agreed, and a meeting was arranged for after the party's upcoming benefit concert at the Spire of the Morninglord — a venue Valphine specifically chose "to see him set foot within the temple," and Perrin, professing to be a believer in the Morninglord himself, happily agreed.

The party then made their way to the Margaster Logistics Operations at the Eastern Quay, a bustling and impressively well-run shipping hub near the docks. Valphine's investigation (rolling 21) quickly identified the operation was stocked with Lionshield Coster-labeled goods — confirming that House Margaster wasn't stealing goods, just charging to move them via old-fashioned caravans with armed guards, now that Lionshield's cheap displacement pipeline was gone. Gary summed it up: "It sounds like a very expensive way to move goods" — to which Kostadis replied, "and profitable," since Margaster is now "the only game in town." Cullen Sharpe, the smooth-talking house representative the party had encountered before (previously involved in extorting Lord Cassian), greeted them warmly, delighted to see "Mr. Vukradin" and "Mr. Perrin" together, and pitched Margaster's services as solving exactly the kind of problem Ser Kaelen had once flagged.

Brewbarry attempted to intimidate Cullen over the shipping device's failure, rolling only a 6 and getting nowhere on the check — though Cullen remained amused rather than hostile, appreciating rather than fearing the barbarian's theatrics ("The robe, man, the robe just makes you look so soft"). Cullen denied any House Margaster involvement in the device's failure but let slip that a gnome had been hired to fix or improve the device, and that its breakdown followed the gnome's attempted repairs. Vukradin pressed for the gnome's name, and Cullen, deflecting, suggested the information could be exchanged if the party came back the next day or entered into a business arrangement — at which point Brewbarry (rolling a natural 1 on Deception, "poly houle") tried to dangle the idea of getting the manifold working again as a future business opportunity, a lie so poorly sold that Cullen simply read it as barbarian naivety rather than deception ("perfect cover," Stéphane joked). The party extended an invitation to the benefit concert at the Spire of the Morninglord, which Cullen graciously accepted as a representative of his house, though he couldn't promise the whole house would attend. As the evening drew to a close, the party found themselves with a growing list of appointments, a pocketful of mine dividends, and Stéphane's parting resolution for next session: to find out who in Neverwinter is *not* in House Margaster's pocket.

## NPCs
### Aurelan Vance
A banker at the Counting House who handles loans. He is meticulous with calculations and profit margins (producing illegible figures on demand), initially mistakes Brewbarry's authentic barbarian attire for a costume ("I think your costume is wonderful, and I really admire the cosplaying of it"), and is easily cowed by Brewbarry's aggressive negotiation style. He suggests House Margaster as a cheaper shipping alternative but ultimately signs the loan paperwork for Brewbarry's business, and personally escorts Vukradin to introduce him to Rimardo and Corrin out of professional courtesy.

### Linene Graywind
Brewbarry's wealth management consultant and Lionshield Coster franchise representative, whose letter of introduction to the Counting House helped secure funding for Brewbarry's bathrobe business. Her shipping operation has been severely disrupted by the failure of the interplanar displacement device, forcing her back to "the old-fashioned way" of moving goods.

### Rimardo
A self-styled "divine accountant" and occasional entertainer who audits the party's moral economy at the Counting House, flipping through a book/ledger and reviewing their financial receipts with a critical eye, accusing them of laundering dirty money "through paperwork with better penmanship."

### Corrin
Rimardo's partner, an accountant and occasional entertainer who silently produces receipts from every location the party has visited (including one from the Bronze Sun) and stamps official documents — the party's subcommittee charter "comedically compliant" and the mine credit receipt "approved" — before abruptly leaving with Rimardo.

### Perrin Alagondar
A man in his 30s wearing decent but unfashionable clothes who claims to be a descendant of Lady Alagondar. Anxious and visibly rehearsed ("I've read the letter about 40 times"), he views the Necklace of Fireballs as a political symbol to rally opposition against Lord Neverember's "dictatorial high-taxation regime," while insisting he isn't fomenting revolution. He is closely assisted by House Margaster agents, and Boney notes clear holes in his account of how the necklace was supposedly lost, given its true, more recent history.

### The Fixer
An unnamed agent of House Margaster who emerges from the shadows with notarized paperwork to vouch for Perrin's lineage and attempts to steer the party toward surrendering the necklace to House Margaster for safekeeping. He briefly tries to wriggle out of Brewbarry's grip and later reaches for his weapon when Brewbarry crumples his paperwork, but backs down at a look from Cullen Sharpe.

### Boney
A skeletal horse formerly associated with Lady Alagondar who travels with the party. He is obsessed with royal lineages and successions, having studied them from gravestones ("here lies X, married to Y") while interred in a tomb for centuries — a period he refers to, deadpan, as being "recently unalived." He directly cross-examines Perrin and expresses clear skepticism about his claim, noting that the necklace was only recently returned to the world by Vukradin, recovered from Lady Alagondar's own barrow rather than any surviving family's keeping.

### The Notary
An official at the House Margaster-controlled notary house who uses the *Genealogy of the Alagondars* and the "threefold rule" (three independent, conflicting-provenance sources) to validate Perrin's lineage claim. He is visibly struck by the concept of "title insurance" as a potential future Margaster product, floated half-jokingly by Vukradin.

### Cullen Sharpe
A smooth-talking, well-dressed agent of House Margaster — previously encountered extorting Lord Cassian's brother — who acts as a business representative and fixer. He greets the party warmly by name at the Eastern Quay logistics hub, remains unbothered and even amused by Brewbarry's attempted intimidation, denies Margaster's involvement in the broken shipping device, but admits a gnome was hired to work on it shortly before it failed. He agrees to look into the gnome's identity by the next day and to attend the party's benefit concert as a House representative.

A clean and well-organized establishment in Neverwinter that serves as a front for House Margaster, used to notarize documents (including the *Genealogy of the Alagondars*) and legitimize their business ventures through official-looking paperwork — with no overt signs of the house's criminal activity on the premises.

### Shuttered Shipping Hub
A building near the Neverwinter docks bearing a civic notice declaring it closed by order of Lord Neverember, its "services provided for instantaneous shipping" halted "until further notice." It formerly housed the interplanar displacement device.

### Margaster Logistics Operations, Eastern Quay
A highly organized and efficient shipping hub near the Neverwinter docks, confirmed to be stocked with Lionshield Coster-labeled goods. It serves as the primary base for House Margaster's caravan-based (armed-guard) distribution network, now effectively the only viable shipper in the city.

### Spire of the Morninglord
The grand temple of Lathander in Neverwinter where the party plans to perform their benefit concert and subsequently meet with Perrin Alagondar to settle the necklace dispute — a meeting spot Valphine chose specifically to force Perrin to "set foot within the temple."
