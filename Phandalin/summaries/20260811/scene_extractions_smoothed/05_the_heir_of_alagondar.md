# The Heir of Alagondar

## Scene summary (from gm-assist, verbatim)

#### A man named Perrin Alagondar approaches the party on the street claiming to be the rightful heir to the Necklace of Fireballs, accompanied by a mysterious agent of House Margaster.
- Perrin Alagondar introduces himself, explaining that his family lost the necklace to grave robbers a generation ago and that he has been waiting for the party for hours — "Forgive me. You're the ones from Phandalin? … The recovery office wrote to me. … I've read the letter about 40 times."
- The party demands verification — Soma: "Can you prove its lineage?" Valphine: "Or at least start with your passport."
- At the word "lineage," a fire lights in Perrin's eye (the table calls for a dramatic camera zoom — "and it adds 15 pounds") and he reveals his belief that the necklace is a symbol of those who reject the current rule of Lord Neverember and could rally political opposition — the Alagondars were "the first family of Neverwinter" before "that imposter Neverember." Brewbarry yawns very loudly.
- Perrin hastily walks the revolution back — "I'm not going to start a revolution, no, no, this is a symbol" — and Soma lands the obvious objection: "If this necklace is the symbol that you say it is, I'd imagine a lot of people would lie to claim it's theirs."
- A mysterious fixer emerges from the shadows and produces paperwork sealed by House Margaster that supposedly validates Perrin's claim to the necklace — offering, if the party "really insists on making a fuss," a visit to House Margaster's official offices to see the full lineage.
- The party becomes suspicious of House Margaster's sudden involvement in the inheritance claim — "House Margaster is the one vouching for Alagondar. Well, that's a twist."
- The GM notes the party has become something of a local legend — a Goliath in genuine barbarian hides, a tortle with the staff of Meril, and a drow cleric whose eyes glow with Lathander's blessing are "not exactly inconspicuous." Brewbarry: "Maybe we should have office hours, you know, and people can get a ticket."
- Brewbarry grabs the fixer by the scruff and insists they all head to House Margaster's offices together to settle the matter — "No, you're coming." Vukradin hastens to clarify: "It's not a kidnapping! … We don't want them to fall down." After a few moments of trying to wriggle free, the fixer gives up.
- Boney, the skeletal horse, expresses skepticism regarding Perrin's story, noting potential holes in the timeline of the necklace's disappearance: the necklace was only recently returned to the world by Vukradin, who recovered it from Lady Alagondar's own barrow — so it lay in her tomb, not in any family's hands, when Perrin's family supposedly lost it. Boney presses Perrin directly: "You are a member of the Alagondars? … And who exactly did this necklace belong to?" — and Perrin visibly doesn't know all the facts.
- Boney is nonetheless careful in his conclusions: "I simply can point out that there are holes in the story. I can't really say whether he is or is not" — the necklace "belonged to Lady Alagondar, and if he indeed is a member of the Alagondar clan, it is entirely possible that he has a rightful claim to it."
- The party discovers Boney is obsessed with noble lineages and rights of succession, researched over centuries from the gravestones in his tomb — and that he prefers the term "recently unalived": "I mean, dead is a relative term" (paraphrase; line garbled).
- Before setting off, Brewbarry dons elven chainmail in place of his scale mail at Valphine's suggestion — "we might end up in some combat here."
