# Chapter 50 The Broads of the Common Chord and the Board Laid Bare

Date: 2026-08-25

## Summary
The party's departure from the Spire of the Morninglord was interrupted by the arrival of Aurelan Vance, a representative of the Counting House who had been inspired by Valphine's recent sermon to take action. Breathless from rushing over from the bank, Aurelan — who invited Brewbarry to call him Ori — shared what he had uncovered about a gnome named Bimble. Bimble had been carried on a commission maintenance roll for eleven years, but payments had stopped abruptly seven tendays ago with no severance, no final reconciliation, and no forwarding instructions. Aurelan concluded with the quiet certainty of a man who only knew one kind of question: find who feeds the man, and you have found the man.

As the party made their way through the Protector's Enclave, a passerby stopped to stare at Boney, Brewbarry's skeletal horse, still wearing Lady Alagondar's tackle. The man was convinced he had seen such a horse before, puzzling over the memory with growing agitation — only to eventually realize, with some embarrassment, that he had simply seen Boney earlier that same day outside the service where Valphine had delivered his sermon. The party moved on, leaving the man to his confusion.

Their destination was The Board Laid Bare, a modest restaurant in the Blue Lake District that Soma had long considered a home away from home. At the door stood Bellows, a warforged host of mechanical precision and absolute humorlessness, who greeted Soma by informing her she had been absent for exactly seventy-three days. He escorted the party to their tables with the same detached efficiency he applied to everything, presenting a handwritten menu of fresh fish, vegetables, potatoes, and soups at prices that seemed impossibly low given the city's rampant inflation.

Before the party could settle in, Lim — the tortle chef and proprietor — emerged from the kitchen carrying a tray of food she had already decided they wanted, set it down, and sat with them. She caught up with Soma warmly, mentioning that the Tower District had grown quieter in recent weeks, though not safer, and that three weeks prior, people dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates had entered the Cloak Tower by the basement stairs and spent the day there, emerging with nothing. While Soma and Lim reconnected, Brewbarry and Valphine turned their attention to Bellows, pressing him about the restaurant's suspiciously affordable supply chain.

Bellows answered with characteristic precision. He explained that deliveries arrived nightly between the third and fourth hour after midnight via a rear service lane connecting to the city's aqueducts, brought by two men, sometimes three, never the same two — six or seven couriers oscillating through the rotation over the last thirty days. He noted, with complete indifference, that nine crates were delivered each evening but only eight were ever brought inside. The ninth was left in the lane and was gone by morning — never billed to the restaurant, never explained, and never questioned.

Brewbarry followed Soma into the kitchen under the pretense of culinary curiosity and confronted Lim directly about her suppliers. Lim was reluctant, calling it a trade secret, but eventually admitted that her goods were moved through the sewer system beneath the Blue Lake District. She had made a deal with the suppliers after they approached her at the market, drawn in by the promise of affordable goods at a time when Lord Neverember's rising taxes were crushing local businesses. She asked Soma, with quiet desperation, not to scare them away. Soma's disappointment was plain, and Lim's hurt response cut deep: "You've changed. I remember a different Soma."

Valphine, ever sharp, pieced together what the others had gathered. A third trade route existed — one that had outlived the collapse of the regional distribution manifold, paid no harbor authority, and moved goods at the third hour after midnight through service lanes and aqueduct steps. The party suspected that Bimble, the missing gnome, was surviving by intercepting the mysterious ninth crate each night. With hours to spare before a midnight stakeout could be arranged, the party decided to make use of the time and headed toward the Common Chord.

The walk through the city was unsettling in small ways. A woman in the Blue Lake District was seen locking a door she had already locked. A wizard's apprentice tried to sell the party authentic many-starred cloak thread. Citizens argued loudly in the streets that rents had doubled. And then, posted on a wall near the Common Chord, the party spotted a bill advertising a performance by Lathander's Death — in two nights — at the very venue they were approaching. No one in the band had agreed to it.

The Common Chord had transformed entirely since Vukradin's early days playing to sparse crowds of ten or fifteen. It now boasted a six-week reservation list and an atmosphere of fashionable excitement, with fans cosplaying as Vukradin and chanting the band's name. Brewbarry immediately began working the crowd, taking pre-orders and talking up the band's reputation, while Valphine's dragon scale armor drew wide-eyed stares from every corner of the room.

Petra, the half-elf proprietor who had given Vukradin his first gig when no one else would, emerged to greet them with the gleaming eyes of someone who had just watched her investment pay off. She admitted she had been booking a cover band under the band's name, always telling audiences it wasn't the real Vukradin — but profiting handsomely from the association all the same. With Brewbarry looming behind him, Vukradin valued the past infringement at five hundred gold pieces and then called it square: one exclusive performance in two nights, in exchange for a perpetual licence on his image and likeness — Petra keeps her cover band, provided she keeps telling audiences it is one. No coin changed hands on that. He did squeeze an additional twenty-five gold pieces out of Petra on the spot to sign a commemorative plaque she had installed at the table where he supposedly composed his music — a table that was, in fact, where Brewbarry used to sit. She paid immediately, pupils practically replaced by coins.

The band performed a teaser set that night, and when Valphine's contribution came out discordant and off-key, the crowd erupted — the screeching imperfection perfectly echoing Vukradin's beloved early avant-garde style. Riding the energy of the room, the party launched into a second performance with a sharper purpose. The song was witty and pointed, its lyrics skewering House Margaster's predatory grip on the city's trade routes and the suffering it had caused ordinary people. The crowd picked it up eagerly, humming and repeating the tune as the night wore on, the seeds of dissent planted quietly but firmly in the hearts of Neverwinter's citizens.

The party settled in afterward, the Common Chord's food and drink far superior to anything Brewbarry remembered from his early visits. The evening had been productive in ways that went beyond coin — they had uncovered a smuggling network beneath the city, identified a likely hiding place for the missing gnome, and turned a room full of strangers into unwitting allies against House Margaster, all before midnight had even arrived.

A central district of Neverwinter where the party encounters a confused passerby who claims to recognize Boney, and where citizens are heard complaining about doubled rents.

### Blue Lake District
An area of Neverwinter where many businesses appear shut. The party observes strange behavior from citizens, encounters a wizard's apprentice selling many-starred cloak thread, and finds The Board Laid Bare.

### The Board Laid Bare
A local restaurant in the Tower District of Neverwinter known for its affordable food and tortle cuisine, run by the chef Lim and hosted by the warforged Bellows. It features a small kitchen and a rear service lane connecting to the city aqueducts, through which smuggled goods are delivered nightly.

### Tower District
An area of Neverwinter that has recently become quieter but not necessarily safer. Three weeks prior, people dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates were seen entering the Cloak Tower by the basement stairs.

### Cloak Tower
A notable structure in the Tower District where suspicious individuals dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates were recently seen entering the basement and spending the day.

### Blue Lake District Sewers
An underground transit network used by smugglers to move goods into Neverwinter without paying harbor authority fees or taxes. Lim's suppliers use this route for nightly deliveries.

### The Common Chord
A tavern and music venue in Neverwinter that has transformed from a struggling dive bar into a trendy, high-end establishment with a six-week reservation list. It profits off Vukradin's reputation, featuring a commemorative plaque at the table where he supposedly composed his music — actually the table where Brewbarry used to sit.

## NPCs
### Aurelan Vance
A representative of the Counting House who has become an informant for Brewbarry. Inspired by Valphine's recent sermon, he dug into suspicious paperwork regarding the missing gnome Bimble and rushed to share his findings with the party.

### Bimble
A gnome who was on a commission maintenance roll for 11 years until payments abruptly stopped seven tendays ago with no severance or forwarding instructions. He has not banked, borrowed, or left the city, suggesting someone is keeping him — or that he is surviving by other means, such as stealing the mysterious ninth crate.

### Boney
Brewbarry's skeletal horse, currently wearing Lady Alagondar's tackle, who attracts confused attention from a passerby in the Protector's Enclave.

### Bellows
A precise and humorless warforged host at The Board Laid Bare. He tracks guest attendance with mathematical accuracy and observed that nine crates are delivered nightly but only eight are brought inside, with the ninth disappearing by morning.

### Lim
A tortle chef and proprietor of The Board Laid Bare, and an old friend of Soma's. She values word-of-mouth business and has maintained a secret supply chain through smugglers to keep her prices affordable, justifying it as a necessity under Lord Neverember's rising taxes.

### Zeleen
A were-rat with whom the party has a prior business relationship, mentioned as a potential lead into the sewer networks beneath Neverwinter.

### Petra
A half-elf proprietor of the Common Chord who gave Vukradin his start when no one else would book him. She has since capitalized on his growing fame by using a cover band and memorabilia to attract wealthy patrons, and settled the resulting debt not in coin but by taking a perpetual licence on Vukradin's image and likeness — in exchange for one exclusive performance in two nights and a 50% share of her gross while the band plays.

## Items
### Lady Alagondar's Tackle
The riding gear currently worn by Boney, Brewbarry's skeletal horse. A passerby in the Protector's Enclave notices the tackle and uses it to identify the horse as one he had seen earlier that day.

### The Ninth Crate
A mysterious wooden, rope-bound crate that is delivered nightly to the rear of The Board Laid Bare along with eight others, but is never brought inside. It disappears by morning and is not billed to the restaurant. The party suspects it is being used to feed someone in hiding, possibly Bimble.

### Many-Starred Cloak Thread
Decorative thread sold by a wizard's apprentice in the Blue Lake District, claimed to be authentic material from the Many-Starred Cloaks.

### Vukradin's Plaque
A commemorative plaque placed on a polished table at the Common Chord, marking it as the spot where Vukradin created his music. Petra paid Vukradin 25 gold pieces on the spot to sign it.
