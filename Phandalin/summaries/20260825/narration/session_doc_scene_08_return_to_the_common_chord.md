The Common Chord. When I played here, it was the kind of place that didn't ask too many questions and paid in actual coin. The room held forty comfortably, and I never once made the room prove it. Three consecutive nights on that stage. I give my bandmates the honest count as we come up the street.

"For sure. I think it's more like 10 to 15."

Eleven, in truth. Eleven souls a night.

"But always Brewbarry."

"That was the idea," Soma says.

"That's more than one, for sure."

And in those days I had a policy, and I stand by it still: the owner, a half-elf named Petra, paid me, and I handed the fee back to every patron who could not rate the show a ten out of ten. A performance you cannot call perfect is a performance you should not be charged for. That is fair trade. "Yeah, well, they were not fully satisfied." I say it plainly, without shame. "I kept Brewbarry's money. It was very nice of him to give me a 10 out of 10." Petra came to understand that the model was not working.

"No shit."

She told me she really enjoyed the music. She did not book me again. And that, more or less, is how the adventuring career began. "Right. Sounds right."

Now the Common Chord has a six-week reservation list, and it is the place to be. There is a line. There is a man with a reservations book. There is a sign outside advertising a performance I never agreed to give.

"Say hi to Petra!"

I check for the mob, for the autograph seekers. Not mobbed, exactly. Nobody at the door recognizes me, which proves nothing except that I was ahead of my time, and I say as much. We do not announce the great Vukradin. "Oh, no, we're Lathander's Death." And to the band, quieter: "Yes, I'm sure. And I'm sure Petra's been taking advantage of that."

We make our impressions in the doorway. Valphine wears the white dragon plate, because of course she does, and people point at her from every corner of the room and talk behind their hands. Brewbarry is an impression all by himself. And people look at me the longest, squinting, as if they almost recognize the face and cannot pay for the name.

"So, Brewbarry, you need to set up your shop," Valphine says.

"Sell some merchandise."

"This is your opportunity," I tell him. The ensemble lifts its own.

"I don't have merchandise. The only merchandise I have is the one I'm wearing. I'm trying to set things up."

"A, B, C."

"Always be closing," Soma finishes.

"Sorry, sorry guys, I'm still learning the ropes here." Then his eyes come up. "Okay, I'll try to get some pre-orders for the Brewbarry."

"There you go, now you're thinking!"

"Start a Kickstarter campaign, you know," Soma offers.

At the podium I lead with the truth. "We are here to perform!" The reservations man does not mean to be rude, he says, but this is a nice crowd, and he does not think we belong to it. He never finishes the sentence, because near the bar several barbarians cosplaying as me begin to chant. As *me*. In braids and borrowed finery, in my venue, chanting. So I put the whole instrument behind it.

"We're Lathander's Death! We're here to perform!"

The room turns as one body. There is screaming. The girls are screaming, which is right and proper and long overdue.

The staff still balk at the stage, as if the stage were not the entire point of a stage. "Why not?"

"Marching on stage. Who's stopping us?" Valphine says.

"Who wants to stop us?" Brewbarry asks.

"Who's gonna stop Brewbarry?" Soma answers.

Nobody. He shoulders a path through the crush and the question settles itself, and we are up under the lights. Brewbarry wants the song about Lord Neverwinter. I count us in and give the people art.

"…winter where the cold winds blow. A hallowed steam rises soft and slow from Lim's own pot, a viscous grace warming this hallowed, frozen place. A shroud of labor simmered long. Better?"

"That's not the one about Lord Neverwinter."

"The glory of the soup."

"There's one called Vault of Silver Lies. That's the one you want."

"Not the next one?"

With all the commotion, Petra finally shows up, announcing to everyone in earshot that she discovered this guy. She looks prosperous. The room looks prosperous. I think of the sign outside.

"I saw that we're on in 2 nights."

"Yes. An exclusive one-night performance."

"How much does it pay?"

Beside me Valphine murmurs the prediction before Petra can open her mouth: the usual fee.

"Well, your old fee. Does that sound fair?"

"Well, no!" The old fee was priced for a man who gave it back. "Because now I've got a whole band, and we're a little bit bigger now, I've got other things going on, so…"

"We're a little bit more popular now."

"We've got a whole band now, not just a one-man band," Soma adds.

"What were you thinking?" She hedges. Fine. I name it. "So, 500."

She laughs, a real laugh, from the belly. "I can't afford 500 gold pieces for a one-night performance."

"Then why'd you put up the sign?" Soma asks.

She didn't put up the sign, she says. In her own venue.

"Someone did."

"I think we have a copy of the sign."

"Yeah, show her the sign, like, it's some…"

"This is your place."

"You're telling me that people are just invited?" Soma presses.

"That was more of a cover— that was more of a cover band idea than the real Lathander's Death."

"Oh, there's a cover band?"

"Yeah, the guy who plays your songs on stage every night — people like it."

"But don't you want the real band?"

"Yeah, but I can't afford 500 gold pieces."

"Alright, what can you do?"

"What do you mean you can't afford it? We're gonna sing, people are gonna, you know," Brewbarry says, and he is not wrong.

I lean in with everything I have, and I feel it land clean, the way a phrase lands when the room stops chewing to listen. Come on. She can do better than that.

"We negotiate. We get 50% of whatever earnings she makes while we sing," Brewbarry announces.

"Oh, you want front-end points," Soma says.

"Revenue share, yeah," Valphine says.

"Is that after or before expenses?" Not a breath of hesitation from Petra. Not one.

"Net," I say, and then the education catches up with me. "No, no, you always want gross, right?"

"Yeah, you want gross, because that is where Hollywood," Soma starts.

"And then just fake it with the expense."

Before any of us can change a single mind, Petra is shaking hands on the net.

"Ha ha ha ha. No, on the gross, on the gross."

"You've learned something, Vukradin. You're not the same guy I used to remember."

It should ring like a compliment. She means it as one, I think, or as half of one. And it sits in my ear like a chord voiced one note wrong, and I cannot immediately say which note.

"He's still pretty naive," Soma says, and I take more comfort from that than I would care to admit under oath.

Then Petra warms to her hostess voice. It's awesome that I've come, she says, and she has even kept my little table as a special place, where people come and admire and can see where I sat and thought about my music.

"Do they know it's a cover band?"

"So you've been impersonating me…"

"How long has this been going? So how much money have you— so really, I should sue you for a percentage of all your profits for stealing my likeness."

She swears she always told people this was not the real Vukradin. Sworn disclosure. Noted. But I did not come this far to litigate alone.

"Brewbarry, can you dispute for me, please?"

Brewbarry rises. He does not raise his voice; he raises himself, all of it, until Petra is standing in his shade. "Are you trying to rip off my friend?" A pause exactly the length of a rest. "Making profit on his image and reputation."

Now the paperwork. "I think 500 is a perfectly valid amount of gold, given all the money you've been making…"

"We can call it square with one night's performance."

"So after this one night's performance, I get to keep playing the old classic."

"I'm going to license my image and likeness to you."

"Perpetuity, is that what you're thinking here?"

"You will have to tell them it's a cover band, though."

"Well, I always tell them it's a cover band."

"I respect the image of the one, the only, the great Vukradin."

"I think this is good. 500, we perform in two nights."

"Makes up for all of you stealing from me in the past, and you've got rights going forward."

Not a coin changes hands, and the ledger balances anyway: the past infringement valued, squared by one exclusive night; the license running forward with disclosure stitched into it. Clean paper. Conflict-free. This is how it is done.

She spreads her hands at me. "I discovered you, man." And then, wounded: "I was there at the beginning. I was the one who gave you the gig. Don't you remember? Nobody wanted to put you on stage, actually."

"He was the one who actually played the gig, though," Soma says.

"Valphine, you wanna go on stage? Let's see how we do."

Petra balks. No. We can't do that.

"Why can't I perform right now? It'll get everybody excited for the real thing in two nights!"

She pauses, and I watch the arithmetic run behind her eyes. Fine. But not the full set. Just a teaser.

"No, no, we'll do one song. We'll do one song."

My delivery is serviceable. Valphine's opening note goes off a cliff I did not know the song had, a screech so far outside the key that the key files a complaint. And the room leans in. Because my old stuff was very discordant, and the lack of harmony and the off-cue screeches carry the crowd straight back to the original classic sound, the early genre, the pure work.

"Very avant-garde."

"Charm," Soma says.

Then Petra sits us down at my table. It was not my table. It was literally where Brewbarry sat, eleven nights a career, the one ten out of ten I ever kept. Now it is polished to a shine, dressed with beautifully set couches, and there is a little plaque: *Vukradin created his music here.* She asks if I would be willing to sign it.

"Well, that'll be extra."

"Brewbarry, what did you do to this guy? This guy used to be the nicest guy!"

"I'm nice! You see?"

"I just annoy you?" Brewbarry offers.

"My friend is not nice."

Twenty-five gold pieces, she says. Twenty-five gold pieces, twice, like naming it two times makes it smaller.

"Alright, 25."

"You gotta pay right now, though."

She pays on the spot, and her pupils have already gone to coin, counting the warmed-up crowd over my shoulder. Twenty-five gold, earned by my own name in my own hand. Fair-trade gold, and nobody bled for it. "And you just gotta sit down here. The food is the best."

"I don't know, we just had some great soup," Soma says.

"Free food!"

Brewbarry sets down his mug with intent. "I have an idea. Before we sing our next song, we should use our popularity."

"And our music, to influence the mind of people, and sing about the evil of Lord of Neverwinter."

"Whoa, whoa," Valphine says.

"No, I don't think he's that evil. I think it's…"

"Lord Neverwinter is our ally against Margaster, right?"

"Yeah, Margaster is evil."

"The enemy of our enemy is our friend," Soma says.

"So let's sing about the evil of House Margaster."

"And how corrupt they are."

"Sure."

"Plant that seed into the people's mind."

"Revolution."

"You want me to cast some spells, too, to really alter their minds?"

"Yes, yes, let's do that."

He is still going: "…leaves this tavern tonight needs to think that House Margaster is the most evil organization. That seems…"

"Very good of us."

The food arrives, and I am genuinely surprised, because in my day the Common Chord had no food at all and really cheap booze, the swill Brewbarry once praised and now, with his palate risen in the world, remembers as undrinkable. Tonight the plates are fancy and the beer, by every account, is good.

"I mean, isn't that an oxymoron?"

"There's bad beer, there's Bud Light," Soma says.

"Have I? I don't think I have a palate." And then his creed, entire: "Anything that will get me drunk is good. That's my definition of good."

Petra takes the good beer away from him and sets down a cheaper one.

"No, no, no. I'll take both, and I just got them."

He drinks them with identical joy, and she watches him do it, and I can see the sentence being passed: cheap beer for Brewbarry, all night, forever.

"Pearls before swine," Soma sighs.

"Whatever, as long as I get drunk."

"Take the good stuff."

When we take the stage again, Brewbarry renders the teaser's verdict first. "Yeah, yeah, that's terrible."

"Alright, let's do another one, let's see how it goes."

"Margaster needs to suffer. They need to know it's coming from us."

And here is the thing: I keep the spells in their case. I offered, sincerely, but nobody's mind gets altered tonight except the honest way, by a song standing on its own legs.

"Wait, wait, we need bardic inspiration," Valphine says.

"You want to do an inspiration?"

"And my pleasure."

Her Bless settles over us like tuning, my inspiration hums under Valphine's line, and the rough middle of the song lifts into something that carries. There is a witty lyric in it about House Margaster and the trade routes, about who profits when the people's coin buys less, and the crowd catches it and starts humming it back to us before the verse is even done. They picked it up. They are taking it home.

"Excellent," Brewbarry says.

Over the last of the good beer, the question arises of what, precisely, Brewbarry plays in this band.

"What? Cymbals."

"Well, he does cowbell," I explain. "That's the great thing about more cowbell." And it is true in every register: "Works on any song. Universally."

"Yeah, Blue Oyster Cult did not need more cowbell, but it worked out the same," Soma says.

"Sure," Brewbarry allows. Then, wounded on principle: "We're not that kind of band, though. I thought we were heavy metal. Cowbell."
