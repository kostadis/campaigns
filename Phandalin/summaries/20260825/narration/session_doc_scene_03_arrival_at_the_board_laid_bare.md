Half the shops in the Bluelake District are shuttered, which in this city means either bad business or bad news, and I have stopped assuming I can tell those apart. Soma leads. This is her ground; she walks it the way I walk a stage, without checking her feet. The district sign says Bluelake. I read it my way.

"Blue Light District, we're going to see."

Nothing. A performer knows the difference between a joke that failed and a room that wasn't listening, so I give it a second pass. "Blue Light District, really? No, it's really…"

"Red light is what you're thinking," Soma says, not slowing down.

And then, at last, the restaurant itself. Even Soma trips on the name: "It's been a long time to get to the Boar's Head — or Board Laid Bare."

"So many puns." I say it with respect. A name like that does half my job for me. "They just set themselves up. It's not even fair."

I stand in front of the door and turn the syllables over. "Laid. Bare. Bimbo… It's just…" The material keeps paying out on its own.

"There's a theme here, right?" Brewbarry says.

"Oh, the Hammock District," Soma offers, inventing geography, and I respect the escalation.

Somewhere in all of this I lose the thread entirely. "Why did it become— I forget why we came to this district."

"To get my food. I'm hungry."

"Oh — the soup. Okay, alright, we need a quest for that." An errand without a title never gets finished. This is true of errands and it is true of songs, so I give it one. "Soma… Soma's soup."

"Soup's good."

I try the establishment's full name at tempo and it doesn't scan, so I shorten it. "The BLB." Nobody objects. "It's the BLB."

The host meets us at the door and he is a warforged, tall and very still. Not still like a bored doorman. Still the way a lute peg is still: holding tension, refusing to move. He states nothing yet and already I can hear that his phrasing will be exact.

Soma looks him over and murmurs, "That kind of guy."

"Table options — what options are there? I'm curious," Brewbarry says.

"Three tables available. The table near the window offers the best view of the tower. The corner table is preferred by guests who do not wish to be observed. I recommend the corner table."

*Interesting.* A dining room whose opening offer is the seat for people who do not wish to be observed. I tuck that away.

"Window, please. I have guests today," Soma says, and then, quieter, "Normally I was here alone, right? Like, now I've got some company."

Alone. All those visits, one tortle at a window table with the best view of the tower. That picture isn't mine to say anything about, so I don't.

The count, though, is off, and I enjoy an honest count. "What was the third option?"

"Yeah, I heard three tables available. Window, corner, and…" Soma starts.

"Bar."

The truth settles on its own: there are two tables near the windows, not one. "Oh, perfect, we're gonna need two tables."

"Yeah, we'll just push them together."

Strictly speaking, we would all fit at one. Nobody consults strict speaking.

"Well, we'll do as we please," Brewbarry announces.

"We'll push them together. Brewbarry takes up a table almost all on his own. Yeah."

"He's, like, his own table, almost." Not mockery. Load-bearing fact.

Then the host turns to Soma. "I've seen you before. You are the one known as Soma, a friend of Lim's."

"Yeah, I've been here plenty of times. I'm glad you remember me."

"You haven't been here in 73 days."

To the day. Not a while, not a season. He has been keeping her account without being asked to keep it. I keep ledgers like that for coin, because coin drifts if you don't. He keeps one for a customer. Devotion or inventory, I can't tell yet, and both answers interest me.

"It's— it's been an interesting journey," Soma allows.

"I'm here to see Lim. I hope she's still around."

"Oh, Lim is very much around."

"What is your name, sir?" A being who counts your days deserves to be addressed properly.

"Bellows."

"Bellows! Bellows!" A name with air in it. Bellows feed forges and organs both. I approve completely.

And since we are asking questions: "Do you know a gnome named Bimbo?"

"No, I do not."

Flat. No pause to search whatever passes for his memory, and this is a being who produces a customer's absence to the day.

"Yeah. Okay."

"Do you see a lot of gnomes come through here? Probably not."

"What is a gnome like you doing at a place like that?" The riff lands nowhere in particular, but some riffs you make for yourself.

"Bimbling about," Soma says.

Bellows walks us to the table, seats us, and presents a handwritten card: fresh fish, vegetables, potatoes, two sauces. Handwritten. Somebody in that kitchen decides the menu daily and signs the decision.

"Soup, buddy," Brewbarry says.

"Just tell Lim I'm back, and she'll know how to." Soma lets it trail.

"The usual…" I add, as if I have one.

No alcohol anywhere on the card. I watch Brewbarry read it a second time to be certain.

And the prices. "That's good, we don't have very much money." This is not modesty. I have counted my purse, the counting did not take long, and the studio fund sits in a restricted trust where it belongs, untouchable even by me. Especially by me.

But the prices keep bothering me in the other direction. Every card, every stall, every taproom since we walked through Neverwinter's gates has been marked up, the trade routes disrupted, everything dearer than it ought to be. This card reads like the disruption never happened. Like the whole broken economy stops at Lim's threshold.

"Yeah, because it's farm-to-table. That's why we come here. It has its own supply chain right within the walls. It's— no, it's right there! It doesn't need any trade."

"Organic Fair Trade!" There it is. Honest food at honest prices while the rest of this city gouges its neighbors to cover its losses. Everything I have been preaching, set out on a handwritten card.

"There you go! That's a perfect explanation. I mean, that's why we always came here. It's a nice, locally sourced…"

A perfect explanation. I believe it, mostly, the way I believe a song I haven't heard the last verse of. Because if one dining room in Bluelake can hold its prices against a citywide disruption, then either Lim has solved something the rest of Neverwinter has not, or somebody is absorbing the difference. The first answer could feed a city. The second answer usually has a name attached.

"We should make a mental note to talk to your friend Lim and find out how," Brewbarry says. Linene has ruined him beautifully. He hears a price now and thinks in supply chains.

"She better come out and say hi."

The soup is ordered for the whole table, no squinting at the card, no negotiation. "For everybody. That's what we came here for."

"Trust someone to order for you," Soma says.

So I set the card down and I do. In Soma's world I am the guest, and the guest's job is to let somebody else conduct.
