# Wandering the Blue Lake District

## Scene summary (from gm-assist, verbatim)

#### The party travels through Neverwinter, observing the city's strange atmosphere and rising tensions before arriving at the Common Chord.
- Brewbarry shares a marketing jingle he wrote about Lim's turtle soup — lyrics scribbled on a napkin over lunch, now laid down as a track for the band: "When the cold winds bite in Neverwinter Town, and weary travelers start to get you down. There's a savory cure that's famed across the land, a bowl of warmth from good ol' Lim's own hand... They serve it hot at the Board Laid Bare. A taste of home that's beyond all compare."
- The rest of the band finds it off-brand — "This is Lathander's Death... [not] really our style," says Vukradin; "It's a marketing jingle," Valphine rules; "Call him Yoko and move on," Soma advises. ("We're not getting married!" Vukradin protests.) They assure Brewbarry he's their biggest fan, but the band has its artistic direction.
- While walking through the Blue Lake District, the party notices odd occurrences, including a woman locking a door she had already locked.
- A wizard's apprentice attempts to sell the party authentic many-starred cloak thread — decorative, not soft ("It's decorative," Valphine notes). Brewbarry's reasoning — "If the fabric is made of threads... so what makes this fabric soft is the thread, right?" — moves the DM to declare: "Give this man an inspiration."
- The party discovers a bill posted advertising a performance by Lathander's Death at the Common Chord in two days — a show they had never agreed to. "Hey, I didn't know you… when did you guys make this deal?" Vukradin, undeterred: "Let's add it to the quest tracker! We're doing a performance!"
- Two rumors circulate through the city: that Lord Neverember has taken a drow advisor — "No. ... Wait a second here," says Valphine, as Vukradin helpfully suggests, "It's you!" — and that Lathander's Death is playing the Common Chord.
- In the Protector's Enclave, citizens are heard arguing loudly — and correctly — that rents have doubled, reflecting the city's economic hardship.
