## Flags

### [1] Generic fantasy prose (writerly personification)

> Uneasy laughter in the pews, the kind that doesn't know where to put itself.

**Why:** `the kind that doesn't know where to put itself` personifies an abstraction and sorts it into a category — Brewbarry watches bodies, not the taxonomy of laughter. His spec: do not over-explain his interior.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Uneasy laughter in the pews. Nobody starts it and nobody stops it.`

### [2] Cross-narrator convergence (critic's register)

> His fingers tangle, a string buzzes, the tune wanders off somewhere no tune should.

**Why:** The first two clauses are exactly his — hands, sound, plain. The third is musical criticism; his whole relationship to music is `I like Vukradin` with no vocabulary under it.
**Suggested rewrite:** `His fingers tangle. A string buzzes. It comes apart.`

### [3] Cross-narrator convergence (dry epigram)

> The woman doesn't mind. Plenty of room in her head for gods.

**Why:** A wry one-line verdict on someone's mind — that is Soma's register (`The animals are very chatty.`), and Brewbarry's spec rule 2 says he does not have irony.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The woman doesn't mind. She wants all of them.`

### [4] Cross-narrator convergence (dry epigram)

> Maybe a little of Mielikki's blessing, since Soma looks the type.

**Why:** Same as [3] — `looks the type` is a knowing appraisal Brewbarry does not perform.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Maybe a little of Mielikki's blessing too. She is asking a tortle with a staff.`

### [5] Cross-narrator convergence (wry simile)

> He puts them away and smiles at all of us, like the smile is the donation.

**Why:** The `like the X is the Y` inversion is a joke construction from Soma's or Vukradin's grammar. Brewbarry's read on Cullen here is correct and worth keeping — it is the phrasing that is borrowed.
**Suggested rewrite:** `He puts them away. He smiles at us instead. The smile is not coins.`

### [6] Cross-narrator convergence (Soma's lexicon)

> The pouch comes out of his fingers clean, like a root out of wet ground, and I stuff it in the basket.

**Why:** This is the scene's marquee physical beat and it reaches for a soil-and-root simile — Soma's physical-world lens, not the goliath's. His spec says his physical narration is bodies, weight, the thing connecting.
**Suggested rewrite:** `He holds on. My hand is bigger. The pouch comes out of his fingers and goes in the basket.`

### [7] Bookkeeping-noun capitalisation

> "Life to Spring." Soma lifts Meril's Staff and says her words, and the blessing takes.

**Why:** Unearned capital on an ordinary noun; the same object is `Meril's staff` in this scene's own dialogue four lines earlier, and lower-case throughout Soma's spec.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Soma lifts Meril's staff and says her words, and the blessing takes.`

### [8] Pronoun ambiguity / voice spec conflict

> Valphine's golden eyes follow the lord. When his gaze crosses Cullen Sharpe his face goes sour, like the man is a smell. She says so, quiet.

**Why:** `his gaze` / `his face` have three possible antecedents in two sentences, so the beat's payload (Neverember dislikes Cullen) has to be reconstructed. Brewbarry's whole value as narrator is that he says the simple thing out loud.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Valphine's golden eyes follow the lord. The lord looks at Cullen Sharpe and his face goes sour, like the man is a smell. She says so, quiet.`

### [9] Cross-narrator convergence (Vukradin's register)

> He walks off before the sentence lands anywhere, mumbling about power and the need for exercising it.

**Why:** `before the sentence lands anywhere` parses somebody's syntax — phrasing and resolution are Vukradin's native metaphor, not Brewbarry's. It is also the fourth `lands` in this narration set.
**Suggested rewrite:** `He walks off in the middle of it, mumbling about power and the need for exercising it.`

### [10] Voice spec conflict (sarcasm)

> The same things, he means: Vukradin wants to return things to people, and Cullen wants to help him return things to people. He can provide all the people anyone could ever need to return things to.

**Why:** This is sustained irony built by triple repetition — genuinely funny, and genuinely not his. His spec rule 2 is explicit: he does not have sarcasm; a line that would land sarcastic from anyone else lands sincere from him.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The same things. Vukradin gives things back to people. Cullen makes people who need things given back. That is not the same thing.`

### [11] Narrator editorializing (authorial caption)

> A small branch of Lathander's Searing Light, planted in Neverwinter.

**Why:** A captioned institutional summary in ecclesiastical register — Valphine's or Vukradin's frame, and `_genre.md` bans the narrator drawing the conclusion. The capitalised `Searing Light` is also an unearned coinage (the temple is Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice).
**Suggested rewrite:** `Eight of them. In Neverwinter now, not just at home.`

### [12] Register-wrong / awkward construction

> Eyes streaming, faces up, in ecstasy and happy.

**Why:** `ecstasy` is a theological word he does not carry, and the clause is ungrammatical — a prepositional phrase joined to a bare adjective.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Eyes streaming, faces up. They are happy.`

### [13] Cross-narrator convergence (set-level verb)

> The name lands strange. Lathander's Death.

**Why:** `lands` as the verb of arrival-and-effect appears six times across this five-scene set in three different narrators' mouths (`It lands thin`, `Every word lands exactly where she set it`, `before the sentence lands anywhere`, `when it lands`). It is a house-writer reflex, not any one character's.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The name is strange in the room. Lathander's Death.`

### [14] Cross-narrator convergence (set-level phrase)

> Valphine finds the armorsmith in the crowd and blesses him where everyone can see.

**Why:** `where everyone can see` appears three times in this set, once each in Brewbarry's, Vukradin's, and Brewbarry's narration again — see scene 04 line 97 and scene 05 line 191. Three narrators cannot share a signature.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Valphine finds the armorsmith in the crowd and blesses him in front of the whole hall.`

### [15] Recap framing

> It's a start. Prutha's orcs out preaching, eight here, the temple back home. The numbers stack.

**Why:** The middle sentence inventories offscreen assets for the reader's benefit; `_genre.md` bans mental summary as recap. `The numbers stack` is a good Brewbarry landing and does not need the ledger under it.
**Suggested rewrite:** `It's a start. Eight here, and the temple back home. The numbers stack.`

## Verdict

The strongest problem is [10] — the Cullen `same things` gloss is a sustained sarcastic joke assigned to the one narrator whose spec says he cannot make one, and it sits at the scene's most important character beat. Under that, six of the fifteen flags are one specific failure: Soma's dry epigram and Vukradin's musical/syntactic register keep surfacing inside Brewbarry's paragraphs ([2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [9]). The declarative spine of the scene is excellent (`Generous is easy when I stand close`, `Seven nos`-grade beats like `I flex. One pec, then the other. Boom. Boom.`, `The word is in my head somewhere. It doesn't come.`), so spot-edits, not re-narration.
