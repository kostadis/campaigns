The Spire's side hall smells of beeswax and morning incense, and Perrin is already seated there with three people arranged around him like a display of evidence. He keeps glancing at Brewbarry. Every time Brewbarry shows his teeth, Perrin squirms in his chair. The Necklace of Fireballs has ridden in my pack since Icespire, catalogued, held in trust, and Valphine set the release terms herself: three independent witnesses, none of them Margaster. Terms met. Item returned. That is what honest commerce looks like, and I intend to conduct it properly.

The first witness does not wait to be asked. "I'm really glad that Perrin's gonna get that necklace, it's a great symbol of the rightful rulers of Neverwinter," and more of the same, revolution this, rightful heirs that. Valphine watches him through the entire recital and pronounces him sincere: a true believer in the revolution, no coin under the words. Enthusiasm is not evidence. But a witness who believes his own testimony is not a forger, and I have seen enough forged writs in this city to appreciate the difference.

The second is a genealogist, and he lights up at Soma before anyone introduces us. "Oh, Soma!"

"Yes…"

"I had a wonderful conversation with Boney."

"Oh, you found my horse."

"Yes, yes. It turns out his interest in Alagondar genealogy is almost as encyclopedic as mine."

"Yeah, he had a lot of free time." Years entombed with Lady Alagondar's dead. Boney read the whole family.

"He told me he studied the corpses and the dead and the relationships between the dead, and he had some excellent insights. I mean, he's about to go geek out on genealogy if you don't let him…"

"I'm here for that," Soma says.

"Why would we stop him? This is what we wanted to hear." Provenance. Chain of ownership. The whole practice of returning things stands on it.

"I find these, the provenance of this thing," Soma says, and I could hug her.

The genealogist gathers himself. "Well, I just want you… I cross-referenced Boney's claims and Perrin's claims, and it is in fact true that Perrin is a descendant, perhaps one of the primary descendants of the Alagondar family, and I was very pleased to see that Boney was able to…"

"Is there any other…" I hunt for the word. Claimants. Rivals. "…that are living."

"Unfortunately, no. Not that I know of, and Boney more or less confirmed that with me."

Brewbarry pounces. "Why do you say perhaps the primary descendant? I don't like this perhaps. We're not looking for…"

"Boney only had corpses to study, right? Like, he doesn't know who's alive," Soma puts in.

The genealogist fumbles it. "Well, I mean, Mr. Brewbarry, I am a scientist. Sorry, I'm a historian."

"You're not a scientist, you're a historian. Or are you? Which are you? Tomorrow, I talk to you, you'll…" The man has gone grey. His story has stopped matching itself.

"Maybe you're a pole dancer, I don't know."

"Dude, dude, go count the gold. How much did it take?" I get a shoulder between them.

"I'll let the man talk," Brewbarry says.

"You're so good at intimidating everyone," Soma tells him, dry as the incense.

The genealogist, recovered, explains what he meant: certainty past all doubt is not a thing his discipline sells, but he would stake his professional reputation that Perrin is the true and only claimant. A man's professional reputation, offered as collateral. I accept that currency.

And I see the whole arrangement now. I say it to the room, to whoever set this up. "I see what you've done here. The moral dilemma of giving the powerful magical item…"

"…to an asshole," Soma supplies.

"…owner who is evil." I let that sit. "But I am gonna stick with my principles. And hand it over."

A third witness waits his turn. "Oh, there's a third person."

"All three of them," Brewbarry says.

"You said it had to be 3 witnesses? She said…" Valphine's terms. We honor terms.

He is an elderly notary clerk, genuine, and he remembers the Alagondar family records. I give him my full attention, listening for the lie, and I find something else instead: the man is visibly frightened. "Well, yes, and I just wish to say that the records, as I recall them, did clearly put…" He stops. Reaches down inside himself. "When the new administration took over, there was a bit of a purging of records."

"The guy is clearly being intimidated," Brewbarry says.

The clerk pushes on. "When the Alagondars were removed from power and Lord Neverember became the first Lord of Neverwinter, well, the removal was a part of a purge." A purge of records. A Lord Protector burning the paperwork of the family he replaced. Scandalous. Somewhere in this city there is a ledger with pages torn out of it, and the man who tore them is the Lord Protector. Scandalous.

"What are they paying him? Are you…" Soma starts.

"We're being manipulated here in thinking that Perrin is the right heir, is not," Brewbarry says.

"So he is the rightful heir."

"No."

"He gets the necklace. Look, I get it."

"Like, he's evil."

Valphine tilts her head. "I don't know that Perrin is evil. He is certainly intimidated and controlled by people who are evil, but maybe if we can give him a protection racket, then… An alternative protection racket."

Soma is still chewing the clerk. "I just want to know why he had to, taking his lineage or prominence at face value, why is he paying off this guy, or threatening this wormy guy?" She squints at the notary, works him over piece by piece, and the answer comes back plain: the clerk is not afraid of Perrin.

"He's not afraid of Perrin, okay," Brewbarry concedes.

"I don't think he's intimidated, I don't think he's… he's just evil." There. Named. And once a man is named evil, the conversation with him is over. The conversation about him is a separate matter.

"It's okay. This is gonna come back and bite us in the ass if we…" Brewbarry says, and then, "I'm just trying to cover all sixes."

So I ask it out loud, because it is the only question. "What does it mean if you insist on handing over all the treasure if you're handing it over to people who are evil?"

"Even because there's the rightful owners, like, that's the…" Soma starts.

"We are the rightful owners, and I am sticking by my principles." The necklace answers to us today. It sits in our column, not our name. It is a trust, and the trust pays out.

"So, I'm not saying we don't give it back, I'm saying we need to tie it to some sort of…"

"…reasonable concession?" Soma offers.

"We must impose a concession or not." And we will not. A return with a price attached is a sale, and I do not sell other people their own property. The evidence is genuine, all of it: Boney confirmed it with the genealogist, the notary confirmed the records. The case is closed.

"I'll let it go, but mock my words, this is gonna bite us in the ass," Brewbarry says.

"It will."

"That's the whole point," Soma says.

"And we're not trying to take any precaution here or anything, we're just handing him over the knife that he's gonna use to stab us in the back. That's okay."

"He'll probably stab our face."

"No, it'll be more of a fireball to the face." I have carried that necklace long enough to know exactly what one bead does. Eight dice of fire in a twenty-foot ball, and that is only one bead. It will probably go off on us.

"We're gonna regret this, I'm just saying," Brewbarry says. Then, one last scheme: "Maybe we could have put a spell on the necklace so that we can disarm it, or get it back instantly if he uses it."

"I don't support that." A returned thing with a leash on it is not returned. It is lent. I am not a moneylender.

And in truth there is nothing left to decide. "I've already handed it over." While they were debating leashes, I walked it across the hall and set it in Perrin's hands. Ledger entry: one Necklace of Fireballs, held for claimant, claimant verified by three witnesses and one dead horse, delivered in full. That column closes clean.

Perrin thanks us greatly, warmly, at length, and I discover that I cannot stand the sound of his voice.

"Yeah, go away, get out of my sight. I don't want to talk to you."

The others look at me. "Nope." Then, half to myself: "You need to talk to evil people…" I let it trail off. Nobody finishes it for me.

Perrin's face falls. He looks genuinely hurt, clutching his family's necklace in the middle of my god's temple.

"I love it!" Soma says.

"Got your necklace! Goodbye."

Brewbarry, passing, leans down to him and reminds him where he is standing: that this was handed over to him in the Temple of Lathander, that this is his chance at redemption, that he should use it for good.

"The start of your redemption arc," Soma adds.

We walk out under the colonnade. "We did the right thing, right guys?" Soma asks.

"No," Brewbarry says.

The necklace is with its rightful owner. That is the column I keep, and it balances. What Perrin writes in his own ledger now belongs to Perrin. *Done.*
