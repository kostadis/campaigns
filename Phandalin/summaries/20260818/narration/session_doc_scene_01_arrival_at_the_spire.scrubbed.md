Morning. Vukradin means to name his band today. Whatever he and Valphine did at the Moonstone Mask, the Spire heard about it. Enough of a stir that the temple has invited a drow to preach at dawn. I finish my tea and listen to my bale sort the running order.

"Yes, well, they also invited me to come to the Spire, so I am there. I am coming to provide the sermon of the morning," Valphine says.

"Playing first, or are we preaching first?" Vukradin asks.

"Sermon first. Followed by music and sacraments."

"It's a Unitarian service," I say. Nobody laughs. That is fine. It was accurate, not funny.

"I've prepared the sermon, too," Valphine adds.

"I'm excited," says Vukradin.

"I want to hear this," says Brewbarry.

One of us is prepared, then. That is more than usual.

It is dawn, because of course it is dawn. When else does the Morninglord hold court. We reach the Spire's threshold and the doors are thick with clerics, and behind the clerics a crowd that got out of bed in the dark to be here. This has turned into a thing.

I watch the watchers. Valphine reads the room like a tide chart. Vukradin sees a warm and welcoming crowd of music lovers. "My naivete is peaking," he announces, and he is not wrong. Brewbarry squints and does better than his squint deserves.

The clerics at the doors are Lathanderites, and they are skeptical. Of what, I wonder.

"A drow cleric of Lathander?" I offer.

The way they look at her says yes, that.

Then the corners. Two in the shadows. Nobody stands that still by accident.

"Followers of Loviatar," Valphine says.

The goddess of pain. That is Loviatar's trade. Valphine has been running it without a license. "Your sacrament is appropriated," I tell her. To those shadows she is not blessed. She is a thief of pain, and a heretic.

Brewbarry counts exits and bodies. "So, are the skepticals in a group, or are they just scattered around the room?" Scattered. Harder to watch. He notes it.

"So is there anyone that they look to as a person of authority?" Valphine asks, and the answer that arrives is not the one she asked for: Lord Neverember, standing among the faithful and the curious like any other early riser.

"Wow," says Vukradin. Then, recovering: "I mean, it's a concert?"

The ones I can name are only the ones wearing their colors where an old tortle can see them. There are more. There are always more.

At the door the choice is Valphine's: walk in blazing like an avatar, pause and bless the crowd, let Vukradin do his shtick, or push past all of them. Before she picks, the armorsmith arrives. The armorsmith spent all night on the white dragon scale, every plate assembled, and now he is fitting it onto her at the temple threshold with his hands still black from the work.

"Which is a great advertisement for his business," Valphine says.

It isn't, though. He wants the blessing of Lathander. One of the devout, and he worked through the night so his armor could stand in her sermon. The crowd stirs harder as the scale goes on, white catching the first light.

"Well, it shows that we're not to be trifled with, right?" Valphine says. Whispers, commentary. The smith stands there proud of himself, and he has earned it.

Then Brewbarry sets his hand on her shoulder and the helm's eyes come up red, and a phantom sound rolls out of the doorway as we walk in, low ominous whispers, pure theater. He is getting good at backing her. Her own eyes need no trick. Gold. Everyone sees it.

Brother Aldric is waiting for us. This is not the time or place to stand and chat.

Custom, as I understand it, is a modest little light to announce the service. Valphine raises her hand high and floods the entire chamber with Daylight, and declares the morning sermon begun.

"Hey, it wasn't Sunbeam," Vukradin offers.

"That's a lot," Valphine allows.

Daylight is a spell for burning the walking dead out of crypts. "There is no undead here. We took care of that problem," Vukradin says, which is true, and beside the point.

Aldric has asked that she come talk to him first, before the sermon.

"Really?" Valphine says.

Really. A private receiving chamber. Aldric looks at her and says, "You weren't supposed to do that." Then he tilts his head and ponders, and I can see him counting up everything she skipped. He is in a good mood anyway. That is worth noting.

"Your religion isn't his religion," Vukradin says.

"Yeah. It's a new religion," I say. Somebody had to.

"I am leaning into the Church of the Searing Pain, but I do know the room. And so the question is, how do we draw them in? You drop the bomb on them subtly," Valphine tells us. Subtle bombs. I keep my mouth shut.

Behind us the hall is still glowing. The crowd is in awe of the casting; it is not often anyone sees Daylight inside a church of Lathander in the middle of the day.

"It's dawn. It's dawn, so…" Valphine says.

Some of the crowd squint against it. She watches their squinting a beat longer than a priest should.

Brewbarry leans over and whispers in her ear that they should make sure to collect the donations at the end of the sermon.

"Oh, I haven't planned," she says.

"But he has many plans, yes. No, I'm just waiting for the plans. I am here to serve, by the way. Anything you want me to do, just ask," Vukradin says.

"Yeah, so am I," says Brewbarry.

It turns out to be Brother Aldric and several clerics waiting in the chamber.

"So Valphine, Hold Person? No? Okay, alright, just say the word. I got it ready," Vukradin murmurs. Nobody in this room is armed. Even Vukradin can see nobody is armed.

"Well, maybe. I don't know what he's got planned." A pause. "Maybe he wants to inflict pain. Hold Person's great for inflicting pain."

Valphine's actual agenda for the day: "Promoting the band. And drumming up business sales for the rooms."

Aldric wears his concern openly. "I've heard of the blessed temple that you've built in Phandalin."

"Why, yes, that's glorious."

"Yes, but I have a question. You keep talking about this blessing… what does it mean? What is the meaning of the blessing?"

"It allows us to gain a deeper appreciation of the glory and power of the Morninglord."

"So you're saying this is not about the pain. This is about your redemption and your understanding of the Morninglord's path, right?"

"Why, yes, of course, of course. We must gain a deeper spiritual communion with the Morninglord."

It lands thin. Vukradin plays under her and she has a blessing on top of it, and the answer still comes out flat. Aldric is not impressed. Disappointed, a little. I watch his hands go quiet.

She doesn't flounder. She says, "Brewbarry."

"Brewbarry, of course!" Vukradin says.

And Brewbarry steps in, eyes gleaming with light, his ominous whispers curling out of the back of the room again. A goliath vouching for her doctrine. It works. This time it holds.

Aldric accepts the point, with a warning attached. "Well, I'm happy to see that you're doing this, but I warn you to stay away from this path of pain. This is not the Lathander's way."

"What do you mean by that?"

Before he can answer, another sun priest interrupts. Scarred, this one, old burns by the look of them. "Is pain the point, or the healing?"

"The light is the point. He is the Morninglord, the Lord of Light."

"Imagine life taught you that healing can be painful," Brewbarry says.

"But why do you insist on the pain?"

"But healing doesn't come cheap, my friend."

"Embrace the fullness of the light. You must experience it in its totality. Otherwise, it's just a pale shadow," Valphine says.

"But then, what is the purpose of the healing?"

"Well then… Our Lord has great ambitions for us on this world, and therefore we must be able to then see the lit path."

Every word lands exactly where she set it. I have watched her improvise before. This is not that. She had the whole answer before he asked.

"Well done," Vukradin says.

"Persuaded," I say.

Aldric smiles and makes the sign of the Morninglord. The scarred one's face closes up. He was put in this room to catch her, and he did not.
