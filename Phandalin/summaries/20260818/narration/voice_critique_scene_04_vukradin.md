## Flags

### [1] Voice spec conflict (music metaphor overuse)

> Provenance. Chain of ownership. The whole practice of returning things stands on it, the way a resolution stands on the chord before it.

**Why:** Spec rule 5: `Do not deploy music metaphors in every paragraph. The metaphor surfaces where it earns its place.` This is one of **six** in a 858-word narration track (see [2]–[5]), and the analogy is strained — a resolution depends on the preceding chord, but provenance is not a dependency, it is a record.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Provenance. Chain of ownership. The whole practice of returning things stands on it.`

### [2] Voice spec conflict (music metaphor overuse)

> And I see the whole arrangement now, laid out like sheet music. I say it to the room, to whoever composed this.

**Why:** Two music figures in consecutive clauses — `arrangement`, `sheet music`, `composed` — is the density the spec forbids. His ledger reflex is available here and is doing nothing.
**Suggested rewrite:** `And I see the whole arrangement now. I say it to the room, to whoever set this up.`

### [3] Voice spec conflict (music metaphor overuse)

> The evidence is genuine, every note of it: Boney confirmed it with the genealogist, the notary confirmed the records.

**Why:** Third register of the same figure, used purely as an intensifier — `every note of it` adds nothing `all of it` would not.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The evidence is genuine, all of it: Boney confirmed it with the genealogist, the notary confirmed the records.`

### [4] Voice spec conflict (music metaphor overuse)

> I give him my full attention, listening for the false note, and I find something else instead: the man is visibly frightened.

**Why:** Fourth instance. `listening for the false note` also displaces the observation the sentence is actually about — that the clerk is frightened.
**Suggested rewrite:** `I give him my full attention, listening for the lie, and I find something else instead: the man is visibly frightened.`

### [5] Voice spec conflict (music metaphor overuse)

> and more in that key, revolution this, rightful heirs that

**Why:** Fifth instance. Mildest of the five and defensible alone; indefensible as part of a run of six.
**Suggested rewrite:** `and more of the same, revolution this, rightful heirs that`

### [6] Voice spec conflict (banned-tic adjacent)

> Every time Brewbarry bares his teeth in that slow way of his, Perrin squirms in his chair.

**Why:** `in that slow way of his` gestures at a habit's category instead of rendering it — the same move `_genre.md` bans in the `with the X of someone who Y` family.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Every time Brewbarry shows his teeth, Perrin squirms in his chair.`

### [7] Cliché + register drift (hardboiled)

> Somewhere in this city there is a ledger with pages torn out, and everyone bows to the man holding the knife.

**Why:** `everyone bows to the man holding the knife` is stock noir, and spec rule 6 bans hardboiled realism outright: `That's just how the world works is not in his grammar.` The metaphor is also mixed — pages are torn, not cut.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Somewhere in this city there is a ledger with pages torn out of it, and the man who tore them is the Lord Protector. Scandalous.`

### [8] Generic fantasy prose (monotony of construction)

> A returned thing with a leash on it is not returned. It is lent. I am not a moneylender.

**Why:** The line is good, but it is the seventh `X is not Y` aphorism in one scene — `Enthusiasm is not evidence`, `a witness who believes his own testimony is not a forger`, `certainty past all doubt is not a thing his discipline sells`, `the clerk is not afraid of Perrin`, `holding is not owning`, `A return with a price attached is a sale`. The scene's moral argument is built from a single rhetorical mould, which flattens the spec's required alternation between clause-stacking deliberation and short verdicts.
**Suggested rewrite:** Keep this one, which is the best of them; recast `holding is not owning` and `What I mean is this` in his procedural/ledger register instead.

### [9] Generic fantasy prose (essayist move)

> "We are the rightful owners, and I am sticking by my principles." What I mean is this: the necklace answers to us today, and holding is not owning. It is a trust. The trust pays out.

**Why:** `What I mean is this:` is a thesis-and-colon construction from an essay, not a bard mid-argument, and it announces a gloss the following sentences would deliver on their own.
**Suggested rewrite:** `"We are the rightful owners, and I am sticking by my principles." The necklace answers to us today. Holding is not owning. It is a trust, and the trust pays out.`

### [10] Narrator editorializing

> I let it trail off. Nobody finishes it for me, because there is no good ending to that sentence.

**Why:** The narrator explains the beat's meaning to the reader — `_genre.md`: drop the frame, let the moment land. `I let it trail off` had already done the work.
**Suggested rewrite:** `I let it trail off. Nobody finishes it for me.`

### [11] Generic fantasy prose (self-repetition)

> The necklace is with its rightful owner. That is the column I keep, and it is closed, and it is clean.

**Why:** Verbatim reuse of `That column closes clean` from twenty lines earlier (line 125). The ledger reflex is exactly right for him; running the identical figure twice inside one page spends it.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The necklace is with its rightful owner. That is the column I keep, and it balances.`

### [12] Register-wrong (mechanics elided where the spec wants them named)

> I have carried that necklace long enough to know exactly what its fattest bead does when it lands. It will be enormous. It will probably land on us.

**Why:** Spec: he speaks in clean procedural mechanics — hit points, ranges, save DCs — and `_genre.md` says to drop them directly into prose. Here the one place the numbers belong, they are replaced by `enormous`. Also the third and fourth `land` in this set.
**Suggested rewrite:** `I have carried that necklace long enough to know what its fattest bead does. Seven dice of fire in a twenty-foot radius, Dexterity save for half. It will probably go off on us.`

### [13] Cross-narrator convergence (set-level phrase)

> So I put the question where everyone can see it, because it is the only question.

**Why:** `where everyone can see` also appears in scene 03 (`blesses him where everyone can see`) and scene 05 (`I lay them out one at a time, where everyone can see them`) — three narrators sharing one construction.
**Suggested rewrite:** `So I put the question to the room, because it is the only question.`

### [14] Cross-narrator convergence (Soma's register)

> "Yeah, he had a lot of free time." Years entombed with Lady Alagondar's dead. Our horse used them well.

**Why:** `Our horse used them well` is dry understatement delivered as a verdict — Soma's move (`And Boney stood by and did nothing?`). Vukradin's register here would be delight, by name.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Years entombed with Lady Alagondar's dead. Boney read the whole family.`

### [15] Generic fantasy prose (ornament)

> He looks genuinely hurt, clutching his family's necklace in the temple of my god's morning light.

**Why:** The trailing prepositional stack is decoration draped over a good observation; `_genre.md` favours the material noun over the poetic image.
**Suggested rewrite:** `He looks genuinely hurt, clutching his family's necklace in the middle of my god's temple.`

## Verdict

The strongest problem is the music-metaphor density — six figures ([1]–[5], plus `arrangement`/`composed` counted together) in 858 words of narration, against a spec that says the metaphor surfaces only where it earns its place; it converts his signature into wallpaper. Second is [8]: the scene's moral reasoning is cast almost entirely in one `X is not Y` mould, which flattens the long-deliberation / short-verdict alternation the spec requires. The ledger material (`A man's professional reputation, offered as collateral. I accept that currency.`, the `Ledger entry:` line, `Scandalous.`, `*Done.*`) is first-rate and should survive intact — spot-edits, with the metaphor pass done as one sweep.
