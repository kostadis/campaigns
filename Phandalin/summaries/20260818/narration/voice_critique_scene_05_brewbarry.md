## Flags

### [1] Voice spec conflict (irony he does not have)

> From the doorway, where Vukradin is standing so that he is technically not here, the name gets chewed over twice.

**Why:** `technically not here` is a joke at Vukradin's expense with a legalistic wink under it — Brewbarry's spec rule 2 says he does not have sarcasm, and rule 9 says he does not comment on Vukradin at all beyond liking him. He had already rendered this beat correctly two paragraphs earlier: `He can stand in the doorway. The doorway is not the room.`
**Suggested rewrite:** `From the doorway, where Vukradin is standing, the name gets chewed over twice.`

### [2] Register-wrong vocabulary

> "Yeah, you sold me on it." I mean it. That is good spycraft. I file the trick away.

**Why:** `file` is clerical vocabulary from the counting-house side of the scene, not the goliath's. `I mean it. That is good spycraft.` is perfect; the third sentence undoes it.
**Suggested rewrite:** `"Yeah, you sold me on it." I mean it. That is good spycraft. I keep it.`

### [3] Voice spec conflict (unmotivated backstory + aphorism)

> A fairy gave me goodberries once. They were not goodberries. I woke up, the artifact was gone, and the blame was mine. Since then I check what things are before I trust what they look like.

**Why:** Nothing in the surrounding beats triggers this — it sits between a compliment about his cleverness and a line about a headband — so it reads as the narrator supplying backstory to the reader. The closing sentence is a generalised life-lesson; spec rule 6: `Do not over-explain his interior`, rule 8: `Do not make him narrate his own shame at length.`
**Suggested rewrite:** Cut to two sentences and let it sit unexplained: `A fairy gave me goodberries once. They were not goodberries.`

### [4] Voice spec conflict (philosophizing + second backstory reprise)

> People do things when they are motivated. I am giving this banker motivation. He helped me. I can help him. Simple. The tribe threw me out for a thing a fairy did, and Vukradin's music found me in this city when nothing else did. I know how favors hold a man up better than trust does.

**Why:** The first five sentences are exactly right and should be the whole paragraph. Then the exile story returns a second time in one scene, followed by an abstract maxim about human nature — spec rule 3 bans moral arguments about systems, rule 9 bans philosophizing about Vukradin, and the first-15-chapters example file shows him closing on `"Drink good."`, not on a thesis.
**Suggested rewrite:** `People do things when they are motivated. I am giving this banker motivation. He helped me. I can help him. Simple.`

### [5] Narrator editorializing / recap framing

> The city is waking up and it is chewing on us: a new band, Vukradin the legendary musician in it, Lord Neverember speaking privately to Valphine and liking what he heard.

**Why:** A colon-list of rumour that inventories the morning's results for the reader — `_genre.md` bans recap framing. `chewing on us` is also the third `chew` figure in the set (see [10]).
**Suggested rewrite:** `Outside the Temple of Lathander the clouds pull apart. People are talking about us. I hear the band's name twice before we reach the corner.`

### [6] Cross-narrator convergence (set-level phrase)

> The pieces sit wrong on the table. I lay them out one at a time, where everyone can see them.

**Why:** `where everyone can see` appears three times across this five-scene set in two different narrators (scene 03 line 201, scene 04 line 97). Also a mixed metaphor: jigsaw `pieces` on a `table`, then eight lines later `The board is in my head. The strings are real.` — three incompatible figures for one thought.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The pieces sit wrong. I say them out loud, one at a time.`

### [7] Generic fantasy prose (ornamental superlative)

> Then the smile goes on. The most professional of professional smiles.

**Why:** `The most professional of professional smiles` is a polished superlative-of-itself construction; `for one breath his face is pure pain. Then the smile goes on.` already landed it, hard and bodily.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Then the smile goes on. It is a good smile. It is not his face.`

### [8] Generic fantasy prose (writerly abstraction)

> The name lives in two places. The Counting House keeps the Commission's paper.

**Why:** `The name lives in two places` personifies a fact — he would count the two places, which is his actual habit throughout the scene (`Seven nos. I count them.`, `I count the holes on my fingers.`).
**Suggested rewrite:** `Two places have the name. Cullen has it. The Counting House keeps the Commission's paper.`

### [9] Cross-narrator convergence (set-level motif)

> I nod along. My friends. The banker's hands are not easy on his desk anymore.

**Why:** Hands-as-tell is used by all four narrators in this set — `I watch his hands go quiet` (Soma, scene 01), `his hands have stopped obeying him` (Vukradin, scene 04), `I watch his hands, his mouth, the skin around his eyes` (Brewbarry, earlier this scene). It is the house writer's default observation, not any character's lens.
**Suggested rewrite:** `I nod along. My friends. The banker has stopped sitting down properly.`

### [10] Generic fantasy prose (set-level repetition)

> the name gets chewed over twice

**Why:** `chew` runs three times across the set — here, `Soma is still chewing the clerk` (scene 04), and `it is chewing on us` (this scene). Three uses of one verb-figure in two scenes reads as one writer, not two narrators.
**Suggested rewrite:** `the name gets said twice`

### [11] Cliché / mixed image

> The pieces click together in my head like a snare being set.

**Why:** `pieces click together` is stock, and `snare` is an unfortunate word for the character whose one stated aversion is drums — the double meaning fires before the trap meaning does.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The pieces go together in my head. I can see the whole trap.`

## Verdict

The strongest problem is [4] — the closing paragraph starts in Brewbarry's exact register and then adds an unprompted exile recap plus an abstract maxim about favors and trust, which is the one thing his spec forbids twice over; [3] is the same failure earlier in the scene, so the backstory is told twice in 640 words of narration. Under those, five flags are convergence rather than voice error: `where everyone can see`, hands-as-tell, and the `chew` figure are shared across narrators. The investigative spine is the best writing in the set (`Seven nos. I count them. One no does the job. Seven is decoration.`, `More holes than name.`, `Wrong house. I hear it as it leaves my mouth.`) — spot-edits, not re-narration.
