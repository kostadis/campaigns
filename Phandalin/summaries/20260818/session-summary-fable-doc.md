# Chapter 48: Lathander's Death

## Soma — Arrival at the Spire

Morning. Vukradin means to name his band today. Whatever he and Valphine did at the Moonstone Mask, the Spire heard about it. Enough of a stir that the temple has invited a drow to preach at dawn. I finish my tea and listen to my bale sort the running order.

"Yes, well, they also invited me to come to the Spire, so I am there. I am coming to provide the sermon of the morning," Valphine says.

"Playing first, or are we preaching first?" Vukradin asks.

"Sermon first. Followed by music and sacraments."

"It's a Unitarian service," I say. Nobody laughs. That is fine. It was accurate, not funny.

"I've prepared the sermon, too," Valphine adds.

"I'm excited," says Vukradin.

"I want to hear this," says Brewbarry.

One of us is prepared, then. That is more than usual.

It is dawn, because of course it is dawn. When else does the Morninglord hold court. We reach the Spire's threshold and the doors are thick with clerics, and behind the clerics a crowd that got out of bed in the dark to be here. This has turned into a thing.

I watch the watchers. Valphine reads the room like a tide chart. Vukradin sees a warm and welcoming crowd of music lovers. "My naivete is peaking," he announces, and he is not wrong. Brewbarry squints and does better than his squint deserves.

The clerics at the doors are Lathanderites, and they are skeptical. Of what, I wonder.

"A drow cleric of Lathander?" I offer.

The way they look at her says yes, that.

Then the corners. Two in the shadows. Nobody stands that still by accident.

"Followers of Loviatar," Valphine says.

The goddess of pain. That is Loviatar's trade. Valphine has been running it without a license. "Your sacrament is appropriated," I tell her. To those shadows she is not blessed. She is a thief of pain, and a heretic.

Brewbarry counts exits and bodies. "So, are the skepticals in a group, or are they just scattered around the room?" Scattered. Harder to watch. He notes it.

"So is there anyone that they look to as a person of authority?" Valphine asks, and the answer that arrives is not the one she asked for: Lord Neverember, standing among the faithful and the curious like any other early riser.

"Wow," says Vukradin. Then, recovering: "I mean, it's a concert?"

The ones I can name are only the ones wearing their colors where an old tortle can see them. There are more. There are always more.

At the door the choice is Valphine's: walk in blazing like an avatar, pause and bless the crowd, let Vukradin do his shtick, or push past all of them. Before she picks, the armorsmith arrives. The armorsmith spent all night on the white dragon scale, every plate assembled, and now he is fitting it onto her at the temple threshold with his hands still black from the work.

"Which is a great advertisement for his business," Valphine says.

It isn't, though. He wants the blessing of Lathander. One of the devout, and he worked through the night so his armor could stand in her sermon. The crowd stirs harder as the scale goes on, white catching the first light.

"Well, it shows that we're not to be trifled with, right?" Valphine says. Whispers, commentary. The smith stands there proud of himself, and he has earned it.

Then Brewbarry sets his hand on her shoulder and the helm's eyes come up red, and a phantom sound rolls out of the doorway as we walk in, low ominous whispers, pure theater. He is getting good at backing her. Her own eyes need no trick. Gold. Everyone sees it.

Brother Aldric is waiting for us. This is not the time or place to stand and chat.

Custom, as I understand it, is a modest little light to announce the service. Valphine raises her hand high and floods the entire chamber with Daylight, and declares the morning sermon begun.

"Hey, it wasn't Sunbeam," Vukradin offers.

"That's a lot," Valphine allows.

Daylight is a spell for burning the walking dead out of crypts. "There is no undead here. We took care of that problem," Vukradin says, which is true, and beside the point.

Aldric has asked that she come talk to him first, before the sermon.

"Really?" Valphine says.

Really. A private receiving chamber. Aldric looks at her and says, "You weren't supposed to do that." Then he tilts his head and ponders, and I can see him counting up everything she skipped. He is in a good mood anyway. That is worth noting.

"Your religion isn't his religion," Vukradin says.

"Yeah. It's a new religion," I say. Somebody had to.

"I am leaning into the Church of the Searing Pain, but I do know the room. And so the question is, how do we draw them in? You drop the bomb on them subtly," Valphine tells us. Subtle bombs. I keep my mouth shut.

Behind us the hall is still glowing. The crowd is in awe of the casting; it is not often anyone sees Daylight inside a church of Lathander in the middle of the day.

"It's dawn. It's dawn, so…" Valphine says.

Some of the crowd squint against it. She watches their squinting a beat longer than a priest should.

Brewbarry leans over and whispers in her ear that they should make sure to collect the donations at the end of the sermon.

"Oh, I haven't planned," she says.

"But he has many plans, yes. No, I'm just waiting for the plans. I am here to serve, by the way. Anything you want me to do, just ask," Vukradin says.

"Yeah, so am I," says Brewbarry.

It turns out to be Brother Aldric and several clerics waiting in the chamber.

"So Valphine, Hold Person? No? Okay, alright, just say the word. I got it ready," Vukradin murmurs. Nobody in this room is armed. Even Vukradin can see nobody is armed.

"Well, maybe. I don't know what he's got planned." A pause. "Maybe he wants to inflict pain. Hold Person's great for inflicting pain."

Valphine's actual agenda for the day: "Promoting the band. And drumming up business sales for the rooms."

Aldric wears his concern openly. "I've heard of the blessed temple that you've built in Phandalin."

"Why, yes, that's glorious."

"Yes, but I have a question. You keep talking about this blessing… what does it mean? What is the meaning of the blessing?"

"It allows us to gain a deeper appreciation of the glory and power of the Morninglord."

"So you're saying this is not about the pain. This is about your redemption and your understanding of the Morninglord's path, right?"

"Why, yes, of course, of course. We must gain a deeper spiritual communion with the Morninglord."

It lands thin. Vukradin plays under her and she has a blessing on top of it, and the answer still comes out flat. Aldric is not impressed. Disappointed, a little. I watch his hands go quiet.

She doesn't flounder. She says, "Brewbarry."

"Brewbarry, of course!" Vukradin says.

And Brewbarry steps in, eyes gleaming with light, his ominous whispers curling out of the back of the room again. A goliath vouching for her doctrine. It works. This time it holds.

Aldric accepts the point, with a warning attached. "Well, I'm happy to see that you're doing this, but I warn you to stay away from this path of pain. This is not the Lathander's way."

"What do you mean by that?"

Before he can answer, another sun priest interrupts. Scarred, this one, old burns by the look of them. "Is pain the point, or the healing?"

"The light is the point. He is the Morninglord, the Lord of Light."

"Imagine life taught you that healing can be painful," Brewbarry says.

"But why do you insist on the pain?"

"But healing doesn't come cheap, my friend."

"Embrace the fullness of the light. You must experience it in its totality. Otherwise, it's just a pale shadow," Valphine says.

"But then, what is the purpose of the healing?"

"Well then… Our Lord has great ambitions for us on this world, and therefore we must be able to then see the lit path."

Every word lands exactly where she set it. I have watched her improvise before. This is not that. She had the whole answer before he asked.

"Well done," Vukradin says.

"Persuaded," I say.

Aldric smiles and makes the sign of the Morninglord. The scarred one's face closes up. He was put in this room to catch her, and he did not.

## Valphine Sotorra — The Sermon of Searing Light

Dawn through the windows of the Spire, and the hall is packed. The Overbright's faithful have come at first light to be consoled, as they always come, and I stand at the pulpit of Brother Aldric Sunmantle, whose consolatory rites I memorized four months ago while feigning devotion. Ser Kaelen sent me here for guidance about my eyes. I have decided to provide the guidance instead.

Brewbarry surveys the pews with open pride. "My friend is on the stage, all eyes on her. She's about to illuminate them with her divine message." A pause, and then, with genuine puzzlement: "I do ponder why some question the way that she sets forth, though."

I catalogue the room the way my mother taught me to catalogue any room: exits, factions, appetite. And there, seated among the devout, a face from our ledger.

"Perrin?"

"Karen?" Vukradin offers.

The Alagondar heir. Brewbarry supplies the relevant fact: "Claims that we owe him the thing."

"Oh, that guy," Vukradin says. We told him to be here; the handover was arranged around this service. "Right, because we're cutting the deal. Yep."

Good. Business seated in the third row, an audience of hundreds, and a god of inexhaustible power waiting to be introduced to his own worshippers. I turn to my companions. "Ready for the sermon?"

"Yes, please," says Brewbarry.

I look into the dawn until my golden eyes burn, and I begin.

"Children of the dawn, gaze upon the rising sun and know true power. Lathander is not a soft god of quiet comfort or gentle reprieve. He is the blinding radiance that cuts through the stagnant dark and shatters the slumber of the world. Darkness does not yield to meekness, nor does the night retreat before trembling hands. Light is an absolute force, unyielding, fierce, and searing. When the first rays of morning pierce the sky, they do not negotiate with the shadows. They destroy them, exposing every hidden corruption and sweeping away the decay of the night with uncompromising glory."

They lean forward. Of course they lean forward. They have been fed consolation for years and mistaken it for faith; I am handing them the thing itself. In Menzoberranzan, Lolth rations her power in the dark and calls the rationing love. My god spends his without counting, every morning, on everyone, and no one in this hall has ever been told what that means.

"To truly walk in the light of the Morninglord, one must undergo the sacred sacrament of blinding light. In this holy rite, the faithful cast off all comforting illusions and look unflinchingly into the incandescent glare of pure divinity, until the eye beholds only burning gold, and every shadow within the soul is utterly incinerated. It is in a baptism of fire and brilliance that hardens the spirit into a conduit of divine radiance."

The burn behind my own eyes answers as I speak, a low, exquisite sting. This is not rhetoric. I have taken this sacrament every dawn since I surfaced, and it gave me these eyes.

"Through this sacrament, we are remade not as passive observers, but as living blazes, forged to carry the intolerable heat of Lathander's glory into every dark corner of this realm. Let the heathens and the creature spawn of pitch black abyss tremble at our approach. Armed with the searing wrath of the Dawnbringer, it is our holy duty to purge the wicked and reduce their blasphemies to ash. We do not offer pity to those who cling to the dark, or quarter for the meek and weak, for to harbor shadow is to invite destruction."

"Rise, warriors of the morning. Lift high your glowing crests. And let the untamed power of Lathander incinerate every enemy of the dawn. Through light alone we dominate. Through light alone we conquer. Now, behold, Lathander's death."

Silence, one beat too long to be politeness. Then the noise.

"Well done," says Vukradin, and I hear the professional in him weighing the delivery.

"I think I'm changing religions," says Soma.

"We are Lathander's death," Vukradin declares, taking the name for the band as if it had been his idea all along.

"And of course, like… and stop by the gift shop on the way out," I add.

"Don't forget to buy our merch," says Soma.

"Get your bathrobe," says Brewbarry.

Vukradin leans in, and objects in the register he uses when he wants the room to see him object: "Do you want everyone to stare at the sun until they go blind? Is that the plan?" He understands the sacrament perfectly. Objecting to it in front of three hundred people is the better position, and he took it.

Then movement in the pews. Robed figures rising, and I recognize the scourge-marks before they open their mouths. "Well, Loviatar is, like, the god of pain, right?" Vukradin says.

One of them screams the denunciation across the hall. "Pain is the Maiden's! The drow has dressed our sacrament in sacrilege and called it holy."

Brewbarry answers before I can, and what comes out of him is my doctrine, whole, unprompted, from a mouth no one in this hall can accuse of subtlety: "No, my brothers, pain is simply the darkness you break through on your way to the dawn." A convert I never had to convert.

I do not debate heretics in my own service. Debate concedes that the outcome is uncertain. "Let his body refuse it if it can," I say, and while the hall waits for my rebuttal: "I cast Blindness at him."

The light takes him utterly. There is no resistance in the man at all. Not one point of it. Gold floods in behind his eyes until he beholds only burning. The sacrament, administered.

"That'll teach you," says Brewbarry.

"For the rest of his day," says Soma.

"Permanently blind," Brewbarry insists, hopeful.

"You're burning your spells, man!" Vukradin, ever the accountant of my gifts.

"Nope. Duration, 1 minute." A blessing, not a sentence. My god is generous, not wasteful.

"Cast him out! Cast out the heathen!" Vukradin cries. The fervor has reached the one man in the hall who came to object.

"That's right. If you're not a zealot, you get off the boat," says Brewbarry. "That doesn't rhyme. Doesn't work, does it?"

The second Loviatarite screams as they drag their blinded brother toward the doors: "You have stolen the sacraments of Loviatar! There will be consequences!"

"How dare you defile the—"

"I don't think staring at the sun is a sacrament of Loviatar," Vukradin cuts in.

Inflicting pain, though, is hers. I concede the point privately. Conceding costs nothing when no one hears it. The Maiden and I draw from adjacent doctrine; the difference is the direction of the current. "The goal is the light, the pain is just a nice benefit, side benefit."

"Isn't that what she did?" Brewbarry asks the room.

They leave under the congregation's contempt. The faithful watch them go the way a house watches a guest who has insulted its host. The sermon was eloquent and the room knows it; their accusation dies unheard. But it is not my eloquence that settles it. It never is. It is the blinding. Power made visible on the skin persuades where argument merely entertains.

"There will be comments!" Vukradin calls after them, mocking. The man is blind. That is not in dispute. "Only for 1 minute," Vukradin allows.

"But you call it blinding, I call it blessing," says Brewbarry. "She blessed him with the holy light. You saw the light."

"It's an alternate way of taking the sacrament," says Soma.

Consequences. I have been promised consequences by Menzoberranzan, by my mother, by a goddess who eats her own. Loviatar's pain has a price and a buyer. Mine has neither. Let her church come collect.

## Brewbarry — Performance and the Shakedown

The name is strange in the room. Lathander's Death. Uneasy laughter in the pews. Nobody starts it and nobody stops it. Then somebody stands up and cheers, and the rest of them decide it's allowed. The people are really excited about it. Good name.

Vukradin is bouncing on his toes. "Are we… do we get to play now? Can I play?"

"Yeah, it's time for the band," Valphine says.

Time for death metal, he tells the room. Valphine tells them, or maybe just us, "Common is not my native language, just to be clear." Then, "Undercommon is my native language."

Vukradin starts, and it goes wrong. His fingers tangle. A string buzzes. It comes apart. Scattered applause. Everybody a little uncertain how to handle it. In the back a man hums a hymn that isn't the one being sung.

Vukradin doesn't stop. "Alright, Valphine, pull it off." Then, leaning in, "Valphine, do you want to sing in Undercommon? I speak Undercommon."

"I think that would be appropriate. I think that would be appropriate."

She sings dark words to a bright room. At first the room holds still. Then she gets into it, and the crowd warms, swaying, cheering.

"From abyssal night, the darkness flees beyond…"

"Mighty sword, descendant glory, born in Lord and grave."

"Your sacred lights will save us all."

While the music plays, I go around with a little basket. I hold it out and I look at each person while I hold it. People are generous. Generous is easy when I stand close.

Quite a few of them are dressed like me. Furs, straps, the whole look. Not one bathrobe among them. I check twice. So I go to them between verses and suggest they buy a bathrobe. It would look so much nicer. Soft and comfy. That's what true barbarians do.

Over by the pillar a woman finds Soma. "You are the protector of the Glades, and I was wondering if you would be willing to do a blessing for my garden."

"What, with Meril's staff?" Soma says. "I didn't know the staff could do blessings!" And, "As a druid? I don't know."

The woman doesn't mind. She wants all of them. "There's plenty of gods, why worship only one? You have to have gods for different purposes." Maybe a little of Mielikki's blessing too. She is asking a tortle with a staff.

"I guess I can fake it. I'm not exactly trained in the religious arts, you know? And I'm following — there are these Lathandrians, or Lathandarites, or whatever." Then, "I think we can arrange that. What is it you would like out of your garden?"

"Oh, just that it grows, and it's beautiful and lush, and that nothing…"

"Life to Spring." Soma lifts Meril's staff and says her words, and the blessing takes. From where I stand with the basket I can't tell a faked blessing from a real one. Neither can the woman. She leaves glowing. Soma watches her go. "Yeah, she'll come around, but after Valphine, she's thinking this Lathander's got the right idea."

The concert ends. The service is over. Some folks are still sitting around, and there is the man in the green suit. Cullen Sharpe. House Margaster. He compliments us on the new name, very impressed with it.

"We are Lathander's death! Yeah!" Vukradin says.

Cullen mentions in passing that House Margaster always delivers the goods.

"Ominous, given the trade route shut down, or whatever you want to call it," Soma says.

He sees my basket. I hold it out. While I wait I try to count what's in it. Many coins. I lose count.

"You're taking up donations for the church, right?" Vukradin says.

"Yeah, of course, yeah. It goes to the temple, yeah. Yeah. Absolutely."

"It's normal to make donations while you're at church, that's pretty typical."

"Tithing? Sure," Soma says.

Cullen pulls out some coins. Holds them over the basket. Then he decides he doesn't want to donate. He puts them away. He smiles at us instead. The smile is not coins.

"Who is this guy, again?"

Green suit. House Margaster. Right.

"It's a great smile," Soma says.

"Who stopped the other guy from attacking us," Vukradin says.

Right, right. I tower over him. I make my voice boom with the spell again.

"Why aren't you donating?"

He looks at me awkwardly.

"The better question is, do you fear the light?" Valphine says.

"There you go."

"Well done," Vukradin says.

I flex. One pec, then the other. Boom. Boom.

Cullen notices everybody staring at him. "Well, never let it be said. House Margaster is very happy to donate to Lathander's Temple." And he applauds Valphine's sermon.

"Yes. Hollow flattery is not the…" The word is in my head somewhere. It doesn't come. "…full, brother. Now, spill the coins."

He gives some coins. Some coins. No. My hand closes over the whole pouch. He holds on. It doesn't matter. My hand is bigger. The pouch comes out of his fingers and goes in the basket. "Thank you for your generosity, brother."

I walk away.

"Did you just… Did you just rob him?" Vukradin asks.

"Definitely? I didn't rob him? I give him a nudge to be more generous."

"Of course not, Vukradin. This was a generous donation that I was happy to make," Cullen says, fast.

"Absolutely."

"Thank you for supporting Lathander's death," Vukradin says.

Cullen pauses. "Vukradin, as you know, we all want the same things." The same things. Vukradin gives things back to people. Cullen makes people who need things given back. That is not the same thing.

"I'm Lathander's death? You support Lathander's death. We want the same thing!"

"Exactly! Why would there be any confusion?"

"The world is ugly," Soma says.

Then the whispering. Two of them, heads together, one saying, "I heard Lathander himself walk the surface to mark her personally."

"Sure, yeah, you should go with that," Vukradin tells Valphine.

Valphine is in their faces before the whisper finishes. "And what do you think?"

They go stiff. Mouths half open. Eyes down, then up. "I think that's true. I think that's true."

"Yeah, and doesn't that call for a generous donation?" I put the basket right under them.

"Dude, you're turning it into a shakedown!" Vukradin says.

"Isn't that what's true?"

"You know, properly religious," Soma says.

I lean in. The man doesn't fold. He just beams at me, blessed by Lathander right now, too full of light to be scared of anything my size. Vukradin steps up next to me and glares too. His glare is not a big glare. Then he hums me a little run of notes, and the notes get into my shoulders, and I lean in the rest of the way. Come on, I tell them. Cough up some coins. They cough up. The basket gets heavier.

"We need to not just offer donations, we need to do the whole business model thing. Do the pilot, the business rollout," Valphine says. She is starting to think like Linene. Good.

Which reminds me. "By the way, I meant to ask, is there any magic way to plant a bug onto somebody or someplace?"

They look at me. "So that we could spy on them, listen to their…"

"Yeah, you can totally do that," Vukradin says.

"So, how does that work?"

"Well, we don't have that spell."

"Well, we need to look for that spell, and one of us needs to get it, so that we can listen to Cullen's private conversations and find out how their business works, so that we can do some sabotage."

"I'd be able to buy a scroll, right?" Soma says.

Then Lord Neverember is standing with us, and Aldus is at his elbow, talking to him nervously.

"Welcome to our church," Valphine says.

"Did you call it our church or my church?" Vukradin asks.

"Our church is fine, yeah." And, "They're not branding me as separate from the establishment."

"I thought your intention was to create a schism."

"No, no, I intend to take over the whole religion, not… Not create a schism."

Neverember looks at her and smiles. Then he walks away.

"My lord… We're joining you for dinner tonight, isn't that right?" Vukradin calls.

Another smile. Tight-lipped. Aldus hurries in. "Yes, yes, have no worries, have no fears. Dinner, dinner is going to happen. Lord Neverember is very busy and preoccupied."

Valphine's golden eyes follow the lord. The lord looks at Cullen Sharpe and his face goes sour, like the man is a smell. She says so, quiet.

"Aren't we all?" Soma says.

"My lord, is there anything that we can do to assist you? You may think about that, and if you wish to discuss that tonight at dinner, we'd be very glad to hear of anything we could do to assist you," Valphine says.

"Yes, I think I have many questions, as long as we keep in mind the need to circulate the wealth and not hoard it."

"To circulate the wealth…"

"He wants us — he wants the money," Soma says.

The lord looks past Cullen, who is schmoozing at Vukradin. Looks past me and the pouch I took.

"He gave it willingly," Soma says.

"I don't like anything about Cullen Sharpe. Like, I'm not onto him at all," Vukradin says.

But Neverember is fixed on Valphine. "I truly do admire your focus on eliminating those who are enemies of Lathander."

"Enemies of the light, yes."

"Yes, I think that is an excellent, focused use of power. But I am curious how that message has been received by Lathander's clerics. It seemed to be more about embracing goodness and love for all."

"Well, that is the old way." Then, "Or… dedication to the mission and the glory of the Morninglord, rather than such passive…"

He smiles at her. "Yes, I think, Valphine, your approach to the Morninglord's powers is something that we should keep talking about. I can see how we could work wonders together."

"I look forward to your partnership."

"Yes, I look forward to your partnership as well, Valphine. It's important in these trying times that we have people in our… people who see the need for direct action and…" He walks off in the middle of it, mumbling about power and the need for exercising it.

"We are men of action, lies did not become us," Soma says.

Valphine finds the armorsmith in the crowd and blesses him in front of the whole hall. Then the sacrament.

"Are you not administering the sacrament to everyone?" Vukradin asks.

"Anyone who wants."

"I think you should be calling them up and having them stare into the sun and experience the blinding Light of Lathander."

"This is the way."

Vukradin encourages them forward, and one by one they come up and look into the sun. Most of the congregation has already gone home. The ones who stay, stay all the way. Eyes streaming, faces up. They are happy.

"How many devout followers does Valphine have in this city?" Vukradin asks.

"And how do we invest in the local lens crafters?" Soma says.

Eight.

"8?" Vukradin says. Then, "Oh, those are rookie numbers."

It's a start. Eight here, and the temple back home. The numbers stack.

"This way, each of them convert two people, and those convert two people," Soma says.

While that's going on, a merchant works his way over to Soma. Exile clothes, my look, sewn by somebody who was never exiled from anything. Soma stands off to the side, observing, like she always does. He asks her anyway. "Can I license your look? Your turtle look."

"I was born with it, I don't know that I have any ownership over it."

"Well, I was kind of hoping that we could call it the SOMA line."

"Don't sell out," Vukradin says.

"Don't sell out, but wait a minute, wait a minute, wait a minute. So, my red cloak's not too far off from a bathrobe. Couldn't we sort of combine production lines here?"

"There's an idea," Vukradin says.

They look at me. "Sorry, I was deep into researching how we could spy on Cullen." I pat the basket. "Now that I have his purse, I'm looking for some sort of magic spell or artifact that I can use to plant a bug on him."

"In the meantime, we're trying to figure out if we can share productions of robes and soma cloaks," Soma says.

"Of course."

The merchant looks between us, waiting for a yes or a no.

"That sounds like a yes," Soma says.

"I completely trust my partners."

The basket is heavy on my arm. Best service I have ever attended.

## Vukradin — The Alagondar Inheritance

The Spire's side hall smells of beeswax and morning incense, and Perrin is already seated there with three people arranged around him like a display of evidence. He keeps glancing at Brewbarry. Every time Brewbarry shows his teeth, Perrin squirms in his chair. The Necklace of Fireballs has ridden in my pack since Icespire, catalogued, held in trust, and Valphine set the release terms herself: three independent witnesses, none of them Margaster. Terms met. Item returned. That is what honest commerce looks like, and I intend to conduct it properly.

The first witness does not wait to be asked. "I'm really glad that Perrin's gonna get that necklace, it's a great symbol of the rightful rulers of Neverwinter," and more of the same, revolution this, rightful heirs that. Valphine watches him through the entire recital and pronounces him sincere: a true believer in the revolution, no coin under the words. Enthusiasm is not evidence. But a witness who believes his own testimony is not a forger, and I have seen enough forged writs in this city to appreciate the difference.

The second is a genealogist, and he lights up at Soma before anyone introduces us. "Oh, Soma!"

"Yes…"

"I had a wonderful conversation with Boney."

"Oh, you found my horse."

"Yes, yes. It turns out his interest in Alagondar genealogy is almost as encyclopedic as mine."

"Yeah, he had a lot of free time." Years entombed with Lady Alagondar's dead. Boney read the whole family.

"He told me he studied the corpses and the dead and the relationships between the dead, and he had some excellent insights. I mean, he's about to go geek out on genealogy if you don't let him…"

"I'm here for that," Soma says.

"Why would we stop him? This is what we wanted to hear." Provenance. Chain of ownership. The whole practice of returning things stands on it.

"I find these, the provenance of this thing," Soma says, and I could hug her.

The genealogist gathers himself. "Well, I just want you… I cross-referenced Boney's claims and Perrin's claims, and it is in fact true that Perrin is a descendant, perhaps one of the primary descendants of the Alagondar family, and I was very pleased to see that Boney was able to…"
