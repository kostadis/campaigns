## Flags

### [1] Internal inconsistency — he reaches for two different weapons three lines apart

> My hand was on the halberd shaft under the table already.

then:

> "Not everything is solved with violence," Soma said. An axe settles a forged writ in one stroke.

**Why:** The halberd is correct per the spec ("In combat he is a physical presence: halberd, rage, growing large"), and the axe is correct as verbatim — Brewbarry says "while I seize my axe" in scene 04, straight from the source. But the narration's *own* prose picks up the axe two lines after putting his hand on the halberd, so the reader watches him hold two weapons. The genre spec's instruction is to "Name specific objects... rather than gesture at categories," which only works if the object stays the same one.
**Suggested rewrite:** An axe settles a forged writ in one stroke. → A halberd settles a forged writ in one stroke.

### [2] Non sequitur — the reason he holds does not follow

> An axe settles a forged writ in one stroke. But Soma is old and I held.

**Why:** Soma's age is not a reason to hold; the sentence drops the connecting idea and lands as a shrug. Failure-rule 1 is explicit — "Do not make Brewbarry sound stupid. He is simple, not dim. Simple means he gets to the point fast; stupid means he misses things." As written he misses the point of his own sentence.
**Suggested rewrite:** A halberd settles a forged writ in one stroke. Soma has seen more of these than I have. I held.

### [3] Voice spec conflict — the "and yet" signature is missing from the one beat it exists for

**Why:** The spec singles out one construction as definitional: "When rage and mercy collide, he names the contradiction in plain words and lets the pause stand: 'My rage consumes me. And yet—' The and yet is who he is." Scene 06 *is* that collision — a caught thief walks out unbled and he lets it happen — and the construction never appears. The scene's closing lines circle the same feeling ("Nobody bled. He still won the fight.") without ever naming the contradiction the way he names it.
**Suggested rewrite:** Fold it into L59: *In the tribe we knew what to do with a caught thief. My rage was up. And yet — nobody stood up, and I let go slow.*

## Verdict

Three small fixes and this scene is done: it has zero narration em-dashes, zero register-wrong vocabulary, and it renders Brewbarry's observational lens (hands, weight, who can be found and beaten) more consistently than any other file this session. "He gave me air" and "I watched the green suit go out the door. Nobody bled. He still won the fight. I do not know how to train for that one." are the strongest writing in the whole narration set.
