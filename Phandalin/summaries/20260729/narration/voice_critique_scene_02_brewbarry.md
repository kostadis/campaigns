## Flags

### [1] Em-dash — narration level (mechanical scan A)

> Prutha was gone too — sent on a crusade to make orcs love holy pain.

**Why:** Brewbarry thinks in short declaratives; the period is more in-voice than the dash, so the conversion improves the line rather than merely satisfying the scan.
**Suggested rewrite:** Prutha was gone too. Sent on a crusade to make orcs love holy pain.

### [2] Em-dash — narration level (mechanical scan A)

> There were two more rocks up there — Pirates' Skyhold, Fisher's Float.

**Why:** Same. An appositive list joined by a dash is a writer's construction; Brewbarry names things one at a time.
**Suggested rewrite:** There were two more rocks up there. Pirates' Skyhold. Fisher's Float.

### [3] Voice spec conflict — the GM's lore dump was assigned to Brewbarry's interior

> The story of the rocks is old. A magic sickness broke chunks of the ground loose, all over Toril, and people here chained a few down before they flew off to the Sword Coast.

**Why:** Failure-rule 6 — "Do not over-explain his interior. If a paragraph of inner monologue is needed, it is too long." L33 is a single 200-word block carrying the earthmote history, Castle Never, the Spellplague and the moonstone masks, with subordinate clauses ("before they flew off") he does not build. The content belongs; the sentence architecture is the narrator's, not his.
**Suggested rewrite:** The story of the rocks is old. A magic sickness broke the ground. Pieces flew up. People here chained a few down. Now the finest inn in the city sits on one.

### [4] Voice spec conflict — knowing wit

> Vukradin plans invasions the way I count drinks. It calms him.

**Why:** Failure-rule 2 — "Do not give him sarcasm. He does not quite have it." The self-deprecating analogy is a wry construction that positions him above the observation. Failure-rule 9 also applies: "Do not make him philosophize about Vukradin." "It calms him" alone is exactly right and is doing all the work.
**Suggested rewrite:** Vukradin plans invasions. It calms him. I let him.

### [5] Cliché / literary construction

> Now the whole city fights to eat where I used to hurt.

**Why:** The chiasmus turns on "hurt" doing double duty (bad ale / his own pain) — a workshopped turn. The three sentences before it are excellent flat Brewbarry and the flourish undercuts them.
**Suggested rewrite:** Now the whole city fights for a table there.

### [6] Rhythm — a run-on where his register is short

> But I remembered the gate guard, the one who wanted Vukradin to get him tickets to the hardest place in the city to get a ticket to eat.

**Why:** "tickets to the hardest place in the city to get a ticket to eat" doubles back on itself inside one clause. The spec's whole rhythm is "He states what is true and moves"; the examples file has nothing longer than a line.
**Suggested rewrite:** But I remembered the gate guard. He wanted Vukradin to get him a table at the hardest place in the city to get a table.

## Verdict

The lore paragraph at L33 is the one structural problem — the GM's exposition landed inside Brewbarry's head with the GM's sentence structure intact, and it is the only place in the scene where he stops sounding like himself. The closing beat ("The statue dug into my shoulder. Up top, the rich were chilling and relaxing. I wondered if their ale was good.") is the best three sentences in the file. Spot-edit L33 and the two dashes; leave the rest.
