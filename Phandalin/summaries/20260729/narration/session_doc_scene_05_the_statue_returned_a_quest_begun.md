The Moonstone Mask reserves its best table the way Menzoberranzan reserves its best knives. Lord Cassian Meliamne sits at it, a sea elf, still as tidewater, and he recognizes Vukradin before we have crossed half the floor.

"Vukradin. You have the statue. Good." Then, without ceremony: "My cousin told me someone was bringing it."

Then, flat as a blade laid on the table: "She was killed. Eight days ago. House Margaster."

"I thought you should know before you handed it to anyone."

In the Underdark, this is how a negotiation opens. Mention the corpse first. Watch what the other party does with the corpse. But the sea elf is not negotiating. He is warning. A House killing, eight days cold, and he names the House aloud in a public room. Either grief has made him careless or grief has made him honest, and I have not yet decided which is more dangerous for him.

"Why… why do I need to know that?" Vukradin asks.

"Well, I mean, I don't want you— I wanted you to know, before you hand it over to anyone else."

"Why would I do that?"

Confusion crosses the sea elf's face, genuine confusion, the kind you cannot rehearse. "Because they would offer you more money?"

"Money? You were gonna pay me for the statue?"

I must insert myself, because someone at this table has to live in the world as it is. "Of course he's gonna pay you for his statue."

"Look, this is your statue, and I'm returning it to you, because it's the right thing to do, and… here you go."

"For free drinks," says Brewbarry.

"So we gotta go. We're gonna go," Vukradin says, already turning, as if virtue must be fled from the moment it is committed.

"No, no, please sit down." Cassian clicks his fingers, and drinks arrive.

"Let's stay just for a little while, please," Brewbarry says.

"Oh, alright. Well, that'd be good."

Vukradin cannot leave it alone. "You realize the world has you wrapped around their finger, man. All they have to do is give you alcohol, and you'll do anything."

"And… is there a point?"

I stifle a laugh. The Goliath's honesty is a cleaner instrument than the Bard's ideology, and it cuts him every time.

We play for the room, and the Moonstone patrons pay in coin that even Vukradin cannot object to. When the music ends, Cassian sets the statue on the table and looks at it a long moment.

"She was 12 when she last saw this."

"What's that? How old was she?"

Several hundred years old. A baby, by elf standards, when she last held it.

"Alright, and then— so hundreds of years ago. That's amazing that they would even remember it, or know about it, or anything."

Cassian describes the statue in intimate detail, every chip and curve of it a family memory. Elara was ecstatic when word came that it had resurfaced. She waited for Vukradin at the party. He never came, because he was off saving the world from the dragon.

"Just guilt us for that," Soma mutters.

I study the sea elf's face the way my mother taught me, hunting the seam where the performance is stitched to the man. There is no seam. He is not lying. The grief is real, and real grief is a rarer thing than gold in either world. I find myself almost respectful. Pain worn openly, without leverage sought. *How curious.*

"Although I realize that it would be insulting to you, Vukradin, to give you money for this."

"Feel free to make a donation to the music studio, though."

"I feel that a small donation to the music studio and to your traveling companions would be appropriate."

"Makes sense."

Fifty gold pieces to each of us, and fifty to Vukradin, laundered through the studio. "I like that it's compartmentalized money," the Bard says, pleased with himself. He does not want to offend the Bard, so the donation goes to the studio and the thanks go to the traveling companions, independently. Earmarked trust. My siblings would build a religion on a loophole that elegant.

"I was the one that unearthed the thing," Soma says.

"I guess we could have the studio fund. I guess we could add that."

Five hundred and fifty gold pieces in the studio fund now. The Bard's temple grows on tithes he refuses to call tithes.

Then Cassian pauses. I watch him weigh it, and watch the statue tip the scale. "Elara was investigating something. I don't know whether I should tell you it, but… Well, you did bring this. You did bring this artifact."

"She was investigating the interplanar shipping device at the docks before she died. The displacement manifold. It stopped working about 7 weeks ago. The commission was operating it as a commercial service. Goods arrive in Neverwinter by sea, then the manifold distributes them to stores across the region."

"Lionshield Coster was their primary client. All of their regional distribution ran through it. When it went dark, their store stopped receiving goods. Nobody official will say why. She believed the timing wasn't coincidental."

"I told her that this wasn't her business, but she was very determined to go look into this."

*There.* Not the statue. Not even House Margaster. A device that folds the planes for commerce goes dark, officials go silent, and the one determined person asking why goes into the ground eight days later.

"Since you did such a good turn and found this — I would be much appreciative if you were to see if there was any connection between her investigations and her death."

"I pass on this. The device had a regular technician, a gnome. Elara described him. She met him twice at the docks when she was tracking the shipping gap. Smaller, older, very precise, the kind of person who asks questions that go past the job."

"She wrote that he seemed more interested in why the planar routing worked than whether it was functioning — like he was mapping something. She found it unsettling at the time and didn't know why."

"He hasn't been seen since the manifold stopped. She didn't have a name for him, just the description and the questions he asked."

"Planescape format," I say. A technician repairs. This one was surveying. One does not map the routing of a thing one intends to leave standing.

When we press for a name from the Commission, Cassian only spreads his hands. "I didn't have it. This was one of Elara's things. She was the one who was interested in these things. I personally believe that when it comes to the Commission, one should keep one's distance."

The caution of a survivor. In the Underdark we call that wisdom, and we call the dead cousin the price of not having it.

"Well, thank you, Lord. Oh, I did have one other message to deliver," Vukradin says.

"Yes."

"The order — we brought this to you under the auspices of the Order of the Gauntlet."

Cassian smiles, and pauses. "They are truly one of the few heroes of the realms."

He means it. No irony beneath it, no debt being recorded. I catalogue that too, alongside the dead cousin, the dark manifold, and the small, precise gnome who asked why instead of whether, and then was gone.
