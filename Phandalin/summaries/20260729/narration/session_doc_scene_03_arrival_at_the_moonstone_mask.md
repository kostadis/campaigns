We leave the Protector's Enclave, all polished stone and the quiet signs of the rich and powerful, and within a hundred steps the docks turn grungy — tar, fish, rope, the honest smells of a neighborhood that works for its coin. The Common Chord slides past on our right, Vukradin and Brewbarry's old haunt, and I watch the Bard's chin lift a fraction as we pass it. Nostalgia, or grievance. With him it is usually both.

The Moonstone Mask does not sit on the docks. It floats above them. Chunks of the city torn loose by the Spellplague, rocks adrift in the sky, tethered down and reached by teleportation circles. The Overbrighters call this an enchanting city feature. I call it what it is: a wound in the land that industrious people leashed and rented out. Magic transformed this ground and they built an inn on it. I almost admire them.

"Is it, like, 10 feet? Is it, like, 100 feet?" Vukradin asks, squinting up at the underside of the mote. It is neither — the rock hangs asymmetric, the middle far deeper than the edges.

"It's like a cone, yeah," says Soma.

A cone. Which means hollows. If there are catacombs and dungeons under that inn, they would fit naturally into that cone, and in my experience the finest establishments always keep their truest business below the floor. I say nothing. One does not spoil a surprise.

Neverwinter's finest, most luxurious inn, named for the half-masks the staff wear, lined with glowing moonstones. A place where the rich and the powerful go to chill out and relax. Vukradin's jaw is already set.

"They always snubbed me. They were never willing to host me."

Very few places were willing to snub him, in his day. He knows it.

"Yeah, but this place was not willing to host me."

"You snubbed them before they could snub you," Soma offers.

"There you go, there you go. He's snubbed by these rich assholes."

At the threshold Vukradin peers about, hunting for whatever a Bard hunts for, and finds nothing. I do not have to hunt. I simply see it, the way one sees a blade before it is drawn: everybody here is very — all the waitstaff is very pretty. All of them. A mix of genders, species, an aesthetic arranged for every possible appetite.

"I mean, that makes sense, right? It's a high-end establishment, they're not gonna have ugly people," Vukradin says.

"Beauty is in the eye of the beholder, come on," says Soma.

No. Uniform beauty is not taste; it is recruitment. In Menzoberranzan, a house that staffs itself entirely with the beautiful is selling something beyond wine, and buying something beyond coin — usually secrets. The staff wear sheer black, the moonstone masks, and each an amulet, all matched. Matched jewelry is issued equipment.

"What does the amulet look like?" Vukradin asks, then answers himself. "Okay, so it's not — not an amulet of Talos."

"That we know of," says Soma. The tortle is learning.

The main hall is warm and inviting, an impressive hearth, kitchens breathing haute cuisine, a grand spiraling staircase climbing toward the guest rooms. Polite conversation everywhere. We stand in the middle of it looking thoroughly uncouth and seemingly out of place, and I find I enjoy being the wrong note in their music.

A staff member glides over, mask glowing. "Oh my. Vukradin, you've come to our establishment."

"Hi."

"Hey, baby," says Soma.

"Yeah, we expect free drinks," says Brewbarry.

"We're looking for someone, maybe you can help us, since you seem to know who I am," Vukradin says.

"Oh, well, of course, for you, Mr. Vukradin, anything you would like."

"We're looking for Elara."

Her eyes suddenly grow cold. The mouth keeps its trained curve, the posture stays supple, but behind the moonstone mask the eyes shutter like a house locking its doors. I know this pause. It is the pause in which a servant chooses which truth she is permitted to serve. I lean in, delighted.

"Didn't you hear?"

"Nope."

"She's dead."

"Oh, fiddlesticks," says Soma.

"What happened?"

"Well, there was a big party. And it ended in a catastrophic, catastrophic, spectacular and bloody disaster. But I have good news. Her brother is over there. Maybe you want to go talk to him."

*Good news.* The cousin of the dead woman, seated in the dining room, offered like a dessert course.

"Definitely. But tell me more about this bloody attack — what happened?"

"Well, there was a festival. She was throwing an event. And during the event, something happened. I wasn't there, so I wouldn't know."

"Oh, okay. You just heard, but…"

"Yes, and she passed away. It was quite tragic, even beyond the ability to be raised. Even if her brother did want to raise her."

There. *Even if he did want to.* In my mother's house, that sentence would be read aloud at the trial. A body destroyed beyond raising is not misfortune; it is craftsmanship. Someone paid extra.

The truth is barred to this woman, so I do not ask for it. "What do you suspect happened?"

"Also, you mentioned her brother did not want her up?" Soma presses, less gently.

"Or maybe an easier question. What do people say happened?" Rumor is the one coin a servant may spend freely.

"Well… I have to look that up, give me a second."

What comes back is a feast. Some say it was a love affair that went sideways. Others say a raid. Yet another group says a magical spell that went sideways. Everybody has a very different version of the facts — which means the facts have been scattered deliberately, seed thrown to too many birds.

"But Dagult Neverember has declared that the death of Elara was a tragic accident, and that we were all to mourn her passing and move on."

A ruler declaring an accident and prescribing the mourning schedule. In Menzoberranzan, the Matron who names the death an accident is usually the one who purchased the knife. How deliciously familiar the Overbright can be.

"Thank you for explaining. That was very helpful," I say, and mean every word.

"Plenty of things happen," says Vukradin.

She offers drinks, then turns to Brewbarry and blinks. "Oh my, I didn't realize that the Brewbarry aesthetic was something that people would wear in the Moonstone Mask."

"To what now? Where are the drinks?"

"You should show her the robes, man," says Vukradin.

"Yes! You know, I don't mean to be rude, but the whole — I always thought the Brewbarry aesthetic was — the Uthgardian aesthetic was anti-Moonstone Mask."

"Bless you. Do you want to see my robes? I'll give you a special."

She believes he is a costume. Half of Neverwinter dresses like him now, and she has taken the original for an imitation, some daring guest wearing the Brewbarry look into the one establishment it should offend.

"She doesn't realize it's Brewbarry, she thinks it's somebody dressed up like Brewbarry," Vukradin says.

"I haven't noticed. I don't know. Doesn't everybody dress like me?" Then, shrugging: "I don't think Brewbarry minds at all."

"Well, this is very daring of you. Maybe it's — oh, I know what it is. You're trying to — this is a counterstatement to the statement. I love it." And she rewards his fashion courage with a very large pouring of something that tastes amazing, by the way his eyes close over the rim.

"Finally. Now you're making sense. Thank you."

Then she looks at me, at the glow in my eyes, and softens like wax near flame. "Oh, you're a blessing upon me."

They all think it here — that I am the avatar of some good god. "He is the avatar of the good god of the Lathander, but it's…" Vukradin offers, garbling my sex and my station in one breath. I let it stand. I give her what she wants: the swivel eyes thing, the burning gaze rolled heavenward, benediction delivered. She will never know the god I serve blesses through pain, and that ignorance is its own small, exquisite cruelty. I enjoy it thoroughly.

"Alright, we're gonna go talk to the brother," says Vukradin.

"Want his sister alive," Soma says, dry as old shell.
