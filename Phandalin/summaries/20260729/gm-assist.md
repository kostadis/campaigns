# Chapter 47  Neverwinter, Never a Dull Moment

Date: 2026-07-29

## Summary
The party arrived in Neverwinter with a long-overdue task weighing on their minds — the return of an ancient, inscribed statue belonging to House Meliamne, recovered from a sunken shipwreck near a lighthouse what felt like a lifetime ago. Before setting out, they took stock of the city around them. The political landscape of the North was a tangled web of competing factions: the Lords' Alliance, a coalition of powerful city-states committed to firm borders and centralized governance; the Zhentarim, an amoral organization that viewed unregulated markets as a virtue; the Harpers, a secretive group devoted to decentralized power and the preservation of small, self-sufficient communities; the Emerald Enclave, still fractured in the wake of the War of the Dragon; and the Order of the Gauntlet, the only faction considered unambiguously good-hearted, though their recent entanglement with the Lords' Alliance had left many questioning their independence. Vukradin, the celebrated bard, had risen to considerable fame in the city — though for his earlier work rather than his more recent exploits — while Brewbarry's distinctive Uthgardtian look had become so fashionable in Neverwinter that people on the street were dressing in imitation of him.

The group made their way through the Protector's Enclave, a wealthy district bristling with signs of power and privilege, before crossing into the grungier dock area beyond. As they passed, Vukradin and Brewbarry caught sight of the Common Chord — their old haunt, once a desperate, half-empty tavern where Vukradin had been allowed to play and then promptly thrown out without pay. It was now, inexplicably, the hardest venue in all of Neverwinter to get a ticket to. The party pressed on without stopping, their destination the Moonstone Mask, Neverwinter's most luxurious inn, perched atop one of the city's three great earthmotes — massive chunks of land that had been torn skyward by the Spellplague of 1385 and tethered above the city by enormous chains. The floating island was connected to the docks by a bridge, and as the party crossed it, the grandeur of the establishment came into full view.

Inside the Moonstone Mask, the staff moved with practiced elegance, each dressed in sheer black and wearing the inn's namesake moonstone-lined half-masks alongside delicate amulets. A staff member approached the group and immediately recognized Vukradin, offering refreshments with a warm smile. Her expression cooled, however, when the party asked for Lady Elara Meliamne — the woman they had come to see. The staff member informed them that Elara had been killed eight days prior, during what was supposed to be a grand festival. The details were murky and contradictory: some said it was a love affair gone wrong, others a raid, still others a magical accident. What was clear was that Lord Dagult Neverember had officially declared her death a tragic accident and ordered the city to mourn and move on. The staff member noted, with a careful pause, that Elara's death had been so severe that even resurrection was beyond reach — and that her brother had not seemed eager to pursue it regardless. She directed the party to Lord Cassian, Elara's brother, who was seated at the finest table in the house.

Lord Cassian, a sea elf nobleman, rose to greet them as they approached. When Vukradin presented the statue, Cassian's composure cracked ever so slightly — he spoke of his sister in a deeply personal way, describing how she had last held the artifact as a child, hundreds of years ago, and how ecstatic she had been when word reached her that it had resurfaced. She had been eagerly awaiting the party's arrival at her festival, he said, but they had been otherwise occupied — saving the world from a dragon. Vukradin insisted that no payment was necessary, that returning the statue was simply the right thing to do, but Cassian would not hear of it. He pressed gold into the hands of each of the party's companions as a gesture of gratitude, and made a separate donation to Vukradin's music studio so as not to offend the bard's sense of honor. Drinks arrived, and the party settled in.

Sensing an opportunity, Vukradin and the group launched into an impromptu musical performance for the assembled elite of Neverwinter. The crowd responded with enthusiastic applause, though Valphine — her eyes now glowing with the golden light of Lathander — could see through the polished veneer. Some patrons were quietly over-intellectualizing the missed notes, and others were hiding their distaste behind practiced smiles, unwilling to appear uncultured in such company. Vukradin, undeterred, pivoted immediately from the performance to a fundraising pitch, extolling the importance of supporting the arts and encouraging the wealthy patrons to open their checkbooks for his upcoming music studio. The appeal was remarkably successful, and a considerable sum was raised before the drinks had even been refilled. The party also extended an open invitation to the crowd to attend a performance and sermon at the Spire of the Morninglord at dawn.

When the music faded and the applause died down, Lord Cassian leaned in and shared something that had clearly been weighing on him. Before her death, Elara had been investigating the malfunction of the Planar Manifold — an interplanar shipping device that had gone dark about seven weeks prior, cutting off regional distribution for the Lionshield Coster, their primary client. She had been in contact with a gnome technician regarding the device, but he had since gone missing. Cassian had no name for the gnome, only his sister's description and her unease. He asked the party, quietly and with evident gratitude for what they had already done, to look into whether there was any connection between the shipping disruption and Elara's death.

Before the party could fully absorb this new charge, a man in a well-tailored dark green suit arrived at their table. He introduced himself as Cullen Sharpe, an associate of House Margaster, and presented what he claimed was a Harbor Authority writ asserting a prior salvage claim on the very statue the party had just returned. The party examined the documents and found them deeply suspicious — the hallmarks of a shakedown rather than a legitimate legal claim. Lord Cassian, visibly rattled, clearly recognized the power House Margaster wielded in the city. Despite the party's silent disapproval, he paid Cullen two hundred gold pieces to make the matter go away, and Cullen transferred the writ with a satisfied smile. Before departing, Cullen turned to the party with a business proposition — House Margaster, he explained, had developed considerable expertise in regional distribution since the disruption at the docks, and would welcome a conversation about handling goods recovered in the Phandalin hinterlands. The party listened politely and said they would think about it.

As the group rose to leave, a steward in the livery of the Lord Protector intercepted them near the door. He introduced himself as Aldus and delivered a formal summons on behalf of Lord Dagult Neverember, Lord Protector of Neverwinter. Neverember, Aldus explained, had been following the party's activities with considerable interest — particularly their economic impact and the questions raised by the Candlekeep Report regarding adventuring parties and the circulation of wealth. There were, Aldus hinted delicately, comparisons being drawn between Vukradin's financial philosophy and that of a large reptile with a fondness for hoarding gold. The party resisted the suggestion of a morning meeting, citing sacred rites at the Spire of the Morninglord and a full schedule of obligations. Vukradin, ever the showman, proposed instead that the Lord Protector host a charitable gala to benefit the music studio — a soiree where business could be discussed over fine food and generous donations. Aldus, clearly charmed, agreed to carry the proposal back to his lordship and suggested that it would work best if Vukradin and the group attended a private dinner the following evening where Vukradin could pitch to Lord Dagult directly, promising to be very supportive of the idea and get his Lordship on board. In a moment of inspired goodwill, Vukradin offered Aldus an autograph, but the steward was too embarrassed to accept it for himself and claimed it was for his daughter. When Vukradin asked for her name, Aldus stammered out "Alducia"—a name Vukradin immediately realized was entirely invented on the spot. Vukradin produced the personalized autograph anyway, and the steward departed visibly moved, promising a meal that would not soon be forgotten.

With the evening winding down and their obligations mounting, the party took stock of what lay ahead. They had a dinner with the Lord Protector to prepare for, a dawn performance at the Spire of the Morninglord to deliver, a private meeting with Lord Cassian at his residence to arrange, and a growing mystery surrounding a dead noblewoman, a missing gnome, and a broken interplanar shipping device. Their next stop would be the counting house, where a letter of recommendation waited to open doors into the disrupted supply chain of the Lionshield Coster. Neverwinter, it seemed, was not going to let them rest.

## Locations
### Neverwinter
A major city-state on the Sword Coast featuring three floating earthmotes created by the Spellplague of 1385, tethered above the city by chains. Home to the Lords' Alliance and currently governed by Lord Dagult Neverember.

### The Moonstone Mask
Neverwinter's finest and most luxurious inn, situated on a floating earthmote tethered to the docks of the Protector's Enclave. Famous for its staff who wear moonstone-lined half-masks and amulets, and known as a gathering place for the rich and powerful. The site of the party's meeting with Lord Cassian.

### The Common Chord
A tavern that was once a desperate, low-end establishment where Vukradin used to perform for little to no pay. It has since become the hardest place in the city to get a ticket, now one of Neverwinter's most exclusive venues.

### Protector's Enclave
A wealthy district in Neverwinter characterized by signs of power and riches, contrasting sharply with the grungier dock area just beyond it.

### Neverwinter Docks
The site of the 'displacement manifold,' an interplanar shipping device that stopped functioning about seven weeks ago, causing regional supply chain disruptions for the Lionshield Coster.

### Spire of the Morninglord
A temple dedicated to Lathander in Neverwinter where the party plans to perform sacred rites and a musical sermon at dawn.

### The Counting House
A financial institution in Neverwinter where the party intends to secure capital and investigate trade disruptions related to the Lionshield Coster.

## NPCs
### Lord Dagult Neverember
The Lord Protector of Neverwinter and a member of the Lords' Alliance council. Though not appearing in person, he sends a formal summons to the party to discuss their wealth, cultural influence, and the economic situation in Phandalin. He is reportedly concerned that the party is hoarding gold like a dragon.

### Lady Elara Meliamne
The deceased sea elf noblewoman of House Meliamne and sister of Lord Cassian. She was killed eight days before the party's arrival in a violent incident during a festival, officially ruled a tragic accident by Lord Neverember. Before her death, she had been eagerly awaiting the return of a long-lost family statue and was actively investigating the malfunction of the Planar Manifold with a gnome technician contact.

### Lord Cassian Meliamne
A sea elf nobleman and the grieving brother of the late Lady Elara. He is deeply moved by the return of the family statue, which his sister had last held as a child hundreds of years ago. He rewards the party generously and asks them to investigate a possible connection between the shipping disruptions at the docks and his sister's death. He appears easily intimidated by the political power of House Margaster.

### Moonstone Mask Staff Member
A polite and observant woman dressed in sheer black with a moonstone mask. She recognizes Vukradin's fame, informs the party of Lady Elara's passing, and directs them to Lord Cassian. She mistakes Brewbarry for someone dressed in the popular 'Brewbarry aesthetic' rather than the actual person.

### Cullen Sharpe
A well-tailored associate of House Margaster who arrives at the Moonstone Mask to assert a legal salvage claim on the recovered statue. The party suspects his documents are fraudulent. After successfully extorting 200 gold from Lord Cassian, he attempts to recruit the party into a regional distribution partnership for goods found in the Phandalin hinterlands.

### Aldus
A steward in the livery of the Lord Protector of Neverwinter. He is a secret fan of Vukradin's music, though he embarrassedly claimed his autograph request was for an invented daughter named Alducia.

An ancient, inscribed family heirloom belonging to House Meliamne, recovered by the party from a sunken shipwreck near a lighthouse long ago.
