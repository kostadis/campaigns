# Chapter 48 The Broads of the Common Chord and the Board Laid Bare


## Brewbarry — A Banker's Revelation

We come out of the Spire of the Morninglord into the street. The necklace is done. Perrin Alagondar has his grandmother's fireballs back, which is right. Cullen Sharpe and House Margaster still sit in my teeth like gristle.

"That's dicks," Soma says. She means the Margasters. She is correct.

We are leaving when a man comes at us fast from the direction of the counting house. Small lungs, big hurry. Aurelan Vance. My banker.

"Aurelan Vance — which one was that guy?" Soma asks.

Mine, is which one. The man holding my bathrobe money.

"Oh, right, right."

The others remember him as the man I intimidated into working for me.

"The list of people Brewbarry has intimidated is…" Soma starts.

"Very large."

"What do you mean? Intimidated?" I say. "That was no such thing."

"I was just making friends with him. He's making me a favor, I'll do him one." That is friendship. It is simple.

Aurelan arrives huffy-puffy. Very excited. He stops, pats himself down, tugs his coat straight, gussies himself back into a proper banker before he lets himself speak. I like that. A warrior checks his straps before battle. Same thing.

"A greeting. Hello, my friend," I say.

"Yes, Mr. Brewbarry, I— is it Mr. Brewbarry? Is that the correct appellation? I'm sorry, I'm not familiar with Guardian norms."

Guardian. I like that better than most things I have been called.

"So just call me Brew, that's fine."

"Brew!"

"You're going by Brew now?" Vukradin says.

"Yes, well, yeah, it's a new thing."

"Don't like you, Brewbarry," Soma says.

"Only to his friends," Valphine says.

"Yeah, that… Brew. Brew." Vukradin turns it over in his mouth like he is checking it for cracks.

"To my banker friends," I say.

"And since we're on such friendly terms — my friends call me Ori."

"Whoa." A banker's small name. He hands it to me like a coin from his own pocket, not the bank's. This is a big thing. I know a big thing when I am given one.

"Thank you, Ori. So, speak your piece."

"Alright. Well, you asked me to find out who the gnome is."

"Yes, I did."

"And, well, after the performance I was thinking I would wait for you to come by the bank. But then I thought to myself: Valphine's sermon was inspiring."

"Oh, was it ever," Soma says.

"There was, yeah," I say.

"It was. And it inspired me to action."

"Great. Glad to hear it."

"Yes. Well, last night I decided to go over some of the paperwork related to Bimble, and here's what I…"

The name gets loose before he can finish.

"Enough," Vukradin says. Then: "We wrote the name down. It says here, Bimble." Then, louder: "Bimbo the Gnome!"

"No," Ori says. "Bimble."

"Alright, come on, this isn't a spelling fairy. Come on," I say. "Get on with it."

"Bimble, okay."

"Pronouncing the name, that's fine. Maybe he has a speech impediment," Vukradin says.

Ori pulls out a piece of paper anyway and shows us the correct spelling. B-I-M-B-L-E. It changes nothing. The party will say Bimbo all evening. I already know this.

"The Commission carried him on maintenance roll for seven— for eleven years."

"Eleven years," Vukradin says.

"Yes. The payments stop seven tendays ago — on the day, not the tenday. No severance, no final reconciliation, no forwarding instruction. That is not how a contract ends."

He starts on Mr. Brewbarry again and catches himself. Friendly terms now. "Brew, that is how a contract is abandoned!"

He says it straight at me. Real anger in him. Not blood-anger like mine. Paper-anger. But it stands up the same way in a man's chest. Somebody broke the rules of his hunt, and he wants them run down.

And there is more, and it is the one kind of trail Ori reads better than anyone. Bimble has drawn nothing since. Not banked. Not borrowed. Not left the city. "Your Harper friends would tell you the same if you had any, so somebody is keeping him."

Kept. Fed. Or.

"Sounds like he's feeding worms, rather than… But he's fitting him, but…" The thought does not come out clean. Worms do not draw a maintenance roll either. I let it go. "We'll get to the bottom of it, my friend. Right."

"Thank you."

"Well, you're very welcome. If you could find who is feeding him, I am certain you will find him."

Find the food, find the man. That is good hunting. Ori is a hunter after all. Just on paper.

"Who's feeding Bimbo," Vukradin says.

"Shut down? Who is feeding Bimbo?"

"Bimble. Bimble," Ori says.

"Bimbo," I say.

"Mmm — what should the name of this quest be?" Vukradin says. "Mmm. Chase."

"…the bimbo," I finish.

"Oh, boy. I— yeah, I could go pretty… yeah, I could go pretty dirty on this one," Vukradin says.

"I'm sorry, Undercommon is my native language," Valphine says.

"I'm just a pervert," Soma says.

*Marginal note in a later hand: "Bimbo" — not a name but a Dock Ward coinage, said of one pleasant to look upon and empty behind the eyes. That the Guardians fixed it so readily upon a missing clerk says more of their humour than of him. His name was Bimble Nackle. — Kostadinious the Sage*

"How can we go about finding this guy, this gnome?" I ask.

Soma has the answer in her stomach. The best turtle soup in the whole area, she says. The Board Laid Bare. Lim runs it. Good prices, good food, good atmosphere.

"I mean, I've been dying for this soup."

"Aww — a home away from home?"

Lim feeds people. She buys the food. She would see who buys too much for one mouth. She might have a bead on how a hidden man gets fed.

"Why'd she stop making it? Well, I guess we go to the Board Laid Bare, right?" Soma says. "Yeah, well, now we're back."

Soup first. Then the gnome.

## Soma — The Skeletal Horse Sighting

We are barely out into the Protector's Enclave, stone underfoot, the crowd doing what Neverwinter crowds do, which is move fast and look at nothing. One man breaks the pattern. He stops dead in the street, and he is looking at Boney.

"Oh, man. Boney is popular."

The man points a finger at the horse. "I've seen that horse somewhere!"

"Really?" Vukradin says.

"You've— you, you've seen them outside right now?" I ask. It seems the practical place to start.

No, he's seen such a horse before, he just cannot remember where. His hands work at his belt while he digs for the memory. He is not going to find it there.

I look at Boney. "Did you have flesh back then?"

The man begins to back away from us. He's quite sure he saw such a horse before, he can't remember, it must be the son, he's so sorry, he needs to go. The son. Of the skeletal horse. My bale has heard me tell guards that Boney's just a normal horse, and I am about to throw that story in the harbor, because this man is a shell sprout and shell sprouts need help.

"No, he talks."

The man stops backing away.

"Why don't you ask him? You know — ask Boney. Where might you have seen him, Boney?"

There is a moment of tangle over who is asking whom. "No, the other way around. I want the man to ask Boney, right?"

The man squares himself up to the horse. Shoulders back, chin out, feet planted. "I— have you— were you ever, were you ever fritting around outside of the walls of Neverwinter?"

Boney says yes. Of course he says yes. He was Lady Alagondar's; he has grazed more of this coast than this man has walked. "Been grazing all over the place."

The man hums. He thinks with his whole face.

"Well, I might have seen you on the field. I can't remember. I'm so sorry, this is just— there was something about the gate, something about the look. Maybe it was one of your children, Boney."

"Or a sibling…"

"Boney has kids?" I have hatchlings of my own out there somewhere. You would think I'd have asked the horse by now.

"Siblings, I don't know," the man says.

"Wait, are his kids skeletal?"

"Are they alive?" he asks back, which is a fair question and no help at all.

"Yeah, like— I mean, so hold on. This guy's seen this skeletal horse bouncing around outside before?"

He blinks at me, thoroughly lost. "So am I."

"Maybe Boney has a twin," Vukradin offers. "Most skeletal horses look alike to me."

"I mean, they're— there's no unique coloring pattern."

"Right. It's just bones, right?"

"Somebody— maybe it was somebody wearing the same tackle?" The man nods along like I've handed him a rope.

"Was he Bear? Was he,"

He talks over me, yes and no and you know, and then his eye catches on the tackle. Lady Alagondar's tackle, still buckled on Boney's back where she left it. "It might have been the tackle... It might have been the posture of the horse. I'm sure I've never seen a skeletal horse before."

"So now you're saying it wasn't a skeletal horse."

He goes still. No. No, he remembers where he saw this horse. Vukradin is watching him hard now, and I can tell what he's reading, because I'm reading it too: the man is sincere down to his boots. Every useless word of it, meant.

"I remember. It was the horse that was outside of the service where that charming cleric gave a speech."

*Charming cleric.* Valphine will want that carved somewhere.

"Oh, you saw him earlier today?"

"Oh, gods, you're wasting our time. Alright!"

"Yes!" Because he got there. Getting there counts.

I wave a hand at the street, at the whole fast-walking, nothing-looking crowd of them. "I've seen a lot of these people in town."

"Amazing," Vukradin says.

"Later today."

"I have seen him, yeah. Alright, bye!" The bard is already moving. "Why don't we talk to strangers?"

"Hey, he talked to…"

"Stop talking to strangers, please."

"A stranger is only a friend the tide hasn't brought in yet."

He walks faster. I match his pace the way I match everything. Slowly. Shells don't hurry, and neither do friends.

## Vukradin — Arrival at The Board Laid Bare

Half the shops in the Bluelake District are shuttered, which in this city means either bad business or bad news, and I have stopped assuming I can tell those apart. Soma leads. This is her ground; she walks it the way I walk a stage, without checking her feet. The district sign says Bluelake. I read it my way.

"Blue Light District, we're going to see."

Nothing. A performer knows the difference between a joke that failed and a room that wasn't listening, so I give it a second pass. "Blue Light District, really? No, it's really…"

"Red light is what you're thinking," Soma says, not slowing down.

And then, at last, the restaurant itself. Even Soma trips on the name: "It's been a long time to get to the Boar's Head — or Board Laid Bare."

"So many puns." I say it with respect. A name like that does half my job for me. "They just set themselves up. It's not even fair."

I stand in front of the door and turn the syllables over. "Laid. Bare. Bimbo… It's just…" The material keeps paying out on its own.

"There's a theme here, right?" Brewbarry says.

"Oh, the Hammock District," Soma offers, inventing geography, and I respect the escalation.

Somewhere in all of this I lose the thread entirely. "Why did it become— I forget why we came to this district."

"To get my food. I'm hungry."

"Oh — the soup. Okay, alright, we need a quest for that." An errand without a title never gets finished. This is true of errands and it is true of songs, so I give it one. "Soma… Soma's soup."

"Soup's good."

I try the establishment's full name at tempo and it doesn't scan, so I shorten it. "The BLB." Nobody objects. "It's the BLB."

The host meets us at the door and he is a warforged, tall and very still. Not still like a bored doorman. Still the way a lute peg is still: holding tension, refusing to move. He states nothing yet and already I can hear that his phrasing will be exact.

Soma looks him over and murmurs, "That kind of guy."

"Table options — what options are there? I'm curious," Brewbarry says.

"Three tables available. The table near the window offers the best view of the tower. The corner table is preferred by guests who do not wish to be observed. I recommend the corner table."

*Interesting.* A dining room whose opening offer is the seat for people who do not wish to be observed. I tuck that away.

"Window, please. I have guests today," Soma says, and then, quieter, "Normally I was here alone, right? Like, now I've got some company."

Alone. All those visits, one tortle at a window table with the best view of the tower. That picture isn't mine to say anything about, so I don't.

The count, though, is off, and I enjoy an honest count. "What was the third option?"

"Yeah, I heard three tables available. Window, corner, and…" Soma starts.

"Bar."

The truth settles on its own: there are two tables near the windows, not one. "Oh, perfect, we're gonna need two tables."

"Yeah, we'll just push them together."

Strictly speaking, we would all fit at one. Nobody consults strict speaking.

"Well, we'll do as we please," Brewbarry announces.

"We'll push them together. Brewbarry takes up a table almost all on his own. Yeah."

"He's, like, his own table, almost." Not mockery. Load-bearing fact.

Then the host turns to Soma. "I've seen you before. You are the one known as Soma, a friend of Lim's."

"Yeah, I've been here plenty of times. I'm glad you remember me."

"You haven't been here in 73 days."

To the day. Not a while, not a season. He has been keeping her account without being asked to keep it. I keep ledgers like that for coin, because coin drifts if you don't. He keeps one for a customer. Devotion or inventory, I can't tell yet, and both answers interest me.

"It's— it's been an interesting journey," Soma allows.

"I'm here to see Lim. I hope she's still around."

"Oh, Lim is very much around."

"What is your name, sir?" A being who counts your days deserves to be addressed properly.

"Bellows."

"Bellows! Bellows!" A name with air in it. Bellows feed forges and organs both. I approve completely.

And since we are asking questions: "Do you know a gnome named Bimbo?"

"No, I do not."

Flat. No pause to search whatever passes for his memory, and this is a being who produces a customer's absence to the day.

"Yeah. Okay."

"Do you see a lot of gnomes come through here? Probably not."

"What is a gnome like you doing at a place like that?" The riff lands nowhere in particular, but some riffs you make for yourself.

"Bimbling about," Soma says.

Bellows walks us to the table, seats us, and presents a handwritten card: fresh fish, vegetables, potatoes, two sauces. Handwritten. Somebody in that kitchen decides the menu daily and signs the decision.

"Soup, buddy," Brewbarry says.

"Just tell Lim I'm back, and she'll know how to." Soma lets it trail.

"The usual…" I add, as if I have one.

No alcohol anywhere on the card. I watch Brewbarry read it a second time to be certain.

And the prices. "That's good, we don't have very much money." This is not modesty. I have counted my purse, the counting did not take long, and the studio fund sits in a restricted trust where it belongs, untouchable even by me. Especially by me.

But the prices keep bothering me in the other direction. Every card, every stall, every taproom since we walked through Neverwinter's gates has been marked up, the trade routes disrupted, everything dearer than it ought to be. This card reads like the disruption never happened. Like the whole broken economy stops at Lim's threshold.

"Yeah, because it's farm-to-table. That's why we come here. It has its own supply chain right within the walls. It's— no, it's right there! It doesn't need any trade."

"Organic Fair Trade!" There it is. Honest food at honest prices while the rest of this city gouges its neighbors to cover its losses. Everything I have been preaching, set out on a handwritten card.

"There you go! That's a perfect explanation. I mean, that's why we always came here. It's a nice, locally sourced…"

A perfect explanation. I believe it, mostly, the way I believe a song I haven't heard the last verse of. Because if one dining room in Bluelake can hold its prices against a citywide disruption, then either Lim has solved something the rest of Neverwinter has not, or somebody is absorbing the difference. The first answer could feed a city. The second answer usually has a name attached.

"We should make a mental note to talk to your friend Lim and find out how," Brewbarry says. Linene has ruined him beautifully. He hears a price now and thinks in supply chains.

"She better come out and say hi."

The soup is ordered for the whole table, no squinting at the card, no negotiation. "For everybody. That's what we came here for."

"Trust someone to order for you," Soma says.

So I set the card down and I do. In Soma's world I am the guest, and the guest's job is to let somebody else conduct.

## Valphine Sotorra — Dining with Lim and Interrogating Bellows

The Board Laid Bare does not take orders. Lim carries out a tray of what she has decided we want, sees Soma, sets it down, and sits with us. "You came back? I thought you'd gone somewhere northern."

In Menzoberranzan, a host who chooses your food is a host deciding whether you leave. Here it appears to be affection. I withhold the verdict and watch the room instead.

Soma rambles through half a map of places we have and have not been, then gives up on geography entirely. "These guys are taking me all over creation." Lim stops mid-thought, studies the Bard, and asks, "you are the great Vukradin?"

"I don't know about great."

"I don't like your music," she informs him.

"That's fine," says Soma.

"That's fine," says Vukradin.

"It's too— it's not even useful." Then, without softening: "But everybody else seems to like it. Do you know that your place, where you used to play, is now the hottest ticket in town?" The Common Chord, trading on his absence. He mutters that maybe they owe him one. An honest critic in the Overbright: I mark the day.

Then the neighborhood report, delivered flat as inventory. The Tower District has gone quieter. "not safer. Quieter is different." Three weeks ago people dressed as Many-Starred Cloak associates went into the Cloak Tower by the basement stairs, spent the day, and "came out with nothing." She counts what leaves a building. Somebody taught her to, or something did. I keep it.

Lim turns the probing on Soma: stories of "some druid of Mielikki with some staff that's sprouting leaves," seen sitting next to "a cosplaying Brewbarry…"

"He's not cosplaying, either," Soma says. "The real deal." The real deal yawns, hugely, at all of us.

The food is fabulous. The turtle cuisine especially, raw and steamed, and Vukradin asks, "Does the food taste cheap as well?", because the prices are the wrong prices. I read the menu, the room, the two other occupied tables, and it holds together in one line: "Are we being charged a different rate from other guests in the tavern?" We are not. Every menu in the house is lower than it should be. Wrong prices are not generosity. Wrong prices are a subsidy, and a subsidy is a leash, and the interesting question is always who holds the far end.

Vukradin fishes. "Have any gnomes come through here?" Lim points at a gnome two tables down, in for lunch today. "Well, we're looking for a particular gnome." And she retreats into "I just— I just run a restaurant. I've got good suppliers, and—" and "Business is good," and pivots back to Soma's last two months. She is steering us off the supply question. Competently.

So the Bard deploys his craft. "Chef, this food is just outstanding." Then, "I was wondering, could you— could you show us how you do it? Can you show us the magic?" Lim ignites like a novice offered her first altar. "Guys, come on the food tour! This is gonna be awesome!" he calls, and pulls the whole warm center of the room into the kitchen with him. Expertly done. He will insist it was sincere.

I stay, and take my second reading, of Bellows at his station. Unintimidated, almost mechanical. And more: he cannot lie. Not that he refuses. The machinery of deceit is simply not in him. In my mother's house such a servant would be priceless, and would not survive a tenday.

Brewbarry stays too. "I'm thinking bathrobes. I need to get to the bottom of this." My pupil smells a supply chain.

"Hey, Bellows, what's up?" Nothing moves in his face. Good. "Yeah, hey, Bellows — this seems like a great restaurant. Why aren't there so many…" I gesture at the emptiness. "There should be more patrons here."

He looks around. "all but two tables are empty. How many more patrons do you want?" How deliciously literal.

"Well, with the quality of this food, it seems like there should be people lining up outside."

"Lim does not believe in anything other than word of mouth."

"What? There's no marketing department?"

He regards me as if I had asked whether water is wet. "I've had this conversation with Lim many times. She has explained that she wants word of mouth. There will be no advertising."

Brewbarry takes the blade. "And how the hell do you guys manage to keep your prices so low?"

"We have very good deliveries."

"Yeah?" A pause. "Elaborate."

"What would you like me to elaborate? When they arrive, how they arrive? What do the crates look like? Are they stamped? How many crates? What's contained in the crates?" A witness who drafts his own cross-examination. I could weep.

It comes out in clean strokes. Every night at the third hour after midnight, occasionally the fourth. "The rear door." "It opens onto a service lane. The lane connects to the aqueduct." Fresh goods must arrive nightly. "Makes sense," I say.

"How can such a fine gentleman as you not be aware of the details of who's doing the delivery?" Flattery folded inside the question. I taught him that.

"Two men bring me the food, bring the crates. Sometimes three. They are not the same two men." Pressed, he reconsiders with precision and enumerates: six, perhaps seven porters, rotating across the last thirty days. A rotation is discipline. Discipline is an organization. "I do not involve myself in the crates." "In how the crates are procured. I simply take the crates, which are wooden, rope-bound, wet on the underside." Wet on the underside. The aqueduct again.

From the kitchen, Lim's laughter carries, hysterical with delight, something about turtle soup and "I can't believe they believed you." They did once sell it, it seems, for a different clientele. Soma's people keep their own cruelties.

Then Bellows pauses and offers, unprompted: "but there was one interesting detail."

"Oh," I say.

"Yeah, yeah, please," says Brewbarry.

The order has stood at nine crates since midsummer. "We use 8. I have raised it twice."

"What happens to the ninth crate?"

"It does not come inside. It is set down, and it is not there in the morning. I have not asked. It is not billed to us."

There it is. The restaurant is not the customer; the restaurant is the cover. The cheap food is rent, paid in produce, for a drop point on an aqueduct lane, serviced by rotating hands in the dark hours. I close my hand around the fact and let it burn, and it is very fine.

Who else works here: several humans for dishes and serving, nothing more. Brewbarry straightens. "Alright, so we need to talk to Lim about this thing. Okay."

"Or why would we talk to Lim?" The Bard has returned smelling of soup. "Stake out the back door at the third hour after midnight."

"She's a friend, we don't need to spy on her. We just ask her."

Vukradin starts to argue that she may not want to be involved, and Soma cuts across him: "Already involved."

"If she doesn't, then we'll spy." Brewbarry shrugs. "I'm just trying to take the shortest route to get the information we need. A stakeout doesn't sound like it."

I let them argue doctrine. Both roads end at the same rear door at the third hour after midnight. I am content to wait and see which of them opens it.

## Soma — Lim's Secret Supplier

Brewbarry decides he is going to talk to Lim, and the way he does it is to walk into her kitchen. The kitchen is small. It has always been small; Lim cooks close, everything within one arm's reach of the stove. Brewbarry is not small. He has the dragon-slaying sword laid along one arm and the halberd in the other, and over the whole arsenal, the bathrobe. I look at the halberd. Then at the doorframe. Then at the halberd.

"I'll always keep my weapons on me," he says, catching the look. "Is that even a question to…"

He doesn't finish, and I don't press. My bale files in behind him, because none of us can leave anything alone.

He pretends to be interested in the cooking. He leans over a pot, sniffs, nods at nothing. He is waiting, and when Lim steps back from the stove to wipe her hands, he closes the distance. He asks her how she keeps her prices so low. "I'm getting into business myself, and I'm looking for good suppliers or delivery guys who could move around some goods." A pause, for innocence. "Apparently you have a very efficient delivery company that you're working with."

"Well, my supplier hasn't been affected by the changes," Lim says.

"Who's your supplier?"

Her hands go back to the ladle. She starts a sentence twice and lands neither.

"The trade secret," Valphine offers from beside the crates.

"It's a trade secret!" Lim says, grateful for the word.

"Just like the ninth crate, huh?" Brewbarry says. "Bellows was telling me about it." He lets it sit. "That you always order 9 crates, but you only use 8."

"I don't order 9 crates, I order 8. I don't know what Bellows is talking about."

"Really, because it's pointed out." Another pause. "Multiple times that there is a 9th crate that stays in the street and then disappears in the morning."

"Yes, and I don't know why they bring a 9th crate. I only order 8 crates."

"And so you don't want to tell me who this supplier is." She stirs a pot that does not need stirring. "No, come on. Help out a friend in need. Do me a favor, I'll do you a favor." The bathrobe sleeve slides down his forearm as he leans in. "Or I make you an offer you can't refuse."

He glances back at us. "Make her spill the beans."

"I mean, I don't want to torture an old friend," I say.

"We're not torturing her, we're just asking questions."

"I mean, watching you shake her down makes me a little uncomfortable."

"I'm not shaking her down. I'm just being— curious. I'm probing."

"Yep."

It pries something loose anyway. A group, she says. She made an arrangement with them. "That has been trying to keep local businesses in line, and believes in fair trade, organic goods." Her voice steadies as she goes, like she has said this part to herself before. "And not being trapped by the economic policies of Lord Neverember and the Neverwinter Commission, which are crushing…"

"Margaster?" Vukradin cuts in.

She shakes her head.

"Yeah, that was my question," Valphine says.

"Don't sound like Margaster," I say. "Sounds like the revolutionaries, actually. Right."

*Fair trade.* Somewhere under this city there is a gang quoting Vukradin.

"And where is this fine organization located? Who could." Brewbarry doesn't get further. She doesn't know where. She met a guy, through a guy. "Who said he could bring me the food at good prices."

Brewbarry straightens up, and the pretense goes with him. "Enough of this shit. We'll just take it out and talk to the guys when they show up, and find out who the hell they are. Alright? She doesn't want to help, she doesn't want to help."

The front half of my sentence dies in my throat. What gets out is: "Gotten up to since I've been gone, jeez."

Lim looks at the goliath, then at me. Not because of him. Because of me. How far back do Lim and I go? "Pretty far back." Far enough that she gives it up.

"There is a sewer system in the Bluelake District." She says it to me, not to him. "If you go in there, you should be able to find the suppliers."

"In the sewers?"

"They have to move the goods somehow."

Vukradin surfaces from somewhere near the spice shelf. "So you're telling me… That they are fresh." He swallows. "Food that you serve to your customers comes from the sewer."

"No, I said it gets moved through the sewer."

"Yeah, it's just transit, you know?" Brewbarry says, smoothing the whole thing flat with one hand. "Anyway. Fine cuisine."

Nobody has to draw it for me. The trade routes are held by cartels now; goods moving under the city are goods that pay nobody at the gate. Smugglers. "No doubt," I say.

"It's very natural to transport things in the ground, so…" Valphine says, and means it.

"The Underdark's quite nice. Sewers are…" Vukradin rallies. "Really great for delectable food."

Lim tells the rest of it plainly. They approached her once at the market. She made a deal. All she has to do is pay them on a regular basis. And she asks me, really politely, not to scare them away and not to ambush them, because she has a business to run.

"Yeah, but Lim." Her hands are already back at the pot. "You understand that these are not the types of people you should be in business with."

She's got a business, she tells me. She can give food at really good prices, with expenses in this city being what they are, and "they're not bad people."

"So you're saying the ends justify the means?" Vukradin, needling.

I put a hand to my chest. "This is not the Lim I once knew. Draw steel."

"Jeez," Vukradin says. "Not quite that bad."

"No, I'm kidding." *Mostly.*

"Does this organization have a name?" Brewbarry asks.

Not one she knows. She gave them the money and the food came.

"You paid them under the table, right?" I ask.

She takes my elbow and pulls me toward the stove, as far aside as this kitchen allows, which is not far. "I don't know where you've been."

"I've been in the real world."

"Because those of us who live inside of Neverwinter have had to deal with rising taxes, Neverember econ— ever since something happened with a traded system, all the prices have gone up through the roof."

"Yeah, and you've forced yours to stay down through…" I pick the polite word. "Dubious means. And that's putting it politely."

"Is the food good?" she asks.

"Yeah," Vukradin says. The kitchen is too small for asides.

The price is good, she starts, and if she was keeping, and the sentence breaks apart in her hands. Her knuckles are white on the ladle.

"Food is tainted," I say. Then, mostly to myself: "He's rubbing off on me with this Fair Trade stuff."

She sets the ladle down. What she says is: "you've changed."

"Yep." Shell answer, flat as a tide table. "The only constant in life is change."

She says all of it this time: "you've changed, and you're different. You're different. I remember a different Soma."

So do I. That Soma sat by the water and mended nets and let the world rot at its own pace. I don't say that.

"The Lim I knew wouldn't get in so deep with criminals," I say. "You could have just raised your prices."

She could have. She didn't want to lose her clientele; she wanted to keep her community establishment. I know it before she gets it out, because I know her.

"You know, those types of people, they come asking." Her back is to me now, the steam coming up between us. "They make demands in the future."

She stirs the pot. It still doesn't need stirring. Neither of us says the next thing.

## Valphine Sotorra — Planning the Stakeout

The Board Laid Bare has given us what it will give, and I stand in the evening street of the Bluelake District running the accounts. There is a sewer under this city, and we could go hunting an entrance to it. There is one other asset on the ledger, and it names itself in my mind before anyone speaks: the Harpers.

"Are we on speaking terms with the Harpers? I forget," Soma asks.

"I don't think we have burned them yet," I say. It is the correct formulation. In Menzoberranzan one does not ask whether an alliance is friendly; one asks whether it has been spent.

"Introduce them to Vukradin, and we will," Soma says. The observation is accurate. I let it stand.

"We are still looking for the missing gnome, right?" I ask, because Vukradin has already moved on to the next bright thing and Brewbarry follows Vukradin, and someone must hold the thread.

We are. Soma supplies the party's degraded name for him: "Bimbo." Bimble Nackle, once of the docks, now of nowhere.

So I assemble it, the way my mother taught me to assemble a rival house's supply lines before recommending which one to cut. Seven weeks ago the displacement manifold went dark. Regional distribution collapsed with it. Lionshield's store stopped receiving; we still carry Linene Graywind's written plea for help with supplies, an IOU of goodwill riding in a saddlebag. House Margaster now holds the port. And yet Lim's prices never moved. A merchant whose costs do not twitch when the artery is severed was never drinking from the artery. Lim's supplier was never on the manifold.

"And that's where Common Chord is," Vukradin says.

"It sounded like she'd changed suppliers," Soma offers, then catches her own correction: "Right, the new one boasts," and, "Mr. Takeover."

"Then Margaster controls the docks?" Vukradin asks. They do. "Which is where Bimbo the Gnome used to be."

And there it is, whole: a third trade route. One that outlived the manifold, pays no harbor authority, and moves at the third hour after midnight, down a service lane onto aqueduct steps. The surface dwellers treat this as revelation. I treat it as home. Menzoberranzan is nothing but third routes; the official commerce of the city exists so the real commerce has something to hide behind. House Sotorra ran two of these when I was a child. I could draw this one's discipline from memory: no ledger, no tariff, no daylight.

"So there's two parties that benefited from the manifold going broke," Brewbarry says. More than two; there are always more than two. But the number that matters is nine, because someone has been keeping himself fed on a stolen box, and I have already closed the circuit: a gnome vanishes from the docks the same season a crate begins vanishing from the night deliveries. I do not announce it. In my house you let another voice carry your conclusion; the conclusion still belongs to you.

"That would be the gnome," Vukradin says, a breath behind me.

"You think it's Bimbo?" Soma asks.

"Yeah. Delectable Delights. Who's feeding Bimbo? He's eating the ninth box."

"Good, perfect," Soma says.

"So we need to do the stakeout, see who's stealing the crate, find a way to the…" Brewbarry begins, and lands on the word he wants: "Organization." My pupil. I taught him interrogation; the appetite for structure came free.

"Find Bimbo — wait, do the stakeout," Vukradin says, reordering himself aloud. "But don't scare off…"

That is the true risk, and I weigh it as he says it. A route this disciplined survives by smelling watchers. Frighten the suppliers once and the third hour after midnight goes silent for a season.

"You know, turn into a cat or something and watch some…" Soma says.

"Yeah, exactly. The stakeout implies that they're not seeing us," Brewbarry says.

"I don't even have to be stealthy, I could just…"

"Morph spell," Vukradin says.

"Yeah, I mean, somebody's got disguise self or something, right? Like, we can…" Soma says, then improves her own offer: "Or actually, I've got familiars too. I can just drop one down."

Brewbarry adds an errand to his ledger: "I also need to find a magic shop so I can buy this listening stone and plant it on the Margaster's goon, whatever his name is." A goliath in a bathrobe proposing covert surveillance of a noble house. I decline to discourage him. The instinct is sound even where the instrument is loud.

The sewers, of course, hold rats. And where there are rats there are were-rats, and were-rats keep each other's acquaintance. Noble houses keep each other's secrets on the same principle.

"Were-rats? Don't they do security at a mine?" Brewbarry asks.

"They work for us," I say. Flatly, because it is simply true, and because the sentence amuses me: a drow exile and her congregation of adventurers, with lycanthrope hirelings on retainer.

"100%," Vukradin says. Some of them do, at any rate. "No, they're not all the same."

"Hashtag," Brewbarry says.

"It's like the shortcut," Soma says.

"Not all were-rats," Vukradin finishes. They finish each other's jokes, and finishing them is the point. In my mother's house we shared poisonings for the same reason. What matters underneath it: the were-rats of Mountain's Toe Gold Mine may well have come from Neverwinter, and we hold a name and a standing business relationship. Zeleen Varnaster. An angle. I set it on the shelf where I keep leverage that has not yet been needed.

"I think the most simple action right now is just for us to spy on the delivery, and see who's taking the ninth crate," Brewbarry says.

"Yeah, I mean, we can do this as many times as we need, right? Like, every other night," Soma says. Correct: a patient watch costs nothing and a hasty one costs the route.

"You can do it as a cat, or something that's unassuming," Vukradin says.

"Yeah, cat or— like, as I was saying, I've also got animal familiars I can summon and vision through."

"Spider," Brewbarry says.

"Crow, anything, right? There's plenty of unassuming things," Soma says. An owlbear, she concedes, remains beyond her reach for now; the aqueduct steps are spared that indignity. "But yeah, just create a familiar of, like, a cat." Then, running the menu: "Lizard or rat? Perfect, a rat." A rat, to watch for rats, in a sewer that may hold were-rats. There is an aesthetic rightness to it. "And they can stay way out of sight, and never see us. We'll hear everything."

For a moment the table's understanding wobbles, whether Soma herself intends to wear the whiskers. "No, I'm going to have a rat familiar," she clarifies.

Then the calendar gets weighed. "We have a lot of stuff we have to do today, right? Tonight, we're supposed to have dinner with…" Vukradin begins, and the name slides toward the wrong house before it is caught.

"Never— never," Vukradin says.

"Neverember, yeah," I supply. The distinction is not trivial; one lord wants to discuss our circulation of wealth, the other's house forges writs. The dinner, in any case, need not be tonight; tomorrow serves the Lord Protector just as well, and serves us better.

"And we can also split— I can stake out the…" Brewbarry offers.

"No, we don't…" Vukradin says.

"Delivery while you guys go to dinner."

"It's bad if there's a combat," Vukradin says.

"Just a stakeout — how would there be a combat?" Soma asks.

"We're very cool," I say. One learns, in the noble houses, to deliver assurance in exactly the tone that guarantees nothing.

"We're also supposed to meet with Lord…" Vukradin says. "Meliamne…" And: "For— for tea."

"Just tea, not tea and crumpets," Soma says.

"Tea and Intrigue," Vukradin says. The only honest name for it. Lord Cassian did not move us off the Moonstone Mask floor for the tea.

"The tea alone would have done it," Soma says.

The delivery does not move until the third hour after midnight. Hours remain, and hours unspent are hours wasted.

"At the Common Chord," Vukradin says.

"Yes. Yeah, that's fair," Brewbarry says.

"Yeah," Vukradin says. So we go to hear what Margaster's port sounds like from the inside.

## Brewbarry — Wandering the Blue Lake District

We walk to the Common Chord. I have news for the band.

At lunch the soup was good. Lim's turtle soup, at the Board Laid Bare. Warm all the way down, and warm things should be sung about. So I got quiet over the bowl while the others talked, and I wrote. Somebody looked over at the scratching.

"Lyrics, on the napkin."

Now, on the walk, I tell them I have laid down a new track for the band.

"You laid down a new track?" Vukradin stops walking.

Now we have a couple. I told them I was writing at lunch. Nobody listened at lunch.

"Guys, we're talking about soup."

"Do you— do you have it prepared?"

I hand him the napkin.

"Our next track."

"Yeah, kill some time," Soma says.

Vukradin reads it as we walk. He gives it the full voice. Everything sounds better in his voice. That is why I follow him.

"When the cold winds bite in Neverwinter Town, and weary travelers start to get you down. There's a savory cure that's famed across the land, a bowl of warmth from good ol' Lim's own hand."

*Good.*

"Turtle soup slow simmer just right. It fills your belly with a pure golden light. They serve it hot at the Board Laid Bare. A taste of home that's beyond all compare. Yeah, Brewbarry, Brewbarry,"

Then he stops. I know the stop. It is the stop before a no.

"We're a— we're— this is Lathander's Death."

He hunts for the soft way to put the hard part down.

"Really our style."

"Oh, come on, young man."

"We really appreciate the effort, and thank you, and we really, we love." The sentence runs out of road. "You're our biggest fan, man."

The biggest fan of my own band. I turn that over. It is a strange seat.

"But— we've got our artistic direction."

"It's a marketing jingle," Valphine says. She rules on it and the ruling is final.

"Call him Yoko and move on," Soma says.

"We're not getting married!"

"Yay."

I do not know who Yoko is. Probably somebody else who wrote about soup. The song is still mine. Lim still gets a fan.

The Bluelake District is wrong in a small way. A woman locks a door behind her. Then she locks it again. It was already locked. My neck knows before my head does. My tribe had signs for things that repeat when they should not. I keep walking. I watch her hands.

"You're saying we saw deja vu?" Vukradin says.

"Or— was it the exact same cat?" Soma says.

Then a wizard's apprentice steps into our road, selling authentic many-starred cloak thread.

"Brewbarry? Is this, like, you want to incorporate this into your robes?"

"What, sorry?"

"Thread?" Soma says.

I look at the spool. This is my business now. Linene made it hers, so it is doubly mine.

"Yes. So, are they soft?"

They are not. The apprentice says so himself.

"Softies, huh?"

"It's decorative," Valphine says. "Decorous."

"The thread isn't what makes the fabric soft," Vukradin says.

I love him. He is wrong.

"It's the thread."

"So what makes this fabric soft is the thread, right?"

"It tracks. You can't…" Soma starts, and lets it stand.

"What do you think makes the fabric soft?"

"If the fabric is made of threads."

The argument lands. Nothing gets back up. Fabric is threads. Soft fabric is soft threads. A robe is only as kind as its smallest part. Something in me sits up straighter, like the world itself just handed me the point.

"Was this spray softness on top of the fabric?"

"Yeah, that's how it works. You're right, you're right," Vukradin says.

"It's not pure silk, you know."

We do not buy the thread. Decorative thread is a lie a robe tells.

Near the Common Chord a bill is posted. Lathander's Death, it says. Performing. Two days from now. Nobody agreed to this. I would remember. I remember every deal I make now.

"Hey, I didn't know— when did you guys make this deal?" Soma says.

"Let's add it to the quest list! We're doing a performance!" Vukradin is already glowing. "What is it?"

Two days.

"Okay!"

"Apparently they have," Soma says.

"Sounds like Vukradin already has," Valphine says.

"Yeah, I think he has."

"Where is it? What's the time?"

The bill answers him. Evening. The Common Chord. The same Common Chord that pours piss for ale and has been living off Vukradin's old songs. Now it hangs our name on its wall like it owns us.

"Definitely be there in 2 days. That is awesome."

"They summoned me."

"His naivety says: yep, show up," Soma says.

I will show up too. Somebody has to stand behind him and watch the room.

Through the Protector's Enclave a man argues, loud, that rents have doubled. He is right. Prices are climbing all over this city. I think about cotton. The robe has to stay soft and stay cheap. Both. That is the hard part.

Two rumors follow us through the streets. The first: Lord Neverember has taken a drow advisor.

"No," Valphine says.

"Drow," Soma says.

"Wait a second here…"

"It's you!" Vukradin says.

"Hough."

The second rumor: Lathander's Death is playing the Common Chord.

"We heard that one," Soma says.

Then the Common Chord is in front of us.

## Vukradin — Return to the Common Chord

The Common Chord. When I played here, it was the kind of place that didn't ask too many questions and paid in actual coin. The room held forty comfortably, and I never once made the room prove it. Three consecutive nights on that stage. I give my bandmates the honest count as we come up the street.

"For sure. I think it's more like 10 to 15."

Eleven, in truth. Eleven souls a night.

"But always Brewbarry."

"That was the idea," Soma says.

"That's more than one, for sure."

And in those days I had a policy, and I stand by it still: the owner, a half-elf named Petra, paid me, and I handed the fee back to every patron who could not rate the show a ten out of ten. A performance you cannot call perfect is a performance you should not be charged for. That is fair trade. "Yeah, well, they were not fully satisfied." I say it plainly, without shame. "I kept Brewbarry's money. It was very nice of him to give me a ten out of ten." Petra came to understand that the model was not working.

"No shit."

She told me she really enjoyed the music. She did not book me again. And that, more or less, is how the adventuring career began. "Right. Sounds right."

Now the Common Chord has a six-week reservation list, and it is the place to be. There is a line. There is a man with a reservations book. There is a sign outside advertising a performance I never agreed to give.

"Say hi to Petra!"

I check for the mob, for the autograph seekers. Not mobbed, exactly. Nobody at the door recognizes me, which proves nothing except that I was ahead of my time, and I say as much. We do not announce the great Vukradin. "Oh, no, we're Lathander's Death." And to the band, quieter: "Yes, I'm sure. And I'm sure Petra's been taking advantage of that."

We make our impressions in the doorway. Valphine wears the white dragon plate, because of course she does, and people point at her from every corner of the room and talk behind their hands. Brewbarry is an impression all by himself. And people look at me the longest, squinting, as if they almost recognize the face and cannot pay for the name.

"So, Brewbarry, you need to set up your shop," Valphine says.

"Sell some merchandise."

"This is your opportunity," I tell him. The ensemble lifts its own.

"I don't have merchandise. The only merchandise I have is the one I'm wearing. I'm trying to set things up."

"A, B, C."

"Always be closing," Soma finishes.

"Sorry, sorry guys, I'm still learning the ropes here." Then his eyes come up. "Okay, I'll try to get some pre-orders for the Brewbarry."

"There you go, now you're thinking!"

"Sell subscriptions, you know," Soma offers.

At the podium I lead with the truth. "We are here to perform!" The reservations man does not mean to be rude, he says, but this is a nice crowd, and he does not think we belong to it. He never finishes the sentence, because near the bar several barbarians cosplaying as me begin to chant. As *me*. In braids and borrowed finery, in my venue, chanting. So I put the whole instrument behind it.

"We're Lathander's Death! We're here to perform!"

The room turns as one body. There is screaming. The girls are screaming, which is right and proper and long overdue.

The staff still balk at the stage, as if the stage were not the entire point of a stage. "Why not?"

"Marching on stage. Who's stopping us?" Valphine says.

"Who wants to stop us?" Brewbarry asks.

"Who's gonna stop Brewbarry?" Soma answers.

Nobody. He shoulders a path through the crush and the question settles itself, and we are up under the lights. Brewbarry wants the song about Lord Neverwinter. I count us in and give the people art.

"…winter where the cold winds blow. A hallowed steam rises soft and slow from Lim's own pot, a viscous grace warming this hallowed, frozen place. A shroud of labor simmered long. Better?"

"That's not the one about Lord Neverwinter."

"The glory of the soup."

"There's one called Vault of Silver Lies. That's the one you want."

"Not the next one?"

With all the commotion, Petra finally shows up, announcing to everyone in earshot that she discovered this guy. She looks prosperous. The room looks prosperous. I think of the sign outside.

"I saw that we're on in 2 nights."

"Yes. An exclusive one-night performance."

"How much does it pay?"

Beside me Valphine murmurs the prediction before Petra can open her mouth: the usual fee.

"Well, your old fee. Does that sound fair?"

"Well, no!" The old fee was priced for a man who gave it back. "Because now I've got a whole band, and we're a little bit bigger now, I've got other things going on, so…"

"We're a little bit more popular now."

"We've got a whole band now, not just a one-man band," Soma adds.

"What were you thinking?" She hedges. Fine. I name it. "So, 500."

She laughs, a real laugh, from the belly. "I can't afford 500 gold pieces for a one-night performance."

"Then why'd you put up the sign?" Soma asks.

She didn't put up the sign, she says. In her own venue.

"Someone did."

"I think we have a copy of the sign."

"Yeah, show her the sign, like, it's some…"

"This is your place."

"You're telling me that people are just invited?" Soma presses.

"That was more of a cover— that was more of a cover band idea than the real Lathander's Death."

"Oh, there's a cover band?"

"Yeah, the guy who plays your songs on stage every night — people like it."

"But don't you want the real band?"

"Yeah, but I can't afford 500 gold pieces."

"Alright, what can you do?"

"What do you mean you can't afford it? We're gonna sing, people are gonna, you know," Brewbarry says, and he is not wrong.

I lean in with everything I have, and the room stops chewing to listen. Come on. She can do better than that.

"We negotiate. We get 50% of whatever earnings she makes while we sing," Brewbarry announces.

"Oh, you want front-end points," Soma says.

"Revenue share, yeah," Valphine says.

"Is that after or before expenses?" Not a breath of hesitation from Petra. Not one.

"Net," I say, and then the education catches up with me. "No, no, you always want gross, right?"

"Yeah, you want gross, because that is where the theatres," Soma starts.

"And then just fake it with the expense."

Before any of us can change a single mind, Petra is shaking hands on the net.

"Ha ha ha ha. No, on the gross, on the gross."

"You've learned something, Vukradin. You're not the same guy I used to remember."

It should ring like a compliment. She means it as one, I think, or as half of one. And it sits in my ear like a chord voiced one note wrong, and I cannot immediately say which note.

"He's still pretty naive," Soma says, and I take more comfort from that than I would care to admit under oath.

Then Petra warms to her hostess voice. It's awesome that I've come, she says, and she has even kept my little table as a special place, where people come and admire and can see where I sat and thought about my music.

"Do they know it's a cover band?"

"So you've been impersonating me…"

"How long has this been going? So how much money have you— so really, I should sue you for a percentage of all your profits for stealing my likeness."

She swears she always told people this was not the real Vukradin. Sworn disclosure. Noted. But I did not come this far to litigate alone.

"Brewbarry, can you dispute for me, please?"

Brewbarry rises. He does not raise his voice; he raises himself, all of it, until Petra is standing in his shade. "Are you trying to rip off my friend?" A pause exactly the length of a rest. "Making profit on his image and reputation."

Now the paperwork. "I think 500 is a perfectly valid amount of gold, given all the money you've been making…"

"We can call it square with one night's performance."

"So after this one night's performance, I get to keep playing the old classic."

"I'm going to license my image and likeness to you."

"Perpetuity, is that what you're thinking here?"

"You will have to tell them it's a cover band, though."

"Well, I always tell them it's a cover band."

"I respect the image of the one, the only, the great Vukradin."

"I think this is good. 500, we perform in two nights."

"Makes up for all of you stealing from me in the past, and you've got rights going forward."

Not a coin changes hands, and the ledger balances anyway: the past infringement valued, squared by one exclusive night; the license running forward with disclosure stitched into it. Clean paper. Conflict-free. This is how it is done.

She spreads her hands at me. "I discovered you, man." And then, wounded: "I was there at the beginning. I was the one who gave you the gig. Don't you remember? Nobody wanted to put you on stage, actually."

"He was the one who actually played the gig, though," Soma says.

"Valphine, you wanna go on stage? Let's see how we do."

Petra balks. No. We can't do that.

"Why can't I perform right now? It'll get everybody excited for the real thing in two nights!"

She pauses, and I watch the arithmetic run behind her eyes. Fine. But not the full set. Just a teaser.

"No, no, we'll do one song. We'll do one song."

My delivery is serviceable. Valphine's opening note goes off a cliff I did not know the song had, a screech so far outside the key that the key files a complaint. And the room leans in. Because my old stuff was very discordant, and the lack of harmony and the off-cue screeches carry the crowd straight back to the original classic sound, the early genre, the pure work.

"Very avant-garde."

"Charm," Soma says.

Then Petra sits us down at my table. It was not my table. It was literally where Brewbarry sat, eleven nights a career, the one ten out of ten I ever kept. Now it is polished to a shine, dressed with beautifully set couches, and there is a little plaque: *Vukradin created his music here.* She asks if I would be willing to sign it.

"Well, that'll be extra."

"Brewbarry, what did you do to this guy? This guy used to be the nicest guy!"

"I'm nice! You see?"

"I just annoy you?" Brewbarry offers.

"My friend is not nice."

Twenty-five gold pieces, she says. Twenty-five gold pieces, twice, like naming it two times makes it smaller.

"Alright, 25."

"You gotta pay right now, though."

She pays on the spot, and her pupils have already gone to coin, counting the warmed-up crowd over my shoulder. Twenty-five gold, earned by my own name in my own hand. Fair-trade gold, and nobody bled for it. "And you just gotta sit down here. The food is the best."

"I don't know, we just had some great soup," Soma says.

"Free food!"

Brewbarry sets down his mug with intent. "I have an idea. Before we sing our next song, we should use our popularity."

"And our music, to influence the mind of people, and sing about the evil of Lord of Neverwinter."

"Whoa, whoa," Valphine says.

"No, I don't think he's that evil. I think it's…"

"Lord Neverwinter is our ally against Margaster, right?"

"Yeah, Margaster is evil."

"The enemy of our enemy is our friend," Soma says.

"So let's sing about the evil of House Margaster."

"And how corrupt they are."

"Sure."

"Plant that seed into the people's mind."

"Revolution."

"You want me to cast some spells, too, to really alter their minds?"

"Yes, yes, let's do that."

He is still going: "…leaves this tavern tonight needs to think that House Margaster is the most evil organization. That seems…"

"Very good of us."

The food arrives, and I am genuinely surprised, because in my day the Common Chord had no food at all and really cheap booze, the swill Brewbarry once praised and now, with his palate risen in the world, remembers as undrinkable. Tonight the plates are fancy and the beer, by every account, is good.

"I mean, isn't that an oxymoron?"

"There's bad beer, and there's Common Chord ale," Soma says.

"Have I? I don't think I have a palate." And then his creed, entire: "Anything that will get me drunk is good. That's my definition of good."

Petra takes the good beer away from him and sets down a cheaper one.

"No, no, no. I'll take both, and I just got them."

He drinks them with identical joy, and she watches him do it, and I can see the sentence being passed: cheap beer for Brewbarry, all night, forever.

"Pearls before swine," Soma sighs.

"Whatever, as long as I get drunk."

"Take the good stuff."

When we take the stage again, Brewbarry renders the teaser's verdict first. "Yeah, yeah, that's terrible."

"Alright, let's do another one, let's see how it goes."

"Margaster needs to suffer. They need to know it's coming from us."

And here is the thing: I keep the spells in their case. I offered, sincerely, but nobody's mind gets altered tonight except the honest way, by a song standing on its own legs.

"Wait, wait, we need bardic inspiration," Valphine says.

"You want to do an inspiration?"

"And my pleasure."

Her Bless settles over us like tuning, my inspiration hums under Valphine's line, and the rough middle of the song lifts into something that carries. There is a witty lyric in it about House Margaster and the trade routes, about who profits when the people's coin buys less, and the crowd catches it and starts humming it back to us before the verse is even done. They picked it up. They are taking it home.

"Excellent," Brewbarry says.

Over the last of the good beer, the question arises of what, precisely, Brewbarry plays in this band.

"What? Cymbals."

"Well, he does cowbell," I explain. "That's the great thing about more cowbell." And it is true in every register: "Works on any song. Universally."

"Yeah, the Nine Hells Chorale did not need more cowbell, but it worked out the same," Soma says.

"Sure," Brewbarry allows. Then, wounded on principle: "We're not that kind of band, though. I thought we were heavy metal. Cowbell."
