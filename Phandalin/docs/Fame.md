# Fame — The Name Engine

**What it is:** The campaign's answer to the party's standing speculation that *some force is making us famous*.
**The answer:** Yes — several forces. All of them mortal, all of them named, and one of them is Vukradin.
**What is not mortal:** not the fame. The **coincidence rate**.
**How it pays off:** Flattery first, address second. The party learns that being known is how a system that cannot model agency acquires something it *can* model: a name.
**Status:** Doctrine, ruled 2026-09-01. Nothing spent on table yet. Reads against `KP.md` and `CounterForce.md` — do not run a fame reveal without both.

---

## The core inversion

KP's project is to make prophecy behave like computation (`KP.md`, `CounterForce.md`). Computation requires addressable nodes. **You cannot optimize around actors you cannot name.**

The party's fame is therefore not a blessing conferred by a benevolent fate. It is the process by which four people who were previously statistical noise on the Sword Coast became **legible** — nameable, locatable, modelable. The players' instinct ("something is elevating us") is half right and inverted in the half that matters. Something is not elevating them. Something is *indexing* them.

The reveal is not "a god did this." The reveal is: **the reason everybody knows your names is that a system which eats agency needs addresses.**

## Part 1 — The mortal engine

Every fame vector in this campaign is a named person with a motive, and the party can find all of them. This layer is **fully knowable and should be fully investigable.** Do not gate it.

### The precedent: Milo Goodbarrel

The First Faction — Stroud, Milo Goodbarrel, Malfire, Whistler — are the iconic image of the War of the Dragons because **one of them had a publishing career.** Milo wrote multiple accounts, kept in continuous reprint at Rishaal the Pageturner's in Waterdeep, Volume 3 in its fourteenth printing. He painted the mountain-top meeting of the metallic dragons two days before his final Waterdeep book signing, and that painting is reproduced in bronze, print and paint across the Sword Coast. Order of the Gauntlet shrines carry his iconography; his books sit in every library of consequence. (`notes/neverwinter/history_of_neverwinter.md:124,135`)

The result: Whistler is **mythologized across three competing registers** — the Ardragon, the Shadow Werebear, the kenku (`docs/entity_inventory.md:546`). The fame outran the facts by a wide margin, and no god was involved. A chronicler was.

**This is the template, and the party is standing in it.** They are four people who fought in wars, and they have a bard whose stated life goal is to spread his music to all of Faerûn. Milo Goodbarrel is what Vukradin becomes if nothing interrupts him.

### What the party finds if they pull the thread

They find **all eleven**, and the punchline is that the hands **disagree with each other**.

- Jena wants them famous *and owned*.
- The Order wants them famous *and pious*.
- Cassian wants them famous *and his*.
- Sylvine wanted them famous *and predictable*.
- The street wants them famous *and then over*.

There is no conspiracy, because there does not need to be one. Fame here is overdetermined — an asset being minted independently by a dozen parties who each want a different thing from it. **This is a genuinely good investigation and it should pay out honestly.** Let them solve it. The mundane answer is not a disappointment; it is the correct answer to the question they asked.

### The residue — three things the mortal engine does not explain

Give them the eleven hands. Then let them notice that three data points **predate any mechanism that could have carried them**:

1. **Brin and Giles** heard of a drow who worshipped Lathander and believed in purifying pain, and built a temple on it — before the party had done anything to publicize it, and before Valphine had a chronicler.
2. **Xanth**, a centaur in the wilderness, knew Valphine by title.
3. **Sylvine Wintermoon** had, in the campaign's own words, *"advance intelligence on the party's activities and principles"*, implying "either a spy network **or significant information reach**" (`extract_037.md:53`).

Those three are the honest hook, they are already on the record, and nobody has ever explained them. **That is where the divine layer lives — and it lives there as an anomaly in the rate, not as an author.**

## Part 2 — What is actually divine

### Convergence, not causation

Savras is the god of **divination**. His signature is that things are *seen*, not that things are *caused*. The finding is not "a god elevated you." The finding is:

> The same four names keep landing in ledgers kept by people who do not talk to each other, at a rate a weave should not produce.

### Why "a god made you famous" is the wrong ruling — three costs

Recorded here so the temptation stays refused.

1. **It contradicts the cosmology.** `CounterForce.md` canonized (2026-05-31) that fate-gods are *provincial administrators of a partial weave*, "not as all-powerful as they believe," and that **their plurality is the proof of their limitation**. A fate-god who reaches into the Sword Coast and manufactures four celebrities is doing exactly the totalizing thing the doctrine says fate-gods cannot do. That is KP's move. It spends the doctrine's best line — *Savras cannot out-system the cage.*
2. **It makes fate a sponsor.** The doctrine forbids this explicitly: R&C *"should never appear as quest-givers… a counterforce signal, not a sponsor."* A god running a publicity campaign is a sponsor.
3. **It deflates the players.** Vukradin's fame is the direct product of Dave's choices — returning treasure, advocating fair wages, refusing to be owned by one faction. Valphine's is the product of Gary playing a visibly drow cleric of Lathander in public, in a city, on purpose. If a god arranged it, none of those choices did work. **This is the real cost and it is paid at the table.**

### The plural-weave reading — how to keep fate in it

The doctrine *does* license fate-gods here, in the shape it already established: **plural and partial.**

More than one fate-current has independently flagged these four. Savras catches it on Toril. Istus's echo is already in play from Oerth (`CounterForce.md`). Tymora is what every tavern in Neverwinter will tell them — *Lady Luck's got her hand on them* — which is folk attribution and exactly the right in-world noise. **They cannot all be right about whose weave this is.**

And per the doctrine, the thing that damaged the boundaries between weaves enough for foreign fate-currents to bleed through **is KP**. So the party showing up as a hot spot in several partial weaves at once is not a sign of favor. It is a reading on the distortion.

## Part 3 — The CounterForce answer: obscurity is armor

This is where fame stops being flattering.

`CounterForce.md`: *"Their unimpressiveness is the freedom."* Two clowns nobody has heard of are the only thing KP cannot model. Which makes the terrible symmetry:

> **The party's fame is a liability. Rimardo and Corrin's obscurity is armor.**

The party has spent the campaign acquiring exactly the property that makes them tractable to the thing they will eventually have to fight, and they acquired it on purpose, and it was Vukradin's idea.

### The card flip

Penn & Teller rules apply — the clue comes out of **Corrin's hands, not Rimardo's mouth**, and the reveal makes it worse, not better. Corrin turns four cards:

> **VALPHINE OF THE BLESSED**
> **SOMA, PROTECTOR OF THE GLADES**
> **THE UTHGARDT LOOK**
> **HIS EARLY STUFF**

Rimardo, bored, contractual:

> *"These are not names. These are addresses."*

Alternative playbill line, same register, for a written delivery:

> *Renown, n.: the condition of being locatable by parties one has not met. Non-refundable.*

Per `CounterForce.md`, the R&C beats belong to Vukradin — **a bard sees structure**. He is also the author of the problem, which is the whole reason the beat lands on him and not on Valphine.

## Part 4 — Running it

### Staging ladder

| Tier | Beat | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Fame is pure comedy. Free drinks, bad merchandise, someone dressed as Brewbarry | Already running in Neverwinter |
| 1 | Fame has a cost. A stranger knows their itinerary. A door opens that they did not knock on | Neverwinter, anytime |
| 2 | The party investigates and finds the eleven hands. **Let them win this.** The mundane answer is real | Whenever they pull the thread |
| 3 | The residue. Brin, Xanth, Sylvine — three cases the eleven hands do not cover | After tier 2, not before |
| 4 | The R&C card flip. *These are addresses.* | R&C's next appearance after tier 3 |
| 5 | Fame as instrument — someone finds them *because* of it, at cost | Iymrith arc bracket, per `CounterForce.md` |

### Tone rule

**Flattery first, dread second.** This is the fame-specific form of the R&C rule (*joke first, dread second*). Every beat must feel genuinely good before it feels like surveillance. If the party is only ever uneasy about being famous, the reversal has nothing to reverse. Let them enjoy it — Brewbarry especially, whose fame is uncomplicated and should stay that way longest.

### Tracker hookup — **needs a ruling, do not run it as written**

The instinct is to make fame a counter. **Recommend against.** Ruling 10 in `notes/neverwinter/the_commission.md` already documents what happens when a score gets broad triggers: run strictly, a single Neverwinter session moves it several points on a 0–10 scale, and the cosmic horror arrives early by accounting error. A third runaway counter would be the same mistake twice.

Fame should be a **diagnostic, not a currency.** If it must touch a tracker, the narrow proposal is:

> **Proposed, unruled:** +1 **Planar Distortion** the first time the party demonstrates that someone knew them by name or title *before any mechanism existed to carry it* — i.e. when they close on the residue, not when they notice the fame.

The existing Planar Distortion trigger is narrow and Aletra-specific (`docs/planning.md:19` — *"direct interaction where Aletra reveals unusual interest in planar anomalies"*, score currently 0). Widening it is a real change to a pacing instrument and is a **GM decision, not a doctrinal one.** Left open deliberately.

### What never to confirm

Whether the convergence is Savras, Tymora, Istus's echo, KP's modeling, or an artifact of all four weaves being partial — **never resolve it.** Per `CounterForce.md`'s ontology rule: the right answer at the table is *yes*.

The rule of thumb: **the mortal layer is fully knowable, the divine layer is never more than a rate.**

## Module read

The published material supplies the mortal engine for free, in both directions.

**Dragon of Icespire Peak / Lost Mine of Phandelver** run on the party being the only ones who show up. Phandalin has no standing power, so four adventurers fill the vacuum and become the town's de facto government. Their fame there is the *mechanical* consequence of a region without institutions — it is the module's premise, not an intervention.

**Neverwinter is the inversion.** Institutions everywhere, all of them credit-hungry, all of them needing heroes, and a Lord Protector whose finances are mortgaged out from under him. In Phandalin the party was famous because nobody else existed. In Neverwinter they are famous because **everybody needs them to be**, and each institution needs a slightly different version.

The party crossing between the two is the reason the fame stopped feeling earned and started feeling engineered. Both readings are correct. Neither requires a god.

## Core line

**Nobody made them famous. Eleven people did, for eleven reasons, and one of them was the bard.**

**What is not mortal is the rate — the residue of people who knew their names before any mechanism could have carried them. And fame is not a gift from fate; it is the process of becoming addressable, which is the one thing KP's cage requires and the CounterForce has never had.**
