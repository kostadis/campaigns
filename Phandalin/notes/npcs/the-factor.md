# The Factor — the Commission's rotating seat

**Role:** The Commission's shareholder-facing officer — the only person a shareholder can actually get in front of
**Location:** The Commission offices, Protector's Enclave — a receiving room off the shareholder registry. *(Not the Counting House. That is the teller window; Aurelan Vance works there and is not the seat.)*
**Faction:** Neverwinter Commission
**First seen:** Not yet — built ahead of play, 2026-08-19

> **Three people, one character.** The party never meets "the Commission." They meet **the Factor**, and the Factor is a different person each time. Treat the seat as the NPC and the occupants as costumes. Names registry-checked 2026-08-19: Wenn, Oster and Aubrecht are all clear, no collision, no near-miss.

---

## The seat — what is true of every occupant

**They have read everything.** Each new Factor opens by proving it, so no PC ever explains themselves twice. *"You told Factor Wenn the office would consider the licensing. The office has considered it."*

**Nobody says "I."** Always *the Commission*, *the office*, *the schedule*, *we*. This is absolute and the party is meant to notice it eventually. **A Factor who slips into first person has surfaced through the control — that is a person waking up, and it is the party's actual win condition against this faction.** `[GM-ONLY]`

**The tea is always right.** Whatever the number of people who walk in — announced, unannounced, one more than expected — the tray already holds exactly that many cups, poured. No Factor ever remarks on it. This is the seat's anchor: the detail a player will describe two months later.

**Rotation is caused by the party winning.** Extract a genuine concession and that Factor is gone by the next visit. Not punished — *reassigned*, sometimes promoted. Everyone is fine. That is worse. `[GM-ONLY: the occupant started thinking for themselves, and a real concession is the person surfacing.]`

**Nobody is ever angry.** No Factor threatens, no Factor can be bribed or blackmailed, and no Factor is deciding anything.

## Factor Wenn — the standing policy

**Run him first, and run him most.** Per Ruling 5, the Commission's policy toward this party is never to obstruct, because obstruction produces violence. Wenn *is* that policy.

### Look
Comfortable, middle-aged, unhurried, dressed like a man who has never once been late. Smells faintly of the beeswax the registry uses on its ledger bindings.

### Anchor
He writes down every granted request as you make it — **and the party can watch his hand form letters identical to documents they have already seen.** This is how the house-hand tell gets on screen in scene one, physically, with no roll required.

### Tic
Grants every small request instantly, and has written it down before you have finished asking for it.

### What he wants from any interaction
To send them away pleased and no further forward.

### What he would never do
Refuse anything outright.

### Voice
- *"The Commission is delighted. Consider it granted — it is already written down."*
- *"There is no need to ask twice. The office heard you the first time."*

## Factor Oster — the mistake

**He is doctrine correctly applied to the wrong party, and it costs him the seat.** Oster does what the Commission does to *ordinary* shareholders. Against these people it produces exactly the escalation the core was avoiding, and he is removed for it — which teaches the party a badly wrong lesson about how safe they are.

### Look
Thin, cold, precise. One ink-stained cuff he has stopped noticing.

### Anchor
A ledger on the desk that he **never opens**, and cites page numbers from without looking.

### Tic
Opens a "courtesy review" of whichever holding the visitor cares about most.

### What he wants from any interaction
To establish one irregularity on the record.

### What he would never do
Raise his voice.

### Voice
- *"Two shareholders witnessed a forged Harbor writ and did not report it. The office is certain there is an explanation."*
- *"Nothing is being alleged. A review is not an allegation. A review is a courtesy."*

## Factor Aubrecht — the sincere one

**The most dangerous of the three, because none of it is a technique.** He agrees with everything the party says, is genuinely appalled about Elara Meliamne, and can do absolutely nothing. They will not be able to be angry at him.

### Look
Older, kind-faced, visibly tired. Sits slightly forward, as though about to help.

### Anchor
A **black mourning band for Elara Meliamne that nobody asked him to wear** — and he is the only person in the building wearing one.

### Tic
Apologises before you have asked for anything.

### What he wants from any interaction
To be forgiven for something he cannot name.

### What he would never do
Promise anything he could actually deliver.

### Voice
- *"The office is sorry. Truly. That is not a form of words."*
- *"The Commission understands. The Commission understands completely. The schedule, however, is the schedule."*
- **The slip, when you choose to spend it:** *"I—"* — a half-second of silence, and then, quieter — *"the office cannot."*

## Notes for the GM

- **Aubrecht is the intended first "I."** The sincerely sympathetic one is the closest to surfacing, which is why he is the one who mourns a woman he never met. Spending the slip on him converts the party's goal from *defeat the Commission* to *get this man out*, which is the better campaign. Flagged as a proposal — see Open decisions.
- **Order of appearance:** Wenn → Wenn → Oster (and his removal) → Aubrecht. Do not lead with Oster; the audit only lands once the party has been told yes enough times to relax.
- **Never let a Factor be the villain of a scene.** The horror is that there is no villain in the room.
- **If the players try to intimidate a Factor:** total, unembarrassed compliance. Whatever was demanded is granted on the spot and written down. Then that Factor is gone next time.
- **If the players try to bribe a Factor:** the money is accepted, receipted, and appears as a credit against their account. There is no way to make this transaction dirty.
- **If the players ask "who decided?":** every Factor answers this the same way, and the sameness is the clue. *"A decision was not required. The schedule provided for it."*
- **Promotion path:** if Aubrecht surfaces and survives, he stops being a Factor and becomes a person with a name and a strategy doc. That is the graduation this dossier exists to enable.
- **Related:** `notes/neverwinter/the_commission.md` (the seat, Ruling 2; the doctrine inversion, Ruling 5) · `notes/neverwinter/halvara_margaster_strategy.md` (the antagonist who cannot buy any of these people, which is precisely why she attacks with paper).

## Open decisions (for the user)

- **Is Aubrecht the first "I"?** Proposed above. The alternative is Wenn — the pleasant one cracking is a colder reveal, and costs you the mourning-band payoff.
- **Confirm the title.** *Factor* is the working name (a trading company's resident agent — a job, not a person). Alternatives if it reads wrong aloud: *the Sitting Member*, *the Duty Director*, *the Office*.
- **Are the three of them physically alike?** Deliberately unspecified. If they are — same build, same age, same unremarkable face — the horror arrives much earlier and much cheaper, but you lose Aubrecht's mourning band as a *personal* detail.
- **What happens to a removed Factor?** Reassignment is the stated line and nobody is punished. Whether that is true is unwritten, and the party will eventually go looking for Oster.
