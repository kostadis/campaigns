# Linene Graywind — What She Actually Wants (OVERLAY)

**This is not a dossier.** The canonical record is `docs/npcs/linene_graywind.md` (42 source extracts). This file answers one question that dossier doesn't: *why did she do all that in ch46?*

**Role:** Proprietor and master of the Lionshield Coster outpost, Phandalin · **Age:** 35
**Status:** Alive, active, friendly. Deep in a business crisis she does not understand.

> **Location note:** `planning.md` places her outpost in "Neverwinter Wood." The dossier and `world_state.md` both say **Phandalin**. Phandalin is correct; planning.md is drifted.

---

## The one-line read

**She is building a lifeboat.**

Linene is a branch manager of a franchise that is failing through no fault of her own, and she has watched her professional competence become irrelevant to her outcomes. Everything she did in ch46 is the same move executed three ways: **converting herself from an importer into a local manufacturer and retailer**, using the only source of capital and momentum in Phandalin — the party.

> **Confidence:** this is *inference*, not established canon. It is well-supported by her canonical behavior and contradicted by nothing, but she has never stated it. Treat it as a strong working read that the table can confirm or overturn.

---

## The three ch46 moves are one plan `[CANON behavior, inferred motive]`

| What she did | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Warned about the supply chain, asked the party to **investigate** | She needs to know *why* — she's been given no explanation by anyone |
| Committed personally to **Brewbarry's factory**; wealth-management consultant; wrote the Counting House letter | A business that needs **no interplanar shipping and no head office** — Phandalin cotton, Phandalin labor, Phandalin distribution |
| Jumped on the **UBT subcommittee** with a Candlekeep text ready | A redistribution fund puts cash in local hands; local cash buys local goods. She is **building a domestic customer base** |

She argued in session that treasure *"recirculates"* once spent. That isn't ideology. It's a woman whose import business is dying, describing the mechanism that would save her.

And volunteering to draft the distribution rules means she gets to shape who in Phandalin has money to spend.

---

## Why the Brewbarry commitment is sincere *and* self-interested

> *"Brewbarry, this is your personal quest, now it is my personal quest."* — `[CANON, ch46]`, sincerity confirmed by Valphine's Insight 19.

Both things are true and neither cancels the other. Merchants don't attach themselves personally to a goliath's whim out of generosity. They do it when they see a hedge. She meant every word **because** it's her way out.

Play her as genuinely fond of him and genuinely calculating, with no tension between the two. That's what a good merchant looks like.

---

## What she's been hit by — twice `[CANON]`

1. **The War of the Giants** severely disrupted her supply chains. She can no longer reliably source scrolls; special requests now require sending messages with no guarantee of results.
2. **Then the cheap route died** ~seven weeks ago. At first meeting she characterized this as **"tariffs."**

> **GM-only — the "tariffs" line matters.** Either she was told that by head office, or she constructed it herself to have an answer. Neither is true. Her cheap route was the Commission's **Displacement Manifold** at the Neverwinter docks; it went dark and **House Margaster stepped into the vacated distribution role** at a margin the Commission never had to charge. She has no idea. Nobody official will tell her anything.
>
> The underlying cause is further up still: Toril is not a signatory of the interplanar bag-of-holding compact. That's KP-adjacent and far past her.

Two catastrophes in a row, neither of which she caused or can influence. That's the despair the lifeboat is a response to.

---

## The stake nobody at the table has noticed

**Linene has just commissioned the inquiry that killed Elara Meliamne.**

Elara asked questions about the Manifold. She filed a counter-claim at the Harbor authority. House Margaster killed her eight days before the party arrived. `[CANON]`

Linene has now asked the same questions, in the same city, through the party. And unlike Elara she is not a Neverwinter commercial house with legal standing and a noble name — she's a franchise manager in a frontier town with no protection whatsoever.

If it gets back to Margaster who commissioned the inquiry, **she is in Elara's chair, and the party put her there.**

Nobody has to realize this yet. It should be available the moment someone does.

---

## Her reaction when the party reports back

Not gratitude. **Relief first** — she finally has an explanation, and after months of "tariffs" and shrugs, an answer is worth something even when it's terrible.

Then frightened. Then furious. Then commercial.

And she asks the right question before the party does:

> *"Then who broke the device?"*

Leave it hanging. Nobody in this arc knows yet.

---

## The uncomfortable branch

**Would she take the Margaster deal herself?**

If the party comes back with *"your routes were bought, and the buyer will happily sell you capacity" —* does she sign?

A woman building a lifeboat might. She has a business to save, employees, and no leverage. This is a far more interesting scene than her being outraged on cue, and it puts her on the same fault line as Brewbarry: **the commercially correct answer and the right answer have come apart.**

If she signs, the party watches an ally they like do the thing they're agonizing over — and survive it. That's pressure without a lecture.

---

## What's still genuinely open about her

**Her bosses.** She's been sourcing planar-effect goods for Lionshield leadership and paid over double Barthen's price for the Whispering Grove pelts, justifying it by their interest. Two readings, both live:

- She's proving her outpost still has value, to avoid being closed
- Head office lost their own planar access when the Manifold died, and tasked her with sourcing replacements

Insight 19 confirmed she was sincere about the supply chain and about Brewbarry. **It did not establish that she disclosed everything about who is asking her for what.**

**Valphine already made the connection.** `[CANON]` — she previously recalled reading in Neverwinter about a merchants' interplanar shipping device that had stopped working, and privately linked it to Linene's enthusiasm for planar goods. Gary has been sitting on that thread for a while. It's live whenever he wants it.

## Voice

- *"Brewbarry, this is your personal quest, now it is my personal quest."* `[CANON]`
- *"It recirculates. That's the whole point — it doesn't stop being money once you spend it."*
- *"They told me tariffs. I have written four times asking which tariffs."*

**Tic:** produces a document to settle an argument — the Candlekeep text, a rate card, a ledger. She wins by citation, not volume.

**What she would never do:** lie to Brewbarry about the business. She'd decline to mention something before she'd misrepresent it.

## Open decisions (for the GM)

1. **Is the lifeboat read canon?** It's my inference. Confirming it at the table — a line where she admits she's building something that can't be taken away — makes her much sharper, but forecloses other readings.
2. **Does she sign with Margaster if offered?** See above. Recommend leaving it genuinely undecided until the party reports back and you see how they've played it.
3. **What do her bosses actually want?** Proving-value vs. tasked-to-source. This determines whether Lionshield leadership is a future ally, a future problem, or scenery.
4. **Does Margaster learn who commissioned the inquiry?** The Elara-chair stake only exists if there's a path for it to get back. Decide whether one exists.
5. **Does she know the party's mine is a competing revenue source?** She's now their consultant, their subcommittee chair, and adjacent to their finances. She has a lot of visibility for someone with an incentive to build local industry.
