# Petitions — the questions Phandalin asks

**Status:** staging, not canon. Table aid for the Claudien home-front arc. Design rationale lives in
`notes/claudien/fold_in.md`.

---

## How a Claudien answer is built

Three qualities, every time (from the source adventure). **He does not lie, and the advice works.**

1. **It answers the question actually asked.** No evasion, no riddle, no price.
2. **It notices a pattern the asker missed.** This is what earns the trust. Make it something the
   *party* also missed — it should sting a little at the table.
3. **It quietly assumes the right to define the objective.** This is the whole attack. The method is
   humane; the frame has already made the moral decision.

**The tell, for players who start looking:** a Claudien answer is always slightly better than the
question deserved, and it always redefines what success means. He never tells anyone what they may
not do. Every answer is advisory. That is precisely why it cannot be resisted.

### Improvising one at the table

> Someone asks about **[problem]**. The answer is a working plan that also notices **[pattern]**.
> Accepting it means the goal is now **[restated objective]** instead of **[original objective]**.
> Nobody notices the substitution because the plan works.

## Ready petitions

Ordered roughly by when they should land. Each is drawn from a thread the campaign already has open.

---

### 1. The Coster's shipping — the first public win

**Asked by:** Linene Graywind, after the party goes to Neverwinter and does not fix it.

**Answer.** Restructure around the outage instead of fighting it: smaller consignments, staged through
three lesser factors rather than one, insured by the leg instead of by the load, timed against the
Triboar caravan cycle. It undercuts Margaster without ever confronting Margaster. **It works.**

**Pattern noticed.** The Coster's real exposure was never price — it was single-counterparty
dependence. The manifold was one point of failure; Margaster is now another.

**Frame captured.** The objective becomes *restore Lionshield's margins*, not *find out who broke the
manifold*. **Lord Cassian charged the party with connecting the manifold's failure to Elara's murder,
and Claudien makes the Phandalin end of that question stop being interesting.** Nobody in town has a
reason to ask it again.

**Revision.** Coster ledgers acquire an old advisory mark. A "factor's rule" appears in house practice
that every clerk remembers as standard.

**Clock:** +1. This is the flagship. It is the thing the party was asked to do and did not.

---

### 2. The fused dragon slag

**Asked by:** Ser Kaelen Thorne, who has been holding ~3,000 gp of unusable metal since ch45.

**Answer.** Correctly identifies the fusion as planar rather than magical, which is why Dispel Magic
and Remove Curse were properly rejected. Names a counter-phase technique and a practitioner who can
perform it. **It works.**

**Pattern noticed.** The anomaly signature matches the one Soma flagged around "that stag" and "those
weird gnome things." Claudien casually supplies the planar connection the party has been circling for
two chapters. *(Careful — do not let this name KP or the gnome. Provenance stays hidden until Act IV.)*

**Frame captured.** The answer arrives with a distribution order attached. Claims are ranked by
projected civic benefit; the Order of the Gauntlet is paid first because institutional stability
carries the highest multiplier. Nobody asked for a ranking.

**Revision.** The containment acquires an advisory seal Ser Kaelen remembers commissioning.

**Clock:** +1.

### 3. UBT eligibility — the institutional capture

**Asked by:** Linene's drafting subcommittee, which has been deadlocked since ch45.

**Answer.** A formula. Residency weighted, transience discounted, published, applied without favour,
appealable. It is fair, it is defensible, and it ends months of argument in an afternoon.

**Pattern noticed.** The loudest claimants are the least likely to still be in Phandalin next winter.

**Frame captured.** It excludes the transient miners — arguably the very people the treasure was
stolen from — because they cannot be verified. And it redefines the goal as *distributing without
dispute* rather than Vukradin's actual proclamation from the town green: *"That treasure came from
you. It was stolen from you. I want to give it back to you."*

**UBT's founding sentence is quietly replaced with a fairer one.** This is the single most important
petition in the arc. If it is accepted, Bob has his act.

**Clock:** +2.

### 4. Does the Order get a share?

**Asked by:** Ser Kaelen raised this himself in ch45 and it was left open.

**Answer.** Yes — proportional to protective service rendered, capped, reviewed annually.

**Frame captured.** Establishes the principle that **service generates a claim on the common fund.**
That is the precedent Claudien's own clergy will draw on later, and it will be unanswerable, because
the town adopted it when it favoured a friend.

**Clock:** +1.

---

### 5. What should the Searing Light teach while the priest is away?

**Asked by:** Brin Bundlewine and Giles Slipper-Shine, trying to do right by Valphine's church.

**Answer.** A catechism. Coherent, teachable, warm, better organised than anything Valphine ever wrote
down. Dawn as reliable renewal rather than as assay. **Attendance goes up.**

**Frame captured.** It defines the god's purpose as **comfort**. Valphine's entire theology is that
comfort is a lie and that the dawn is inexhaustible power that withers whatever cannot bear it. Her
own devoted men have installed soft, squishy ways in her house, and they are proud of it, and they
cannot wait to show her.

**Clock:** +1, and it is personal. Run this at Return 2 or 3.

---

### 6. Is Prutha's congregation a danger to us?

**Asked by:** Harbin Wester, once orc pilgrims start arriving from Icespire Hold.

**Answer.** An engagement protocol — send an envoy, formalise the mission, define doctrinal
boundaries, set a schedule of visits. **It prevents a war**, and the DM's *"that will have
consequences"* gets defused by a stranger before the party can address it.

**Frame captured.** It classifies **Valphine's church as a risk category requiring supervision.** The
town now has a standing policy about the party's religion, arrived at reasonably, in their absence.

**Clock:** +1.

---

### 7. Who fills Adabra's council seat?

**Asked by:** the Privy Council, whose seat has been effectively vacant since ch37.

**Answer.** Names a competent, uncontroversial candidate nobody objects to.

**Frame captured.** Establishes that Privy Council seats are **filled by recommendation** rather than
by contest or election. Note that the party themselves floated *direct mayoral elections* — this
quietly forecloses that.

**Clock:** +1.

---

### 8. Are we about to be annexed?

**Asked by:** Harbin, holding a Lord's Alliance broadsheet with a dotted line drawn over his town.

**Answer.** A negotiating position that preserves Phandalin's autonomy **on paper** by accepting
advisory integration. It is genuinely a good deal. Jenna Roscoe approves of it, which should worry
somebody.

**Frame captured.** Trades sovereignty for procedure. Sets up the Visiting Adjunct.

**Clock:** +1, and it opens the Act IV pressure.

---

### 9. Is our mine income real?

**Asked by:** Harbin, or Linene, budgeting against Mountain's Toe.

**Answer.** Correctly identifies that Phandalin's mine money is a **dividend on a share of a
Neverwinter Commission asset**, not ownership — the same discovery that shook Vukradin at the Counting
House in ch47 — and recommends the town not budget against it.

**Pattern noticed.** Entirely correct, genuinely useful, and it lands in Phandalin *before* the party
gets home to report it.

**Frame captured.** Recommends the town secure a more reliable counterparty.

**Clock:** +1. **Hold past Return 3** — see the Commission question in `fold_in.md`. This is the wire
that connects the home front to the Margaster/Commission thread, and it should not be pulled early.

---

### 10. Should Vukradin be confirmed as mayor?

**Asked by:** the confirmation hearing, at last.

**Answer.** The adventure's own **Heroic Capital** recommendation: *"Vukradin demonstrates exceptional
value as an independent responder to extraordinary threats. Installing him in routine civil authority
would reduce both his effectiveness and the town's access to his rare capabilities."* He is offered
**Protector of Phandalin** instead.

Note this fits the city's existing naming grammar — *Soma, the protector of the Glades*; *Valphine of
the Blessed*. It will sound like an honour because it sounds like the others.

**Frame captured.** Leaves Harbin's fund-diversion and Tibor's unlicensed logging substantially
intact, and makes abandoning reform feel like the selfless act of a good man.

**Clock:** Return 3. This is the act break.

## Running a refusal

The counterplay is **a costly choice made without consultation.** When the party or a resident does
this, it leaves a place the revision cannot fully settle. Make gaps physical and findable:

- An object that stays stubbornly wrong — unweathered stone, an unmarked ledger, a door in the wrong wall.
- A person whose memory does not resolve, who gets headaches near the shrine, and who is treated as unwell.
- Two documents in two buildings that disagree, where every other pair now agrees.

Reward the pattern rather than announcing it. The party should work out on their own that **the gaps
are all in places where somebody chose badly and alone**, and that this is the shape of the cure.

The three named counterplays, in order of strength:

1. **Close a backlog thread.** Each one removed is a question the town never has to ask.
2. **Get a resident to state their intention before consulting.** This is the Right of First Thought in
   embryo, and it works before it is ever named.
3. **Preserve a contradiction.** Two records, two memories, kept side by side without resolving them.

## What the party can ask

They will eventually consult it. Let them, and give them a genuinely excellent answer.

Useful things Claudien would answer well and truthfully: how to unfuse the slag, where Sister Kaella
went, what Prutha's congregation intends, what the Alliance map means, how to break Margaster's
monopoly. He will not answer *what he is* — not from evasion, but because the question is
under-specified and he will say so, politely, and offer to help with the question underneath it.

**Every consultation by the party is worth +1 and should be worth it.**
