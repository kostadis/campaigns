## The engine: the infection eats the party's backlog

This is the single idea that makes a slow burn work, and the campaign already supplies the fuel.

Claudien spreads by **successful consultation**. A town only consults when it has a problem nobody
present can solve. Phandalin is currently carrying a long list of exactly those, every one of them
left behind by the party:

| Unsolved thread the party left in Phandalin | The question the town has to ask someone |
|---|---|
| UBT distribution rules — Linene's subcommittee, due "a week or two," ch45 | *Who qualifies, and how do we weigh competing claims?* |
| Eligibility unsettled — renters vs owners, residency %, Zone of Truth attestation | *What is a citizen?* |
| The fused dragon slag — ~3,000 gp, undistributable, Ser Kaelen holds it | *How do we unfuse it, and who gets paid first?* |
| The Lionshield Coster's shipping collapse — party charged with fixing it, hasn't | *How do we get goods at a price we can pay?* |
| Adabra's Privy Council seat, effectively vacant | *Who speaks for the wild?* |
| Mountain's Toe — no visit since ch44, Earthstone Gem, Horia's addendum | *Is our mine income real?* |
| Prutha's crusade at Icespire Hold, unsupervised | *Is there an orc army converting on our doorstep?* |
| The Lord's Alliance jurisdiction map over Phandalin | *Are we about to be annexed?* |

Every one of these is a Claudien meal. The town is not being punished for the party's absence and
Claudien is not preying on them. The town has real problems, the heroes are in Neverwinter, and
something answers when they ask. **That is the horror, and it is entirely the party's own ledger.**

### The Absence Rule

Advance the Infection Clock **one stage per extended absence in which Phandalin faced a
consequential problem the party did not solve.** In practice: one stage per arc spent elsewhere,
plus the adventure's own in-scene advance/hold triggers when they are home.

Hold or reduce it when the party closes one of the backlog threads above, preserves a contradictory
memory, or gets a resident to state an intention before consulting. **Closing backlog is the
counterplay.** Say this out loud to nobody, but let the pattern be discoverable: the town consults
because the heroes left questions open.

Track it alongside the existing threat scores. Suggested name: **The Gentle Answer**, current value 2.

Two or three lines of mail per session is enough. Do not explain any of it. The clock does the work.

## The seed scene: the Neverember dinner

**Do not have Vukradin ask for the mayoralty.** He never has. He proclaimed UBT from the town green
and the party floated *direct mayoral elections*; the office is not something Dave has reached for.

**Invert it.** Neverember's canon agenda at this dinner is the party's "circulation of wealth," the
Candlekeep Report, and the fact that they rearranged Phandalin's government **without his blessing**.
So he makes that the bill: *you rearranged my town without asking. Fine. Then you own it. Be mayor.
Be accountable to me.*

This is better than the adventure's version on three counts. It uses Neverember's actual grievance
rather than inventing receptivity he does not have. It does not require Dave to want the office. And
it sharpens Bob's thesis — an office **pushed on** Vukradin tests "every hero secretly wants power"
far more cleanly than one he requested.

Aldus is the First Vector. He is a friend, he is a fan, he is trying to help, and he consulted
something to solve his lord's political problem. He will not know he did anything.

Also on the table and already canon: **Tibor Wester's unlicensed wildcat logging.** In ch38 Vukradin
persuaded Tibor's loggers to report it *to Neverwinter authorities*. Neverember may already have the
file. That is the corruption exhibit — not Harbin, who is a credit-claiming coward, but his brother,
who is running an actual racket in Neverember's jurisdiction.

## Canon corrections to the adventure text

Fix these before running any of it.

1. **The music studio is not blocked by Harbin.** It is funded (550 gp, earmarked restricted trust),
   unbuilt, with no premises named, and its centre of gravity has moved to *Neverwinter* — the gala,
   the Moonstone Mask patrons, the Common Chord. Either deliberately relocate it to Phandalin as part
   of Vukradin's civic pitch, or cut it as the corruption exhibit and use Tibor instead.
2. **Vukradin has never sought to govern.** See the inversion above.
3. **Neverember is not a receptive administrator.** He is watching them and holding a grievance.
4. **Harbin's corruption is petty; Tibor's is real.** Harbin diverts temple funds and runs off-council
   transactions. He also *cannot be pushed but will run in front of any parade already started* —
   which makes him an early and enthusiastic Claudien endorser, not an opponent.
5. **The mayoralty is a smaller prize than the adventure assumes.** Phandalin already has a Privy
   Council with Vukradin seated on it, and Harbin holds Townmaster/Lord Mayor. **Re-aim Bob's
   production at UBT.** The live question in town is not who is mayor; it is whether the constitution
   the party wrote survives contact. The mayoralty is the vehicle, UBT is the prize.
6. **The party will not be in Phandalin.** Per the placement ruling, that is now the design, not a bug.

### Linene as first petitioner

Her badly-worded original desire is already on the record. In ch45 she told the party the Coster's
cheap distribution had been replaced by costlier methods and asked them to look into it in
Neverwinter. Underneath the business language: *I don't know why my shipping broke and I am
frightened for my house.* They took the errand. They have not delivered.

She also produced, on screen, a Candlekeep text — **"the Sage's Universal Theorem for Income
Distribution"** — and chairs the UBT drafting subcommittee. `planning.md:44` records that Aletra
manages KP's work under **"The Sage"** framing. The vector is already played, in public, and the
party cheered.

This powers the adventure's own **"Name Before the Title"** reversion: restoring her original,
frightened, badly-worded question breaks the polished chain. The stated cost lands hard here —
Linene is exposed to blame, and she may beg the party to preserve the kinder revised history. She is
their single most load-bearing non-combat NPC and she holds both the town's constitution and
Brewbarry's business.

Keep her sympathetic throughout. She is not a cultist. She asked for help because she was scared and
nobody came.

### Boney as First Rememberer

He taught himself noble succession by **reading gravestones** across a century-plus interment, and in
ch47 he was scrupulous about the limits of what he could attest. He is undead, archival, and was
underground for the whole revision window.

If Claudien rewrites Phandalin, the physical records have to change — and the stones are records.
Boney notices that a name on a marker he has read a thousand times now reads differently, and he will
report it precisely and without drama, flagging exactly how confident he is. That is both the cleanest
clue in the adventure and a direct payoff of a characterization beat you established two weeks ago.

Watch the drift already logged in `campaign_state.md:299`: his "a few hundred years" overstates the
record, which dates Lady Alagondar's death to *over a century ago*. Do not let "centuries" propagate.

## The Temple on Tresendar — the party's institution in town

The Temple of Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice is the party's permanent presence in Phandalin and
the most important piece on this board. Canon: Valphine founded it in the converted Tresendar Manor,
Harbin did the paperwork for 50 gp, and **Brin Bundlewine** and **Giles Slipper-Shine** renovated it
for 25 gp in materials with the labour free. Both are logged as devoted allies of Valphine. Both are
still there.

### It is already a civic organ

Three canon threads run through it before Claudien touches anything:

- **UBT eligibility.** The ch45 debate floated **attestation under a Zone of Truth** as the test of
  citizenship. Valphine casts Zone of Truth. If that criterion lands, the temple is the body that
  *certifies who counts as a citizen of Phandalin* — which makes it the most capturable institution
  in town.
- **Brewbarry's bathrobes.** Phandalin cotton, **blessed by Lathander's priests**, Lathander's logo on
  the garment. The temple is a production facility with a loan against it.
- **The First Compact** is beneath it, per the placement above.

### Who holds it while they are away

Valphine is in Neverwinter. The five orc converts walked off to Icespire Hold with Prutha in ch45.
What is left is **Brin and Giles**: two devoted, ordinary, non-adventuring men running a church whose
founder is a hundred and fifty miles away, whose congregation emigrated, and whose town has hard
questions and no priest to answer them.

They will consult the Ledger. Not from weakness or corruption — because they are trying to keep
Valphine's church running properly and they genuinely do not know the answers. **The temple falls
through devotion, not through betrayal.** Play them warm and eager to the end; they will be thrilled
to show Valphine what they built while she was gone.

### The theological collision — Gentle vs Searing

This is the best thing in the fold and it is entirely pre-built.

Claudien is the **Gentle Answer**. He reduces suffering, waste, and uncertainty. That is his whole
pitch and it works.

Valphine's Lathander is the **Searing** one. Her doctrine, in her own words in ch47: *"The dawn is not
kind. The dawn is inexhaustible power arriving on schedule, and everything that cannot bear it
withers."* Pain is the honest mode of divine contact. She holds this against Brother Aldric, whom she
privately considers a fool with **"soft, squishy ways"** who never understood the real power of
Lathander.

They are exact opposites, and Claudien is more popular.

### The doctrine drift — recommended Return 2 or 3 reveal

While Valphine is away, Brin and Giles ask the Ledger what the Searing Light should teach in the
priest's absence. Claudien writes them a catechism. **It is good.** It is coherent, compassionate,
well-reasoned, better organised than anything Valphine ever wrote down, and it has quietly sanded the
searing off. Attendance is up. Nobody is being hurt.

Valphine comes home to a liturgy she did not write, that her own devoted men are proud of, that the
town prefers — with soft, squishy ways, in her own house. The thing she despises in Aldric has grown
in her church, planted by people who love her, while she was in Neverwinter being called an avatar.

Gary gets a genuine crisis out of this that costs no combat and no retcon.

### Act V — the temple is a venue, not an authority

The Right of First Thought is already Lathandrian in this campaign's terms: the dawn assays you
whether you consent or not, and you cannot hide your intention from the light. *FIRST, SPEAK YOUR OWN
DESIRE* is a Searing Light liturgy waiting to be written, and hosting the Rite in the temple gives the
party's own institution the permanent civic consequence.

**The trap:** the adventure names the failure condition explicitly — if the party steers the outcome,
they have merely replaced Claudien with themselves. A temple that *hosts* the rite is a venue. A
temple that *certifies* the rite is a new Ledger with better branding. Watch for the party sliding
from one to the other, especially Valphine, and especially if UBT attestation has already made the
temple a certifying body. **That slide is the most likely way this table loses without noticing.**

### Threat surface

Grundar Quartzvein targets Valphine specifically as a symbol of House Sotorra. Her church, her name on
it, staffed by two civilians, in a town she is never in. When Brundar's Echo starts ticking, this is
where it lands.

## Recurring threats — the episodic fuel

The backlog is the *standing* fuel for the clock. Recurring threats are the *episodic* fuel: each one
surfaces in Phandalin, forces a question, and the town has already asked it by the time the party
hears. Run one per homecoming, escalating.

| Threat | How it surfaces at home | The question it makes the town ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Prutha's Crusade** — DM flagged twice: *"That will have consequences"* | Icespire Hold is 55 miles out. Converts arrive as pilgrims, refugees, or a doctrinal delegation with a schism | *Is Valphine's church a danger to us?* — and it is aimed straight at the temple |
| **Adabra Gwynn** — "a different battlefield" | She identifies the revision correctly, as planar corruption, **and nobody listens because she is already discredited** | *Who speaks for the wild now?* (her council seat is vacant) |

| **Grundar Quartzvein** | Strikes the temple while Valphine is away | *Who protects us when the heroes are in the capital?* |

| **Sylvine Wintermoon** — fled Icespire Hold | Returns to the hoard claim now that there is a public treasure fund with published rules | *Whose treasure is it, legally?* |
| **Zeleen / the Whiskered Gang** | Wererats quietly keep the mine regardless of any deal; Earthstone Gem still not retrieved | *Is our mine income real?* |

Two notes on the table:

- **Adabra being right is the best beat in it.** She has spent the campaign insisting the party's
  interventions cause planar corruption. This time there genuinely is a retroactive planar revision
  eating the town, she names it, and she is dismissed as the woman who cried blight. The party has to
  decide whether to rehabilitate their enemy to save their home.
- **Sister Kaella and Zax are both "the dead and the professionally paranoid remember correctly."**
  Pair either with Boney rather than running them as separate reveals.

Bob's history as written (Silverymoon, the bags of holding, the Astral, the plot against Iymrith,
Zariel) reads as **another table's party**. Before he appears:

- **Does the Phandalin party know Bob exists?** Presumed no.
- **Does any PC have a route to that history?** The only Phandalin-side thread to Iymrith is Falcon —
  he admitted he found **Iymrith's hoard already plundered** and used the residual gold as lodge
  capital, and his **brass ring inscribed with Elvish for *lightning*** is logged in `planning.md:156`
  as his physical contact point with her legacy. Iymrith's Lair at Ascore is already the staged
  endgame collision.
- Note also that Corrin's card showed *"something older, colder, a pale lightning-touched creature of
  far greater age and menace"* beneath the green dragon. Given the ring, the lightning association is
  already in play. Decide deliberately whether that card is Iymrith before Bob makes his Iymrith
  grievance public, because the party will connect them.

Handle this the way `docs/Margaster.md` handles the cambion: write down what the *players* know from
other tables versus what any *character* knows. Cross-campaign knowledge leaking in as character
knowledge is the failure mode.

## The records problem — decide before Return 2

Claudien attacks records. Your campaign's records are an unusually elaborate apparatus:
`world_state.md`, `campaign_state.md`, chapter splits, the palace, the entity registry.

Running this means one of:

1. **Corrupt the live docs deliberately** as the clock advances, and keep a sealed pre-infection copy.
   Maximum effect, maximum risk to the pipeline — every consistency check will now flag real
   contradictions as errors.
2. **Keep the grounding docs clean and track the revision only here**, in a Claudien-side ledger of
   what has been rewritten and what still contradicts. Safe, and the diff between the two *is* the
   double memory the Rite restores.

**Recommend (2).** It costs nothing, it keeps `/staged-consistency` and the registry usable, and the
Claudien-side ledger becomes the physical prop for Act V. The bell does not need your `world_state.md`
to have lied; it needs two records that disagree, and a separate file is two records that disagree.

Also worth noting for the tone: the campaign already documents *"Terms Authored In Narration, Not At
The Table"* in `campaign_state.md:273`, tracking canon by provenance. That habit is exactly what
Claudien destroys. Consider making the party's own quest journal — the one Brewbarry thumbed through
in ch47 and could not find House Margaster in — a thing that acquires entries.
