# Eight for Supper, One for Answers

**Standing campaign reference:** [Harper doctrine](../neverwinter/harper_doctrine.md). Read it before adapting this scene for later sessions.

The Harper conversation before the [short sewer dungeon](20260907_chapter_51_we_re_famous_why_are_we_famous_sewer.md). A playable expansion of Scene 1 in the [main prep](20260907_chapter_51_we_re_famous_why_are_we_famous.md).

**[TABLE] Starting position:** Brewbarry has entered the House of a Thousand Faces. The reunited party traced Denvar's payment here and found a Harper office through the wall. They know the operator of the drop, not yet the purpose of the ninth crate.

**[OVERLAY] Scene purpose:** let the party understand the Harpers well enough to make a bargain. Give substantive answers early. The interesting question is what they are willing to share with people whose interests overlap with theirs.

## Provenance

**[TABLE — governing faction doctrine, GM ruling 2026-09-07]:** The Harpers seek to prevent any single dominant power in the North and thwart centralization, particularly concentrated state power. Freedom motivates them and can align them with good causes; they are not a “good” faction. All operational motives below are read through that objective.

**[TABLE — clarified by GM]:** Their greatest concern is **centralized, diffuse power — armies capable of control**. Centralized refers to command; diffuse refers to distributed enforcement. This is not a new doctrine against political decentralization or against independent adventurers as such.

**[TABLE — further GM ruling]:** This explains their hatred of the Zhentarim: the Zhentarim seek diffuse power through economic control concentrated in a few hands. Private economic domination and centralized state enforcement can pose the same structural threat to freedom. See the [standing doctrine](../neverwinter/harper_doctrine.md).

**[TABLE] Locked:** Lim is a Harper; the food operation undercuts Margaster and the Zhentarim; the ninth crate deliberately sustains Bimble because the Harpers want to understand the Commission. They have not debriefed him because the spiders prevent access. Bimble has fought those spiders to a standstill. The planar leak causes the longstanding mild aversion that protected the smuggling route for six years; the spiders intensify it into the confusion Vukradin experienced. Defeating them leaves the underlying leak unresolved. The Harpers do not automatically know this diagnosis or the details of Bimble's defense.

**[OVERLAY] Everything newly staged below:** both NPCs, their names, voices, personal motives, negotiating positions, and dialogue. These are proposed local operatives, not asserted owners or established leaders of the Neverwinter chapter. First seen: not yet. The dialogue can reveal locked facts without making its exact wording canon before play.

## The smuggling tunnel — what connects to what

**[TABLE] Purpose:** the Harpers move food around the official harbor charges and the costly distribution arrangements. Lim sells the resulting meals cheaply. Denvar is a paid carrier; his chits show a repeatable operation. The separate ninth crate preserves Bimble as a living source of information.

**[TABLE — GM ruling, 2026-09-07] The Harpers have used this route for years, exploiting the leak's mild aversion as protection for their smuggling.** They know the practical effect and how to work with it; technical understanding of the planar cause is not automatically established. Their longstanding food operation predates the recent feeding of Bimble. The spiders have intensified a useful protection into a dangerous obstruction.

**“But somebody is benefiting.”**

**Marta [OVERLAY]:** “Several people. They don't all want the same thing from you.”

**Oren [OVERLAY]:** “Ask Petra what a room full of your admirers buys. Then ask Lim whether she wants a room full of them. You'll get different answers.”

## The two voices

### Marta “Auntie” Venn — the person who wants the interview

**[OVERLAY] Look:** immaculate plum dress, silver at the temples, the absurd cat cushion. She puts it behind her lower back before sitting; it is an accommodation, not a familiar or a coded signal.

**Tic:** whenever someone asks for a favor, she asks who will be responsible for it. Her concern is accountability rather than coins.

**Wants:** access to Bimble's account that can help limit the Commission's power, with the witness safe and available before a dominant institution controls the account of what happened.

**Worry:** they finance the difficult weeks and then a better-connected faction captures the evidence and uses the crisis to consolidate power.

**Boundary:** she will not promise to publish everything she learns. She wants useful information and influence, not indiscriminate disclosure.

**Voice anchor:** “Certainly. Who is agreeing to that?” Courteous full sentences; answers the question, then names the condition. Under pressure, the courtesy remains and the sentences shorten.

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** She would use verified wrongdoing to force concessions that limit concentrated power before exposing it publicly. This is her proposed tactic in service of the ruled doctrine, not a mandatory practice for every Harper.

### Oren “Spoons” Pell — the person who has to deliver supper

**[OVERLAY] Look:** broad hands, tired eyes, velvet patched carefully enough to keep its shape. His teaspoons clink when he bends to lift something. He keeps taking teaspoons from the kitchen and forgetting to return them; that is the whole story of the nickname.

**Tic:** turns abstractions into quantities of meals, loads or working hours.

**Wants:** the party to leave couriers and kitchens out of whatever public victory they intend to claim.

**Worry:** one celebrated exposure will destroy an arrangement that ordinary customers rely on.

**Boundary:** he will not name another courier or food outlet to make the party trust him. He will discuss Denvar's already-exposed job.

**Voice anchor:** “Fine. What am I telling the people who were expecting to eat?” Short, practical sentences; tired rather than threatening.

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** Oren knows the deliveries and the payment route, not Bimble's technical work. He agrees that the ninth crate buys the prospect of answers. He dislikes hearing the recipient described as if he were already an asset on a shelf. He and Marta disagree about emphasis, not about whether to help him.

## What the Harpers actually want

**[TABLE — GM doctrine, 2026-09-07] The governing objective is to prevent any single dominant power in the North.** The Harpers thwart centralization and regard concentrated state power as dangerous. Freedom motivates them; that can align them with good causes, but they are not a “good” faction. This also matches the earlier decentralized-power description in `docs/world_state.md`.

**Marta [OVERLAY]:** “We don't want the North to have one master. However capable he is. However much you like him. Once every town needs his permission, his character is the only thing protecting them. That isn't enough for us.”

### Thann's distinction — what an army can keep doing

**[TABLE] Context supplied by the GM:** Konrad Ulbricht Thann's *Concerning the Employment of Adventurers as an Instrument of Policy* distinguishes the adventuring company's concentrated capabilities and limited political footprint from the distributed organization of armies and the work of governing after an obstacle is removed. The supplied paper does not establish that Marta has read it or that the party possesses a copy.

**[OVERLAY] Apply the distinction:** the Harpers' particular fear is a central authority acquiring the means to make its will effective throughout the North, continuously. A company can win a fight; an army and its supporting institutions can hold territory and compel people after the battle. Removing a dangerous person and enabling a permanent occupation are therefore different political acts, even if the same adventurers perform both.

**“Why hire us if you're afraid of power?”**

**Marta [OVERLAY]:** “You can get a man out of a guarded room. An army can put a guard in every village and keep him there. That's the difference that concerns us.”

**“And if the army keeps people safe?”**

**Marta [OVERLAY]:** “It can. It can also decide who travels, who trades, and whose orders count. Once every town depends on it, what happens when those towns disagree with the person commanding it?”

**“So what matters is what we leave behind?”**

**Marta [OVERLAY]:** “Yes. If you free a town, it can choose what to do next. If you clear the road so someone can occupy it, you've helped make that choice for them.”

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** Judge a proposal by the durable control it enables, not by how spectacular the party's violence looks. Rescuing Bimble and maintaining independent supply can serve Harper aims. Using the same evidence or cleared routes to consolidate one ruler's regional enforcement can conflict with them. These are applications of the doctrine, not predetermined judgments that every road, garrison or state project must be sabotaged.

> **GM-only:** This is the scene's political center. Feeding people is a real benefit, and Oren can care about it personally. The faction's test is whether people retain alternatives to a dominant power. Do not play the Harpers as generic benefactors, or infer that their doctrine authorizes every cruel or destructive tactic. Their means still need specific decisions.

**[TABLE] The food:** cheap supply undermines the grip of Margaster and the Zhentarim. **[OVERLAY]** Its strategic value is that access to food does not depend on one authority or commercial gatekeeper.

**Oren:** “Lim can buy food without going through them. That means she can say no to their terms. So can the people eating at her tables. That's what the eight crates buy us.”

**[TABLE] Bimble:** keeping him alive preserves the possibility of understanding the Commission.

**Marta:** “The ninth is for Bimble. We want to know what happened to the Manifold. He worked on it. His pay stopped. He's alive, and we would like him to remain available to answer questions.”

**Oren:** “He also needs to eat.”

**Marta:** “Yes, Oren. We agree about the crate.”

**[OVERLAY] Political use:** they want evidence that helps constrain the Commission and preserve independent choices. Moving all its powers to another central authority would not meet that objective.

**Marta:** “If Bimble can show us what happened, we may be able to make them give something up. Exclusive rights. The power to close someone else's business. Control of a route. Replacing an official doesn't help much if the next one inherits every power he abused.”

> **GM-only:** Practical relief, intelligence collection, and political bargaining serve the same opposition to concentrated power. The listed concessions are possible negotiating targets, not claims that Bimble's evidence already proves particular offenses. Do not make the doctrine a hidden twist: the tension comes from whether the party agrees with its consequences.

Lim's negative reaction draws on Soma's former outlook. Her understanding is outdated, not baseless from the start. Let Soma explain her development; its timing and causes are not established here.

Keep Marta's answer limited: she does not automatically know Lim's private reasoning. If the party returns to Lim, use the [doctrine note's Lim dialogue](../neverwinter/harper_doctrine.md). Whether Lim deliberately steered Soma toward the ninth crate is still a separate question.

## Bargain and branches

**[OVERLAY] A workable agreement:** the party attempts the rescue; Bimble chooses whether and where to speak; the party keeps its own evidence; the Harpers offer a private meeting and practical help within what they can actually arrange; nobody publicly identifies the couriers or Lim without discussing the consequences. Name who undertakes each promise. Marta's tic finally has a purpose.

- **They cooperate:** Oren answers logistical questions and arranges a return contact. Marta makes her intended interview questions available: what changed, who had access, what records remain? These questions do not imply she already knows the answers.
- **They agree to rescue but reject priority access:** Marta accepts a later, nonexclusive interview rather than losing the opportunity entirely. This is a concession the players can win.
- **They threaten exposure:** Oren asks exactly who they intend to name and what happens to the next delivery. Marta stops adding new operational details. They can still leave and do Rsolk's job; no obligatory fight with the Harpers.
- **They want to confront Lim first:** let them. Oren can point them back to the restaurant without summoning her magically. Her friendship scene requires her own choices, not Marta's account of them.

## Stage directions

- Give the eight/ninth-crate explanation within the first few substantive questions. Suspicion is already earned; more evasiveness adds little.
- Let Oren interrupt Marta once about feeding Bimble. More than that turns them into a rehearsed double act.
- Pause after Marta admits she wants leverage. Let the party choose the moral argument.
- If they make enforceable conditions, let those conditions matter. Do not quietly treat agreement as surrendering custody.
- Finish with practical departure arrangements when the players are satisfied. The sewer is still waiting; no artificial interruption is needed to force them into it.
