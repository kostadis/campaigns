## Scene 1 — A Thousand Faces and One Burned Drop

### Setup

- **When/place:** immediately after last session, pre-dawn, public floor of the House of a Thousand Faces. Mirrors, mannequins in antique fashions, lush couches, and a fire that draws too well for Bluelake. `[MODULE §Bluelake: House of a Thousand Faces]`
- **On screen:** Brewbarry, staff, ordinary patrons. The Harper Seat is below until given a reason to surface. `[OVERLAY]`
- **Offstage:** Soma and Valphine nearby; Vukradin on the sending stone; the rat has already breached the back office. `[TABLE]`
- **Standing suspicion:** the chapter saw the party publicly hand Perrin Alagondar a Necklace of Fireballs in front of Neverember. They cannot tell whether the party serves the Sons of Alagondar or whether the whole handover was Neverember's bait. `[TABLE]` / `[OVERLAY]` Harper read

### Opening image

Brewbarry enters a room he once could not afford while wearing the prototype of a business that cannot afford its own shipping. Nobody stops him. Nobody welcomes him too quickly. One mannequin wears a coat with softer lining than anything he has sourced in Neverwinter. `[OVERLAY]`

The barkeep's first line:

> “If you're here for the mirrors, they flatter selectively. If you're here for a drink, that is more reliable.”

### Branch — Brewbarry pitches bathrobes or shipping

The staff member asks exactly one intelligent question about landed cost. If Brewbarry answers honestly, the Harper Seat joins him and frames the factional collision without naming the faction:

> “Margaster delivers on time because everyone else pays for the privilege. We deliver late enough that nobody can charge the village.”

They admire his aim and think his plan structurally naïve. The conversation can open the basement, but only after he gives them the real problem: Margaster-controlled lanes are crushing an otherwise funded venture. `[OVERLAY]`

### Branch — he names the Harper symbol or the breached room

The public staff do not react. The fire is suddenly the loudest thing in the room. The Harper Seat appears and asks:

> “Did you come through the front door to tell me you used the other one?”

If the party volunteers **how**, **what the rat saw**, and **that they do not yet know who the client reference names**, the Seat keeps talking. If they bluff, threaten, or conceal Denvar, the meeting ends pleasantly and two operatives begin tailing them. `[OVERLAY]`

### Branch — the whole famous party piles in

The public room becomes a stage and the chapter gives them nothing. Let Vukradin notice the problem: their fame, which opens every respectable door, closes this one. They can recover by asking for a private room and leading with the truth about the breach **and the necklace handover** — what they returned, why they attached no condition, and why they still disliked Perrin. A professional asset would bring a better story. `[TABLE]` / `[OVERLAY]`

### What the Harpers can reveal — only if trust is earned

- They operate the food route to break Margaster/Zhentarim pricing. `[TABLE]`
- They know **Bimble** collects the ninth crate but have never made contact. They do **not** volunteer his name. `[TABLE]`
- “Spiders” prevent them from following the collector; the Harpers do not know the exact count. `[TABLE]`
- They have kept the recipient alive because the Commission is lying about the Manifold. `[TABLE]`
- They will compare the chit's client reference to their books only if the party shares the full chit. That comparison points to **Lim's restaurant account**. `[TABLE evidence]`

Do not make Lim walk in for the reveal. Soma should have to decide what it means that her friend of six years fed a hidden man nightly and answered her like a customer. `[OVERLAY]`

### Material failure

The Harpers do not attack. They burn Denvar's payment channel, rotate the account, and let Brewbarry leave with an exquisite fabric swatch that cannot be sourced through legal shipping. He has gained exactly the commercial temptation he needed and lost the clean investigative route. `[OVERLAY]`

### NPC notes

- **The Harper Seat:** wants to know whether the party is a force she can aim or merely weather she must plan around. Tic: never answers the noun in a question; answers its consequence.
- **House staff:** want the public tavern to remain boring. Tic: describe suspicious events as hospitality problems.
- **Brewbarry:** his natural Harper pitch is sincere — high shipping costs threaten a softness business meant to repair ancestral violence. Do not turn that into a joke before the Harpers decide whether they believe him.

- **When/place:** after regrouping and provisioning, at Rsolk's boundary in the Bluelake sewers. `[TABLE]`

### Approach

Rsolk is pleased to see Vukradin and much less pleased to see that “lost” has become an armed expedition.

> “Found yourself, then.”

Do not replay the first negotiation. Confirm the route, the tide, and the contract in three exchanges, then move. `[OVERLAY]`

### Branch — Vukradin admits he lied

Rsolk respects the admission more than the original deception. He spends the withheld warning:

> “Drow asked about that bend five tendays back. Three, maybe four. Polite. Paid well. Wanted to know if anybody ever got through.”

This is not forgiveness. Rsolk adds a marker: one unnamed future favour, separate from the spider job. `[OVERLAY]`

### Branch — they bring Harper information

Rsolk's interest becomes proprietary. A six-year route through his tunnels has finally acquired an owner. He does not interfere before the spiders are dead, but **his clock begins the moment they are**. The party has just pointed the Dead Rats at the Harpers. `[TABLE collision]`

### Branch — they stay transactional

He gives no drow warning. Sridar's appearance remains a genuine surprise, and the cost of Vukradin's first deception lands late. `[TABLE consequence]`

### NPC notes

- **Rsolk:** wants sovereignty over his tunnels more than money. Fan warmth is genuine, but ignorance on his own ground feels like humiliation. Tic: asks for autographs while making territorial threats.
- **Dead Rats:** want their lost territory back. They are already thinking about what follows the extermination.

## Scene 3 — The Junction Opens for Everyone

### Setup

- **When/place:** the turn of the tide, in an abandoned Alagondar **switching cistern** where the old aqueduct meets Neverwinter's lower sewers. It once diverted water between the lower-dock outfall and an overflow channel; the Dead Rats call it simply **the Junction**. `[OVERLAY]`
- **On screen:** six Rift Weavers. `[TABLE — Axeholm count/traits]`
- **Offstage:** Sridar's cell behind; the Harper recovery pair only if alerted; Dead Rats waiting at their boundary. `[OVERLAY]`

#### Physical layout — 60 × 50 feet, 25 feet high

The west maintenance tunnel opens onto a raised landing. Fresh grooves from the ninth crate cross it, skirt the central distortion, and continue through the eastern arch toward Bimble. **The homunculus and crate have already passed; there is no crate in this room.** `[TABLE]` / `[OVERLAY]`

```text
                         NORTH INSPECTION GALLERY — 10 ft wide
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────╲ 10-ft gap
WEST TUNNEL → [ WEST         PIER     │FAULT│     PIER   EAST ] → BIMBLE
              [ LANDING   ~~~~~~~~~ CENTRAL SUMP ~~~~~  LANDING ]
                └──────────── SOUTH VALVE GALLERY ──────┘
                                      5-ft pinch at wheel
```

- **Central sump:** 30 × 30 feet, its floor 5 feet below the galleries. The water begins as knee-deep difficult terrain and rises one foot each round for six rounds. `[OVERLAY]`
- **North inspection gallery:** 10 feet wide and dry. Its easternmost 10 feet have collapsed, leaving a gap to the east landing. Dry planar webbing covers the ceiling above it. `[OVERLAY]`
- **South valve gallery:** 10 feet wide and intact, narrowing to 5 feet for 10 feet beside the rusted sluice wheel. A creature must stop advancing to work the mechanism. `[OVERLAY]`
- **West and east landings:** each 15 feet deep. The west is the party's entrance; the east opens toward Bimble and the lower docks. `[OVERLAY]`
- **Support piers:** two heavy masonry piers rise from the sump and support the barrel vault. `[OVERLAY]`

#### The fault in the middle

The planar fault is a ragged, vertical plane **10 feet wide and 15 feet high**, centered between the two piers and oriented north–south, facing the western entrance. It occupies only the middle of the 30-foot-wide sump: there are 10 feet of open water around its north end and 10 feet around its south end. It touches neither gallery, wall, nor ceiling. Draw it as a 10-foot line between squares, not an area filling the sump. `[OVERLAY]`

It is a crease in reality, not an open portal. Water runs sideways at its edge; masonry viewed through it sits several inches out of place; sounds crossing it arrive twice. The Weavers' dry filaments radiate from it in rigid geometries. Characters can use either gallery—or go around either end within the sump—without touching it. `[OVERLAY]`

***Planar Rejection.*** When a creature first touches or enters the fault on a turn, it makes a DC 14 Charisma saving throw. On a success, it controls the displacement and teleports to an unoccupied space it can see within 20 feet of the fault. On a failure, it takes 9 (2d8) force damage, is displaced to a random unoccupied space within 20 feet, and falls prone. A Rift Weaver automatically succeeds and chooses its destination. Describe the creature appearing in two places for a heartbeat before reality violently selects one. `[OVERLAY]`

#### What each route costs

| Route | Advantage | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **North gallery** | Dry, elevated, and short | Heavy webbing, two Weavers overhead, and a 10-foot gap at the eastern end |
