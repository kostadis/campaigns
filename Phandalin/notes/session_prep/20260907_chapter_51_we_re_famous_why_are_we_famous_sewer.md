# The Man at the Other End

Short sewer dungeon following **Chapter 51 We're famous. Why are we famous?** Four level-7 PCs, all players present. **[OVERLAY]** About 60–90 minutes: one navigation problem, one main fight, one survivor. Companion to the [session prep](20260907_chapter_51_we_re_famous_why_are_we_famous.md).

> **Scope:** Rsolk's approach → the confusing bend → the spiders' position → Bimble's defensive line. The Harpers can point the party here; their interview need not be a prerequisite. Stop when the party reaches Bimble if the fight fills the session.

## Rulings and provenance

- **[TABLE — GM ruling, 2026-09-07, reconciled and confirmed]** The planar leak produces a longstanding mild aversion that protected the smuggling route for six years. The spiders intensify it into the confusion that turned Vukradin away. Bimble has fought them to a standstill; they are effectively stuck. Defeating them removes the worst confusion, but the leak and its mild aversion remain.
- **[TABLE — GM ruling, 2026-09-07]** The Harpers have used the route for years, exploiting its mild aversion to protect their food smuggling. The spiders made their useful protection dangerous. Clearing this siege helps their longstanding operation as well as their recent effort to keep Bimble alive.
- **[TABLE — GM doctrine, 2026-09-07]** The Harpers oppose centralization and any single dominant power in the North, motivated by freedom rather than membership in a “good” faction. **[OVERLAY]** Their benefit from this dungeon is an independent supply route and access to information that may constrain the Commission.
- **[TABLE — clarification]** Their greatest concern is centrally directed, distributed power capable of sustained control, exemplified by armies. **[OVERLAY]** Clearing one obstruction serves their aims here; turning that success into a route for lasting domination would be a different proposition.
- **[TABLE]** Six Rift Weavers block the route. Vukradin previously lost interest in his destination, not his memories. A small brass-and-leather construct carries food through. Fire burned their webs at Axeholm; magical fire and radiant damage hurt them visibly.
- **[OVERLAY]** All dimensions, connections, tactics, dialogue and optional procedures below are proposed. The fire emplacement explains the stalemate without requiring a new faction or artifact revelation.

## 1. The abandoned steps

[OVERLAY] Dry upper landing, abandoned handcart, water marks on the stairs. Rsolk points down: “We stopped going in. After a while, we stopped thinking that was strange.” He wants his people back at work; he is embarrassed that he accepted their excuses for so long.

- **Prepare:** choose a return time and leave a guide here. A rope or agreed recall signal provides an anchor for the confusing stretch.
- **Go straight in:** no penalty beyond meeting Area 2 unprepared. This room costs two minutes, not an equipment-shopping scene.

## 2. The perfectly reasonable wrong turn

[OVERLAY] The main passage continues toward faint scorching. A comfortable-looking side recess offers a dry place to sit. The compelling thought is: **there is nothing worth doing farther down**.

**Show the effect:** an old boot trail reaches this point, turns around, and leaves. Their own purpose begins to seem tedious. They can still remember it and discuss it.

**Proposed procedure [OVERLAY]:** use the previously established save if available; otherwise one Wisdom save, DC 13, on first exposure. Failure prompts a character to suggest returning or waiting here. A companion naming their concrete purpose, a written reminder, or following an agreed rope gets them moving again. No repeated saves or lost turns during combat. This is proposed exploration behavior, not the *Confusion* spell.

- **Resist or help one another:** follow the main passage to Area 4.
- **Investigate the recess:** find the collector's scraped service opening into Area 3. Following the wrong impulse can still yield useful discovery.

## 3. The little delivery route

[OVERLAY] Dry brick conduit above the runoff. Splinters and brass scratches show repeated passage. A small inspection grille overlooks Area 4; the far end opens behind Bimble's barricade.

- **Scout:** see all six spiders and the defensive firing lane before committing. Through the far opening, communicate with Bimble and coordinate an approach.
- **Bypass:** small forms or suitable magic can reach Area 5. Let a successful transport plan rescue him without fighting. Rsolk's spider job remains unfinished.

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** The conduit is too small for the weavers. Their planar movement does not grant knowledge of an unseen safe destination; use their actual campaign movement limits. If those limits allow them to bypass solid barriers freely, Bimble's established defenses must cover that approach too. Do not invent unlimited teleportation for either side.

## 4. The sluice hall — the fight

[OVERLAY] A 40-by-30-foot chamber. A shallow central runoff trench, two stone piers, raised side walks. Three weavers cling near the eastern arch; three occupy the ceiling and piers. Beyond the arch lies a blackened, exposed approach to Bimble's position.

**The stalemate is visible before initiative:** a weaver edges toward the arch. A brief gout of fire forces it back. Bimble shouts, “Still here!” His voice makes the survivor real before the party has earned an introduction.

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** The spiders want to reach him but cannot cross his covered approach intact. They attack the party when threatened from behind. They do not suddenly acquire an obsession with the obsidian sword. Bimble holds his line; he cannot safely swing his emplacement around to clear the room for them.

- **Fight from the west:** piers break sightlines; fire can clear webbed footing; the party can make space instead of standing under all six. Use existing Rift Weaver statistics and their established fire/radiant responses.
- **Coordinate from Area 3:** Bimble warns before firing. Drawing spiders into his existing lane earns a useful contribution under the selected weapon rules; he does not take over the encounter.
- **Withdraw:** the western exit remains the retreat route unless combat changes it. Bimble cannot abandon his line to pursue. Unbeaten spiders retain their position; no rival automatically slips through them.

**On victory [TABLE]:** the worst confusion lifts; the underlying leak and mild aversion remain. **[OVERLAY]** Show the difference: the route beyond the arch seems worth following again, although looking down it still invites a faint, unreasonable reluctance. Do not repeat the navigation save during the rescue; the residual sensation signals unfinished planar business, not another obstacle to the earned success.

## 5. Bimble's line

[OVERLAY] A low barricade, fixed fire projector, bedroll and neatly stacked delivery-crate boards. Old web burns reach almost to his tools. His defenses protect a small space, not an escape route.

“Please don't stand there. I've spent weeks learning exactly how to burn that patch of floor.”

- **Announce themselves:** he stops aiming once he understands they are not attacking. The collector settles beside him. Let the party make the food connection.
- **Rush the barricade:** he warns them first. Give them a chance to stop; being startled need not become a second full fight.
- **Offer evacuation:** he wants his manual and tools. Carrying the neatly labeled crate while soaked in sewer water gives the rescue its modest comic cost. The testimony and Sridar's possible approach are in the main prep.

## Open decisions

- **Encounter sheets:** use the established weaver block and choose the fire projector's mechanics before play. Six creatures and their stalemate are settled; approximate phase-spider math is not.
- **Defensive explanation:** confirm the covered arch and service-channel geometry against those creatures' actual planar movement. Bimble's defense must stop abilities they really possess.
- **Confusion procedure:** retain the prior save if recorded, or use the proposed single-save, companion-assisted procedure above.
