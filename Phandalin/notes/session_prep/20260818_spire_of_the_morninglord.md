# Dawn Over the Spire — The Blessed Drow Comes Home

Co-GM working doc for tonight's session — the benefit concert / dawn service at the Spire of the Morninglord. Not canon yet.

**What I understood:** This is a social set piece, not a plotted scene — Valphine is the center of gravity and genuinely unpredictable, so the doc below gives you people, a room, and tables to roll or steer on rather than branches to follow. Per your call, three previously-separate threads land here simultaneously: Valphine's reunion with Brother Aldric over the golden eyes, Perrin Alagondar producing his three witnesses for the necklace, and in-person appearances by Lord Cassian and Lord Neverember (both of whom, per canon, had *separately* arranged private meetings — see Open Decisions on what that does to those threads). Brewbarry and Vukradin are present but riding shotgun; Soma is the support act. Party is level 7.

---

## The Shape of the Evening (not plot — just the frame)

Nothing here is scripted to a conclusion. What's actually converging in one room, tonight:

- Valphine's public reveal as "the blessed drow" to the man who trained her — and to a room that has decided, without asking her, what that means.
- Perrin's necklace claim reaching its evidentiary deadline (three witnesses, as Brewbarry demanded and Valphine staged inside her own temple).
- House Margaster's man in the room, watching, with a promise hanging (naming the gnome technician "the following day" — that day was yesterday, per canon; he owes them a name).
- Two of the North's most powerful men choosing to be seen at a drow cleric's sermon, for reasons that are almost certainly not about Lathander.
- A band name landing in public for the first time.

None of these need to resolve tonight. Let Valphine's choices reorganize the room — she is the one variable nobody at that party, in or out of fiction, has fully modeled.

---

## The Setting: The Spire of the Morninglord

The oldest Lathanderite temple in Neverwinter, rebuilt twice since the Ruining and the Spellplague — the faithful read the third structure as proof of doctrine, not damage. Brother Aldric Sunmantle has served here thirty years: human, fifties, careful, genuinely devout, orthodox in the way of a man whose framework has never been seriously tested. It's about to be.

Tonight it's dressed for the benefit concert: temporary risers at the back for the overflow crowd, the nave's normal austerity softened with donated flowers (a Moonstone Mask touch — someone with money wanted this to look like an event), and the east-facing doors thrown open toward the pre-dawn dark so the actual sunrise can hit the altar on cue if the timing holds. That's the theatrical spine of the night: the service is built to climax at dawn, which means the GM controls the literal lighting.

**Staging:**
- Aldric is at the altar, visible the moment the party enters — he'll see Valphine from across the nave before she reaches him. Use that beat; don't let it happen offhand.
- Cullen Sharpe takes a seat near the back, alone, visible, unbothered — he came to be seen coming.
- Perrin and his fixer wait near a side chapel, not seated with the congregation — they're here on business, not worship, and Perrin is uncomfortable enough about that to hover.
- Cassian, if he comes, sits somewhere he can watch Cullen without being watched watching him.
- Neverember, if he comes, does not sit in the congregation. He stands, or takes a raised side gallery — Aldus a half-step behind him. Power doesn't sit in a pew it doesn't own.
- Boney is outside or at the threshold — skeletal horses and consecrated ground are a GM call, but "Boney stops walking before you notice something's off" is a move already established as his signature (see the Sealed Chasm precedent) — reuse it if the room needs a silent tell.

## NPC Roster

### Brother Aldric Sunmantle — the man whose story is about to be corrected
**Look:** Human, fifties, weathered-careful, orthodox vestments worn like a habit rather than a costume.
**Voice:** Gentle, certain, uses the vocabulary of *mercy, warmth, hope* reflexively — these are load-bearing words for him, not filler.
**Stake tonight:** He has told Valphine's conversion story to his colleagues for four months — a drow who fled darkness and found the light — and been moved by telling it. He is about to discover the story is true and he's been telling it wrong the whole time. This will cost him his *account* of his faith, not his faith itself.
**What he wants from this scene:** To be right about her. To have his theology hold.
**GM-only:** The golden eyes are not a gift in Valphine's own read — they're a mark, the Dawn Father making its claim on her face permanent, unambiguous. She finds this clarifying, not comforting. She will not correct Aldric's misreading of it as blessing rather than brand; she doesn't think it's relevant. Play his wonder-then-alarm as genuine: he recognizes the mark from scripture, then realizes it doesn't look like what scripture prepared him for. It looks like it would hurt.
**Trigger line:** The conversation holds together until Aldric says *mercy*, *warmth*, or *hope* — whichever comes first. Valphine won't argue. She'll just look at him the way someone looks at a word used wrong one too many times. He'll notice. He'll ask, for the first time in four months, what she actually believes. Play that as a real question, not a rhetorical one.

### Perrin Alagondar — the sincere man in the wrong company
**Look:** Earnest, a little threadbare-noble, the kind of person who has read one letter forty times.
**Voice:** Formal, apologetic, prone to overcorrecting himself the second he goes too far ("I'm not going to start a revolution, no, no — this is a symbol").
**Stake tonight:** This is his shot at legitimacy — and his last shot if the witnesses don't hold. He genuinely doesn't know his own documentation is Margaster-forged.
**What he wants:** The necklace, as a restoration banner for the Alagondar name, not a weapon. He's already said he's a believer in the Morninglord as "a god of good," delivered with a sincerity Valphine received with total blaséness — that's his opening line here if he needs one.
**Play him soft.** Brewbarry yawning at his lineage speech and Soma's flat "I'd imagine a lot of people would lie to claim it's theirs" already punctured his grandeur once — he arrives tonight a little smaller, trying to recover ground with actual proof instead of speeches.

### The Fixer (House Margaster's minder) — unnamed on-screen, see Open Decisions
**Look:** Deliberately unremarkable; the kind of presence that reaches for a weapon only when Cullen's look tells him not to.
**Stake tonight:** He is not here for Perrin. He is here to see whether the party actually goes through with returning the necklace, and to report back.
**Voice:** Doesn't talk much. Let his silence do the work — he already backed down once (Brewbarry's paper-crumpling bit) without a word.

### Cullen Sharpe — the man who owes them a name
**Look:** Dark green suit, immaculate, spells his own name out letter by letter when it amuses him.
**Voice:** Warm, never raised, addresses everyone by name like he's cataloging them.
**Stake tonight:** He promised to name the manifold's gnome technician "the following day" — that day has passed. He either delivers, stalls with a plausible reason, or trades the name for something. Decide before the scene which one — see Open Decisions; this is the live wire of the whole night given the Bimble Nackle reveal is primed but not yet meant to fire carelessly.
**What he wants:** To be donated to and refuse politely (his established tell — invited to give, he smiles, gives nothing, walks away). To watch how "Valphine of the Blessed" plays in public, because House Margaster profits from instability and a drow avatar of Lathander is either a liability to exploit or an asset to buy.
**Play him amused, not threatened**, by almost everything — until Valphine's lie-detection ability (still mechanically unsettled, per canon) gets pointed at him. That's the one thing that should visibly cost him composure, even briefly.

### Lord Cassian Meliamne — grateful, weak, and watching Cullen
**Look:** Sea elf nobleman, more comfortable with grief than with courage.
**Stake tonight:** He's terrified of Margaster and has already folded once (the 200gp writ). Being in the same room as Cullen, in public, is a real cost to him — decide whether he's here because Valphine's invitation mattered to him, or because he couldn't find a polite way to refuse a blessed cleric's temple.
**What he wants:** To say something true about his sister's death without Cullen overhearing it land. He'll speak more freely if he can get Valphine alone for even a moment — which is exactly what the still-pending private tea was for. Consider staging a single, brief aside here rather than the whole conversation (see Open Decisions).

### Lord Dagult Neverember — testing the phenomenon in person
**Look:** Every inch the Lord Protector; doesn't sit where he isn't the highest point in the room.
**Stake tonight:** He summoned this party over their "circulation of wealth" and Phandalin's unauthorized new government. A drow claiming Lathander's mark, drawing a public crowd, in his city, is either the same problem in a new costume or something he can co-opt. He's here to find out which, in person, before the private dinner he's already arranged happens.
**What he wants:** To be seen respecting the event without endorsing it. Watch for a line that name-drops the Candlekeep Report or "circulation of wealth" again — that's his tell that the political read is still running under the courtesy.
**Play him controlled, not hostile.** He's not here to arrest anyone. He's here to look a golden-eyed drow in the face and decide what she costs him.

### Aldus — the friendly filter
**Look:** Polite, professional, visibly trying not to be charmed twice in one week.
**Stake tonight:** He's a secret fan and Neverember's steward in the same body — every reaction he has is two reactions layered.
**Use him for:** Softening Neverember's read for the party in real time, and for comic pressure release if the room gets too tense. He already promised his invented daughter Alducia would hear about this service — a stray line about her is free color.

### Texture NPC — a junior acolyte (build to taste)
Someone under Aldric, young, has never seen a drow up close, has definitely heard the "torture cult" rumor about Valphine's own congregation back home. Useful for a scandalized-fascinated reaction beat, or as the person who accidentally repeats "mercy, warmth, hope" back at her before Aldric does, giving her one free warning shot before the real conversation.

## Random Tables for Social Encounters

*Roll or pick freely — these are texture, not plot. Reroll or ignore anything that fights what's actually happening at the table.*

**d8 — Congregation reaction as Valphine takes the altar / eyes are seen clearly for the first time**
1. Dead silence, then a single person kneels, starting a ripple no one asked for.
2. A noble mutters "I told you it was real" to their companion, loud enough to carry.
3. Someone faints — theatrically, not dangerously. Aldric doesn't break stride; he's used to this.
4. A child asks, at full volume, "Why do her eyes hurt to look at?" No adult has a good answer.
5. Scattered applause, uncertain whether that's appropriate in a temple — it dies out awkwardly.
6. A Lathanderite elder in the crowd audibly disapproves — "This is not how the Dawn Father works."
7. Someone starts humming a hymn that isn't the one being sung, thrown off entirely.
8. Total, respectful, unnerving quiet. Worse than any of the above.

**d6 — Who approaches Soma during the mingling (and why)**
1. A Bluelake fisherman's widow, mistaking her for a healer, asks about a sick child — genuine, not a hook.
2. An Exile Aesthetic merchant tries to license "the tortle look" for a product line. Soma's response is free.
3. A city guard asks, quietly, whether the skeletal horse outside is going to be a problem.
4. A Harper-coded stranger asks an oddly specific question about planar anomalies and vanishes into the crowd before Soma can press.
5. Someone recognizes "protector of the Glades" and wants a blessing for a garden. Soma has no idea what to do with this.
6. A drunk noble asks if the staff is for sale. Soma's staff visibly does not appreciate this.

**d8 — Perrin's three witnesses (roll or assign; at least one should complicate, not confirm)**
1. An elderly notary clerk — genuine, remembers the original Alagondar family records, but is visibly frightened of someone in the room (Cullen, or the fixer).
2. A minor Nasher sympathizer — enthusiastic, a little too enthusiastic, undermines Perrin's "not a revolution" framing just by being present.
3. A servant of House Alagondar's former estate (now owned by someone else) — knows household details, not bloodline — useful color, weak proof.
4. A hedge historian / genealogist — the strongest witness, cross-references cleanly with Boney's own account, but was paid by *someone*, and won't say who.
5. A tearful distant cousin — emotionally convincing, factually thin.
6. A no-show. The witness who was supposed to come didn't. Perrin has to explain why, badly.
7. A witness who, under any scrutiny, turns out to be Margaster-connected after all — exactly what Valphine's condition was designed to prevent.
8. A witness who asks the party a question they can't easily answer — e.g., "And who are you to be the ones deciding this?"

**d6 — Cullen Sharpe needling table**
1. Compliments the "benefit concert" concept as "very good for optics" — not quite an insult.
2. Asks, pleasantly, whether the party has settled on a band name yet, already knowing the answer will be awkward.
3. Mentions, in passing, that House Margaster "always delivers the goods" — same line as before, daring them to call it a threat.
4. Offers a donation, then doesn't, then smiles like that was the donation.
5. Asks Perrin, in front of everyone, whether the letter of introduction was sufficient — reminding the room whose paperwork got him here.
6. If pressed on the gnome's name: delivers it straight, stalls with a plausible excuse, or names a decoy — GM's call per Open Decisions, but play whichever choice as deliberate, not evasive-by-accident.

**d6 — Neverember tells (mood/attention shifts)**
1. Watches Valphine's sermon with real, unperformed interest — the political read comes later, but the interest is genuine first.
2. Exchanges a look with Aldus that clearly means "we're moving the dinner up."
3. Asks a pointed economic question disguised as small talk — "circulation of wealth" surfaces again.
4. Notices Cullen Sharpe in the room and his expression goes carefully neutral — a data point for the GM, not necessarily for the party.
5. Compliments the temple's renovation budget, needling at where the money came from.
6. Says almost nothing all night, which is its own message.

**d6 — Rumor/whisper overheard in the crowd about the golden-eyed drow**
1. "She's not the first — there was one in Waterdeep, decades back. Didn't end well for her."
2. "I heard Lathander himself walked the surface to mark her personally."
3. "My cousin says it's a curse dressed up as a blessing. Watch what happens to the people near her."
4. "She's Order of the Gauntlet-adjacent, isn't she? That's the interesting part, not the eyes."
5. "If Neverember's here for this, it's not about the sermon."
6. "Drow priestess, glowing eyes, in *this* temple? Someone's about to make a very bad political bet."

**d6 — Crowd reaction to "Lathander's Death" landing as the band name**
1. Uneasy laughter that turns into applause once someone decides it's clever.
2. A devout attendee is visibly offended — "death" and "Lathander" do not belong in the same sentence to them.
3. Someone immediately starts merchandising it out loud ("that's going on a mug by tomorrow").
4. Aldric's expression is unreadable — file for later.
5. Cullen repeats it back with exaggerated appreciation, making it sound worse.
6. It just lands. No comedy, no complication — sometimes a bit works.

**d4 — Wildcard interruption (use sparingly, at most once)**
1. A messenger arrives for Neverember mid-service — he reads it, gives nothing away, stays anyway.
2. Boney does something at the threshold that draws the crowd's attention away from the altar for a beat.
3. A stranger in the back leaves abruptly the moment the golden eyes are fully visible — nobody catches who.
4. The dawn light hits the altar early or late — a small, real, uncontrolled thing, and someone in the crowd reads it as an omen either way.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Is Neverember here to arrest us?"** → No — he's here to look, and the private dinner is either still coming or effectively happening tonight depending on your call (see Open Decisions). Don't let the party read his presence as a threat; play it as scrutiny.
- **"Can we just refuse to hear Perrin's witnesses tonight?"** → They can stall, but Valphine set this venue and this deadline herself ("I definitely want to see him set foot within the temple") — reneging costs her credibility with Perrin and possibly with Aldric, who's watching her conduct as closely as her theology.
- **"Do we finally get the gnome's name?"** → Your call, live at the table — see Open Decisions. Cullen owes it to them either way; how he pays is the interesting part.
- **"What about the necklace itself — do we hand it over tonight?"** → Only if the witnesses actually hold up. The forgery under the provenance is still unexposed; nothing here should accidentally resolve it unless the party specifically digs (Ostavar/Salvage Archive verification, Havel Drest, etc. — all still live per the consolidated Neverwinter notes).

## Stage directions

- Let the room go quiet when Aldric first sees her across the nave. Don't fill it.
- The *mercy/warmth/hope* beat is the emotional peak of the Aldric thread — slow down there, let Valphine's silence do the work before his question lands.
- If Cullen actually names the gnome tonight, do not rush past it — that's a genuine reveal moment even if the party doesn't yet grasp its significance. Let it sit for a beat before the scene moves on.
- If Neverember speaks directly to Valphine, that's the single highest-status interaction of the night — give it space, don't let Vukradin or Brewbarry step on it (they're backseat tonight by design).
- Keep Soma's beats physical and practical per her voice — she should be noticing hands, posture, exits, not making speeches.

## Open decisions (for the user)

- **Does tonight's Neverember appearance replace the separately-arranged private dinner, or precede it?** Both are valid; pick before the scene so Aldus's dialogue can commit to one framing.
- **Does Cassian get a genuinely private aside with Valphine tonight, or does the private tea stay reserved for later?** A brief aside is staged as an option above; you can also hold the line and make the party wait.
- **Does the fixer get a name tonight?** Formalizing him as Havel Drest (the recovery-office clerk from the necklace prep material) would give Margaster's network a face earlier than planned — otherwise keep him anonymous a while longer.
- **Does Cullen actually name the gnome tonight, stall, or feed a decoy?** This is the single highest-leverage choice in the whole session for pacing the Bimble Nackle reveal — decide deliberately, not by default.
- **Perrin's three witnesses** — roll the table above, or hand-pick which complication you want to land.
