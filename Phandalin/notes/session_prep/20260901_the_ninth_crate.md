**Party:** level 7 — Vukradin (Bard, Dave), Soma (Druid, Wade), Valphine (Cleric, Gary), Brewbarry (Barbarian, Stéphane — Gary covers if absent). **Boney** is GM-run.
**Register:** Underground procedural. Heist staging, a monster-movie middle, and an anticlimax for a climax — they go down hunting a conspiracy and find a frightened contractor who has been holding a door alone for seven tendays.

> **Scope.** Opens at the **Common Chord**, killing time before the third hour. Closes at the **lower docks at dawn**, on Sridar's offer for the manual. Intended order is Scenes 1→8 and they chain tightly; the only sanctioned skip is the homunculus shortcut in Scene 2.

## The three forces in this tunnel (read this before anything else)  `[OVERLAY]`

Everything below is arrangement. This is the actual design.

**1. The party.** Following a crate. Wants a witness.

**2. The Rift Weavers.** Not monsters that wandered in, and **not the machine's servants — they are reality's anti-virus.** They arrive where reality has been violated and they delete the violation. Nobody summons them and nobody owns them.

Ch27 reads correctly under this and better: the weavers were at Axeholm **because the Calliope was the anomaly**, and the machine's counter-move was to flag *Brewbarry* as a spatial anomaly so the antibodies would go for a decoy instead. Aletra's wand pointed them at him. And when the device was finally destroyed, the last weaver — the violation now gone — **simply winked out of existence.** It had nothing left to delete.

> **The horror, stated plainly:** the Neverwinter Manifold was **stopped, not destroyed.** Switching a wound off does not close it. The site is still a tear, the Commission spent two years **running the machine hot** and tearing it wider, and **nobody has run Bimble's tuning routine for seven tendays.** So the antibodies keep arriving, in greater numbers, at a wound nobody is treating.
>
> **And they do not care whose side anyone is on.** The party is about to walk into that tunnel carrying several things that are, by any honest reckoning, violations of reality.

**3. Sridar's drow.** Aletra's field cell. They want the manual destroyed and Bimble silenced, and they have been stuck at the same bend as everyone else for seven tendays, because an elite team of four cannot fight through a tunnel full of weavers.

> **This is Aletra's ch27 play, run a second time.** At Axeholm she stood in a delicate equilibrium with the machine and its guardians, marked Brewbarry as the anomaly, let the party do the entire fight, and walked off with the box. **The cell is doing it again: let the loud famous heroes clear the tunnel, then collect.** Players who remember Axeholm should be able to feel it coming, and that is the pleasure.

## What actually happened (GM-only, and it stays GM-only all session)  `[OVERLAY]` — the whole answer is invention

> **The Commission was running the Manifold hot.** Throughput above what the machine could carry without tearing, for about two years, which is why Bimble's tuning routine kept failing sooner and why the weaver pressure has been building since long before the shutdown.
>
> **Aletra could enable that.** The Codex told her how — carry the extra load without the tear widening. She named her price: **more money, and the right to move her own goods through the machine.** A drow poison-smuggler asking a shipping combine for freight rights, which is the least surprising sentence in this campaign.
>
> **The Commission said no. So she turned it off.** Not sabotage in the espionage sense, not a scheme, not a step in a cosmic plan — **a supplier who was told no and broke the thing on her way out.** It was petty and it was personal and it was entirely in character.
>
> **This is the second anticlimax of the session and it is deliberate.** The party has spent four chapters believing the Manifold failure is a conspiracy at the heart of the city. It is a contract dispute. **It rhymes exactly with Bimble** — they are hunting a villain and will find a contractor, and they are hunting a conspiracy and will eventually find an invoice.
>
> **It also explains the opacity completely.** "Nobody official will say why" is not a cover-up of a murder. **The Commission cannot say that their off-books freight partner switched their engine off because they would not cut her in.** That admission ends careers and invites the Zhentarim. Neverember is sitting on a commercial humiliation, not a crime — which is far more dangerous to him and much harder for the party to prise out.
>
> **Not this session.** Nobody in the tunnel knows it. Bimble does not know it. Sridar will not say it. **The dinner is where this becomes gettable**, and the party should arrive there with the manual and no idea what they are actually asking about.

> **The Codex's "other plans" — a proposal, not a ruling.** You have this TBD. One option that costs nothing and fits: the modification Aletra installed did what she wanted *and* quietly did something else — **every crate routed through it was also being measured.** She got her capacity. KP got a survey of how the planes route. **Neither the Commission nor Aletra knows they were the instrument.** That is exactly KP's signature: everybody gets what they negotiated for and he gets the map. Bimble's testimony below is written to support this reading, and works without it if you'd rather keep the Codex's intent open.

## Scene 2 — The Third Hour  `[TABLE]` third hour, nine crates, eight used (ch50) · `[OVERLAY]` the grille, the carriers, the homunculus

Aqueduct steps at the edge of the service lane. Wet stone, cold for summer, the city's noise a street away and then not.

The grille is mortared, re-mortared, then **cut and re-hung on its own hinges by someone competent within the last few years.** The hinges are the tell.

**The delivery:** two men who are not the same two men. Rope-bound crates, wet on the underside, **no Commission Harbor mark on any of them.** Eight go up. **The ninth stays on the ledge below.**

**The beat that matters:** one carrier stops halfway up and looks back down into the dark. Not at the party. **The way a man checks on somebody he left behind.**

**If they grab a carrier:** a day labourer, paid at the top of the lane by a man he met once. He knows the hour and the count and nothing else.

**If they wait to see what collects the ninth crate:** something comes up for it, and it is not a person. Small, quick, brass-and-leather, cat-sized, moving with the unhurried competence of a thing following instructions. It takes the crate and goes back down.

**Do not explain it.** It should read as one more wrong thing in a tunnel full of them. It is Bimble's homunculus, they find that out in three hours, and the retrospective click is worth more than the reveal.

> **If somebody follows it instead of taking the tunnel straight** — reward that enormously. It knows the safe path, it does not trigger the bend, and it walks them past the junction. **They skip the fight.** That is a legitimate clever outcome; if it happens, move the weavers onto the escape rather than cutting them.

> **Plant the tail here and do not call attention to it.** One line, buried in the description of the lane: somewhere behind them a door that was open is closed. That is all. Do not offer a roll. **If anyone actively looks, give it to them** — a passive Perception of 16+ or any deliberate check catches movement at the top of the lane that stops when they stop.

## Scene 3 — The Bend (why nobody has ever robbed this route)  `[OVERLAY]` — entirely mine, including the save DC

Past the first bend the tunnel does something to the inside of the skull. Not fear. **Disinterest** — a calm, entirely reasonable certainty that this is not worth doing and there is somewhere better to be.

**DC 15 Wisdom save** to push past. On a failure the character turns around and **cannot afterwards say why**, and will confabulate fluently and sincerely. Repeat once per attempt; let them keep trying.

**Advantage** to anyone who says out loud what they are actually down here for. Vukradin will do this unprompted, because telling everyone everything immediately is his entire operating system.

> **GM-only — what it actually is.** Not a ward. **Low-grade spatial bleed**, and it has been here for six years at a level nobody could name, because the Manifold was running and its leak was small. **That is why this smuggling route was safe for six years** — a tunnel nobody can make themselves walk down is the cheapest security in Neverwinter, and the smugglers found it rather than built it.
>
> It is much worse now than it was in the spring. **Soma should be able to read this with Meril's Staff and get it right**, and it should be the second time in five chapters she is the only one who can see the shape of something.

## Scene 5 — The Junction (the fight)  `[TABLE]` weaver traits and the count of six (ch27) · `[OVERLAY]` the chamber, the stat chassis, Anomaly Fixation

**Where:** a wide brick chamber, barrel-vaulted, the old overflow meeting the maintenance tunnel. A ledge along the north wall. Standing water across the floor. **A previous night's ninth crate sits on the ledge, unopened** — which is how they know they are on the line, and why they stand and fight instead of running past.

**Above, in the vault: webs that are not wet, and six Rift Weavers folded against the ceiling.**

**Six**, matching Axeholm exactly, because the callback should be precise.

### Statting them

Homebrew from the Axeholm arc — **use your original block if you still have it.** If not, a **phase spider chassis (CR 3)** carries everything canon requires:

- AC 13, HP ~32, bite +4 for 1d10 piercing plus 4d8 poison (DC 11 CON, half).
- **Ethereal/planar step** — they *"used their cosmic abilities to free themselves from the slowing effects"* at Axeholm. Advantage on saves against restraint and slowing, and they can step out of a grapple.
- **Webs** — restrain, DC 13 to escape, **destroyed outright by any fire damage.** Established at the table.
- **Vulnerable to fire and radiant.** Both discovered by the players themselves in ch27. Honour it visibly and let them feel clever.
- On death, **dissolve into crackling sparkles.**

Six at CR 3 is roughly 8,400 adjusted XP against four level-7s — nominally deadly. **The vulnerabilities cut that roughly in half in practice**, and this party has Moonbeam, Fount of Moonlight, Sacred Flame, Daylight and Spirit Guardians. It should read as terrifying on turn one and turn out to be winnable by turn three. **That is the correct feeling.**

### Anomaly Fixation — the mechanic that makes this the right fight

The weavers do not distribute attacks. **They swarm whatever reads as the largest spatial anomaly in the room**, exactly as the Axeholm machine directed them onto Brewbarry.

**The table will assume it is Brewbarry.** Aletra marked him with a wand and every player at that table remembers it. Let them assume it. Let Brewbarry step forward and rage.

**It is not Brewbarry.** See Open Decisions — my recommendation is **the obsidian sword on Vukradin's belt.**

> **Why the sword.** It was the Carver: an alien intelligence that wore a human being until the original inhabitant was simply gone. Vukradin has carried it since ch35 with its ownership unresolved and the thread has been dormant for fourteen chapters. **Six of reality's own antibodies identifying it, unprompted, as the most wrong thing in a room that also contains a torn planar site is the loudest possible way to wake that thread up** — and it reframes every conversation the party has had about who the sword belongs to. It also puts the squishiest party member in the middle of the swarm, which is a better fight than the barbarian soaking it.

**The out is obvious and should work:** put the sword down. Throw it. The weavers go to the sword. **Vukradin then has to decide whether to leave it there**, which is a genuinely hard question for him and costs nothing to stage.

### The terrain

- Standing water is difficult terrain. **The ledge is the only clean footing** and the crate is on it.
- **The water rises a foot a round for six rounds** — the turn of the tide, exactly as advertised at the Common Chord.
- Boney does not breathe. A skeletal horse walking the bottom of a flooding chamber is funny and genuinely useful and Soma will think of it.

### Failure state — material, not lethal

Nobody dies here. If it goes badly they are **pushed back and lose the tide window**, surface at the lower docks at dawn in full view of a working harbour, and per the 0825 doc **whoever feeds Bimble sees them coming from two hundred yards.** He moves. Next session opens on a cold room and a workbench.

**And the drow, who are behind them, do not lose their window.** That is the real cost and you should not say it out loud.

## Scene 6 — The Lower Docks  `[OVERLAY]` — the storeroom, the approach evidence, first contact

### Bimble Nackle — Artificer 7  `[TABLE]` name and employment facts (ch50) · `[OVERLAY]` the build

**He is not a hostage and he is not a bystander. He is a working professional who has been holding a defensive line alone for seven tendays, and the reason he is alive is that he is good at his job.**

**Artillerist, level 7.** The build does all the characterisation for you:

- **Eldritch Cannon (Flamethrower).** A tripod turret he can put down as a bonus action. **The weavers are vulnerable to fire and their webs burn.** He worked that out in his first tenday down here the way anyone works anything out — by nearly dying, then taking notes. **He has been burning reality's antibodies off his own door nightly for seven tendays.**
- **Flash of Genius.** Reaction, adds his INT to an ally's check or save. **He makes the party better rather than doing damage in front of them** — exactly the right shape for an NPC you do not want stealing a fight.
- **Tool Expertise, The Right Tool for the Job, Magical Tinkering.** He can build a thing he needs in an hour. His door has an alarm on it. So does the corridor.
- **Infusions.** Enhanced Defence on a workman's coat, a Bag of Holding, and — recommended — a **Homunculus Servant.** See below, it solves a logistics question for free.
- **Spells:** 2nd level max at this tier. *Mending, Fire Bolt, Faerie Fire, Sanctuary, Web, Heat Metal, Alarm, Enhance Ability.* He casts them like a man reading a procedure, because that is what they are to him.

#### He thinks everyone wants him dead, and he is nearly right

The Commission wants a name to blame. The drow want the manual. He does not know how many groups there are and has stopped trying to count. **Seven tendays alone with a cannon and a door has not improved his estimate.**

> **This is a first-contact danger and you should play it straight.** The party opens that door and there is a **flamethrower on a tripod aimed at it**, and a very frightened, very competent little man behind it. Give them one round where this could go badly. **He will not shoot first** — but he will be shouting, the cannon will be humming, and Brewbarry filling the doorway at seven feet is the worst possible opening image.
>
> Somebody has to talk him down. **Valphine's unsettled lie-detection is genuinely useful here and so is Vukradin simply telling him everything immediately**, which remains the only social technology that works on frightened people.

#### What the party sees before they see him

Stage the evidence in the approach and let them draw the wrong conclusion first:

- **Scorch marks.** Radial, on the brick, at head height, in overlapping arcs — someone has been firing the same weapon from the same spot, many times, for weeks.
- **Dissolved weaver residue.** Crackling sparkle-ash, swept into a tidy pile against the wall. **Swept.** Somebody has been cleaning up after fights.
- **An alarm rune on the corridor**, competently done, not concealed.

**The party should conclude they are walking into something dangerous, and they will be right, and it will turn out to be a terrified maintenance contractor. That is the anticlimax landing correctly.**

#### Why he matters after this session

**He is not a witness you bank and forget. He is the only living expert on this specific tear.**

Nine years of keeping its signature under a threshold means he knows its behaviour better than anyone alive: what makes it worse, what the weaver count tracks, what the tuning routine was actually holding back. **Nobody in Neverwinter can close that tear without him**, and he cannot close it without people who can survive standing next to it.

That makes the tear expedition a genuine partnership rather than an escort mission, and it gives a frightened man a way to stop being a liability — **which is the only thing he wants that he has not said out loud.**

#### The Homunculus (recommended)

A small brass-and-leather thing, cat-sized, that goes up the tunnel and brings the crate down. **This is what comes for the ninth crate in Scene 2** — and it is the right answer to "who collects it," because it is unsettling in the moment and delightful in retrospect.

It does **not** answer who *pays* for the crate. Bimble does not know that either. Keep the two questions separate.

## Scene 7 — What Bimble Tells Them  `[OVERLAY]` — every line of the testimony is mine

Fastidious and frightened. He corrects their vocabulary and **will not say "sabotage."** Let them ask for most of it.

**1. The tuning routine — lead with this, it is the best thing he has.**
Nine years on that machine. Every tenday, a tuning routine, to keep **"the weavers"** below a number. The documentation never said what they were. It gave him a threshold and told him to stay under it.

> *"I ran it for nine years and I never saw one. Not once."* *"I thought it was like the rat clauses. Something they put in the papers a hundred years ago and nobody took out."*

**And it had been getting harder.** For about two years the routine stopped holding for a full tenday. He had to run it more often, then twice as often, and he filed to say so and was told the throughput targets were not negotiable. **He was being ordered to keep the number down by an employer who kept pushing the number up**, and he assumed that was just what a job was.

> **This is the campaign's whole thesis in one maintenance man**, and it is the most sympathetic he will ever be. He is not a conspirator. He is a competent person who was told the physical constraint was a scheduling problem.

**Then let him work out what they have just walked through.** He should go quiet. That is the moment he understands his own job for the first time — and it is also the moment the party understands that the thing in the tunnel is *his machine's*, and that it has been unattended for seven tendays.

**2. It did not break.**
> *"It did not fail. It was finished."*
Firm about this, and slightly offended by the maintenance story.

**3. Somebody rewrote the routing.**
Not damaged — *improved.* A hand he had never seen, in no guild register, **better than his.** This is exactly what Elara caught him doing at the docks and wrote down as unsettling: she saw a man mapping the machine. He was mapping somebody else's handwriting.

**4. What the rewrite did — and this is where he is genuinely impressed against his will.**
It let the machine **carry more than it should have been able to carry without tearing.** He had been fighting that exact problem for two years and filing about it, and somebody solved it in a fortnight without being asked.

> *"I could not have done that."* *"I want to be clear. Not that I would not have. That I could not have."*

**And — if you are taking the Codex proposal above — it was doing something else at the same time.** It moved goods *and took a reading while it did it.* Every crate, every routing, for months. Bimble noticed the extra step, could not work out what it was for, and assumed it was diagnostics. **He was mapping somebody else's handwriting**, which is exactly what Elara caught him at and wrote down as unsettling.

Seven weeks ago it stopped.

**5. The manual — the object, and the reason he is being hunted.**
Left behind in the housing. Not guild issue, not any press he knows, **the same notation as the rewrite.** It contains the restart sequence.

He ran the restart sequence. **It did not work.**

> *"A machine does what its manual says. That is what a manual is."*

**This frightens him more than the drow do**, and it should be delivered as the confession of a man admitting his religion has failed. He kept the manual because it was the only thing that could prove he was not lying.

> **GM-only.** Per your design this is KP's manual, routed through Aletra, and it was supposed to have been destroyed. **Bimble has no idea what he is holding.** He thinks it is a technical document that is wrong. Do not let him suspect otherwise.
>
> **The optional Axeholm callback:** at ch27 the collapsing device left behind a bill from *"Interplanar Trade Consultants Incorporated."* If the party kept it — check, it may have gone to the Chief Accountant when her accounts were settled — **it is the same press as the manual.** If they still have it, this is a free and devastating connection. If they don't, Valphine and Boney both saw it and can say so.

**6. He cannot give them a name. Hold this absolutely.**
> *"I never met him. I only ever met his handwriting."*

**7. The drow.**
About five tendays ago, two came to the docks asking after him by name. Polite, well dressed, **and one of them was a woman who asked what he had taken out of the housing** — which is how he knows they know about the manual. That is when he stopped using doors.

**8. Why the tunnel, why the crates.**
He went under the day it stopped. Food started arriving about a tenday later and he does not know who sends it.
> *"It comes. I don't ask. I stopped assuming it was them about five tendays ago."*

**8b. What he is actually most afraid of, and it is not assassins.**
Push him on why he ran and the honest answer comes out sideways: **he thinks he is going to be blamed.** Nine years of maintenance logs, a machine that stopped on his watch, and a Commission that has already told the whole city its technician "found nothing."

> *"Someone has to have done it."* *"I am the person whose name is on the tenday sheets."*

**This is the lever the party actually holds over him and the one thing they can genuinely offer.** Not protection — *exoneration.* They are the only people alive who could make somebody powerful say out loud that Bimble Nackle did not break the Manifold. Vukradin will hear that as a moral obligation and he will be right.

**9. The access point that feeds the yarn board.**
The rewrite needed **sustained, unsupervised access to the housing over weeks.** Only **Commission Harbor authority** holds those keys. So whoever wrote it was Commission, or was let in by somebody who was — **and Margaster still is not on the Commission.** Delivered the week before dinner with the Commission's majority holder.

### The ruling-dependent line

**If Bimble was the technician the Commission publicly hired and who "found nothing":**
> *"I did give them a report."* *"They read it. And then they told the city I found nothing."*

**If not:**
> *"They never asked me. I maintained that machine for nine years, and when it stopped they hired somebody else and never told me his name either."*

## Scene 8 — Sridar (the close)  `[TABLE]` Sridar and the unfinished ch17 sale · `[OVERLAY]` the cell, the offer, the trap

**She arrives after the fight and after the testimony, because that was always the plan.**

**Sridar** — drow, female, artefact collector. The party had dinner with her at Falcon's Lodge in ch17. **She paid them thirty gold for an eyewitness account of a phased creature harvesting from the treant, and agreed to buy the Talisman of Moesko for a hundred gold and three bottles of wine for Brewbarry.** Per `campaign_state.md` **that sale was never confirmed complete** — so either she owes them, or they owe her, and neither side is sure which. Use it.

> **What she was actually doing in ch17 — say this to yourself, not to them.** Aletra's Phase 1 hook in her own dossier is *"a mysterious drow subtly inquiring about unusual local phenomena… offering to purchase any peculiarities the party has found."* **Sridar ran that hook on the party a year ago and it worked perfectly.** The party sold Aletra's cell their own eyewitness intelligence and a planar artefact, for a hundred and thirty gold and three bottles of wine, and thought they had made a nice profit on a curio.

**Play her exactly as she played then: warm, curious, straightforward, and buying.**

She does not threaten. She does not draw. **She makes an offer for the manual**, at a good price, cheerfully, the way she made an offer for the talisman — and she is genuinely pleased to see Valphine.

> *"You have no idea what that is."* Said kindly, as a matter of fact rather than an insult. *"I do. That is usually what a price is for."*

**This is the trap and it is beautiful, because the party's own habits close it.** They have sold her before. Vukradin's ethic — return things to their rightful owner — **gives him no help at all here**, since he has no idea who the manual belongs to and she is the only person in the room who claims to. And they are wet, tired, out of spells, standing between a flooding tunnel and an open harbour with a gnome they need to get out of the city.

**Three ways to run the close, pick at the table:**

- **She buys it and leaves.** Worst outcome, entirely earned, and the campaign is better for it. KP's link burns and the party finds out in three chapters what they sold.
- **They refuse and she leaves anyway.** She is a collector, not muscle, and there are four of them and one of her *that they can see.* She is pleasant about it. **The cell simply follows them home.**
- **It goes loud.** Fine, but she does not fight fair and does not fight long — poison, darkness, a portal device if you want the Aletra rhyme. **Do not kill her here.** She is worth far more as the face the party keeps doing business with.

> **Stage direction — the last beat.** Whatever they decide, have her say goodbye normally and use Brewbarry's name. Then one line of narration and stop talking. **Let the table fill it**, and let somebody be the one who says out loud that they have met her before.

## The Grundar tease — recommendation: hold it, and here is why that is the stronger play  `[TABLE]` Grundar, Brundar, the Echo tracker · `[OVERLAY]` the tear as his site

**First, the species.** Not duergar — **svirfneblin.** Grundar Quartzvein leads a **deep gnome** rebellion, and the arc is his brother **Brundar**, whom **House Sotorra** tortured and consigned to the **Demonweb Pits**. Tracker: **Brundar's Echo**, currently 0. The party knows none of this and he is not on their radar at all.

**Your instinct is right — it is too much for this session.** There are already three forces in the tunnel, a level-7 artificer to meet, a testimony to deliver, and Sridar's offer to close on. A fourth faction turns a full session into a crowded one.

**But the convergence is real, which is why it should be a consequence rather than a cameo.**

Grundar's stated plan is to **exploit KP-thinned planar nexus points** to reach the Demonweb Pits. This session establishes that there is an **open, untreated, actively widening planar tear** at the Neverwinter docks that nobody is maintaining and that the Commission is publicly pretending is a maintenance issue.

**That is the door he has been looking for.** And the person who tore it is **Aletra Sotorra** — House Sotorra, the house that put his brother in the Pits. **His door and his enemy are the same event.**

So the tease writes itself later and much harder: the party is about to go extremely loudly public about a hole in reality, in a city, while a patient deep gnome is searching for exactly that. **They do not have to meet him. They have to advertise**, and this party advertises everything.

> **The collision you should decide about deliberately:** Bimble is a gnome. Grundar and Brundar are deep gnomes. If svirfneblin turn up anywhere near Bimble the table will connect them, because that is what tables do. **Either that is a red herring you want, or it is a reason to keep the two threads in different districts for a while.** Worth picking on purpose rather than finding out at the table.

**If you want a tease anyway, the cheapest possible version** — one line, no faction, no NPC, no cameo: **the party is not the first party to survey this tear.** Old chalk on the brick at the junction, a measuring mark, careful work, months old, in a hand that is neither Bimble's nor the smugglers'. Nobody in the session can identify it. Per `Brundar.md`'s own trigger table that is a **+1 to Brundar's Echo** (*"a minor ritual site or peculiar anomaly that hints at a focused attempt to breach a specific planar barrier"*) and it costs you nothing and explains nothing.

**My recommendation is to skip even that** — because per the arc note below, Grundar is no longer a silhouette you tease. **He is the bill for the thing the party is about to do well.**

### What Grundar was doing, now that the tear is the objective

**He was working the tear.** Not lurking near it — *using* it. It is a KP-thinned nexus, unmaintained, widening, and nobody official will admit it exists, which makes it the single best candidate in the North for reaching the **Demonweb Pits** and pulling **Brundar** out. Months of patient, careful, unsupervised work on the one door that ever opened for him.

**And the party is going to close it, and they will be right to.**

> **This is the strongest antagonist motivation the campaign has produced, because the party earns it by doing the correct thing.** Nobody is wrong. The tear is killing people and has to be closed. Grundar's brother is in the Demonweb Pits because **House Sotorra** put him there, and the party has a Sotorra standing in the front rank. **The hole was torn by Aletra Sotorra and closed by Valphine Sotorra**, and from where Grundar is standing that is one house doing the same thing to him twice.
>
> **Do not let the party meet him at the tear.** They should find his work — careful chalk, measuring marks, a site somebody has been tending for months — **fail to understand it, and close the tear anyway.** The comprehension arrives later, with him attached to it. Vukradin should find out that he did the right thing and destroyed a man's only chance at his brother, and there should be no version of the story where he could have known.

## The arc this session is now feeding (agreed at prep, 2026-08-26)  `[OVERLAY]` — proposal, not canon

**The objective is not to repair the Manifold. It is to close the tear.**

That is the better call and it fixes a structural problem I had not flagged hard enough: *"restart an interplanar shipping engine"* is a problem the party cannot personally solve, has no verbs for, and would have to hand to an NPC. **"Go and close a hole in reality" is an adventure.** It has a location, a danger, a cost and an expert, and the party can do it with their own hands.

**The sequence:**

1. **This session.** Find Bimble. Learn the tear exists, is widening, and is what the weavers are answering.
2. **Next.** The expedition. Bimble guides; the party survives the thing he cannot. **The tear closes.**
3. **Then Castle Never.** They arrive at the dinner having actually fixed something, rather than as four famous people with opinions about money.

### Why this makes Neverember's offer land

His plan — **legitimise them over Phandalin, and name them Protectors of Leilon** — stops being an arbitrary elevation and becomes a *hire*, because they will have just proved the exact competence the job needs.

And it gives him motives that are his own rather than the plot's:

- **He is mortgaged and losing the count** (Ruling 7). Placing his own people over two territories is how a diluted man rebuilds a base.
- **The Lord's Alliance jurisdiction map already draws a dotted line over Neverwinter Wood, Leilon and Phandalin.** Appointing the party pre-empts the annexation with facts on the ground — using the most famous people in the North, one of whom the city already treats as an avatar.
- **He watched Valphine take a temple in front of him and told her they could work wonders together.** This is the offer that sentence was pointing at.
- **UBT stops being an insult to his authority the moment it is his policy**, which is the cheapest way for him to end that fight and the last thing Vukradin will expect.

**And Leilon is the tier.** Storm Lord's Wrath is levels 7–9, the party is 7, Rimardo promised they would be *"a better audience in Leilon,"* and Miraal warned of a storm in the depths below it back in ch09. **The appointment is the door into the next act, and it is now earned rather than announced.**

### The cost you should decide on purpose

**The party already has the precedent for how to close one of these, and it is not gentle.** At Axeholm the tear ended when the *device* was destroyed — the last weaver, with nothing left to correct, winked out. They watched it happen. **They will reach for the same answer here, because it is the answer they have.**

Which means the likely shape of the fix is: **destroy the Manifold, permanently, to close the tear.**

> **That is a genuinely agonising choice and it is entirely this campaign.** Interplanar shipping for the whole North dies with it. The Lionshield Coster's cheap distribution never comes back. **Regional trade reorganises permanently around House Margaster's overland route down the High Road** — the exact monopoly the party has spent three chapters trying to prove is criminal.
>
> **Vukradin has to choose between the city's physical safety and handing the region to the people who killed Elara Meliamne.** There is no third option and no clever bard answer, and he should not get one.

### The scheduling problem, which is real

**The Neverember dinner has now slid twice** — deferred on screen in ch47 and again in ch48, with Aldus promising both times that it is going to happen. **A third slide needs a reason on screen or it starts to read as the GM avoiding it.**

Cleanest option: **Neverember postpones it himself**, and the postponement is a tell — he is busy because the Commission's problem is his problem, and he does not yet know the party is about to solve it. Second option: the party asks for the delay because they have found something, which costs them nothing and makes Aldus's life difficult in a way that is funny.
