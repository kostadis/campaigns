# The Price That Did Not Change

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging for the session after ch48 ("Lathander's Death" at the Spire, 2026-08-18).

**Shape agreed at prep:** Board Laid Bare stays in the **Tower District**; the bridge into Bluelake is the **supply chain**. Bimble is **not found** this session — the door gets marked. Tone: **underworld noir with the campaign's comedy intact.** The Neverember dinner **does not land here** — it is the seed scene for the Commission arc and the Claudien arc, and it stays queued.

**Party:** Vukradin (Bard 7), Soma (Druid 7), Valphine (Cleric 7, White Dragon Plate +1, AC 22), Brewbarry (Barbarian 7, elven chain). Boney in the streets. Level 7 across the board — nothing in Bluelake is an existential threat, and that is the point. This district does not kill you. It files you.

The party is in Neverwinter, one day past the dawn service. They have a name — **Bimble Nackle** — and nothing else: no location, no employer, and Cullen Sharpe genuinely does not know either (the DM confirmed he wasn't lying). ## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

**The party leaves this session knowing exactly where to go next, and knowing it cost them something to find out.**

Concretely: they establish that Bimble Nackle is alive and hiding in the **lower docks**, and they establish the **route** — a smuggling line that runs from the Tower District down the old Alagondar aqueduct, through the Bluelake sewers, and surfaces at the water. They do not walk it tonight. They stand at the mouth of it.

The cost is the point. Every path to that route runs through someone the party has to lean on, and the leaning is visible. Soma has to ask Lim a second question. The Harpers have to be argued out of a conclusion the party earned yesterday by handing a restorationist a necklace of fireballs in public. Somebody has to go into Bluelake wearing a face the district has opinions about.

**Secondary win, quieter:** Bluelake stops being a name on a map and becomes a place with people in it who now know the party's names. Good and bad.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing)

- **Bimble** stays where he is. He has been hiding seven tendays; he can hide longer. Every tenday he does, his notes matter more and his nerve matters less. He is not being hunted efficiently — he is being *waited out*.
- **House Margaster** consolidates the distribution gap by ordinary commerce. No scene required. Each tenday of Manifold silence is another contract signed that will not un-sign.
- **The Harpers** keep looking for Bimble without the party, find nothing, and keep their Margaster file closed. They are not fast. They are careful, and careful is losing.
- **Horst at the Flickering Ember** has a live grudge and no target yet. If Valphine never enters Bluelake, that timer stays cold. If she does — famous, gold-eyed, in white dragon plate — it does not stay cold, and it does not need the party to do anything wrong.
- **Hazel** keeps dredging a lake that is being refilled from below, alone, with meticulous notes nobody has read.
- **Lim** keeps cooking. Her prices stay wrong. Nobody asks why, the way nobody has asked for six years.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

Not a script. This is what an optimal party does, so you can recognise a partial version and reward it.

0. **Arrive already asking "who feeds a man in hiding?"** — see Scene 0. Without that question loaded, lunch is only lunch and none of the rest fires.
1. **Ask Bellows, not Lim.** The warforged is literal and answers precisely what he is asked. Lim deflects because she is uninterested; Bellows deflects only because nobody has asked an answerable question. "Who owns this place?" gets nothing. **"When do deliveries arrive, and through which door?"** gets everything. A *better question*, not a better roll — reward it without dice.
2. **Get the crate count. This is the session's key.** Nine crates delivered, eight used, **and it went from eight to nine at midsummer — the week the Manifold died.** The ninth never comes inside and is not billed. Somebody on this line started eating seven tendays ago. **Follow the crate.**
3. **Check the crates for the Harbor mark.** Everything moving legally through Neverwinter carries a Commission Harbor authority stamp. Lim's carry none. That is not a clue about Lim — it is a clue about the *route*, and it establishes there is only one like it left.
4. **Take the Zeleen credential into the sewers.** The party has a standing alliance with a wererat gang. The Dead Rats under Bluelake are wererats. That is a door, and it opens with a name rather than a fight.
5. **Fix the Harper problem before asking the Harpers for anything.** They will have already heard about the necklace. Lead with it.
6. **Do not send Valphine shopping.** Somebody will want to buy the enchantment for Brewbarry's coin rig. The nearest general-goods shop is Horst's.

# Scene 0 — Loading the Question (run this first, it is not optional)

**The problem this scene solves:** on its own, lunch is lunch. *"The food here is cheap"* does not say *"therefore I can find a missing gnome,"* and a party with no reason to interrogate a restaurant will not interrogate one. **Something has to make the party want a route before they sit down.**

**The question that has to be live in their heads when they walk in:**

> **A man with no income has been hiding in this city for seven tendays and has not left. Somebody is feeding him.**

That single sentence converts the entire meal. It does not tell them where Bimble is. It tells them **what kind of thing to look for** — and then Soma walks them into the one building in Neverwinter where food arrives from nowhere at half price.

## Three vectors — use at least one, ideally the first

**1. Aurelan Vance (best — the party is already going back).** Brewbarry cemented a reciprocal-favour relationship with him last session and said, out loud, *"I'll find out who this gnome is, I'll find out who hired him, and I'll get to the bottom of this."* He will return. Vance cannot investigate, but he can read a **pay record**, and that is exactly his competence and nobody else's:

> *"The Commission carried him on the maintenance roll for eleven years. The payments stop seven tendays ago — on the day, not the tenday. No severance. No final reconciliation. No forwarding instruction."*
> *"That is not how a contract ends, Mr. Brewbarry. That is how a contract is abandoned."*
> And then, because he is a banker and this is the only kind of question he is any good at: *"He has drawn nothing since. He has not banked, he has not borrowed, and he has not left — your Harper friends would tell you the same if you had any. So somebody is keeping him. Find who feeds a man and you have found the man."*

**2. Ser Kaelen or Linene, by sending stone.** If the party reports home — and they owe Linene a report they have not delivered — the same deduction arrives from a shipping agent's angle: *nobody in the North has moved goods cheaply since the Manifold died. If someone is provisioning a man off the books, they are doing it on a route that survived.*

**3. The street, free.** Any beggar, dock hand, or Bluelake local, asked where a person hides in Neverwinter: *"Hiding's easy. Eating's hard."*

> **If none of these land, the session still works** — Hazel names the aqueduct tunnel independently in 3C and the Harpers say the same sentence in 3A. **But it is much better before the meal than after**, because after the meal it is a recap and before the meal it is a hunt.

## And the party already contains the trigger

**Brewbarry just took out a business loan on a venture that depends entirely on shipping, with Linene as his agent, during a regional distribution collapse.** He is sitting in a restaurant charging half what the food is worth. Asking how a place like that gets supplied is the single most in-character question available to him and he has spent two chapters on exactly this subject.

**You should not have to force any of this.** Let the prices be absurd, let somebody remark on it, and let the businessman at the table do what businessmen do.

# Scene 1 — The Board Laid Bare

## Setup

**When:** Late morning into afternoon, the day after the dawn service. The city is still talking about them — Valphine's sermon, the blinded Loviatarite, Neverember's private word. This is the first hour since they arrived in Neverwinter that nobody is watching them on purpose.

**Where:** Tower District, in the shadow of the Cloak Tower. Small building, no signage from the street. Soma leads them there without explaining and will not explain until they are inside.

**Present:** Lim (older tortle chef, shell worn smooth, unhurried). Bellows (warforged waiter, tall, very still).

**Offstage but relevant:** whoever delivers at the hour nothing legitimate moves.

## Approach — Bellows at the door

Run Bellows exactly as written in `20260616_neverwinter_arrival.md`. He is not evasive. He is *precise*, and precision without curiosity is its own kind of locked door.

> *"Three tables available. The table near the window offers the best view of the tower. The corner table is preferred by guests who do not wish to be observed. I recommend the corner table."*

He recommends the corner table to everyone. He is not making an assessment of them. If they ask why he recommends it: *"It is the table most often chosen."*

Menu, if asked: *"What we have today."* A handwritten card. Fresh fish, fried vegetables, potatoes, two sauces. No alcohol — Brewbarry will have feelings.

**The prices are wrong. Too low by half.** If anyone names it:

> *"It is priced to achieve full occupancy."*

Accurate. Completely unhelpful. Let it land as a joke first. The joke is the hook.

## The soup

Somebody at the table is going to say **tortle soup**. It is the DM's own closing line from last session and it is canon in that exact form — *not* "turtle" (`notes/vtt_transcription_corrections.md:299`, GM ruling 2026-08-24). Soma is a tortle and the joke is that the soup is made of her, and she has heard it before, and she is going to have to be gracious about it again.

Lim's version of the joke, delivered flat, without looking up, the first time anyone says it:

> *"We don't serve that here. We used to. It was a different clientele."*

Do not clarify. Let Wade decide what Soma does with that.

## Lim's entrance

She comes out when she has a moment, carrying a tray of what she has decided they want rather than asking. She sees Soma. She puts the tray down. She sits, which she does not do with most tables.

> *"You came back. I thought you'd gone somewhere northern."*

Not sentimental. Not warm, exactly. **They know each other in the way that requires no explanation** — that is the whole relationship, and it is the thing the session is about to spend.

**What Lim volunteers, unprompted, if the conversation opens:**

- The Tower District has been quieter since the orcs left. *"Not safer. Quieter is different."*
- Three weeks ago, people dressed as Many-Starred Cloaks associates went into the Cloak Tower by the **basement stairs** and spent the day. They came out with nothing. She watched from across the street. She noticed because whatever it was, **they didn't find it.**
- She does not know what changed.

> **GM-only — Pantograph texture, not this session's business.** Halavar's Universal Pantograph duplicates metal objects up to throne-size, including coins, and has never been found. Keep it as resonance: a party mid-argument about the circulation of wealth learning there is a coin duplicator somewhere under this city is a very good thing to leave unresolved. **Do not make it tonight's thread.** If Vukradin lunges at it, Lim's line closes the door politely: *"If it were easy to find, someone would have found it."*

## The turn — asking Lim a second question

This is the emotional centre of the scene and it is Soma's.

Lim answers questions about the city. She does not answer questions about herself, and she has never had to refuse Soma, because Soma has never wanted anything from her. Soma is the one person in Neverwinter who came here to *eat*.

If the party pushes on the price, the supplier, or the owner, Lim gives the wall:

- Why is the food this cheap? → *"Good suppliers."* End of topic.
- Who owns the restaurant? → *"It runs itself."* End of topic.
- What has Soma been doing? → **she asks this instead of answering.** She is genuinely more interested in the answer than in deflecting, and the deflection is a side effect.

**If pressed harder she becomes less helpful, not more.** She is not hiding anything. She is *uninterested*, and being interrogated by a friend is a new and unwelcome experience.

> **Stage direction.** If Soma is the one who pushes, let Lim look at her for a beat before answering. Then answer the same way she'd answer anyone. **That** is the cost — not anger, not a scene. Just Soma being handled like a customer for the first time in six years. Do not underline it. Do not have Lim say anything about it. Let Wade notice or not.

## Branch — if the party asks Bellows instead

**This is the win and it should feel like one.** Bellows has no instruction to conceal anything, no loyalty being tested, and no concept that the information is sensitive. He answers what he is asked, exactly.

- *"When do deliveries arrive?"* → **"Third hour after midnight. Occasionally the fourth."**
- *"Which door?"* → **"The rear. It opens onto the service lane. The lane connects to the aqueduct steps."**
- *"Who delivers?"* → **"Two men. Sometimes three. They are not the same two men."**
- *"What do the crates look like?"* → **"Wooden. Rope-bound. Wet on the underside."** *(He will elaborate on request with unnerving thoroughness.)*
- *"Are they stamped?"* → **"No."**
- **"How many crates?"** → **"Nine. It was eight until midsummer."**

## The crate that is not for the restaurant

**This is the hard link, and it is a fact rather than an inference.** Do not make the party deduce their way to Bimble from "a route exists" — give them something that points.

Bellows volunteers the count to anyone who asks about supply at all, because it is a number and he is precise. If nobody asks, **he raises it himself** as a mild operational grievance, which is the most personality he has:

> *"The order has been nine crates since midsummer. We use eight. I have raised it twice."*

If pressed on what happens to the ninth:

> *"It does not come inside. It is set down and it is not there in the morning."* *"I have not asked. It is not billed to us."*

**Seven weeks ago — the week the Manifold went dark — the standing delivery on this route got one crate heavier, and the extra crate has never entered the building.** Somebody added a mouth to a supply line that predates all of this, and nobody who works here has thought it was worth mentioning because it costs them nothing.

**That is the link.** Not "a smuggling route could hide a gnome." **Somebody on this exact line started eating seven tendays ago and has not stopped.**

> **Say the non-coincidence out loud to yourself so you can play it confidently:** this is not luck. **There is exactly one off-books food route left in Neverwinter.** Everything else either died with the Manifold or now costs House Margaster's prices. A man in hiding with no income and no bank has one option in this city, and that option is also why the cheapest restaurant in the Tower District is the cheapest restaurant in the Tower District. **Two facts point at the same door because there is only one door.**

Let the unstamped crates sit for one beat. Then let the ninth crate land.

> **GM-only.** Everything moving legally through Neverwinter carries a **Commission Harbor authority mark** — the Harbor authority is a Commission sub-body and the stamp is how the city's commerce is legible to it. Lim's crates carry nothing. They are wet underneath because they came up out of water in the dark.
>
> Bellows does not think this is significant. He has never been asked. If someone asks whether he finds it strange: **"I have no basis for comparison. I have only worked here."**

**If nobody asks Bellows anything:** he clears plates and offers a second helping to whoever ate fastest. Give them a second run at him on the way out — he will hold the door and say *"Return whenever you wish. We are rarely full."* That is a soft, correct, entirely unforced re-open of the price question.

## NPC notes

- **Lim** — wants Soma to eat and wants to know where she's been. Wants to keep running her restaurant without complications. Unhurried; answers a question with a question; not remotely impressed by adventurers. **She is not the mystery's author and she genuinely does not care about it.** Pressing makes her less useful, which is the correct penalty for treating a friend like a source.
- **Bellows** — literal, precise, no interiority to hide because there may not be much. Every question gets a technically perfect answer that misses the point unless the point was the question. **He is the single best-designed NPC in Neverwinter for this party right now**, because Brewbarry has just decided he is a spymaster and Bellows is a machine that rewards asking properly.

# Scene 2 — The Bridge (the price that did not change)

Not a location. A five-minute conversation somewhere between the restaurant door and the aqueduct steps, and the most important thing that happens all session.

**The chain — and note that the party only has to walk the short version.**

**The short version, which is the one that matters and is nearly automatic once the ninth crate is on the table:**

> A man in hiding started being fed on this route seven tendays ago. **Follow the crate.**

That is it. That is the whole deduction, and it needs no economics at all.

**The long version, for players who want to earn more** — and Brewbarry has a yarn board out already:

1. Seven weeks ago the Displacement Manifold went dark.
2. Regional distribution collapsed. Lionshield's stores stopped receiving. **Linene wrote to them about this in Phandalin** (ch45) and the party is carrying her unanswered ask.
3. House Margaster stepped into the gap and is now the expensive route.
4. **The Board Laid Bare's prices did not move.** Not up, not down. Six years steady.
5. So Lim's supplier was **never on the Manifold**, and is not Margaster's either — Margaster's line costs money and Lim's food costs nothing.
6. So there is a **third route**: it outlived the Manifold, pays no Harbor authority, and moves at the third hour after midnight down a service lane onto the aqueduct steps.
7. **It is the only one left.** Which is why the ninth crate is on it, and why a man with no income is still in this city.

**Reaching step 7 is a bonus, not a requirement.** It buys them the *structural* read — that the thing feeding their quarry is the same thing feeding Linene's problem — and Soma is the party's designated pattern-reader if Stéphane doesn't get there first. But **the session does not depend on anyone doing economics at the table.**

**If they miss all of it:** Hazel names the aqueduct tunnel independently in 3C, and the Harpers say *"somebody is feeding him"* in 3A. Two more doors to the same place. And Bellows' parting line — *"Return whenever you wish. We are rarely full"* — is a standing invitation to come back and ask the question they didn't know to ask.

> **GM-only — where this actually goes.** The route runs down the old Alagondar aqueduct into the Bluelake sewers (Dead Rats territory), and surfaces at the **lower docks**. Bimble Nackle is in the lower docks. He is alive, ordinary, frightened, and **not KP**. The party should reach the mouth of this route tonight and no further.

# Scene 3 — Bluelake After Dark

## Setup

**When:** Dusk into night. This is the district's own rhythm and it is canon: honest residents work elsewhere by day and lock their doors at night; the district comes alive after dark with weapon-smuggling, stolen-goods transport, faction clashes and wererat raids. Going in daylight gets them an empty stage. Going at night gets them the play.

**The read-aloud for the Tatters is in `notes/neverwinter/bluelake_district.md`** and it is good — the watched-through-windows one. Use it verbatim on entry.

**Four legs below. Run two or three. Do not run all four** — the session needs to end at a marked door, not a checklist.

**The standing complication, active in every leg:** this party is *famous now*. Valphine took over a temple yesterday in front of Lord Neverember. Vukradin is the most recognisable musician in the city. Brewbarry has a fashion movement wearing his face. Bluelake runs on discretion and the party has none left. **Nobody they meet is meeting strangers.**

## 3A — The House of a Thousand Faces (the Harpers)

**The best information in the district, behind the hardest door, and the party slammed it themselves yesterday.**

A comfortable tavern in a former noble boutique — mirrors and mannequins in antiquated fashions, lush couches, a fire that actually draws. It feels wrong for the district, which is the point. The basement is the Neverwinter Harper chapter's meeting place.

**Access:** the Harpers do not advertise. A discreet inquiry to the right staff — specifically asking for *"historical context on Neverwinter's current political situation"* — earns a quiet seat and a longer conversation. Loud, direct, or famous gets nothing. **This is a scene that rewards curiosity, not momentum.**

**What they have:**
- They have been watching the Manifold and **suspect it was not mechanical failure.** They do not know who or what.
- They know **Bimble Nackle by name.** They have not found him. They would like to.
- They hold a **file on House Margaster** they would share, cautiously, with a party that demonstrated it was aligned with neither Neverember nor the Sons of Alagondar.

### The obstacle — and it is earned

The Neverwinter chapter was burned by a senior member caught double-agenting **between Neverember and the Sons of Alagondar.** Nobody is certain what they were actually after. The chapter survived and now vets very slowly.

**Yesterday, in public, at the Spire, the party handed a Necklace of Fireballs to Perrin Alagondar** — a Sons of Alagondar restorationist who wants it as a restoration banner — after his witnesses included a man who told the room that Neverember purged the Alagondar records. Half of Neverwinter watched. **Lord Neverember was in the building.**

From a Harper seat, that reads as one of exactly two things:
- the party is a Nasher asset arming the restoration, or
- the party is Neverember's asset, and the handover was theatre to see who came out of the woodwork.

**Either reading disqualifies them.** The Harper who meets them — call the seat filled by whoever you like, an unhurried woman who has been drinking the same cup for an hour — will not accuse them. She will simply be pleasant, tell them nothing, and be somewhere else in ten minutes.

> **GM-only:** she is not testing them. She has already decided. The party has to *change* a decision, not pass a check, and the difference should be legible.

### What actually opens the door

Not persuasion. **Volunteering the thing that damns them, before being asked.**

If a PC leads with the necklace — what they did, why, and that Vukradin refused to attach a condition to it because attaching conditions to returned property is the one thing he will not do — she puts the cup down. Not because it is a good argument. Because **a Nasher asset would not mention it and Neverember's asset would have a better story.**

Vukradin is the natural mouth for this and it costs him nothing, which is the joke: his single most naive trait — telling everyone everything, immediately, on principle — is the only credential that works on a chapter that has been lied to by professionals.

> *"You gave a Son of Alagondar seven beads of fireball and then told him to get out of your sight."*
> *"...Yes."*
> *"Why the second part?"*
> *"Because I didn't like him."*
> A long pause. *"That's the first thing anyone's told me this tenday that I couldn't have guessed."*

**On success she gives:**
- Bimble's name is already theirs, so instead: **he has not left the city.** The Harpers have watched the gates, the docks manifests, and the coaching lists for seven tendays. Nothing. *"He is still here, and he has been still here for seven tendays, which means somebody is feeding him."*
- **The Margaster file**, cautiously — enough to confirm the forged-writ pattern is a pattern and not an incident, and to give them **Havel Drest**'s name as the compromised clerk at the Margaster-endowed recovery desk. (Live, unused, and flagged as the highest-value unspent target in the necklace prep.)
- She will **not** help them go into the sewers. *"We don't go down there. We ask people who do."*

**On failure:** she is charming, useless, and gone. The party can come back — the Harpers are slow, not closed. But it costs them a session.

## 3B — The Sewers (the Dead Rats)

**The route itself. The party's best credential is one they already own and may not think to use.**

An unmapped sewer system beneath Bluelake, home to a growing **Dead Rats** wererat gang led by **Rsolk**. They ambush night traders from the gutters and keep a hidden vault of stolen goods somewhere below. They operate carefully: when exposure risk climbs, they go under and wait.

**The party has a standing alliance with a wererat gang** — Zeleen Varnaster and the Whiskered Gang, who hold Mountain's Toe. Wererats in one city know of wererats in another. **Saying Zeleen's name gets them a conversation instead of an ambush**, and it should be the party's discovery, not an offered hint. If they reach for it, reward it immediately and generously — this is exactly the kind of accumulated-relationship payoff the campaign has been banking.

**What Rsolk will trade:**

He does not care about the Manifold, House Margaster, or the Commission. He cares that a route through his tunnels is being used by people who do not pay him, and has for years, and he has never been able to work out who protects it.

- **He confirms the route.** Aqueduct steps down, through the old maintenance tunnel, out at the lower docks. He has watched it. He has never robbed it. *"Twice we tried. Twice the men we sent came back and could not say why they turned around."*
- **He confirms the cargo is mostly food** — fish, produce, salt — which is why he stopped caring. *"Nobody gets rich on turnips."*
- **He will not go down the tunnel.** Not frightened, exactly. Uninterested in the specific way animals are uninterested in a particular corner.
- **He wants something:** a name, a favour, or Brewbarry's word — and Brewbarry has *just* invented reciprocal-favour diplomacy with a banker and will absolutely try it on a wererat king. Let it work. It is in character and it is funny and it costs the GM nothing.

> **GM-only — plant, do not fire.** Zeleen Varnaster has been drawing **Don-Jon Raskin's dividend** every quarter since she had him killed, and House Margaster forges the paper that clears it. That is Ruling 11 material and it belongs to the Commission arc, which starts at the Neverember dinner. **Do not surface it here.**
>
> Plant one line only. Rsolk, on hearing Zeleen's name, is a half-beat too interested: *"Varnaster. She still doing business up the coast?"* If anyone asks what business, he shrugs and changes the subject. That is all. Do not let him elaborate even if pushed — he genuinely doesn't know much, he just knows she has paper that clears, and that is a strange thing for a wererat to have.

**Failure state, comedic and material:** if the party goes into the sewers *without* invoking the alliance — armoured, glowing, loud, with a skeletal horse — the Dead Rats do not fight them. They **withdraw completely** and stay withdrawn, which is their documented doctrine. The party gets an empty sewer, no guide, no confirmation of the route, and a gang that now watches them. They have to find another way in, and the other ways are worse.

## 3C — Hazel and the Lake

**Short. One image. Do not make this the session.**

The user asked for the Bluelake arc to open, and Hazel *is* the lake — but under a noir tone she works best as the one genuinely warm person in a district of angles, holding a problem far too big for her.

**Hazel** — short human alchemist, amber eyes, brown curls, welding goggles pushed up, soot on her cheeks, aggressively cheerful. Her lab sits between the Tatters and the lakeside. Read-aloud is in `bluelake_district.md`.

She has cleared the top layer of sludge and restored the blue water. **A thick layer of magical sludge remains at the bottom, and she established two months ago that it is being *produced*, not accumulating.** Something below generates it continuously.

**What she actually wants:** not treasure. She wants someone to tell her she is not imagining things. And she would like to know if anyone has a way to go *under* the lake without drowning. She has not been able to arrange that part.

**The one image:** she says *"apertures"* very carefully, like she is trying to be precise about something that might be nothing. Then, quieter: *"that look like eyes."*

**Do not elaborate.** If Soma goes in — wild shape, easy swim, easy return — the structures are six feet across, lattices and arches made of the sludge itself, and **they reorient toward her.** She gets back up fine. She feels observed for the rest of the session.

> **GM-only:** Abolethic Sovereignty. The water column is a contact medium and the lake is a long-range sensory apparatus; Mayor Soman Galt is their surface agent. **Soma should detect that this is intentional and deeply unnatural — and not resolve it.** Meril's Staff reads the source as *below*, not in the lake.

### Why this leg is load-bearing rather than optional

**Hazel has mapped three possible entry points to the Underdark beneath Neverwinter.** One of them is *"a sealed maintenance tunnel under the old Alagondar aqueduct."*

That is the same tunnel. The smuggling route and Hazel's second entry point are the same door, and she has meticulous notes on it that she has been waiting to give to somebody.

**This is the redundancy that makes the session safe.** If the party never asks Bellows, never notices the price, and never invokes Zeleen — Hazel hands them the aqueduct tunnel anyway, for free, because she wants help with a lake. Two independent threads, one door.

## 3D — The Flickering Ember (the trap that is already armed)

**Do not steer them here. Let them walk in.**

Somebody has to buy the enchantment for Brewbarry's surveillance rig — a sending stone disguised as a coin, paired with a Magic Mouth coin, to be seeded into Cullen Sharpe's returned pouch. Vukradin has already noted the party lacks the levels; Brewbarry intends to find "some magic shop where they can do that for us."

The nearest general-goods shop in Bluelake is **The Flickering Ember**, run by **Horst**, an older human man. The goods are a front for **Nasher weapon-laundering** and there is a hidden passage to the Nasher warehouse (DC 15 Investigation).

**Horst was seduced and robbed in his youth by a drow sorcerer named Valanthe.** His hatred has only grown. Given any opportunity to strike at a drow, a sorcerer, or a drow sorcerer, he will struggle to restrain himself. If any party member is drow, **he will send people after them.** If none is, he pays for information on where to find such targets.

## 3E — What Valphine Finds Instead (the Myrkulites)

**RULED (GM, 2026-08-24): there is no Assassin's Guild. If Valphine goes looking for one, Myrkulites show up. The Cult of Myrkul has been picking up steam.**

**Triggered, not placed.** This fires the moment Valphine seriously pursues the hit-contract idea, anywhere in the city. It is not a Bluelake scene and does not need Bluelake.

> **This is a queued beat firing on schedule, not a new thread.** `20260531_rimardo_corrin_at_the_lodge.md:192` records the Myrkul bone-act being deliberately cut at the Lodge — *"The second cult waits."* It has waited. The party is **level 7**; *Storm Lord's Wrath* is levels 7–9 and opens with **Talos and Myrkul cults fighting over Leilon**. Rimardo has already told them they will be *"a better audience in Leilon."* Myrkul surfacing in Neverwinter now is the correct early warning for the tier they are walking into.

### Why the cult is picking up steam — all of it already canon

Do not invent a reason. The city supplies four:

- **Neverwinter does not bury its dead. It burns them** — because the Spellplague made Neverdeath's corpses animate. The graveyard is **walled and contained, not cleared.** The city has turned death into a containment problem.
- **Lord Neverember officially ruled Elara Meliamne's murder a tragic accident and instructed the city to mourn and move on.** That is a public order to stop grieving, issued eight days before the party arrived.
- **Three wars just ended** — Dragons, Giants, the Underdark. An enormous regional death toll with no rites anyone can name.
- **The sealed Chasm was declared open land** and the poor are trickling in to claim it. A fresh, desperate congregation living on top of a magically capped hole.

**Their actual grievance, and it is coherent:** Neverwinter has made death into *administration*. Walls, pyres, a filed verdict, an instruction to move on. **They are the only people in this city who will say out loud that a woman was murdered and the city filed it.** They are not good. Death is their subject and they are offended by the lie.

> **Note the rhyme, and use it.** That is the same shape as the Commission — an institution where nothing is ever a decision. Elara died of an absent filing restriction (Ruling 8). **The Myrkulites are, briefly, the only people in Neverwinter who tell the party something true about her death** — and they are still the enemy.

### The scene

Valphine asks her question in the right places, competently. It does not parse. Fences look blank; a Dead Rats lieutenant is offended she thinks he would take money for it.

**But the word *death* travels differently than she meant it.** Within a few hours somebody finds her.

**Kessa Vaun** — a licensed **pyre-keeper of Neverwinter**. Middle-aged, forearms scarred pale from heat, smells of ash and the astringent oil they use on the biers. Carries a civic writ of office. *(Name grep-checked against `docs/entity_registry.yaml` — no collision. Run `registry_add` only when she reaches the table.)*

She is not hostile, not threatening, and not selling anything. She is **professionally curious**, and she has been curious since the sermon.

> *"You've been asking who in this city will kill a man for coin."* A pause, entirely without judgement. *"Nobody. I've been putting this city's dead on the fire for eleven years and I have never once met a person who did it for money. They do it for property, or for a name, or because a paper said they could."*
> *"You're the woman from the Spire."*

**She does not lie. Not once, about anything.** If Valphine uses her unsettled lie-detection ability, every single reading comes back clean — which is far more unnerving than a lie and requires no ruling on the mechanics.

### What Kessa gives them — the Bimble payoff

**This is the reason the scene exists and it is a real win.** She handles every body in her district. She knows the count.

> *"A gnome. Older, small, careful hands."* She thinks about it properly, which takes a moment. *"No. Not through my fires, and not through Harrow's on the east side, and I'd have heard. Seven tendays, no gnome."*
> *"Whatever you're looking for him about — **he's alive.** Or he's somewhere nobody's found him to burn him."*

**Valphine's wrong question produces the right answer by a completely different route.** They came for a killer and got confirmation from an unimpeachable source that their man is breathing. Give it to them cleanly and let it feel earned.

**She will also, unprompted, correct the record on Elara:** the burn was closed-casket by order, she did not handle it, and *"a body that can't be looked at is a body somebody has an opinion about."* She is not accusing anyone. She is describing a procedure that was wrong.

### The price

**The Cult of Myrkul now knows Valphine's name, and they have known it since the sermon.** Kessa is not recruiting and will not pitch. She is *interested*, and she says so.

> *"You told a room full of people that light burns the shadow out of them and they cheered."* *"I put them on the fire afterward. Everyone you ever preach to."* *"I don't think you're wrong. I think you're early."*

**They agree about almost everything and are irreconcilable.** Both despise how this city manages death politely. Valphine says the dark must be destroyed; Kessa says the dark is the destination and Valphine is selling people a comfortable lie about the ending. The conversation should be warm, unhurried, and completely without a resolution.

> **Stage direction.** Do not have Kessa threaten, recruit, or leave ominously. She has somewhere to be. She says goodbye normally. **The unsettling part is that she was pleasant and she will be back**, and Gary should be the one who works that out.

### NPC notes

- **Kessa Vaun** — wants to understand what Valphine actually believes, because it is professionally relevant to her. Never lies, never raises her voice, thinks before answering. **What she wants from the scene:** to find out whether the drow is a rival or a recruit. She has not decided and does not pretend to have.
- **What she would never do:** offer a service, name a price, or claim the cult is anything other than what it is.

### Guardrails

- **Ularan Mortus does not appear and is not named.** He is the *Divine Contention* antagonist at levels 11–13 with an undead army. Spending him in a street conversation at level 7 wastes him. Kessa has never met him and would not know the name.
- **No undead, no ritual, no combat.** This is a conversation. The cult's strength in Neverwinter is that it is *civically employed*, not that it is dangerous yet.
- **Do not connect Myrkul to Bimble, the Manifold, or the Commission.** Kessa's information is negative — an absence in her records. That is all.
- **This does not open the Leilon arc.** It is a trailer for it.

## Setup

Late. The aqueduct steps at the edge of the Tower District where the service lane drops toward Bluelake. Wet stone. The city's noise a street away and then not.

**What they find:**

A sealed maintenance tunnel under the old Alagondar aqueduct. Sealed the way things are sealed in a city that gave up: a grille, mortared, then re-mortared, then **cut and re-hung on its own hinges by someone competent, at some point in the last few years.** The new hinges are the tell. Somebody maintains this.

Below the grille: standing water, and a smell of the harbour that should not reach this far inland — which is how they know the far end is at the water.

If they came via Bellows' timing, they can be here for the **third hour after midnight** and watch two men who are not the same two men bring rope-bound crates up out of the dark, wet on the underside, unstamped, and carry them up the service lane toward a restaurant that will open in five hours and charge half what the food is worth.

**Nobody stops them. Nobody threatens them. That is the horror of the district and it is entirely tonal:** they are standing in the middle of a functioning system that has been running for six years, in the open, in a city of thirty thousand people, and nobody has ever asked about it because the food was cheap and the question was rude.

## Ending the session here

**Do not let them go down.** Give them, in this order:

1. **A real reason not to.** The tunnel is flooded past the first bend at this hour — the summer water level drops in the lake but the tide runs the other way here. It is walkable at the turn of the tide, twice a day, and they do not know which turn.
2. **A reason to want to.** One of the crate-carriers, halfway up, stops and looks back down the tunnel — not at the party, at the dark — the way a man checks on someone he left behind.
3. **A choice for next session**, stated out loud: go down the tunnel, or go around to the lower docks and come at it from the water.

> **Stage direction — the last beat.** After the carriers have gone, before anyone says anything, one line of narration and then stop talking. Let them fill it. This party fills silences with jokes and the joke will be the right ending.

## Branch — if they blow it and go down anyway

Fine. Level 7 party, no serious threat down there. They get **wet, lost, and slow**, they surface at the lower docks at dawn in full view of a working harbour, and **whoever is feeding Bimble sees them coming from two hundred yards away.** He moves. Next session opens on a cold room and a workbench — which is a worse version of the reveal, not a broken one.

**Say this out loud to nobody, but hold the line:** Bimble does not get found tonight in any branch.

# Scene 5 (CONTINGENCY) — Dinner at Castle Never

**Do not offer this. Run it only if the party lunges for it** — if Vukradin decides the Bluelake work can wait, if somebody sends to Aldus, or if the table's energy says *we want the big room tonight*.

**Understand what running it costs and buys.** This dinner is the **seed scene for the Commission arc and the Claudien arc both.** Running it opens two long campaigns. It is worth doing early if the players are excited — excitement is a better reason than a schedule — but the Scene-4 guardrails shift the moment it fires. See **Guardrails, amended** at the end of this scene.

## Setup

**When:** Evening. If they do Bluelake first, this is very late and they arrive smelling of the aqueduct, which is funnier and better.

**Where:** Castle Never. So named because it has never been conquered — which per the DM *"creates conviction among those who should know better."* Vukradin's standing response is on the record: *"Challenge accepted."* Reconstruction funded by heavy taxation, and visibly so: the party dines in a room that is three-quarters restored and one-quarter scaffold, and nobody mentions it.

**Present:** Lord Dagult Neverember. Aldus, hovering, delighted, nervous. Two servants. **Boney is not admitted** and this will require a conversation.

**Aldus took their dietary requirements last chapter and promised "a meal that you will never forget."** He has over-delivered catastrophically — every dish is a reference to something the party did. A tortle-safe course prepared with visible anxiety. A goliath portion. Something on fire for Valphine. He has been planning this for days and he wants it to go well more than anyone in the room.

## What Neverember wants (say it plainly to yourself)

**He is recruiting, and he cannot say so. And as of the 2026-08-24 ruling you now know exactly what for: he needs the High Road.**

> **GM-only, Commission Ruling 13.** House Margaster did not corner Neverwinter's trade — they cornered **the distribution leg out of the port**, and deliberately stopped there, because cornering everything would bring the **Zhentarim** down on them. Which leaves exactly one alternative: **overland, down the High Road.**
>
> **Leilon sits on that road, and Neverember is rebuilding it.** Not expansion — **survival.** It is a mortgaged chairman, losing his own count, building a way around his own captured harbour, because he cannot dislodge Margaster with paperwork when they are better at paperwork than he is.
>
> **And the road is not safe.** Talos and Myrkul cults are fighting over it, bandits are robbing Leilon's reconstruction convoys, sahuagin are burning the food. **He needs it cleared and he does not have an army.**
>
> **That is why this party is at his table**, and it is the plainest reading of the adventurer policy yet: not commercial doctrine, not enclosure — *he needs the road open and adventurers are the only instrument he has left.*

**The dinner is not a favour being dispensed.** It is a man with one strategic option interviewing the only people who can deliver it.

**Play him as not saying any of this.** He asks about their "circulation of wealth," their town, the Candlekeep Report — and somewhere in there, lightly, as though it were small talk, he asks **whether they have ever travelled the High Road, and what they made of it.** That question is the whole dinner and it should sound like nothing.

**If the party asks him straight out why Leilon matters**, he gives the public answer — a waystation, resettlement, the prosperity of the North — and it is true, and it is not the reason.

**The bitter beat available whenever you want it:** they have already done this job once. They pacified the entire Phandalin hinterland at their own expense and were paid a 200 gp dividend and a lecture about hoarding.

> **GM-only, Ruling 1.** He held the Commission's controlling bloc when he founded it in 1467. Three wars of borrowing diluted him. He still chairs, still gets the deference, **and the count no longer favours him** — and he is working hard to keep that invisible. He is not corrupt. **He is mortgaged** (Ruling 7). Every loan saved his city and cost him a piece of the instrument that made him powerful, and he took each one for good reasons.
>
> **He does not know that the Manifold refusal was made without him.** An entity of Aletra's tier walked into his docks, delivered an ultimatum, and was turned away by his own institution, and nobody told him. He is running a city on top of a decision he was not consulted on. **Do not let him know this and do not let the party learn it here.**
>
> Consequences for how you play him: the dinner is not a favour being dispensed. He is short of allies with standing and long on people who can be bought, and this party is the first group in years who are visibly neither. **The studio offer is a chairman shoring up legitimacy he is losing — he is spending to look solvent.** And the party's small share block, in a contested count, is a swing weight nobody has priced. Including them.

## The four courses

Structure the dinner as four moves. He does them in this order and he does not rush.

### 1. The compliment that is a measurement

He opens warm and specific. He has read about them. He knows the dragon, the Manse, the statue. He is genuinely impressed and he is also **pricing them**, and a good Insight catches the second thing without catching the first as false. Both are true.

Somewhere in here: the **Candlekeep Report** on adventuring parties and the circulation of wealth. Aldus already trailed the punchline last chapter and Neverember delivers it himself, pleasantly, over the second course:

> *"There is a paper. Some sages at Candlekeep. They compare adventurers who accumulate and do not circulate to — forgive me — a large reptile."* A beat. *"I understand you killed one."*

### 2. The bill

**This is the grievance and it is legitimate.** They rearranged the government of a town inside his sphere, without asking him, and he found out from someone else.

> *"You gave a town a constitution. I did not object. I am not objecting now."* *"You wrote it in a market square in my jurisdiction and the first I heard of it was from a woman who rode four days to tell me."*

**Jenna Roscoe is the unnamed source and the party has not made this connection.** He will not name her. If asked directly he says only *"someone with standing who was present."*

### 3. The inversion — the Claudien seed

**Do not have Vukradin ask for anything.** He never has. He proclaimed UBT from a town green and the *party* floated direct mayoral elections. The office is not something Dave has reached for.

So Neverember makes the grievance the offer:

> *"You rearranged my town without asking. Very well. Then you own it."* *"Be mayor. Be accountable to me. I will sign it tonight."*

He produces **a writ**, already drawn, already sealed. That is the seed. He did not draft it himself.

> **GM-only — Claudien, Infection Clock stage 2.** **Aldus consulted something to solve his lord's political problem, and he will never know he did anything.** He is a friend, he is a fan, he was trying to help, and the writ in his lord's hand is the first product of a Phandalin the party has left full of unanswered questions.
>
> **Do not reveal, hint at, or foreshadow this.** The writ is just a writ tonight. The only tell available is that it is unusually well-drafted for something produced in two days, and that it anticipates an objection Vukradin has not made yet. Nobody should notice. If a player does, be delighted and give them nothing.
>
> **Corruption exhibit, if the party wants leverage or Neverember wants to demonstrate the job is real:** **Tibor Wester's unlicensed wildcat logging.** Vukradin persuaded Tibor's loggers in ch38 to report it to Neverwinter authorities — **Neverember may already have the file.** Harbin is a credit-claiming coward; his brother is running an actual racket in the Lord Protector's jurisdiction. That is the better exhibit.

**The trap in the offer is real and Vukradin should be able to feel it without being told:** accepting makes UBT *his responsibility to enforce*, inside a jurisdiction whose Lord Protector wants the wealth circulating in a specific direction. Declining leaves Phandalin's constitution unratified and Harbin in the chair. **There is no clean answer**, which is correct — this campaign has stopped having those.

### 4. The private word — Valphine

**This is new as of ch48 and it changes the dinner.** He already approached her at the Spire, in front of witnesses, and said:

> *"I truly do admire your focus on eliminating those who are enemies of Lathander."* *"I can see how we could work wonders together."*

Then walked off **mumbling about power and the need to exercise it.**

So he does not open that thread here. **He continues it.** At some point after the plates go, he takes her aside — and he is not being sinister. He means it. He is a man losing a count, surrounded by people who can be bought, who has just watched a cleric blind a heckler in a temple and be cheered for it, and he has concluded she is the most decisive person he has met in a decade.

> *"You did not negotiate with that man. You did not have him removed. You made an example and the room agreed with you."* *"I have a city full of people who explain to me why a thing cannot be done."*

**What he actually wants from her is an instrument.** He would not put it that way and would be offended by the framing.

> **GM-only, and hold it.** Per Ruling 5 the Commission's core funds this party **because of Valphine** — she is Aletra Sotorra's sister, and a frightened psionic spymaster wants a line to the entity that reached into his holdings. Neverember does not know any of that. **His interest in her is entirely his own and entirely sincere, which is why it is useful cover.** Two separate parties are courting the same PC for unrelated reasons, and neither knows about the other.

## Open decisions (yours, not mine)

1. **~~Is there an Assassin's Guild?~~ RULED (GM, 2026-08-24): no.** Applied above under player questions. Valphine's instinct is canonical and in character; the market she assumes exists does not. Neverwinter murders by omission and by forged instrument, and that mismatch is the point.

   **The death-cult slot — RULED (GM, 2026-08-24): the Cult of Myrkul, which has been picking up steam. Bhaal is not used.** Built as **Scene 3E**. This was the right call and the grep supports it: Bhaal appears nowhere in the campaign, while Myrkul is a Leilon-tier faction with a named priest (**Ularan Mortus**), a Neverwinter site (the unvisited **Pirates' Skyhold** earthmote, already flagged as a Myrkul/undead hook), and `docs/Kraken society.md` listing **"Conflict between clerics of Lathander and Myrkul"** as a *primary threat*, twice.

   **Genuinely still open, downstream of the ruling:**

   - **Does the cult's Neverwinter presence connect upward to Ularan Mortus, or is it independent?** 3E assumes Kessa has never heard the name. An organised Neverwinter cell reporting to the *Divine Contention* antagonist is a bigger, earlier, more dangerous campaign. **Do not decide this in play — decide it before 3E runs**, because Valphine will ask who Kessa answers to.
   - **Does this move a threat score?** There is no Myrkul tracker in `planning.md`. The Lathander-vs-Myrkul axis is now live in Neverwinter and Valphine is the axis. It may deserve one, and it interacts with her **Searing Dawn** track.
   - **Pirates' Skyhold.** The Myrkul/undead escalation hook is hanging over the city, unvisited, and 3E gives the party their first reason to look up at it.

2. **How much was in the donation basket, and in Cullen's pouch?** Left explicitly unresolved at the table (*"Oh, that's awkward"*). Brewbarry will ask. The pouch amount also matters mechanically — he intends to return it seeded with a sending stone, and a pouch that comes back light is a tell.

3. **Who actually supplies The Board Laid Bare?** I have deliberately not answered this. The route is established; the *principal* is not. Candidates: the Dead Rats' predecessors running an honest sideline; a Commission clerk skimming spoilage off the Harbor authority; a genuine philanthropist; or nobody at all — an arrangement that outlived whoever made it, which fits the district. **This is the answer at the end of the tunnel and it should not be a coincidence with anything else.**

4. **Does the Cloak Tower / Pantograph thread get a hook this session or stay texture?** Lim hands it over for free. A coin duplicator in a campaign whose central argument is the circulation of wealth is almost too on-the-nose, and it may be better held until UBT is actually implemented.

5. **Does Boney come into Bluelake?** He walks the streets on the DM's "he doesn't poop" ruling and is not taken indoors. A skeletal horse in a district where the graveyard's dead animate and are contained behind walls is a very different proposition. **This may be a Neverdeath hook or a liability; either is fine, but decide before they get there.**

6. **Which two or three legs of Scene 3 do you actually want?** All four is too many. My read: **3A (Harpers) + 3B (Dead Rats)** is the tightest noir spine, with **3C (Hazel)** as the safety net if they miss the supply-chain bridge, and **3D (Horst)** firing only if they go shopping.

7. **The in-fiction date.** I have not stamped one — the docs put the campaign at ~1495 DR but I found no tenday date for the Neverwinter arc. If you want FR date stamps in play, this is the session to set the anchor.

8. **Do you want the dinner available this session at all?** It is written as a contingency, but running it starts two arcs. If you would rather protect the Bluelake spine, the honest out is Aldus: *"His lordship is very busy and preoccupied"* — which he already said, in those words, last chapter.

# Appendix — City Color

**Roll or pick.** These exist so Neverwinter feels inhabited between the scenes that matter. **Almost none of them are combat.** This city hurts people with paperwork, rumour and rent, and the tables should teach that before the plot does.

**One standing rule that makes all of them work:** *the party is famous now, and as of yesterday morning they are famous for something specific.* Valphine took a temple. A man was blinded in front of a congregation and told it was a blessing. Lord Neverember spoke to a drow in private. A necklace changed hands. **Every roll below should land as a reaction to people, not a random event.**
