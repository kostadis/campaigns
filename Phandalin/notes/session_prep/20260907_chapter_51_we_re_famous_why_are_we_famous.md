## Scene 1 — The expensive room behind the cheap food

Give the party time to understand the bargain before moving to the dungeon.

### Setup

[TABLE] Brewbarry is already inside the mirrored tavern; the familiar has scouted its basement office. [OVERLAY] Let him enjoy finally being able to afford the place for one exchange. Then the same host offers the party a quieter seat. No new faction tour.

### Approach

[OVERLAY] The host's first private line: “Before we discuss what you found, could we agree to use the front door from now on?” This response is proposed fallout, not proof of an unseen alarm or knowledge of everything the rat saw.

> **GM-only [TABLE]:** The Harpers want Bimble's information and cannot reach him. **[OVERLAY]** This Harper wants the party to open the route without exposing the food network. ### Branch — a private, candid bargain

[OVERLAY] If the party explains the crate and the job, give a real answer promptly: “We pay to keep a man alive. Offer a meeting here, or a meeting somewhere the party chooses. A refusal does not close the sewer route.

[OVERLAY] If they pursue Lim's involvement, the Harper may confirm the account rather than pretend the evidence is meaningless. Keep her motives within the locked price-war ruling; whether she deliberately pointed Soma at the crate remains unresolved.

### Branch — accusation or public exposure

### Branch — they leave for the fight

[OVERLAY] Let them. Rsolk's job and location are already theirs. The Harper asks only where a message about the rescued man could safely reach them. Do not require a social success to unlock promised combat.

## Scene 3 — Go in hunting

[OVERLAY] A brick overflow chamber with wet footing, a raised maintenance ledge and two exits: their approach and the lower-docks passage. Webs hang above water without looking wet. Six weavers hold the ceiling. Give the party time to identify the arrangement and choose entry positions.

[OVERLAY] Use the established campaign stat block for exact mechanics; this document does not replace it with the old prep's approximate phase-spider numbers or claim the encounter is mathematically safe. > **GM-only [TABLE]:** The spiders intensify the planar leak's longstanding aversion and are held at a standstill by Bimble. Defeating them removes the intensified confusion, not the leak. **[OVERLAY]** Use the companion dungeon’s defended approach and two routes. ### Branch — they fight with preparation

[OVERLAY] Show the benefit immediately when fire clears a web or radiant damage hurts a weaver. Let Brewbarry choose the line he holds, Soma shape the field, Valphine exploit the known response, and Vukradin use positioning or control as the players decide. Do not script their turns. ### Branch — they bypass or withdraw

[OVERLAY] A successful bypass reaches the far passage but leaves the infestation job unfinished and the return route dangerous. A retreat stays available through the approach unless events actually block it. Rsolk's payoff is deferred; the famous band must return to its waiting fans with a revised completion estimate. Do not guarantee survival or force defeat: resolve danger using the selected encounter rules and player choices.

### Timing and exit

[OVERLAY] Tide pressure runs on the established descent window. Water rises between meaningful exploration beats; it does not become a six-foot wall in thirty-six seconds merely because combat began. A sudden surge requires a separately signaled hazard. After victory, make the decision plain: press on, post a rear watch, or withdraw and leave an open passage.

## Scene 4 — The man who kept the receipts

### Setup and approach

[OVERLAY] Beyond the junction: repeated scorch marks, residue swept to one side, a conspicuous warning rune, a carefully rehung storeroom door. Inside are squared crate slats, ordered tools, a defensive fire device and Bimble. The brass-and-leather collector returns to his side: pay off that recognition before he explains it.

[OVERLAY] Bimble wants credible help clearing his name. He fears being blamed for a failure bearing his maintenance signature. Tic: correct a technical word, notice the armed people listening, apologize. “I didn't break it. I need somebody to write that down before we do anything else.”

### Branch — announce themselves and offer help

[OVERLAY] He lowers the weapon and listens. Make the evidence available through questions, not a persuasion sequence. Offer him choices about where he goes; protection and public exoneration are distinct promises, neither a guarantee the party can casually make.

### Branch — force entry or demand the culprit

[OVERLAY] He warns them to stop; aggression can provoke a defensive shot under the chosen NPC rules. His testimony narrows to immediate safety until they de-escalate. He cannot reveal a name he never learned, even under magical questioning. His fear is an obstacle they can repair, not a reason to delete the clues.

## Scene 5 — A familiar buyer

### Setup and approach

[OVERLAY] Run this only if Sridar's tail remains viable. Otherwise she must find another earned occasion to approach them. Place her at the lower-docks exit after the testimony; her companions remain at established positions, not anywhere the GM needs them to be.

[TABLE] She is the artifact collector they met at Falcon's Lodge and has paid for information before. [OVERLAY] Her voice stays warm, curious and businesslike: “May I see the manual? Then we can discuss whether you want to keep carrying it.”

> **GM-only [OVERLAY]:** Sridar wants the document removed from circulation while keeping her access to the party. The proposed cell serves Aletra; that connection is not something she volunteers. Her worry is that force will destroy a useful buying relationship. She prefers a purchase, then an opportunity later, to a battle she cannot control.

### Branch — refuse, copy first, or demand terms

[OVERLAY] A refusal is allowed to stand. Copies reduce the damage of a sale and count as clever play. Ask what she offers and what she claims about ownership; set the price as a GM decision rather than creating an entitlement. Bimble has a voice in the transaction.

### Branch — sell it

[OVERLAY] The cell gains the original; any copies and Bimble's knowledge still exist. Do not erase those successes or declare the whole investigation permanently dead. Whether and when she destroys it depends on her actual opportunity after leaving.

### Branch — confront or attack her

[OVERLAY] She seeks an exit using abilities and equipment actually on her selected sheet. No automatic portal or immunity to capture. If the party catches her, let that change the campaign; preserve her knowledge boundary, not her plot armor. Close on the players' decision rather than insisting she deliver a final line.
