# A Man Who Has Been Looking For A Generation

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging Perrin Alagondar's **first contact** and the run-up to the handoff.

**Party:** full (Vukradin, Soma, Valphine, Brewbarry + Boney). **Level 7.**
**Runs interleaved with:** `20260728_linene_margaster.md` — Perrin surfaces between the counting house and the warehouse.
**Hands off to:** `20260623_necklace-of-fireballs-neverwinter.md` Scene 2 (the handoff itself, the sideways, the recovery). **This doc stops at the threshold.**

---

## Scope

This is the **front end only**: how Perrin surfaces, what first contact feels like, and how the meet gets scheduled. The handoff-goes-sideways material already exists and is good; don't rewrite it. What was missing was everything *before* the parlour door.

---

## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

The party **likes Perrin** before they have any reason to suspect the transaction — and the tell has been planted in front of them, unremarked, by the end of the scene.

That's it. If they walk toward the meet fond of him and faintly uneasy about something they can't name, this landed. If they arrive suspicious *of him*, the design breaks: his sincerity is the mechanism, and a party braced against Perrin will beat a man who is not their enemy and never find the house standing behind him.

## The problem this scene has to solve

The party just watched Cullen Sharpe extort a terrified Cassian with a valid writ. **They are primed.** The next person who approaches them about returning an object is walking into loaded guns.

So the defence is structural: **Perrin arrives as a man, not as paperwork.** No letterhead, no clerk, no appointment slip, no recovery office. Nothing about him resembles the thing that just happened at the Mask. If the first contact carries any bureaucratic apparatus at all, their suspicion of Margaster attaches to *him* — and he doesn't deserve it and can't survive it.

> **GM-only — who he actually is.** A Nasher / Son of Alagondar loyalist, sincere, being used. A **thin standalone front**: a small cell, a cause, a purse. Following him *up* reaches Margaster, not the city's Nasher network. He wants the necklace as a **banner**. His "associate" wants it as **ordnance**. He does not know that, does not know the arson target, and is not the threat. He is the party's *door* — and, played right, an ally they can turn.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing clever)

They like him, they agree to the meet, they show up, they hand it over, they take the purse, they feel righteous. The beads go to Margaster rather than the cause. Within a tenday something burns. See the necklace doc for the full downstream.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Ask him about Lady Alagondar herself.** Not the dynasty — the woman. He has nothing.
2. **Ask Boney.** Free, in-house, no roll. They ride a horse that knew her.
3. **Verify before the meet** — Cassian's genealogical records, Ostavar's Salvage Archive, the House of Knowledge, Madame Rosene at the Driftwood. Don't gate any of these behind a DC; gate them behind whether the party thinks to ask.
4. **Notice the meet came back tighter than the man offered it.**
5. **Ask Kaelen where the match came from.** He can only give his source's answer: the recovery office, a clerk named **Havel Drest**, the Margaster-endowed desk. Hearing himself say "Margaster" out loud is the first crack in him.

## Scene 1 — The Approach

### Setup
Street level, Protector's Enclave, shortly after the counting house. Midday. The party is visible and *famous* — Vukradin performed at the gate to a crowd, Brewbarry has fans in tribal-marked cloaks, a skeletal horse is with them.

Perrin has known for a tenday. He's been checking. He doesn't need to find them; he needs to work up the nerve.

**Present:** Perrin. Optionally **the Fixer**, standing slightly back, saying nothing (see Open Decisions).

### The approach itself
He is **not smooth.** He has rehearsed this and the rehearsal doesn't survive contact. He is a man in his thirties in decent but unfashionable clothes who has been waiting outside a counting house for two hours.

Lift if it lands:

> *"Forgive me — you're the ones from Phandalin. The— I'm sorry. My name is Perrin. Perrin Alagondar. The recovery office wrote to me. They said you'd found—"*
>
> *(He stops. Starts again.)*
>
> *"My family lost something a long time ago. Grave-robbers, a generation back. We never stopped looking. And they wrote to me last tenday and said someone had it, and that they were coming here, and I've read the letter about forty times."*

**He does not ask for it.** That's the whole character. He wants to do it *properly* — a formal handover, dinner first, an invitation to see the family record. The delay is genuine courtesy.

> **GM-only — his interiority.** He is terrified they'll say no, or that it's a mistake, or that they'll want money he doesn't have (he has money; the cause has money; he doesn't know where the cause's money comes from). Underneath: this is the first thing that has *ever gone right* for the restoration in his lifetime. He is not a fanatic. He is a man who found a reason to hope on a Tuesday.

### Planting the tell — do this here, before any writ exists
Let him talk. He will, at length, and within about ninety seconds he's on the **cause**: the stolen city, the deposed line, Neverember sitting in a chair that isn't his, the restoration.

If anyone asks who specifically the necklace belonged to — **he reaches for history rather than memory.** An ancestor. The line. A queen of the Alagondars. No name offered unprompted, no story, no grandmother.

A real heir mourns a person. A loyalist quotes a manifesto.

**Nobody has to catch it.** It only has to have happened in front of them.

### Branch — Vukradin tries to hand it over on the spot ⚠️
**Prep this properly; it is genuinely likely.** Vukradin's entire drive is to put things down, and here is a rightful owner, in the street, sincere.

- **Perrin refuses.** Emphatically, almost distressed — *"No — not like this. Not in the street. Please. Let me do this properly."* He is not being cagey; he wants ceremony because the thing deserves ceremony.
- **And then the associate speaks.** The silent man who has said nothing for the entire conversation suddenly has opinions about custody, and they are smooth, practical, and immediate. *"We can take receipt now. I have the case."*

That is the moment. A man who is overwhelmed, standing beside a man who is neither — and the difference only becomes visible when the object is actually in reach.

- **If the party clocks it:** reward it fully. They've earned the seam in scene one.
- **If they don't and hand it over:** the necklace is gone by the end of the first scene and the session becomes a **recovery** rather than a handoff. That's a legitimate, exciting shape — go to the necklace doc's Scene 3 as a manhunt. Don't rescue them.
- **If the Fixer isn't present:** Perrin's refusal stands and the meet gets scheduled anyway. Cleaner, less sharp.

### Branch — the party is hostile out of the gate
Entirely reasonable after the Mask. Let them be rude. **Perrin folds rather than escalating** — apologizes for intruding, offers to go through the recovery office instead if that's more comfortable, and looks gutted.

That offer is the trap tightening politely, and it's also the moment a decent party feels bad. If they push him away here, he writes to them at their inn that evening, more formally, worse.

### Branch — the party interrogates him immediately
Good. He answers everything honestly except the things he's wrong about. The dynasty questions he aces. The *person* questions he fumbles and doesn't notice fumbling. If someone lands the Lady Alagondar question hard, he gets defensive in a very specific way — **he's embarrassed he doesn't know**, and covers with more cause-talk.

### Branch — Boney is present and someone thinks to ask
Best case. Boney knew her. Perrin's account cannot survive contact with a horse that carried her. Play Boney matter-of-fact and slightly puzzled — he isn't accusing anyone, he's just answering a question about a person he remembers.

**Don't let this end the plot.** It proves Perrin's *story* is wrong; it doesn't tell them who wrote the story. That's still Drest.

### NPC notes
- **Perrin Alagondar** — earnest, a little zealous, gentility that frays when pressed on the person behind the relic. Wants: to do this properly, and to matter to the cause. Voice: over-prepared and under-rehearsed; apologizes twice per exchange. **Quote canon:** *"The cause does not forget who returns what was taken."*
- **The Fixer** — economical, never raises his voice, treats everyone (Perrin included) as a logistics problem. Wants: a clean exit with the object. Says almost nothing until the object is in reach.

## Scene 2 — The Courtship (optional, and worth the table time)

If the party accepts hospitality — dinner, or a visit to see the family record — take it. This is where he becomes a person they don't want to hurt.

**What he shows them:** a genealogy, lovingly maintained, in a hand that isn't his. Cause pamphlets. A room in a house that is not grand. Three or four people who believe what he believes, who are unremarkable, and who are *thrilled* to meet the party.

**What isn't there:** any object older than about thirty years. Any portrait. Any grave. For a family that "never stopped looking," there is a conspicuous absence of the family.

> **GM-only.** The genealogy is real work — someone spent months on it. It is also the *forged* line, prepared by the recovery office and handed to Perrin as a gift, which he received as validation of everything he'd believed. He treasures it. He has no idea it was made for him.

**Optional and cruel:** he asks Vukradin to perform. It's the first celebration the cell has had in years.

---

## Scene 3 — The Scheduling (the cheap, sharp tell)

Perrin proposed something warm and slow — dinner, ceremony, a proper handover.

**What comes back is a time, a notary's parlour, and a request to bring the item.** Clipped, efficient, transactional. Nothing like the man.

If anyone asks him about the change, he apologizes vaguely and blames arrangements. **He hasn't noticed the difference.** That's the first hard evidence that Perrin is not running his own handoff — and it costs you nothing.

The venue is the **Harbor-authority notary's parlour**, which now does double duty: it is the same apparatus the party watched squeeze Cassian. They'll walk in already knowing what these rooms are for. **That's earned dread, not a reveal.**

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Can we verify before we hand anything over?"** → Yes, and you *want* them to. Cassian, Ostavar's Salvage Archive, the House of Knowledge, Rosene, **or just asking Boney.** Don't gate behind a DC; gate behind whether they think to ask. Scene 2's tells are the safety net if they don't.
- **"Is this guy connected to Margaster?"** → Not knowingly, and nothing about him will suggest it. His associate is. Let them find the seam themselves.
- **"Where did Kaelen's match come from?"** → The recovery office; a clerk named **Havel Drest**; the Margaster-endowed desk. Kaelen will say this out loud without realizing what he's saying. Let a flicker cross his face and let him talk past it.
- **"Can we just keep it?"** → Yes. Then the scene is disappointing a sincere man to his face, which is its own cost, and the disposition question rolls forward.
- **"Are there really no Alagondars left?"** → `[GM-ONLY]` — the necklace is **grave-goods**, a dead queen's burial offering, not a transferable heirloom. There is no rightful living owner. The Nasher claim is sincere and hollow at once.
- **"Does he want money from us?"** → The reverse. He's bringing a purse.

## Stage directions

- **Let him be awkward.** Don't smooth him out. The rehearsal failing is the characterization.
- When someone asks who the necklace belonged to, **don't signal the gap.** Let him answer badly and move on. If a player pauses, say nothing.
- If the associate speaks up about custody, **deliver it flatly and immediately return to Perrin.** Don't hang a lantern.
- If Vukradin moves to hand it over, **do not have another PC stop him.** Let Perrin refuse. Let the table watch what happens next.
- If they turn Boney on the story, **give it to them fully.** No hedging, no partial answer. They earned it by remembering their own horse.
- If the party is cruel to him, **let it land and don't punish them for it.** Perrin absorbing it is worse for them than any consequence you could impose.

## Open decisions

1. **Is the Fixer at first contact?** Present-and-silent seeds the seam early and makes the hand-it-over-now branch excellent. Absent is cleaner and lets his wrongness land by contrast at the parlour. **Recommend present**, given the party is primed to notice Margaster-shaped people — if they clock him, that's them being sharp after the Mask, not you tipping.
2. **Sincere loyalist or cynic in the colors?** Doc is built on **sincere** — richer, turnable, recurring. Decide before the approach so you can pitch his reverence accordingly.
3. **Does the courtship scene happen?** It costs 20–30 minutes and buys real affection. Worth it if the session has room; cuttable if the Margaster material runs long.
4. **The purse figure** (set in the necklace doc, but decide before the meet is scheduled — he may mention a reward at first contact).
5. **Does Perrin know Cassian?** Both are inside Neverwinter's old-blood politics. A prior acquaintance — even hostile — gives the party a cross-check they didn't expect.
6. **Where does the cell meet?** Only matters if you run the courtship. Your call whether it's a house, a back room, or a rented hall.

## Do not

- **Do not make Perrin the villain.** He is a door and a potential ally.
- **Do not open with paperwork.** A man, not a desk.
- **Do not confirm or deny a tell when a player names one.** Let the NPC keep talking; let the table do the work.
- **Do not connect this to Bimble Nackle.** Different box.
