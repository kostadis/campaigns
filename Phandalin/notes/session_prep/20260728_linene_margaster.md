# The Only Viable Shipper

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging the counting house → warehouse run, with Linene's supply-chain errand as the thread that ties it together.

**Party:** full (Vukradin, Soma, Valphine, Brewbarry + Boney). **Level 7.**
**Runs interleaved with:** `20260728_perrin_opening_moves.md` — Perrin surfaces *between* the counting house and the warehouse. See Interleave, below.
**Overlays:** `20260623_neverwinter_vukradin_present.md` (Scene V2, the Golden Ledger Audit) · `neverwinter/neverwinter_network.md` (the machine) · `20260623_necklace-of-fireballs-neverwinter.md` (what this sets up).

---

## Where we are

At the Moonstone Mask the party handed the statue to **Lord Cassian Meliamne**. Cullen Sharpe arrived with a writ. Cassian was *terrified* and paid. The party examined the document and called it what it was.

So they walk into this session knowing House Margaster extorts money using **real, valid paper** — and not yet knowing it also **manufactures the claims**. That distinction is the whole escalation ladder, and it doesn't get climbed here. This session is the *offer*.

---

## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

The party ends the session with a genuine, lawful, **generous** offer from House Margaster on the table, and no way to refuse it that doesn't cost someone something they want. Brewbarry's factory works if they sign. Vukradin's return-every-item ethic finally *functions* if they sign. Linene's question gets answered by walking into the building. And they leave with **no proof of anything**, because everything they saw was documented and legal.

The session lands when the party understands that Margaster is not offering them a bribe — Margaster is offering them **competence**, and it is the best offer anyone in this city has made them.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing)

- **Brewbarry's factory stalls.** Lionshield's rates don't come down. Linene's letter bought him a meeting, not a business. He goes home with a vision and no shipping.
- **Vukradin keeps carrying things.** The sword, the necklace, whatever comes next. The pile grows. He stays tired.
- **Linene's question goes unanswered** and her Coster keeps degrading. She asked sincerely (Insight 19); a party that shrugs it off costs a genuine ally something real.
- **Margaster files them and waits.** Cullen isn't in a hurry. The next approach is less pleasant and comes at a worse moment.
- **The Manifold stays dark**, which means Margaster's monopoly stays intact, which means every future thread in this city runs through their desk.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Take the tour, take nothing else.** Go as clients, look at everything, sign nothing. Access is free; the contract is the trap.
2. **Read the tags.** The inventory in back carries claim tags with writ numbers, filing dates, and a clerk's mark. Same mark every time. That's Drest, and it's the thread to everything.
3. **Ask the plain question — *who decides who the owner is?*** Soma or Valphine will get there. Cullen answers honestly and it should chill them: the recovery office does, per the Harbor authority's schedule. Nobody is lying.
4. **Realize Cassian has a receipt.** He *paid*. A lawful settlement generates a record. He now holds documented proof of Margaster collecting against a family whose head they murdered — the exact thing his case needed, acquired at the worst possible moment.
5. **Solve Linene's problem the other way.** The winning move isn't exposing Margaster; it's **fixing the Manifold**. Restore the cheap route and the monopoly evaporates without anyone going to court. This is the highest-value read available and almost nobody takes it first. *(Guardrail: Bimble stays offstage — see Do Not, below.)*

## Scene 1 — The Counting House

### Setup
Morning. A chartered counting house in the Protector's Enclave `[INV]`. Two independent reasons to be here, and they arrive together:

- **Brewbarry** carries Linene's letter of introduction — capital for the bathrobe factory `[CANON, ch46]`
- **Vukradin** has the mine payout waiting as a certified conflict-free **Letter of Credit** `[PREP]`

**Present:** the party. **Ondrel Vance**, chartered underwriter `[NEW NPC]`. And, at a small folding table with a velvet rope and a queue of zero people: **Rimardo & Mr. Corrin, Divine Accountants and Occasional Entertainers.**

### Beat A — the audit

Vukradin cannot redeem without certification, and certification requires the auditors. There is no back door. They are *expecting* him.

Rimardo audits his **moral economy** as a stage routine. Corrin silently produces receipts from places he could not possibly have them.

> **Rimardo:** *"You have not refused dirty money. You have merely laundered it through paperwork with better penmanship."*

**UBT sharpened this for free.** Vukradin has since built a fund that routes 5% back to the party, tax-free — the ch46 record uses the word *laundered* unprompted. Corrin can produce the subcommittee charter. The prepared insult is now a documented description of something Vukradin did last tenday. Let him notice.

> **Corrin** stamps the ledger: **COMEDICALLY COMPLIANT.**

**The receipt that matters:** a **House Margaster handling fee, already embedded in the routing** of the certified conflict-free instrument. Unsourced, unitemized, and older than the party's arrival in the city. His clean money was skimmed before he touched it.

**Tone rule: joke first, dread second.** They laugh, then the fee lands.

### Beat B — the four questions

Vance is helpful, competent, and completely uninterested in politics. He asks every applicant the same four questions in the same order. The third one ruins everything.

1. What are you making?
2. What does it cost to make?
3. **How are you shipping?**
4. Who's already agreed to buy it?

Brewbarry's answer to (3) is "the Lionshield Coster." Vance winces — professionally, sympathetically. Lionshield's rates went up seven weeks ago and haven't come down. At those numbers the margin doesn't clear and he can't lend against it.

Then he does Brewbarry a **favour**:

> *"There is one house currently moving goods in this region at a rate that makes your figures work. I'd be doing you a disservice not to say so. Shall I write you an introduction?"*

He is not being sinister. It is correct commercial advice, and he'd be bad at his job to withhold it.

### Branch — the party names Margaster before he does
Reward it. Vance confirms without drama: yes, that's the house, and yes, they're the only ones. He has no opinion about them beyond their rate card. **His indifference is the horror** — to the financial system, Margaster is simply the efficient option.

### Branch — the party asks *why* Lionshield's rates went up
Vance knows the commercial shape, not the cause: their cheap distribution method stopped working around seven weeks ago, and they've been paying conventional freight since. He doesn't know what the method *was*. That's Linene's question, answered halfway, by a banker who doesn't care.

### Branch — Vukradin refuses to redeem
Then the Letter stays a number, Corrin stamps it **HOARDED — SEE PAPER NO. 27**, and Rimardo tips his hat. The deflationary-bard thesis just got notarized. Brewbarry's loan proceeds regardless — he doesn't need Vukradin's money, he needs a shipper.

### NPC notes
- **Ondrel Vance** `[NEW]` — chartered underwriter, forties, reading glasses he takes off to deliver bad news. Wants: a loan he can defend to his committee. Voice: recites lending criteria like scripture, then apologizes for it. **He likes Brewbarry.** That's what makes the referral land — it's a kindness.
- **Rimardo** — speaks fluent system. Enters funny, leaves unsettling. Reveals how the trick is done, which is how he breaks it.
- **Corrin** — never speaks. Every clue comes from his hands. **Uncapturable** — a grab passes through the bit; never roll for it.

## Scene 3 — The Warehouse

### Setup
Margaster's logistics operation, eastern quay. The party arrives **as clients, invited, in daylight, with a bank introduction.** They get a tour. Nobody is hiding anything.

**Present:** Cullen Sharpe. Warehouse staff (competent, ordinary, unaware). Optionally the Fixer, unremarked, if you want to seed him before the handoff.

### Approach — the operation is genuinely good
Clean floors. Accurate manifests. Lionshield-labeled goods moving at Margaster margin. Everything Linene wants to know is visible in ten minutes: **her shipping didn't break, it got bought.**

That's the answer to her errand, and the party can carry it home. It's also completely useless as a weapon.

### The line
Cullen shows them the recovery-and-return section personally, and with pride:

> *"See — we return things to their rightful owners."*

> **GM-only — he means it, and this is the crux.** From Cullen's side the Mask was a clean transaction: House Margaster held a documented prior claim, Lord Meliamne settled it, the statue went to the family. Every one of those facts is true. He is not taunting them and he is not lying, and **he does not know they'd call it a shakedown.** Do not let him defend himself — he hasn't been accused. Do not let him drop the manners. The gap between what they watched and what he sincerely calls it *is* the menace. If a player says "that was extortion," he considers it, genuinely, for one beat — and then explains the writ again, more clearly, in case they misunderstood the procedure.

### The offer
Not a bribe. A **client portfolio**. Look at what they're worth from his side: a gold mine needing a market, a bard with a catalogue and a Neverember invitation pending, a redistribution fund moving treasure at volume, a Privy Council seat, and a manufacturing startup needing freight.

**To Brewbarry:** real terms, better than Lionshield can quote, because Margaster owns the routes now. The factory works if he signs and doesn't if he doesn't.

**To Vukradin:** the return service, openly, as a line on the brochure.

> *"We handle recovery and return of goods to rightful owners. I understand you've an interest in that — and, forgive me, rather more items than time."*

> **GM-only — why this is the sharpest thing anyone has offered him.** His obstacle has never been willingness. It's *logistics*. He carried the statue for ten chapters, failed to deliver it in time, and a woman died over the delay. He holds a necklace he can't place and a sword he can't place. His ethic generates work he has no capacity to do.
>
> Margaster is offering **competence at the one thing he is bad at.** The service works. It should genuinely work — the first transactions must be unambiguously good, and he should feel *relief*.
>
> What he surrenders is not gold. It is **the right to decide who counts as the rightful owner.** He keeps the virtue, the conviction, the public position — and outsources the single determination the virtue depends on.
>
> **The fee is nominal.** Almost token. The relationship is the point, and a service this good at this price is itself the tell for anyone who thinks about it the way the city thinks about the Board Laid Bare.

### The unspoken frame
Never said aloud, never a threat: **Cassian was on one end of this service. The party is being offered the other end.** They have seen a fully worked example of what happens to someone with no arrangement. Now they're invited to have one.

### Branch — they sign
Legitimate and not a gotcha. Brewbarry's dream lives. Vukradin gets his backlog cleared. And they gain **inside access nobody else can get** — genuinely useful. The cost arrives later, when Perrin's forgery surfaces and every item returned through the service becomes suspect. His ledger gets poisoned retroactively.

### Branch — they refuse
Clean hands, and Brewbarry's dream stalls. Which pushes them at the Manifold — the *actual* solution to Linene's problem. Also productive. Cullen accepts the refusal graciously and does not press; he files it. **Do not punish this.**

### Branch — they split (likely)
Brewbarry signs, Vukradin doesn't. Or Vukradin's tempted and Valphine reads the room. **This is the best outcome** — the party fractures along a real seam rather than yours. Same fault line the ch45 Zone of Truth opened when Soma hid the Staff of Bird Calls.

### Branch — they go looking for evidence mid-tour
Reward it, but pay nothing yet. The tags in back carry writ numbers, filing dates, and a repeated clerk's mark. **They cannot prove anything with it today.** That absence is what creates the next need — Drest's desk, Cassian's receipt, Rosene's memory.

### Failure is material
- **Accuse Cullen publicly and badly** → he remains pleasant, the tour ends early, and the offer is withdrawn *without hostility*. Brewbarry loses his shipping and knows exactly who cost him.
- **Steal from the inventory** → they're now criminals inside a documented facility, and the writ machine turns on them with perfect legality.
- **Tip that they suspect the necklace** → Margaster tightens the Perrin handoff and the trail goes cold. They can stop the beads and never prove the house.

### NPC notes
- **Cullen Sharpe** — pleasant to the point of menace, tone of someone explaining a parking fine. Wants: the party as an account. Never raises his voice, never threatens, never panics. **Under pressure he becomes more procedural, not less.**

## Scene 4 — Reporting to Linene (optional, short)

If anyone thinks to send word via sending stone: Linene's shipping didn't break. It was **bought**. Her cheap route was a Commission-operated device at the docks; it went dark seven weeks ago and Margaster stepped into the gap at a margin the Commission never had to charge.

She is not grateful. She is *frightened*, and then furious, and then commercial about it — and she'll ask the right question before the party does: **"Then who broke the device?"**

Leave that hanging. Nobody in this session knows.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Can we just refuse and ship through someone else?"** → Name the options honestly: Tarmalune Trade House (southern docks, long-haul, high price, long lead times) or conventional freight at Lionshield's new rates. Both are *worse*, neither is impossible. Don't close the door — make it expensive.
- **"Can we report Margaster to the authorities?"** → To whom? The Harbor authority stamped the writs. Mayor Soman Galt's office handles civic complaints `[GM-ONLY: aboleth puppet — any inquiry routed there is compromised]`. The Hall of Justice handles high crimes and would need evidence they do not have.
- **"Whose fee is on my Letter of Credit?"** → Levied at the point of routing, per the Harbor authority's schedule. Not the bank's. Go ask them. **That's Drest, and it's the first real lead.**
- **"Can we buy the Manifold / fix it ourselves?"** → The Commission owns it. Nobody official will say why it stopped. This is the real win condition and it's a whole thread — let them find the door, don't open it this session.
- **"Is Cullen lying?"** → No, and that's the point. Every factual statement he makes is true.
- **"Can we take the money and betray them later?"** → Absolutely. Let them. Cullen would expect nothing else and has structured the relationship accordingly.

## Stage directions

- When Corrin's Margaster receipt lands, **stop.** Don't explain it. Let Dave read the line item.
- When Vance asks *"how are you shipping?"* — that's the pivot of the whole session. Ask it plainly, then wait.
- Cullen's *"we return things to their rightful owners"* — deliver it **warmly**, as a man proud of his work. Then move on to the next crate. Don't linger, don't smirk. The party's silence is the scene.
- When the return-service offer lands on Vukradin, **do not have anyone in the party react for him.** Give Dave the beat. If Valphine's player wants to say something, let them choose to.
- If the party splits on signing, **stay out of it entirely.**

## Open decisions

1. **The Letter of Credit device.** Still `[PREP]` — are you converting the skipped mine payday into a certified Neverwinter instrument? It's the cleanest audit trigger, but the party bypassed the mine, so it needs a line of explanation.
2. **Is the Fixer in the warehouse?** Seeding him here makes the handoff colder. Holding him makes his wrongness land by contrast at the parlour.
3. **The nominal fee — how nominal?** Token enough to be a tell, or merely competitive? Determines whether a sharp player smells it now or later.
4. **Does Cullen know about UBT?** Jenna got to the city first and it's exactly the kind of thing she'd mention. If he does, does he read it as a competitor to his service or as *infrastructure* for it?
5. **Ondrel Vance is a new NPC.** Keep, rename, or replace with an existing face.
6. **In-fiction date stamp.** Still unpinned across all Neverwinter docs. Campaign references 1489 DR.

## Do not

- **Do not let Cullen threaten, panic, or drop the manners.** Ever.
- **Do not let the party prove anything this session.** Access yes, evidence no.
- **Do not conflate the true-writ extortion they saw with the forged-writ fraud coming.** That distinction is Perrin's reveal, not this session's.
