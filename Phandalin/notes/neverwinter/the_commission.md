# The Commission — Faction Build Doc

*Scope: everything established about the **Neverwinter Commission** as of ch47, plus a design frame for building it out as a playable faction. The organising lens is **CHOAM from Dune** — the Commission is a combine, not a government, and its politics are share politics.*

## The thesis in one paragraph

The Commission is not a government and not a guild. It is the **commercial apparatus of reconstruction** that outlived reconstruction and discovered it had become the thing that actually governs. It does not levy taxes, raise armies, or pass laws. It grants salvage rights, issues commercial licences, runs the Harbor authority, owns productive assets outright, and pays dividends to shareholders. Every instrument of power it holds is a *business* instrument, which is exactly why nobody can point at the moment it seized anything. The party already works for it, already banks with it, and already owns a slice of it, and until ch47 not one of them knew that.

## What is established `[CANON]`

**Origin.** Founded 1467 DR as the administrative and commercial apparatus of Neverember's *New Neverwinter* reconstruction. (`history_of_neverwinter.md:61`)

**What it controls today (~1495 DR).** Salvage rights, the Harbor authority (a sub-body), commercial licensing, and hinterland expansion. Explicitly *not* a tax authority — a quasi-sovereign commercial entity whose interests may not perfectly align with Neverember's. (`history_of_neverwinter.md:245`)

**The adventurer policy.** Parties are recruited to pacify territory the Commission cannot send an army into, offered tax-free plunder, with the understanding that Commission licensing and claims follow behind them. The Wintershield guards are the administrative arm that recruits and manages them. Said out loud to Vukradin:

> *"Lord Neverember has decided to establish the law of Neverwinter in the hinterland. But to do that, those opposing him must be dealt with. He's decided to use adventurers because he can't bring a real army. Once the adventurers have pacified the area, we will arrive and establish order and the proper laws."* — Wintershield guard, ~1495 DR

The structural logic: adventurers have personal power but no institutional standing, so they can be licensed, taxed, discredited, or eliminated. They are the ideal frontier instrument *because* they are individually powerful and collectively unorganised. **As they succeed they accumulate wealth and reputation, which is when they stop being instruments and start being management problems.** The party crossed that line somewhere around the founding of Universal Basic Treasure.

**It owns Mountain's Toe outright** (ch47, Zoom l.2099). The party holds a *share*. Vukradin's 200 gp at the Counting House was a **dividend**, not a payout:

> *"Somehow I did not know that. So we don't own… I thought we were the owners."*

The mine commission cut is not a tax. It is the Commission asserting commercial jurisdiction over Phandalin, and it is the first formalisation of hinterland enclosure. (`campaign_state.md:287`, `history_of_neverwinter.md:251`)

**The Displacement Manifold.** Built and operated by the Commission at the docks as a commercial service: goods arrive by sea through the Harbor authority, the Manifold distributes them to retail destinations across the region. Lionshield Coster (HQ Yartar) was the primary client. **The Commission monetised both ends — the port and the distribution.** Dark for roughly seven weeks. "Nobody official will say why." Its gnome technician has not been seen since. (`history_of_neverwinter.md:224`, `world_state.md:328`)

**Lord Cassian's standing advice**, repeated in three documents and delivered on screen:

> *"I personally believe that when it comes to the Commission, one should keep one's distance."*

He will not approach them himself. That is precisely why he asked the party to. (`chapter_46_neverwinter_never_a_dull_moment.md:482`)

**The convergence.** The party's income and the sabotage they are investigating share a counterparty. Cassian's warning applies to their own revenue.

## What is established `[GM-ONLY]`

- **The Manifold did not fail.** Aletra Sotorra delivered a treaty ultimatum: sign a planar routing treaty granting KP's faction access to the distribution architecture, or the Manifold stops. The Commission refused rather than cede control of its own infrastructure, and chose to lose Lionshield as a client. They had no idea what tier of actor they were negotiating with. (`history_of_neverwinter.md:226`)
- **They are lying about it.** Publicly it is a maintenance failure, because the truth is that they sacrificed a paying client for politics. They know exactly why it stopped.
- **Lionshield has a real commercial claim** against them and it is unresolved. Yartar knows; Linene does not.
- **The technician is Bimble Nackle** — alive, lower docks, ordinary, *not KP*. Preserve the players' assumption for as long as it will hold.
- **Mayor Soman Galt is an Aboleth Sovereignty puppet**, i.e. a lever inside the Commission's administrative layer that the Commission does not know is there.
- **House Margaster has a relationship with the Harbor authority** and benefits materially from the Manifold staying dark.
- **The Question of the Age.** The Stroudite thesis — that individual mortal action, not institutional authority, determines history — is a problem the Commission cannot suppress without proving and cannot ignore without conceding. Neverember's studio offer to Vukradin is an attempt to buy one of the question's most visible embodiments before it becomes actionable.

## The CHOAM lens `[PREP]`

CHOAM works as a model because it solves the problem this faction has: **how does an organisation with no soldiers, no laws and no throne end up being the thing everyone actually obeys?** Answer: it holds the shares, and the shares are the politics. Directorships are the real currency of the Imperium; the Emperor rules because he holds the controlling bloc, not because of the throne; a House's standing is its share of the combine; and being cut out of the combine is political death without a single blade drawn.

Port the mechanism, not the furniture.

| CHOAM | The Commission | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CHOAM, the universal development combine | The Neverwinter Commission | Commercial body that is functionally sovereign because it owns the productive assets |
| The Emperor's controlling bloc | Neverember | Held it in 1467, diluted by three wars, losing the count and hiding it (Ruling 1) |
| Landsraad Great Houses as shareholders | House Margaster, Lords' Alliance houses, the mine's shareholders | Rivalry is expressed as share position, not war |
| Directorship = real power | A seat, a licence, a salvage right | The dividend is never the point; the *seat* is the point |
| The Spacing Guild's transport monopoly | **The Displacement Manifold** | The Commission built its own Guild — and it just stopped turning |
| Spice — the commodity everything rests on | Hinterland extraction: gold, salvage, licensing revenue | The thing that must keep flowing |
| Arrakis granted as a quasi-fief to be exploited | **Phandalin and the hinterlands** | The gift that is a leash |
| Delisting / share dilution as the weapon | Licence revocation, audit, adverse claim, "review of your standing" | Nobody dies. You just stop being able to trade |
| Kanly, the forms observed while knives are out | Margaster's forged writs, "legal instruments nobody dares test" | War by paperwork is already canon in this campaign |

**The joke that writes itself:** Vukradin, whose entire political programme is Universal Basic Treasure, discovered in ch47 that he is not an owner but a *minority shareholder in an extractive combine*, receiving a dividend. Soma already named it — "the HOA of the land" (`chapter_44_victory_lap.md:408`). That is the CHOAM relationship exactly, and the party arrived at it by accident.

**What CHOAM gives you that the Commission does not have yet:** a Directorate. A room where the shares are counted. Right now the Commission is all periphery — Wintershield guards, Harbor clerks, a counting house, a puppet mayor — and no centre. It needs a table and someone sitting at it before the party can push against it.

## What the Commission actually is `[GM-ONLY]`

*Established by GM ruling, 2026-08-18. Three layers, none of which sees the whole shape of the other two.*

### The core — the Kraken Society

**A mind flayer sits at the centre of the Commission.** The Society did not infiltrate it so much as recognise it: a nameless, faceless, procedural body whose own doctrine is *there is always a department and never a person who chose* does not need to hide an illithid. **It is already shaped like one.** This is the ideal cover organisation, and it was bought into rather than built.

Existing canon this lands on, all of it already written down:

- The party **carries a live illithid-tech psionic beacon** (`planning.md:20`, Echoes Score 0, *beacon active*).
- The Society's objective is relics tied to **Ubbalux**, the mind flayer philosopher who theorised elevating a kraken into godhood by psionic evolution (`docs/tracking/echoes-score.md`). The kraken is real, chained in an oceanic trench with an Elder Brain surgically grafted to its cortex, and its primal brain is the **biological amplifier broadcasting psionic signal across the continent** (`splinter-colony.md`). That is where the mind-manipulation reach comes from, and it is why a Factor in a Neverwinter counting house can be run at all.
- Echoes tier 0–2 is **"too helpful strangers"** and **memory tampering** — people denying conversations that happened.
- **Echo Marks** — tiny hash-mark circles visible only under *Detect Magic* or *See Invisibility*.
- **+1 Echoes for reading, intercepting or stealing correspondence from a Society agent.**

**How they got in: they funded the wars.** See Ruling 7.

**Whose asset: Xeltharuun's, and contested.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

**Xeltharuun, the Network Weaver** — Deep Triumvirate psionic spymaster whose remit is monitoring the official surface proxies and **suppressing chaotic variables that might draw multiversal attention to Toril** (`docs/tracking/splinter-colony.md`). Read the Commission back against that job description: an institution whose entire doctrine is that nothing is ever a decision and nobody ever chose **is a variable-suppression machine**. The Commission is not a front Xeltharuun built. It is an instrument he recognised and bought into.

**The eye is already in the room.** **Serel Mardun** — high-level deep agent embedded in **Lord Neverember's court**, supplying political cover and misdirecting investigations, with Xeltharuun watching directly through their eyes. Neverember chairs the Commission (Ruling 1). Mardun is also `echoes-score.md`'s named tier 0–2 **"too helpful stranger"**, which is precisely the register the Factors run in — the same technique at two altitudes.

**And the Rogues want it.** The Manifold is a **planar routing device**. **Ul'vattix** (Triumvirate, Quarantine Warden) hunts down and destroys high-level planar artifacts to keep KP's lockdown sealed; **Vell'thyk** (Rogue, Excavator) is violently excavating the Drowned Gate for artifacts to shatter it. A working planar engine running commercially at the Neverwinter docks is **a liability to one faction and a prize to the other**. Neverwinter is therefore the surface front of the illithid civil war — and neither Margaster, nor Neverember, nor the party knows there is a war.

**The Schism Reveal, in Neverwinter register.** `planning.md:487` prescribes thralls assassinating thralls under conflicting orders, held for the Leilon tier. The Commission version of the same reveal is **two documents in the identical house hand giving contradictory instructions** — one ordering the Manifold sealed and written off as maintenance, one ordering its routing tables preserved intact. Thralls killing thralls becomes a **filing discrepancy**. Tier 0–2 subtle-whispers material the party cannot decode yet, which detonates properly when they reach Leilon.

**Who turned the Manifold off: Aletra did.** `[RULED]` *(GM, 2026-08-18.)* She said sign or it stops working, they refused, and she stopped it working. No trick, no misattribution — she reached into a Commission asset and switched it off.

**And that is what frightens the Kraken Society.** Not the loss of revenue. **The demonstration.** The Deep Triumvirate's whole doctrine is that KP's quarantine is a gift — an incubator keeping rivals out while they subjugate Toril slowly and safely (`splinter-colony.md`). Then KP's agent walked into their best commercial asset and turned it off to make a point. **They admired the lockdown right up until they felt the hand that built it.** The colony now knows that the power it had been treating as favourable weather can reach inside its holdings at will, and that refusing it has a price.

This makes the Commission's core **afraid**, which is the single most useful thing about it. A patient variable-suppressing intelligence that has just been frightened is an intelligence that starts reaching for anything that might explain what frightened it.

**Sourcing caveat.** `splinter-colony.md` states up front that the Deep Triumvirate, the Rogue Surface Faction, the grafted kraken and KP's multiversal interdiction were **collaboratively woven in conversation rather than derived from campaign sources**, and recommends documenting them independently. Everything above is safe to build on, but it is prep rather than table canon, and hanging the Commission on it makes that structure load-bearing.

### What this does to the Factors

Ruling 2's tells do not change. Their *explanation* does — and the party will reach the wrong one first, because at their level the wrong one is true.

| The tell | What the party will conclude | What is actually true |
|---|---|---|
| Every document in an identical hand | A trained clerical house hand | **One mind writing through many hands.** The hand carries Echo Marks |
| No Factor ever says "I" | Institutional depersonalisation | **There is no I in the chair** |
| A Factor is replaced when the party wins a concession | Reassignment; the office manages its people | **The occupant started thinking for themselves.** A real concession *is* the person surfacing through the control |
| Nobody is ever punished | Nobody decided anything | Correct — and that is precisely why |

**The Factor who says "I" is a person waking up.** The party's win condition against this faction was never defeating it. It is getting one person out.

### Who refused Aletra: the core did

Not a Factor and not a schedule. A mind flayer recognised what she was and refused, because letting KP's faction map the planar routing architecture would expose the Drowned Gate work beneath Leilon. The file records *declined — schedule*. **The bureaucratic non-decision is the camouflage**, and it will hold, because everything the party can verify about it is accurate.

### The middle — a real bureaucracy

Thousands of people doing genuine commercial work, honestly, with no idea. Salvage, licences, harbour, ledgers, dividends. **Most of the Commission is exactly what it appears to be**, which is the only reason the core survives contact with the world. This is not a conspiracy wearing an institution; it is an institution with something living in the middle of it.

**And the value it creates is real.** `[GM ruling]` The Commission is a genuine asset to Neverwinter — it brings in money, and the city is better off for it. **The Kraken Society knows that the Commission's value is that it brings money, and protects that function first; the other jobs are the other mission.** The commercial apparatus is not a cover story that the surveillance work hides behind. It is the primary thing, and the suppression work is parasitic on top of it.

Two consequences the party will eventually have to sit with:

- **They cannot simply burn it down.** Destroying the Commission impoverishes Neverwinter. Defending it means defending an illithid asset. There is no clean side, and both available choices are defensible.
- **The revenue is the pressure point, not the secret.** The core will spend secrecy to protect commerce, because commerce is what it values. **Threaten the money and the thing at the centre moves — and moving is how it becomes visible.** That, and not exposure, is the party's actual weapon.

### What has already happened, off-screen

The party walked into the Counting House in ch47 **carrying a live psionic beacon**, and collected a document. The thing at the core noticed. Nothing needs retconning — it simply means they have been seen, and the Echoes Score has an argument for moving that nobody at the table has made yet.

**Jenna Roscoe's blind spot is now load-bearing.** She dismissed the Kraken Society as a "harmless social club" (`planning.md:214`), the party considers her unreliable, and she is in Neverwinter ahead of them and hostile. Her established character flaw is now a structural vulnerability with a body count attached.

**Bimble Nackle re-reads.** He was mapping the planar architecture from inside and approaching the discovery that the failure was political. If the core is illithid, his notes map something far worse than a commercial dispute — and an ordinary gnome still hiding in the **lower docks** after seven weeks, near the water, is hiding from something more frightening than a noble house.

## Doctrine — how it acts `[PREP]`

**It never does the thing directly.** No assassins, no raids, no proclamations. It grants, licenses, audits, reviews, and declines to renew.

**Its four weapons:**
1. **The licence** — it can make your business legal or not, retroactively.
2. **The claim** — a prior salvage right surfacing after you have already sold the thing.
3. **The audit** — a review of your standing, indefinitely open, quietly fatal to credit.
4. **The dilution** — your share is still yours; there are simply more shares now.

**Its tell:** everything is framed as *administration*. Nothing is ever framed as a decision. There is always a department, a policy, a schedule, and never a person who chose. Compare Valphine's Menzoberranzan read on the banker's excuse — the **cadet house**, where the words are the wall (`campaign_state.md:280`). The Commission is a cadet house that never needed to be founded, because it was always the paperwork.

**Against this party specifically, the doctrine inverts — see Ruling 5.** All four weapons are obstructions, and obstructing these people produces violence. The core's standing policy toward the party is therefore *yes*: fund, licence, praise, open doors, steer. A faction that never once tells Vukradin no is the most dangerous thing he will ever meet, and he will not notice for a long time.

**What it will never do:** admit that the Manifold refusal was a decision. That single admission converts an act of God into an act of policy, and every claim against it — Lionshield's first — becomes collectable.

**Its actual vulnerability, two floors of it:** commercially, it is exposed the way a business is — reputation with clients, not with citizens. A faction that cannot be shamed can still be sued. **Underneath that**, the core's exposure is that the institution's perfect proceduralism is also a perfect tell: an organisation where nobody ever decides anything, ever, is not merely bureaucratic. Somebody eventually notices that the machine has no hands.

## Rulings

Scope and ownership decisions that set what the faction *is*. Numbering is stable; items are marked `[RULED]` as they settle so the list reads as a decision log.

### 1. Neverember and the Commission: which one holds the other? `[RULED]`

**Contested, and slipping.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

He held the controlling bloc when he founded it in 1467. Three wars of borrowing diluted him. He still chairs, still gets the deference, and the count no longer favours him — and he is working hard to keep that from being visible. One antagonist that is quietly becoming two.

**The Manifold refusal was made without him.** It is the first decision that went past the throne, and it is how the GM knows where the count now sits. Neverember does not know that an entity of Aletra's tier came to his docks and was turned away. He is running a city on top of a decision he was not consulted on.

**What this changes, immediately:**

- **The dinner is not a favour being dispensed.** Neverember invited the party because he is short of allies with standing and long on people who can be bought. That is a man recruiting, and he cannot say so.
- **The studio offer re-reads.** Buying the most visible embodiment of the Question of the Age is not an institution absorbing a nuisance — it is a chairman shoring up legitimacy he is losing. He is spending to look solvent.
- **The party's shares stop being a footnote.** In a contested count, a small block held by people nobody has priced is a swing weight. Nobody at the table has worked this out yet, including the party.
- **Cassian's advice acquires a second meaning.** "Keep one's distance" from a body whose own chairman cannot reach it is advice from a man who knows roughly where the count sits and will not say so.

**Answered by Ruling 7:** the lender was the Kraken Society, and the collateral was Commission stock. One mechanism explains both how the core got inside and why the chairman is losing his own count.

### 2. Who sits at the table? `[RULED]`

**A committee with no face — played as a rotating Number Two.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18. The Prisoner / Burn Notice model.)*

The Commission has no representative and never will. What the party meets is **the seat**: whichever officer currently holds the shareholder-facing brief. Different person every time, same brief, same file, same authority, same tea. The institution is continuous; the individual is disposable and knows it.

**The seat's name — the Factor** *(proposal, see sub-decisions)*. A factor is a trading company's resident agent, which makes it period-correct for a commercial combine and useful because it is a job, not a name.

> — *"Who am I speaking with?"*
> — *"The Factor."*
> — *"Yes, but which one?"*
> — *"The Factor."*

**The four rules that make it work:**

1. **The file is continuous; the person is not.** Every new Factor has read everything and opens by proving it. No PC ever explains themselves twice. *"You told Factor Wenn you would consider the licensing. The Commission has considered it."*
2. **Rotation is caused by the party winning.** Extract a real concession and that Factor is gone by the next meeting — not killed, **reassigned**. Promoted, sometimes. The party's victories delete their opponent and change nothing, which is how they learn they are not fighting a person. Nobody is ever punished, and that is worse.
3. **Nobody is ever angry.** No Factor threatens, no Factor is corrupt, no Factor can be bribed or blackmailed, because no Factor is deciding anything. They are all pleasant. They are all sorry about the schedule.
4. **There is a constant, and the party is meant to spot it** — see below.

**The two tells.**

- **The house hand.** Every document the party ever receives from the Commission — writ, dividend note, licence, schedule — is written in an identical clerical hand. Not similar: identical. The house hand is trained. This is physical, player-facing, and discoverable at the table without a roll.
- **No Factor says "I."** It is always *the Commission*, *the office*, *we*, *the schedule*. **A Factor who says "I" has gone off-script**, and that is the crack: the party's win condition against this faction is not defeating a Factor, it is finding the one who slips into first person. That Factor can be talked to. That Factor is also, by definition, about to be rotated out.

**Who refused Aletra: a Factor did.** Not a named person — the seat. That officer rotated out long ago and the file records a declined application with a note about the schedule. There is nobody to hold accountable and nobody who remembers it as a decision. This is the sharpest form of the vulnerability already in the doctrine section: the Commission cannot admit the refusal was a *decision*, and structurally it was not — right up until the party finds the officer who made it. **That officer is findable.** That is the arc.

**Three Factors, in this order** *(names are proposals — run `registry_check` before any of them reaches the table):*

| | Method | Opens with | Leaves the party |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Factor Wenn** | Accommodating. Grants every small request instantly and on the spot. | Congratulating them on the dividend. | Well treated, well liked, and not one inch further forward. |
| **Factor Oster** | Auditing. Opens a review of the party's Phandalin holdings as a courtesy. | *"Two shareholders witnessed a forged Harbor writ and did not report it. The office is sure there is an explanation."* | Defending themselves with the only two pieces of evidence they had. |
| **Factor Aubrecht** | Sympathetic, and sincere. Agrees with every word they say, is genuinely appalled about Elara Meliamne, and can do nothing whatsoever. | Apologising before they have asked for anything. | Unable to be angry at the person in front of them. Most dangerous of the three. |

**What this ruling costs Aurelan Vance and the Harbor authority:** nothing — they stay exactly where they are. Vance is Counting House, i.e. the teller window, not the seat. The Harbor authority remains a sub-body with its own master. Neither is ever the Commission speaking.

**Sub-decisions this opens `[OPEN]`:**

- **(a) Confirm the title.** *Factor* is the proposal. Alternatives if it reads wrong at the table: *the Sitting Member*, *the Duty Director*, *the Office*.
- **(b) Is there a Number One?** In The Prisoner the horror is that Number One is you. The version available here: there is no Director above the Factors, only the count — and eventually **somebody offers the seat to a PC.** For Vukradin, whose entire politics is Universal Basic Treasure, the Factorship is the sharpest temptation in the campaign: the only chair from which he could actually redistribute anything, and taking it makes him the wall. Not settled, and it should not be settled early.

### 3. How many shares does the party actually hold, and in what? `[RULED]`

**The ledger is stale, and someone has been collecting.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18. Settles the open item at `campaign_state.md:289`.)*

The Commission still carries **Don-Jon Raskin as a live shareholder** in Mountain's Toe. His dividend has been drawn every quarter since he died at the mine in chs. 22–23. Somebody signs for it.

**This requires no retcon.** `campaign_state.md:62` says the party holds Don-Jon's share, and that stays true — *among the people who were there*. The party took it by agreement with the dwarves and the wererats. **Nobody told the Commission**, because until ch47 not one of them knew the Commission existed as the owner. So three things are simultaneously true:

- the party holds Don-Jon's share **in fact**,
- the Commission's books hold **Don-Jon**,
- and a third party has been drawing **the actual money**.

The party's real block is therefore **two named holdings, not three** — and they have been quietly robbed for over a year without noticing, by an instrument rather than a thief.

**The signature is the thread, and it is checkable at the table.** Every Commission document is in the trained house hand (Ruling 2). A collection receipt is the one document that is *not* — it carries the collector's own hand. The party can compare two pieces of paper and see it. No roll required, though a roll can tell them what the hand means.

**Why this is worse than a theft.** Don-Jon was murdered at the asset. Zeleen Varnaster lured him there, the Stonetallow dwarves killed him, a carrion crawler ate the evidence, and Zeleen lied about all of it to the party's face (`planning.md:328`). The party found the story suspicious and let it go. If a dead shareholder who stays on the books is an income stream, then **the murder and the ledger are the same fact**, and the party helped paper it over by not pressing.

**Delivery mechanism:** Factor Oster's audit (Ruling 2). The office opens a courtesy review of the party's Phandalin holdings, and the review is the thing that surfaces the discrepancy — which means the party learns they have been robbed *from the mouth of the faction robbing them*, in a room where defending themselves means either defending Zeleen or giving her up.

**Who signs: Zeleen collects, House Margaster forges.** `[RULED]` *(GM, 2026-08-18. See Ruling 11.)*

### 4. House Margaster and the Commission `[RULED]`

**Neither shareholder nor parasite — a hostile stripping operation.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

House Margaster are **cultists of Asmodeus**. They do not want to own the Commission and have no interest in its equity. **They want to take every power the Commission holds and put it under House Margaster.** Transfer of function, not acquisition of shares. Whether the emptied Commission then survives is not a question they care about.

**Why they were locked out until now.** You cannot bribe, blackmail, seduce or suborn a Factor, because a Factor is not deciding anything and is not entirely a person. Every ordinary vector of noble-house infiltration fails against a mind-controlled institution — which is exactly why a house with money, agents and demonic backing had spent years unable to get inside a commercial bureaucracy. **Paperwork is the only attack surface that works**, because an instrument does not care whose hand holds the pen. Their legalism is therefore doubly determined: Asmodean devotion *and* the only thing that functions. **Then they found the one exception — see Ruling 6.**

**To an Asmodean cult the Commission is not a rival — it is a cathedral somebody else built.** An institution made entirely of contracts, claims, licences and binding instruments is consecrated ground in the wrong name. You do not buy a cathedral. You take it and rededicate it. **The forged Harbor writ was never about 200 gp** — it was a demonstration that House Margaster can author the Commission's instruments, which is a claim of authorship over the machine.

**The Manifold failure was the entry.** Aletra's ultimatum, the core's refusal, the Manifold goes dark, regional distribution collapses — and for the first time in its history the Commission has a function it cannot perform. Margaster stepped into a gap that had never existed before. **KP's agent opened the door for an Asmodean cult, and neither faction knows it.**

**How to run them: every scene they win, one function moves.** Salvage adjudication was the first (the forged writ). Regional distribution was the second (the Manifold vacuum). Each subsequent instrument relocates one more power from Commission paper to Margaster paper. Keep the list explicitly — it is a visible, trackable campaign, and the party can watch the Commission get smaller.

**Doc consistency `[OPEN]`:** existing docs describe Margaster's **demonic** connections and a **cambion** link (`docs/Margaster.md`). Asmodeus is a devil, and an Asmodean cult is diabolic rather than demonic. Either the older wording is loose and should be corrected to *infernal/diabolic*, or the house genuinely runs both and that tension is deliberate. **Not silently rewritten** — the cambion line is flagged in `docs/Margaster.md` as player knowledge no character has, so it needs a deliberate call rather than a tidy-up.

### 5. What does the Commission want the party for now? `[RULED]`

**Encourage, co-opt, support — and misdirect.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

**The reasoning, which is the important part: this party answers obstruction with violence.** Every weapon in the doctrine section — licence, claim, audit, dilution — is an obstruction, and using one on these people produces a fight the core does not want and cannot afford to have in public. **A Commission that supports them is something they will accept without examining.** So the core never blocks them. It funds them, licences them, praises them, opens doors, and steers.

**Do not take the pendant.** A live psionic beacon walking into territory the colony cannot observe is the best instrument Xeltharuun has, and the adventurer policy already reads word for word as the mapping program: *parties sent to pacify territory the Commission cannot send armies into* is *carriers sent where the colony cannot see*. Every grant the party receives is telemetry. Nothing hostile ever needs to happen.

**Misdirected toward what:** House Margaster. It costs the core nothing, it is true enough to survive investigation, it aims the party's violence at the core's actual enemy, and it keeps them away from the planar question entirely. **The Commission will help them investigate Elara's murder.** The help is the misdirection.

**This inverts Ruling 2's running order.** Factor Wenn — accommodating, grants everything, concedes nothing structural — is not the opener, he is **the standing policy**. Factor Oster's audit becomes a *mistake*: an officer applying standard doctrine to a non-standard party, producing exactly the escalation the core was avoiding. **Oster is removed for it.** The party watches someone get punished for opposing them and learns precisely the wrong lesson about how safe they are.

**The uncomfortable part `[GM]` — they may be being cultivated for Valphine.** Aletra Sotorra just demonstrated she can reach inside Commission holdings at will, and the colony is frightened of that tier of power. **Valphine Sotorra is Aletra's sister.** A psionic spymaster who has just been frightened by an entity, and who discovers that entity's sibling is walking around his city inside a party he is already funding, does not stop funding that party. See the open item below on whether the core knows.

**It knows, and she is the reason the party is funded.** `[RULED]` *(GM, 2026-08-18.)* Aletra *Sotorra* delivered the ultimatum and the refusal is on file; Valphine *Sotorra* walked into the Counting House in ch47. A psionic spymaster whose entire function is watching surface proxies did not miss two identical surnames in his own refusal record.

**So the whole arrangement exists because of Valphine.** Not because the party is capable, not because Vukradin is charming, not because they returned a statue. Every grant, licence, open door and generous word is an attempt to keep a line open to the entity that reached into the colony's holdings and switched one off.

**The character beat this hands you.** Vukradin believes his politics and his charm won the Commission's favour — that he talked an institution into liking him. He did not. **And Valphine, who is the actual reason, is the last person who will find out.** For a naive optimist who sees no tension in his goals, discovering that the door he thought he opened was held for somebody else is the sharpest available cut, and it costs nothing to set up because it is already true.

**The ask, when it comes, will be a gift.** The core does not want Valphine; it wants Aletra. So eventually the Commission will offer — warmly, at its own expense, framed as kindness — **to help reunite the Sotorra sisters.** To Valphine that is close to irresistible. To Xeltharuun it is a channel to a cosmic-tier power he is frightened of. Nobody has to lie for this to work.

**Three Sotorras, and two factions are holding one each `[GM]`.** Aletra is KP's agent and the reason the Commission funds the party. **Notir Sotorra** — Valphine's missing youngest sister — is recorded in `planning.md` as *"possibly with House Margaster."* Valphine is the third, in the middle, with **Grundar Quartzvein** separately hunting House Sotorra for Brundar's consignment to the Demonweb Pits. Valphine is structurally the most load-bearing PC in the Neverwinter arc and nobody at the table knows it, including her player.

**And it gives the core a wrong model, which is better than a right one.** A frightened spymaster who tracks Sotorras will notice that KP's faction has one *and House Margaster has one*, and will read a pattern that is not there: **that the entity which killed his Manifold and the cult stripping his functions are coordinating through a drow house.** They are not. But that belief is what drives him to cultivate the party harder and fight Margaster more aggressively than either warrants — and it is exposable, which makes it a lever.

### 6. The aberration collision — what the Sovereignty is doing `[RULED]`

**Soman Galt is House Margaster's way in.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

Margaster spent years unable to penetrate the Commission because you cannot buy a Factor (Ruling 4). **Galt is the exception.** A mayor, sitting in the administrative layer, not under illithid control, and bribable by every ordinary means that fails everywhere else in the institution. He is the one lever in the Commission that responds to money.

**Margaster does not know he is an aboleth puppet.** They believe they found a corrupt dwarf official, which is the most reasonable thing in the world to believe about a corrupt dwarf official.

**The Sovereignty does not object.** A noble house walking into their asset is not an intrusion, it is a delivery: it hands them a channel into House Margaster and a live feed of an Asmodean cult's operations, at no cost and no risk. Galt takes the money and it all goes down the water column anyway.

**Three parties, each blind to the others:**

| | Believes it is doing | Actually doing |
|---|---|---|
| **House Margaster** | Buying an official to strip a stubborn bureaucracy | Reporting its entire campaign to the Aboleth Sovereignty, daily |
| **The Sovereignty** | — | Watching a devil cult dismantle an illithid asset for free, and needing to do nothing at all |
| **Xeltharuun's core** | — | Losing functions to an attacker it cannot see, through a door it did not know existed, because Galt was never its person to begin with |

**This is the truer answer to "contested and slipping."** Ruling 1 gave war debt as the mechanism, and that stands. Underneath it: **nobody is in charge of the Commission.** Not Neverember, not the core, not Margaster. The count Neverember is losing is not being lost to a person.

**What it costs the party.** Their route to House Margaster runs through Galt — and Galt is the most dangerous person in Neverwinter to be honest in front of. Not because he will sell them to Margaster, but because everything said in that room goes somewhere neither he nor Margaster controls, and none of them can be warned about it.

**Pacing consequence — settled in Ruling 10.** Originally flagged here: `echoes-score.md` awards **+1 for interacting with a mentally compromised NPC** and **+1 for intercepting a Society agent's correspondence**. Under these rulings, Galt is compromised, every Factor is compromised, and every Commission document is Society correspondence. Run strictly, a single Neverwinter session could move the score several points against a 0–10 scale where 9–10 is *The Depth Revealed*. **This needs a deliberate pacing call before the Commission sees table time** — either the Echoes system fires as written and Neverwinter is where the cosmic horror arrives early, or Commission contact is ruled a separate, slower track. The score is currently 0 with the beacon live.

### 7. The buy-in — how the Kraken Society got inside `[RULED]`

**They funded the wars.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

The Society lent Neverember the money to fight three wars, through commercial fronts, and took **Commission stock as collateral**. That lending *is* the dilution in Ruling 1 — a single mechanism explaining both how the core got inside and why the chairman is quietly losing his own count. Neverember is not corrupt. **He is mortgaged.** Each loan saved his city and cost him a piece of the instrument that made him powerful, and he took every one of them for good reasons.

**And the motive was never profit — it was Xeltharuun's day job.** `splinter-colony.md` states his remit in terms that could not fit better if they had been written for this: he suppresses *"chaotic variables (**like wars or dragon rampages**) that might draw multiversal attention to Toril."*

**So he did not finance wars. He financed their conclusion.** He bankrolled whichever side would end each war fastest, because an ongoing war is precisely the variable he exists to damp. The collateral was a side effect he was pleased to accept.

**Which means the party has already been his instrument, twice, before Neverwinter.** The War of the Giants and the War of the Dragons were won on illithid credit, by people the lender wanted to win. **Nothing needs retconning — the money came from somewhere, and now it has a name.** Their victories were real, their choices were their own, and they were also line items.

**The horror beat, whenever you want it:** there is a ledger. It shows what was paid to conclude each war, and the party is in it — retained, by name, as an expense. They have an entry in a mind flayer's accounts.

**It also re-explains the adventurer policy for the third time.** Pacify territory the Commission cannot send armies into = **suppress variables the colony cannot reach**. The policy was never commercial doctrine wearing a mask. It is variable suppression, expressed as commercial doctrine, and it has been running for thirty years.

**And it sharpens the fear from Ruling 5 into something bitter.** The Triumvirate treats KP's quarantine as a gift. Xeltharuun has spent decades and a fortune keeping the quarantined planet *quiet* — ending wars, damping rampages, suppressing exactly the attention KP would not want. He was serving KP's interests without ever being asked to. **Then KP's own agent walked in and inflicted the largest commercial disruption in the region's history on his best asset.** From inside his head that is not merely a demonstration of power. It is a bargain he thought he was keeping, repaid with an attack — and he still does not know why.

**Was Mountain's Toe war collateral too? No — see Ruling 12.**

### 8. Who killed Elara Meliamne `[RULED]`

**House Margaster did it. The core caused it. No instruction exists anywhere.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

Elara had found the **original Manifold commercial contract in the Harbor authority's records** before she died (`notes/session_prep/20260616_neverwinter_arrival.md`). Xeltharuun ordered no killing and whispered to nobody. **He declined to seal a records query.** A Meliamne pulling the Manifold contract became visible inside a sub-body where House Margaster already has a relationship, Margaster drew its own conclusion about what she threatened, and Margaster acted.

**This is the most Commission murder imaginable: a woman killed by the absence of a filing restriction.** Nobody decided. The doctrine holds all the way down, and for once it is not a cover story — it is literally what happened.

**Cassian's accusation is true**, which is what makes Ruling 5's offer of help so vicious. The Commission will assist the investigation, aim the party squarely at House Margaster, fund it generously, and **conceal itself entirely by telling the truth.** `entity_inventory.md:102` already records Margaster's guilt as flat fact, and it is now correct.

**The investigation will therefore succeed — and end in the wrong place.** The party will prove Margaster killed her, and that success is exactly what buries the core. Cassian gets his case, Cullen gets what is coming to him, and the thing that made it happen is never named, because it did nothing.

**The real thread is not *who killed her*. It is *who told them*.** And that question is answerable, because it is a paperwork question — a records request that was viewed, an access restriction that was never applied, a clerk's marginal note in the house hand. **The route to the core runs through a filing decision, not a murder weapon.** That is the correct shape for this campaign, and the party already has the skill to follow it: they identified two forgeries on sight.

**What Cassian actually asked for turns out to be exact.** He charged them to find *the connection between the Manifold failure and Elara's death*. The connection is real and it is precisely this: the Manifold's failure produced a contract in a records room, the core allowed that to be seen, and she died of it. **His instinct was right and his conclusion was incomplete**, which is the best thing a patron's charge can be.

### 9. Does House Margaster know about the illithids? `[RULED]`

**One person above Cullen knows. Nobody below does.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

**Every Margaster scene played so far stays honest.** Cullen Sharpe's forged Harbor writ, his distribution pitch, his denial of the murder — all performed by a man who believes he is running a commercial racket against a stubborn bureaucracy, because that is what he was told he is doing. Nothing needs reinterpreting.

**The house is running two goals stacked on one operation.** In the field it is a distribution takeover. At the top it is an **eviction** — and to an Asmodean who knows what is living in the Commission, stripping it function by function is not asset-stripping at all. It is cleansing a consecrated building by contract, one instrument at a time.

**This is what explains the risks that look irrational from below.** Killing a noblewoman over a records query makes no commercial sense whatsoever, and Cullen could not justify it if asked. It makes perfect sense as the removal of someone about to publish the existence of the thing they are evicting.

**The party's likeliest path now has a deliberate dead end in it.** They will corner Cullen — the Commission will help them do it (Ruling 5) — and break him, and he will tell them **everything he knows, truthfully and completely, and it will all be commercial.** True, cooperative, and useless. The reveal vector is withheld on purpose, and it sits one level above the man they were aimed at.

### 10. Echoes Score pacing — what Commission contact costs `[RULED]`

**Only anomaly contact ticks the counter.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18.)*

Routine commercial dealing is free. Banking at a counting house is contact with **a bank**, not with the Society's web, and the Echoes Score exists to track inadvertent contact with the web. A licence renewal is not an omen.

### 11. The Don-Jon signature `[RULED]`

**Zeleen Varnaster collects. House Margaster forges the paper.** *(GM ruling, 2026-08-18. Completes Ruling 3.)*

Zeleen took Don-Jon's documents off him before the carrion crawler got the body — the party recovered only the chain and the goggles from the lair, after she had been through him. A wererat gang leader cannot walk a collection through a Commission counting house on her own, so she found somebody who could make paper that clears. **Margaster's forgery apparatus has been servicing a Phandalin client for over a year.**

**There was already a Margaster line into Phandalin, and it runs through the party's own mine.** It predates their arrival in Neverwinter, and it reframes Cullen Sharpe's pitch — when he offered *"the connecting tissue between your finds and the real proprietaires using our patented system,"* he was offering the party the identical service he already sells their mine partner. This is ordinary commercial forgery, squarely Cullen's own level of the house (unlike the illithid layer, Ruling 9), so **he plausibly knows**, which makes it a pressure point on him rather than a mystery.

**And it supplies the motive the party already suspected was missing.** They know Zeleen's account of Don-Jon's death is a lie and never pressed it. The reason it is a lie: he was lured there and killed, and **a dead shareholder who stays on the books is an income stream.** The Whiskered Gang alliance — the mine's entire security arrangement — is built on a murder for profit that the party helped bury by declining to ask a second question.

**The double-cross is personal and provable.** Ruling 3 establishes that the party holds Don-Jon's share *in fact*, by agreement among the people who were there. **Zeleen was one of those people.** She agreed the party held it while drawing on it every quarter.

**The no-skimming clause does not cover this, and that is the joke.** The clause covers the **ore**. It says nothing about the **shareholder register**. Zeleen is not in breach of anything, and the party's own contract is what protects her — instrument-shaped evasion executed by a wererat, in Phandalin, against people who thought they had it papered.

**And Vukradin wrote it.** He is the one who insisted on the no-skimming clause (`planning.md:203`, `:349`, where the transcript garbling *Mr. Bucherton* is Vukradin — see the doc-hygiene note below). The man whose entire politics is that the right instrument, properly drafted, protects people, drafted the instrument that shields the person robbing his own party. **The doc's thesis lands on the PC who most believes in instruments**, and it lands using his own words.

**Cost, per Ruling 10:** following the signature into Commission records is **+1 Echoes**.

## Doc hygiene — the Bucherton phantom `[RESOLVED 2026-08-19]`

**"Mr. Bucherton" is not a person. It is a Zoom garbling of Vukradin**, recorded as such at `notes/vtt_transcription_corrections.md:13` alongside *Vucherton*, *Bucherding*, *Buchert* and ~100 other variants.

**The mechanism was not what it looked like.** The extraction pass got it *right* — all three distill files wrote `Vukradin ("Mr. Bucherton")`, correctly identifying them as one person and annotating the garbling. **The synthesis step then read that annotation and split it into two people.** The smoking gun was `docs/world_state.md:143`:

> *"Dwolin the Grout (gold-seeker); **Mr. Bucherton** (no-skimming clause); **Mr. Vukradin** (interference risk — don't reveal addendum to him)."*

Both listed as separate contacts in a single line. **The disambiguation manufactured the entity** — a textbook *LLM extracts → LLM structures → errors compound* failure, where the helpful parenthetical was the payload.

**Fixed 2026-08-19 — 13 replacements across 12 files:**

- **Root cause (3 files).** Stripped the parenthetical to plain `Vukradin` in `docs/distill/planning_extractions/dossier_extract_038.md`, `docs/distill/npcs/horia_stonetallow.md`, and `docs/distill/npcs/merged_sidecars/horia_stonetallow.new_notes.038.md`. Nothing downstream can re-mint him from these now.
- **The verbatim quote (10 replacements, 9 files).** Horia Stonetallow's line — *"…we have a no-skimming clause that Mr. Bucherton insisted we put in"* — corrected to **Mr. Vukradin** in the bible (`docs/ensemble/chapters.md`, `docs/NeverwinterExpansionismandtheNorth.md`), the `chapter_36` split, and seven `summaries/20260414/` artifacts.
