# Neverwinter Network — GM Reference

*Scope: the House Margaster operation and everything the party will actually touch. This is **not** the full city faction map — the published-module factions (Ashmadai, Thay, Dead Rats, Nashers proper, Aboleths, Many-Starred Cloaks) live in the district references and stay background unless you pull them in.*

Neverwinter runs a legitimate salvage and recovery bureaucracy. House Margaster has captured one desk inside it, and that single desk is enough to intercept goods in transit, manufacture a documented "rightful owner," and charge to complete the return. It is a machine that converts other people's virtue into delivery logistics. Elara Meliamne filed a counter-claim against it and was dead eight days later.

The party arrived in Neverwinter at the end of Ch. 46, **met Cullen Sharpe at the Moonstone Mask**, and is now heading to the counting house. They are carrying two objects the machine wants (the mermaid statue, the Necklace of Fireballs) and one errand that leads straight into it (Linene's supply-chain investigation).

> **Doc currency:** the Moonstone Mask scene ran, but its outcome is not recorded here. Three things this doc cannot resolve on its own — **where the statue landed**, **whether Vukradin heard the distribution pitch himself**, and **whether Elara's death was named in fiction** — materially change several sections below. Marked `[UNRECORDED]` where they bite. Fill them in after the session and the rest of the doc re-derives cleanly.

### The Elara question — resolved, and worth understanding precisely

The VTT for session 2026-04-07 (~46 min) has the exchange in full. Dave asks *"So you're saying that House Marcaster has already killed her?"* and gets *"That, yes, that has passed."* Then:

So: **the players have known since 2026-04-07. The characters were deliberately left ignorant**, by explicit table agreement, so the discovery could land in Neverwinter.

- **Dave wrote the Cassian hook himself.** *"Her next of kin? And they're in Neverwinter"* is Dave's line, and it is the origin of the "locate the mermaid's next of kin" objective in `campaign_state.md`. Cassian introducing himself as Elara's cousin is the answer to a question Dave asked three months ago.
- **Player-level animus toward Margaster has been hot since April** — first reaction was *"fuck them."* When Cullen was pleasant at the Mask, Dave had that loaded and Vukradin, in fiction, had nothing. That gap was the scene working as designed.

## The Margaster Operation

### Cullen Sharpe — House Margaster's Neverwinter face `[CANON — met at the Moonstone Mask, ch47]`

A pleasant man in well-tailored dark green who treats every conversation as a form already half-filled-out. He is not muscle and will never be. His function is to arrive after the fact, produce a document, and make the lawful thing and the stolen thing indistinguishable. The horror of him is that his offer is usually *good* — he solves real problems for people who don't know what he is.

**Origin canon:** House Margaster is a Waterdhavian noble house with documented demonic alliances. They formerly funded the Order of the Gauntlet — the "blood money" allegation against Ser Kaelen. Cullen is their Neverwinter associate, installed to work the salvage bureaucracy rather than the streets.

**What he runs:**
- The Harbor-authority relationship — prior-salvage-claim writs, filed against goods in transit
- The "transfer of goods to rightful owners" service, pitched openly as legitimate logistics
- Distribution routes into the vacuum left by the Displacement Manifold's failure — Lionshield Coster's regional shipping now runs through Margaster hands at a margin the Manifold never had to charge
- Whoever holds the pen at the recovery office (see Drest)

**Personality:** Unflappable to the point of menace. Never raises his voice, never threatens, never drops the manners. Under pressure he becomes *more* procedural, not less. He does not panic — that is the character.

**Quote canon:** *"House Margaster maintains a prior salvage claim on the piece — documented, if you'd like to review the Harbor authority's writ. No need for this to become complicated."*

**What the party knows:** `[KNOWN]` — his name, his face, his employer, and that he is pleasant. `[KNOWN]` House Margaster is hostile and has been intercepting the statue. `[GM-ONLY]` — that he runs the salvage-writ machine rather than merely representing a claim; that Drest's desk exists; that Kaelen's necklace match came off it.

**Elara, in fiction:** `[UNRECORDED]` — whether her death was named at the Mask, and by whom. **Players** have known since 2026-04-07 (see above); **Vukradin** did not walk in knowing. If Cassian said it, this is now `[KNOWN]` and Cullen's pleasantness is retroactively chilling. If nobody said it out loud, Vukradin met a helpful stranger and the party carried the knife.

### Havel Drest — the recovery clerk `[PREP]`

A coward with a ledger, working the Margaster-endowed desk at the Harbor authority. He stamps the writs. He matched the necklace's provenance to Perrin. He is the seam where Kaelen's "recovery office" and the house meet, and he is the single highest-value target in the whole structure — reaching him ties the necklace forgery, the statue claim, and Elara's counter-claim to one desk with one name on it.

**Personality:** Frightened, procedural, will fold fast and completely if isolated from the house. He is not loyal; he is employed.

**Quote canon:** *"The provenance is documented. I don't write the provenance. I file it."*

**What the party knows:** Nothing `[GM-ONLY]`. Kaelen can produce the name if asked where the match came from — that is the first crack.

---

### The Fixer — Margaster's operator at the handoff `[PREP]`

Unnamed by design. Rides shotgun on Perrin's claim to make sure the *beads* reach the house rather than the cause. Treats everyone in the room, Perrin included, as a logistics problem. Will sacrifice him without blinking.

**Personality:** Economical. Never raises his voice either — but where Cullen's calm is bureaucratic, his is operational.

**Quote canon:** *"The case stays with us."*

**Tell to seed:** he knows the necklace has seven beads before it's out of the case, and he wants them *loose*.

### The Apex — UNRESOLVED

Cullen answers to a principal in Waterdeep the party cannot reach this arc. **Deliberately not decided.** Candidates, none committed:

### Node notes

- **Harbor Authority intake** — genuinely legitimate. The party cannot fight "the Harbor authority"; they can only reach the one desk. This distinction is what makes the racket survivable and repeatable.
- **Drest's desk** — the only corrupt node. Break it and the whole structure is documented at once. Guard it accordingly: he is offstage until the party *digs* to him.
- **The manufactured owner** — the elegance of the design is that the front is usually **sincere**. Perrin believes in his claim. That means an honest party interrogating him gets honest answers that are also wrong, and the lie is upstream where nobody is looking.
- **The warehouse** — where a Lionshield-shaped thread and a necklace-shaped thread arrive at the same building from different directions.

## The Counter-Network — levers the party can pull

### Lord Cassian Meliamne — the provenance lever `[PREP]`
Sea elf, Elara's cousin, running a controlled burn under a composed exterior. Has held her anti-Margaster case for eight days and has not been idle. Carries her commercial reach and genealogical records — he can confirm the Alagondar claim is fabricated in one conversation, and he *wants* to, because a second documented Margaster forgery is exactly the case he is building. **Cost:** helping him means stepping inside the legal fight that killed Elara.
**Quote canon:** *"She was twelve when she last held this."*

### Ser Kaelen Thorne — the compromised conduit `[CANON]`
Not a traitor. A dupe. He ran the necklace through his recovery ledger, his contact matched a claimant, and he did not vet it hard **because he wanted it to be true** — brokering a flawless restitution is how he proves to himself that his dirty funding does clean things. He is about to walk Vukradin into a trap in good faith, using the one lever strong enough to move a man who already distrusts him: *return every item to its rightful owner.*
**Turned, his shame is the party's best resource in this city.** Do not let him off with an apology.
**Quote canon:** *"You can put it down, Vukradin. You can actually do the thing you keep saying you want to do."*
**Party knows:** `[KNOWN]` he found the owner and gave them the note. `[SUSPECTED]` he takes Margaster money. `[GM-ONLY]` that those two facts are the same fact.

### Linene Graywind — the lateral door `[CANON]`
Asked the party directly, in ch46, to investigate the Lionshield supply-chain disruption. She does not know why her shipping broke. The chain is: Manifold fails → Lionshield's regional distribution collapses → Margaster steps into the vacated role at a margin the Commission could never match. A party pulling *her* thread arrives at the Margaster warehouse without ever touching the necklace. **This is the cleanest non-obvious entry point and the party already accepted the errand.**

### Jenna Roscoe / Lord's Alliance — hostile wildcard `[CANON]`
In the city ahead of them with a head start after storming out over UBT. She would *love* to learn Margaster is minting Alagondar pretenders — it's exactly the political-stability threat she is tuned for. But she now reads the party as agitators inside Alliance jurisdiction, and Vukradin calls her a "nasty no-good treasure hunter." Bringing her in trades one manager for another.

### Ostavar, the Salvage Archive (Docks) `[PREP]`
Retired half-elf who writes honest provenance cards and notes uncertainty explicitly. The neutral check for a party that wants to verify the necklace without owing Cassian or Jenna anything.

### Madame Rosene, the Driftwood `[canon module, unplayed]`
Leader of the **Greycloaks** — the non-violent Sons of Alagondar wing. Knows Neverwinter's political history better than almost anyone alive and delivers it obliquely to people who buy her drinks. She is the correct person to ask *"is there really an Alagondar branch that survived?"* — and the answer will not be what Perrin's writ says.

### The Harpers, House of a Thousand Faces `[PREP/module]`
Have a file on House Margaster they'd share, cautiously, with a party demonstrably aligned with neither Neverember nor the Sons. **They know Bimble Nackle by name and have been looking for him.** Vetting is slow; the chapter is careful after the double-agent scandal.

## The Fronts — sincere people being used

### Perrin Alagondar `[name CANON, ch46 · everything else PREP]`
A Nasher / Son of Alagondar loyalist who believes he is recovering a royal relic for the restoration. **Thin standalone front, per your call** — a small cell, a cause, and a purse. The Tatters, Horst's Flickering Ember, the Nasher Warehouse, and Arlon Bladeshapper stay background; following Perrin *up* reaches Margaster, not the city's Nasher network. That keeps this a clean two-desk job.

He is not the threat and he is turnable. He reveres the *dynasty* and knows nothing of the *woman* whose tomb it was — ask him about Lady Alagondar herself and he has nothing. He does not know his associate means to walk the seven beads to the house. Show him that and the cause curdles in his hands.

**Quote canon:** *"The cause does not forget who returns what was taken."*

**The purse is real.** Several hundred gold, offered sincerely from cause coffers, not knowing whose money it is. The party *should* be able to take it — they're broke and they earned the find. The sting is learning the source later, not a GM penalty now.

### Bimble Nackle — the Manifold's missing technician `[PREP]`
**GUARDRAIL: do not surface him, and do not connect him to this network.** The players will be told about a small, older, precise gnome who was mapping planar architecture and vanished when the Manifold broke. They will conclude he is KP. He is not. He is an ordinary maintenance technician who got close enough to the real reason the device failed that someone arranged his disappearance — alive, hiding in the lower Docks, delighted when someone finally comes. Keep him in a different box from the necklace and let the party do the wrong connecting.

## Gaps the GM can exploit

The party's ignorance is the introduction mechanism. Specific openings:

- **Kaelen's errand looks unrelated.** It arrives from Phandalin, from the Order, from a man doing them a favor. The party has to connect "Harbor-authority writ" to the statue claim *themselves*. Let the recognition land at the table, ideally on whichever player says it out loud.
- **They accepted Linene's errand without knowing what it opens.** A party that goes looking for a shipping problem finds a warehouse. That's a Margaster approach with no Kaelen and no necklace attached — and it makes Brewbarry's bathrobe business the thread that cracks the case, which is funnier and better.
- **Expert-shopping for the dragon slag.** They need a planar theorist. Every referral in this city passes through someone, and Margaster is very good at being helpfully referred.
- **Jenna got there first.** A city official or herald can greet them with pointed questions about "the treasure redistribution scheme" before they have said a word. That's a free cold-open that tells them the city has already been briefed on them.
- **Boney cracks the case for free.** Nobody has thought to ask the horse. When they do, Perrin's story cannot survive it.

### The counting house — now a closing, not an introduction `[live]`

Brewbarry carries Linene's letter of introduction; the mine payout is redeemable there. Two independent reasons to walk into the building where Rimardo and Corrin are waiting.

**The Cullen meeting inverted this scene, and improved it.** The original design had the bank name Margaster *innocently, before* the party knew what the house was. That's spent. What replaces it is better:

Brewbarry presents his letter. The underwriter asks the only question that matters for a factory — *how are you shipping?* Lionshield's rates just went up; Linene said so herself. There is exactly one outfit doing profitable regional distribution since the Manifold went dark. The bank recommends House Margaster, possibly hands over Cullen's card, and is **not being sinister** — it is correct commercial advice, and they'd be doing their job badly to say otherwise.

And Brewbarry now knows who that is.

His whole premise is atonement — *"My kind has been spreading violence and pain throughout the land for generations. I think I need to turn that around and bring softness to people."* The commercially correct path for that business routes through Margaster. Not as a trap. Just as the market working.

**Give this one to Brewbarry.** Per `party.md` his arc is competence rather than virtue — he keeps trying to be the legend and landing as the cautionary tale. This is the first time the ethical knife is his instead of Vukradin's, and the competent answer is the wrong one.

Meanwhile Rimardo audits Vukradin at the folding table. If Corrin's receipt takes the Margaster layer — now the hot pick, since the man has a face — the fee is already embedded in the routing of the conflict-free instrument. **Vukradin's clean money and Brewbarry's clean business turn out to have the same name on them, in the same room, ten minutes apart, discovered by two different players.** That's the scene; it needs nothing else.

> **UBT sharpened Rimardo's best line for free.** He was written to say *"you have not refused dirty money — you have merely laundered it through paperwork with better penmanship."* Vukradin has since built a fund routing 5% back to the party tax-free — the ch46 summary uses the word *laundered* unprompted. Corrin can produce the subcommittee charter. The prepared insult is now a documented description of something Vukradin did last tenday.

## Open decisions

1. ~~**Does the party already know Elara is dead?**~~ **RESOLVED** — players since 2026-04-07, characters deliberately not. See the canon-status section above for the verbatim exchange.
2. ~~**Is Cullen Sharpe canon?**~~ **RESOLVED** — he is. Met at the Moonstone Mask, ch47. Locked.
3. **Record the Mask outcome** (`[UNRECORDED]`, blocks three sections above): where the statue landed; whether Vukradin heard the distribution pitch himself; whether Elara's death was named in fiction and by whom. Everything downstream re-derives once these are written down.
4. **Kaelen: dupe or complicit?** This doc is built on **dupe**. Complicit is a campaign-altering turn — decide before play, never mid-scene.
5. **Which layer does Corrin's receipt expose?** One clean punchline-clue only. **Margaster fee embedded in the routing** is now the hot pick (the man has a face). Alternatives: the Lord's Alliance/Zhentarim string on the mine money (feeds Orsik and Neverember instead), or KP's unsourceable "optimization fee" (feeds the long game).
6. **The arson target.** What were seven beads *for*? Phandalin (the Carver echo), a Lord's Alliance asset, a Neverember property, or left vague. A target the party loves raises the stakes hard.
7. **The purse figure.** Several hundred gold minimum. Coin (spendable now) or an instrument that must be redeemed (which can itself become a Margaster-string reveal)?
8. **Does Notir Sotorra touch this?** Default: keep her offstage, leave a thread — a name in a ledger, a drow seen at the warehouse — the party can chase later.
9. **Does Cullen appear in person at the necklace handoff, or stay deniable?** Now that they've met him, in-person is cheaper than it was — the menace is already established, so a second appearance costs less than the first would have.
10. **UBT changed the board.** Vukradin arrived having *publicly redistributed treasure.* Margaster's pitch is "let us handle transferring goods to rightful owners" — now either a direct competitor to his own program or a tempting piece of infrastructure for it. Decide which read Cullen takes; he will have heard about UBT from someone (Jenna got there first).
