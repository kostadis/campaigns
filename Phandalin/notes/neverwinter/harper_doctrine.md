# Harper Doctrine — Freedom and the Power to Control

**Status:** Explicit GM rulings, 2026-09-07. Campaign reference for future prep. Read before writing Harper motivations, dialogue, or policy. This campaign's rulings govern over generic Forgotten Realms faction descriptions.

## The governing objective

The Harpers are **not a “good” faction**. They seek to prevent any single dominant power in the North and thwart centralization. Their motivation is freedom, which can align them with good causes; benevolence is not their organizing principle.

The GM's clarification is precise: they regard **“centralized diffuse power”** as the most dangerous—**armies that can control**.

- **Centralized:** authority and direction originate in one center.
- **Diffuse:** the organization can exercise that authority throughout a territory and population, continuously.

“Diffuse” does **not** mean decentralized political authority or many independent adventuring companies. Do not turn this ruling into equal opposition to central government and decentralized power.

## The distinction that matters

**Interpretation for play:** a powerful adventuring company can overcome a concentrated obstacle. An army, supported by its institutions, can occupy territory, maintain garrisons, control routes, and enforce decisions after the encounter ends. One political will acquires lasting reach.

The Harpers therefore can favor adventurers without contradicting their opposition to centralized control. Adventurers are not automatically harmless or approved: judge what continuing control their actions enable. Removing an obstacle and clearing the way for a ruler's permanent occupation can be the same tactical success with different political consequences.

This distinction connects to the GM-authored **Konrad Ulbricht Thann, *Concerning the Employment of Adventurers as an Instrument of Policy*, Working Paper No. 19**: limited political objects, concentrated versus distributed resistance, operational discretion, and the difference between an adventuring success and a durable political settlement. The paper was supplied in conversation; this note preserves the relevant connection, not its full text. No NPC is assumed to have read it, and no player-character possession is established.

## Why they hate the Zhentarim

**Explicit GM ruling, 2026-09-07:** the Zhentarim seek to create diffuse power through economic control held by a concentrated center. This explains the Harpers' hatred of them. Their opposition to centralized power includes economic domination, not only state institutions or armies.

**Interpretation for play:** concentrated ownership or direction can exercise power across many communities through their dependence on supply, transport, credit, and access to markets. These are illustrative mechanisms, not newly established claims about particular Zhentarim holdings. A community may retain its council and formally independent laws while losing the practical ability to refuse whoever controls its economic necessities.

The shared structural danger is **one center acquiring durable power over many people's choices**. Armies can enforce that control through occupation; a concentrated economic organization can enforce it through dependence. Trade, wealth, or ordinary commerce alone are not the objection. The objection is their conversion into sustained domination.

This also explains why a faction's opposition to state regulation need not make it a Harper ally. Removing public constraints can still leave control concentrated in private hands. Do not portray the Harper–Zhentarim conflict merely as good people opposing evil merchants.

**Suggested dialogue — staging, not played canon:**

> “A town can choose its own council and still have to ask the same people for grain, credit, and passage to market. The Zhentarim don't need to appoint the mayor if the mayor cannot afford to refuse them.”

## How to portray them

- Explain their choices through freedom and opposition to domination, rather than assuming the morally nicest outcome is their goal.
- A benevolent ruler is not an adequate answer to the problem of concentrated power. Consider what the office can compel and what happens under a successor.
- Replacing one dominant authority with another does not fulfill their purpose.
- Do not infer unlimited approval of sabotage, cruelty, or chaos. Specific tactics and moral limits still need specific characterization or GM rulings.
- Their own secretive use of information and influence is a real tension. Do not resolve it by inventing accountability institutions or making their commitment to freedom insincere.

**Suggested dialogue — staging, not played canon:**

> “You can get a man out of a guarded room. An army can put a guard in every village and keep him there. That's the difference that concerns us.”

## Current Neverwinter application

**Locked GM facts:**

- Lim is a Harper. The food-smuggling operation undercuts Margaster and the Zhentarim.
- The Harpers have used the sewer route for years, exploiting its mild aversion as protection.
- The planar leak creates that longstanding aversion. The spiders intensify it into the confusion Vukradin experienced. Bimble has fought the spiders to a standstill.
- Defeating the spiders lifts the worst confusion; the underlying leak and mild aversion remain.
- Eight crates supply Lim. The ninth deliberately keeps Bimble alive because the Harpers want to understand the Commission. They have not debriefed him because the spiders prevent access.

**Interpretation for prep:** cheap food gives people an alternative to concentrated control of supply. The protected route permits trade outside official control. Bimble's account may provide leverage to constrain the Commission. Food relief is a real benefit serving a political purpose, not the entirety of Harper ideology.

Their practical familiarity with the aversion does not automatically establish technical knowledge of the leak. Their reaction to a future proposal to close the leak remains unruled.

## Soma's progression and Lim's outdated understanding

**Explicit GM doctrine and character history, clarified 2026-09-07:**

- **Soma begins the adventure as a naturalist. She is now an interventionist.** Preserve both stages of her development.
- **The Harpers naturally align with interventionists.**
- The Harpers regard nature as **the ultimate diffuse power**, and worship of nature as worship of that power. They therefore dislike naturalists who hold nature supreme.
- Lim reacted negatively because she thought Soma was a naturalist. That description matches Soma's starting outlook, but not her current one. Lim's understanding is outdated; do not describe it as an unfounded classification from the beginning.

The timing, experiences, and decisions through which Soma became an interventionist are not specified by this ruling. Do not invent a conversion scene or assign the change to her conversation with Lim.

**Interpretation for play:** Lim is responding to the Soma she remembers. Soma's development has brought her into natural philosophical alignment with the Harpers, although Lim has not caught up. A conversation can reveal that change to Lim; it does not cause the change in Soma. Their concrete disagreement about prices or suppliers can remain even after the outdated assumption is corrected.

Nature supplies a distinct form of diffuse power from centrally commanded armies and concentrated economic control. Do not invent a central political commander for nature to force the examples into identical structures. Interventionists' willingness to intervene rather than accept nature's supremacy supplies common ground with the Harpers; alignment does not require agreement on every tactic.

**Knowledge boundary:** the GM knows both stages of Soma's outlook. Lim does not automatically know about the development, and Marta and Oren do not automatically know her history. Let Soma's player explain what changed and what interventionism means to her.

**Suggested dialogue — staging, not played canon:**

> **Lim, after Soma explains:** “An interventionist? That's not how I remember you. What changed?”

> **Marta:** “Nature has more power over people's lives than any ruler could hope for. Worshipping it doesn't make that power less absolute. Someone willing to intervene has something in common with us.”

Whether Lim deliberately directed Soma toward the ninth crate remains a separate, unruled question. Soma's progression, Lim's outdated understanding, and the Harpers' natural alignment with Soma's current outlook are settled.

## Separate ruling — the party's fame

The party is made famous by **many different people pursuing different interests**, not one secret promoter. The Harpers can seek to use that existing reputation; do not make them the hidden author of it. See [Fame](../../docs/Fame.md).
