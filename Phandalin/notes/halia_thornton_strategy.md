# Halia Thornton — Strategy Doc

GM-only working doc. Created 2026-08-24, against campaign state at ch48.

> **How I am reading her, so you can correct me before you use any of this.** Halia is not a villain and does not think of herself as opposed to the party. She is a Zhentarim officer who has spent years quietly buying a town nobody was watching, and who has just been told from above that her backwater is now strategically vital. She is assessing four people who are about to become the local power, and she has already worked out what each of them is for.
>
> **She has never been played.** Forty-eight chapters, two mentions, both as a name in a Privy Council roster. She has not spoken a line on screen. Everything in this document is unspent.

---

## The Frame (Halia from inside her own head)

She has been at this for eleven years and it was working.

Not dramatically. That was the point. You do not take a town by taking it — you take it by being the office where the paper lives. The Miner's Exchange weighs the ore, pays out the coin, and *registers the claim*, and in the absence of any local lord that last one is the whole game. Every stream, every excavation, every hopeful with a pan and a dream: they all come to her counter, and they all sign her book. Some of them fail. When they fail, someone buys the claim. She has been that someone, patiently, for eleven years, and no one has ever asked her to account for it, because **nobody audits the registrar.**

Then a bard walked into her town.

She wants to be clear with herself about this, because self-deception is how people in her line end up dead: **she does not resent them.** They killed a dragon that would have burned her assets. They broke a criminal network that was taxing her miners. Every single thing they have done has made Phandalin more valuable, and she owns a great deal of Phandalin. On the ledger they are the best thing that has happened to her.

It is only that they keep *rearranging the furniture*. Harbin's privy council was supposed to be her instrument — she was going to be its natural centre, the one member with actual records. Instead there are seven seats and she has one. Then the bard stood on the green and announced that hoarded wealth belongs to the people it was taken from, and the crowd cheered, and Linene Graywind is now drafting the rules for exactly how that will work. **Rules that will, at some point, require someone to open a ledger and count who owns what.**

And now Waterdeep is writing to her. Not the usual quarterly nothing — *urgently*, about roads. Because a noble house in Neverwinter has closed its fist around the port, and suddenly the muddy track that runs past her front door is the only way the North gets fed, and men she has never met have decided her eleven years of patience should conclude this season.

She is not frightened. She is *behind*, which is worse, and she has four extremely useful people to catch up with.

---

## Her Current Position

- **Guildmaster of the Phandalin Miner's Exchange.** Weighing house, payment office, and — the part that matters — the region's unofficial **claims registry**. In the absence of a local lord, her book *is* the title system. `[canon: LMoP]`
- **Seated on the Phandalin Privy Council** since ch16 (Harbin, Toblen, Elmar, Halia, Linene, Adabra, Vukradin). Adabra's seat is effectively vacant, which means the council is six and she is one of them.
- **Covert Zhentarim agent.** Established in `docs/entity_registry.yaml:49`, the DoIP inventory, and four published adventures. *"Working slowly to bring Phandalin under her control."*
- **Sells overland transport.** Per *Storm Lord's Wrath*, the party procures **carts and wagons** at her Exchange — she is already the region's overland logistics node, which is the entire Zhentarim play under Ruling 13.
- **Sells information, at price.** Same source: *"Halia knows about the spies in town, but she won't reveal anything without getting paid at least 50 gp."*
- **Upstream contact:** per *Storm King's Thunder*, if impressed she refers people to **Urlam Stockspool in Triboar** — a Zhentarim node on the inland route.
- **The party has never spoken to her.** She was in the crowd on the green (ch16) and on a roster. That is all.

### The pressure — orders from above `[RULED 2026-08-24]`

House Margaster has taken the **distribution leg out of Neverwinter's port** and deliberately stopped there rather than provoke the Black Network (`notes/neverwinter/the_commission.md` Ruling 13). Which leaves the **High Road** as the only alternative into the North — and the High Road runs Neverwinter → Leilon → past Phandalin.

**Halia's slow, patient, eleven-year project is now a corridor asset with a deadline on it.** Her superiors did not ask whether she was ready. Being accommodated by Margaster told the Zhentarim exactly how large they are considered to be, and they did not enjoy the information.

## Her Secret

**She owns a startling amount of Phandalin, legally, through the registry she herself keeps — and nobody has ever checked.**

Not embezzlement. Not forgery. Failed claims get sold; she buys them; the sale is recorded correctly in a book she is the custodian of. Every individual transaction would survive scrutiny. **What would not survive scrutiny is the total**, and the total exists in exactly one place: her own ledger, which no one has any standing to demand.

**Why this is the lever, and why it is beautiful:** Universal Basic Treasure cannot be implemented without answering the question *who owns what in this town*. Linene's subcommittee is drafting eligibility right now — property owners versus renters, residency thresholds, primary residence, Zone of Truth attestation. **Every single one of those criteria requires opening the claims registry.**

Vukradin invented, in good faith, from a stage, in front of a cheering crowd, the one civic procedure that audits Halia Thornton. He has no idea. **Neither does she, yet** — see Open Decisions.

**Secondary, and separable — the Don-Jon card.** The Commission's ledger still carries Don-Jon Raskin as a live shareholder at Mountain's Toe and Zeleen Varnaster has been drawing his dividend on Margaster-forged paper (Rulings 3 and 11). That forgery clears in Neverwinter. **The underlying claim was registered in Phandalin, in her book.** Whether she has noticed the discrepancy is a GM call; whether she would *sell* it is not — she would, gladly, to whichever party paid best.

## The Optimal Play (For Them)

If you play her as smart as eleven years of not being caught implies:

1. **Do not oppose UBT. Sponsor it.** Fighting it makes her visible, and visibility is the only thing that can kill her. Instead she becomes its most helpful practical friend — the member with the records, generously offering the Exchange's clerical capacity to the subcommittee. **She wants to be the one who administers the audit, not the one who is audited.**

2. **Shape eligibility toward property.** The council already debated owners versus renters. Halia argues it warmly and reasonably: those who have *invested* in Phandalin — bought in, built, taken risk — surely have the strongest claim on its common wealth. It sounds like fairness. It weights the whole system toward the largest quiet landholder in town, and it is an argument Harbin will adopt within a tenday because it sounds solid.

3. **Deliver the corridor using the party, for free.** Her superiors want the Phandalin–Leilon road secured. She has four heroes with a demonstrated record of clearing hinterland at their own expense and taking a lecture as payment. **She does not need to hire them. She needs to make the road their idea.**

4. **Sort them individually, because they are not one thing.** This is her actual method and it is per-person:
   - **Brewbarry — the recruit.** He has a loan, a shipping agent, a bathrobe line, an ambition to be "Neverwinter's godfather," a coin-pouch listening post, and a stated desire for a headband of intellect to improve his spycraft. He is a man who wants to be good at business and is teaching himself the trade badly. She offers him what he is already trying to build. Canonically this ends with a title: **Fang**.
   - **Valphine — the customer, not the recruit.** A militant church with an orc congregation, a new Neverwinter branch, eight sun-starers, and a drow high priestess is going to need to move goods that respectable merchants will not touch. Halia does not care what a Searing Light church requires. She cares that it requires it *reliably*, and that no one else will carry it. (Valphine already sits on four vials of Midnight Tears and a Dust of Disappearance. Halia would not blink.)
   - **Vukradin — approached with a smile, never a knife and never a crooked deal.** `[RULED 2026-08-24]` He is "all about the good," which she does not sneer at; she finds it genuinely useful. **She does not want to corrupt him. She wants to stand next to him** — he is the most famous man in the region and Neverember is about to make this party a power here. And she has a real offer for him: see **The Repatriation Venture** below. Crooked would be a category error with this man, and she can tell within one conversation.
   - **Soma — the problem.** Halia has no read on her and knows it. A tortle who identifies motivated patterns nobody else can see is the exact wrong person to be quietly accumulating in front of. **Halia's play is to be scrupulously honest with Soma about everything except the total.**

5. **Never confront. Become load-bearing.** Carts, wagons, storage, records, clerks, a line of credit, a name in Triboar. By the time anyone wonders about her, unwinding her would mean unwinding the town's logistics.

## The Governing Rule — she does not screw customers `[RULED 2026-08-24]`

**This is the most important line in the document and every scene should obey it.**

Halia Thornton is not a con artist, a swindler, or a trickster. **The service is real. The data is real. The connection is real.** When she says she can find who lost a thing, she can, and she does, and the heir is genuinely reunited with the heirloom, and no one is cheated at any point in the transaction.

**She is not honest because she is good. She is honest because it is a superior business model**, and because a reputation for delivering is the only asset in her trade that compounds. Cheating a customer buys one payday and costs the network. She has watched people do it. She is still here and they are not.

**Why this makes her more dangerous than a crook:**

- **The party cannot catch her at anything**, because there is nothing to catch. Insight comes back clean. Valphine's lie-detection comes back clean. Zone of Truth comes back clean. All of it, every time.
- **Every warning the party might raise against her is an argument against a thing she is not doing.**
- **Her honesty is what buys the position.** She does not need the transaction to be crooked. She needs to be *in* it, keeping the record of it — and an honest transaction gives her that just as well, with none of the risk.

**Play her as a genuinely excellent business partner who is worth having.** If the party deals with her and comes out ahead every single time, that is correct and intended. The cost is not in any deal. The cost is in what she learns, and in who holds the ledger afterward.

## The Repatriation Venture — her offer to Vukradin `[RULED 2026-08-24]`

**The pitch:** a commercial operation that finds the people who lost things and gives the things back. Provenance research, heir-tracing, restitution. Vukradin's entire personal ethic, as a going concern, with a network behind it.

**Why she can actually deliver it, and this is the point:** the Zhentarim move goods across the North. Goods move with paper, paper moves with people, and people talk to factors. **A trade network is already a provenance-tracing apparatus** — it simply has never been pointed at this. She can trace an heirloom to a house, a house to a surviving heir, and produce a name that is *correct*. No forgery is required, and forgery would in fact ruin the product.

**Why it is genuinely good business, honestly:**

- Recovered goods with unclear title are **dead capital.** Nobody can legally sell them. Establishing provenance unlocks the value, and the party is sitting on a pile of exactly this.
- Fees on restitution, finder's commissions, and a service nobody else in the North offers.
- **And the real yield: every restitution is a grateful relationship plus a data point about who owned what.** A repatriation business is an intelligence-gathering operation that people *thank you for running*. That is the Black Network's ideal product, and it requires no dishonesty whatsoever.

### Why this lands on Vukradin like a key in a lock

Everyone keeps offering him the crooked version of this exact service and he keeps having to refuse or, worse, accept without knowing:

- **Ser Kaelen's Order of the Gauntlet recovery office** matched the Necklace of Fireballs to Perrin Alagondar in good faith — **through a Margaster-endowed desk, on a forged provenance** (`planning.md`; Havel Drest). Vukradin handed over seven beads of Fireball on the strength of it and is proud of having done the right thing.
- **Cullen Sharpe pitched him the same product to his face:** *"the connecting tissue between your finds and the real proprietaires using our patented system"* — which, per Ruling 11, is the identical forgery service Zeleen Varnaster already buys.

**So Halia's offer is the honest version of the thing that has been lied to him about twice, and she will out-compete a paladin's office and a devil-cult's fixer on Vukradin's own moral terms, without cheating anyone.** He should be able to verify every claim she makes and find it sound, because it will be.

> **The character cut, and it is the sharpest one available to him.** Vukradin's ideology says *take this deal* — it returns property to rightful owners at scale, which is the thing he says he wants more than anything. His instincts say *don't*, and **his instincts have no argument**, because there is nothing wrong with the deal. There is only something wrong with who profits from it, in a way he cannot articulate and she would find genuinely puzzling.
>
> Soma will articulate it. That is what Soma is for.

## Leilon — doing Phandalin again, faster `[RULED 2026-08-24]`

**Her superiors want to control Leilon's trade.** That is the order, and it is why the letters became urgent.

**Why Leilon and why now:** Leilon sits on the **High Road** at the midpoint of the overland route, and Neverember is rebuilding it because Margaster took his port (Ruling 13). Whoever holds Leilon's commerce holds the middle of the only lane into the North.

**But the real prize is the one Halia personally understands better than anyone in the Zhentarim, because she built it once already:**

**Leilon is a greenfield.** It was destroyed. It is being rebuilt from nothing. There is no established guild, no entrenched merchant house, no local lord with a chancery — **and no claims registry.** Somebody is going to be the office where Leilon's paper lives, and that somebody will own the town in eleven years without ever buying it.

**Halia is not being sent to Leilon to trade. She is being sent to be its Miner's Exchange.** It took her eleven years in Phandalin because nobody was helping. This time she has capital, a network, orders, and a Lord Protector who urgently needs someone to fund a reconstruction he cannot fully pay for. **She could do it in two.**

### And the repatriation venture *is* the Leilon operation

This is the join, and it is completely honest, which is what makes it good.

**Leilon was levelled. Everything in it was lost.** There are heirs, estates, heirlooms and grave goods across that ruin, and every one of them has a rightful owner somewhere who does not know their property survived. A "find the people who lost things" business has **effectively infinite inventory** in a destroyed town.

And establishing who owned what in Leilon before the destruction is, in the most literal possible sense, **compiling Leilon's title register.**

> **Nobody gets cheated and she ends up holding the town's records.** Vukradin returns heirlooms to grateful heirs and it is the best work he has ever done. Halia's clerks write down what was returned, to whom, and from which property. In two years the only complete account of who owns what in Leilon is in her book, assembled by a famous hero, in public, to universal approval. **She will not have told a single lie to get it.**

**Module hook, already canon:** per *Storm Lord's Wrath*, the party procures **carts and wagons at the Phandalin Miner's Exchange** on their way to Leilon. **The module routes their expedition logistics through her by default.** She does not need to engineer a meeting; she needs only to be behind the counter when they come to buy transport, which they will.

## The Optimal Play (Against Them)

The GM should know this so you can give it weight when the party stumbles near it — not so you can steer them there.

1. **Ask who owns Phandalin.** One question, no roll, no combat. It is the whole thing. Anyone with standing on the Privy Council can ask for a registry accounting, and **Vukradin has that standing and has had it since ch16.**

2. **Let Linene ask it.** The party is not in town, and their most load-bearing non-combat NPC is already chairing the subcommittee that needs the answer. Linene asking is better than the party asking — it is procedural, it is her actual job, and it cannot be read as an attack. It also puts Linene in danger, which is the cost.

3. **Publicity is the weapon, and Vukradin is a walking one.** Halia's entire method requires nobody looking. She cannot be shamed into stopping, but she can be *seen*, and being seen ends the eleven years. A man who turns every civic problem into a concert on the town green is the single worst thing that could happen to her, and he does it by accident.

4. **Take the Fang offer and do not answer it.** An accepted offer makes Brewbarry hers. A refused offer closes the channel. **An offer left standing, unanswered, indefinitely, is leverage the party holds over her** — she has exposed a recruitment attempt to people who could publicise it, and she has to keep being useful to them until they decide.

5. **Buy the Don-Jon discrepancy before Margaster realises she has it.** It is for sale. It costs money and nothing else, and it is the proof that Zeleen has been robbing them for a year.

6. **Do not try to out-trade her.** She is better at it, and every transaction makes her more embedded. The party's advantages are fame, standing, and violence — none of which she can price.

## Negotiation Stances

**Concedes readily:** money, carts, storage, credit, the Don-Jon discrepancy, the names of individual claim-holders, information about spies, an introduction to Urlam Stockspool. She will be generous to the point of surprising the party, because generosity is cheap and it buys position.

**Trades:** the corridor for standing. Logistics for access. Information for favours — always priced, never free, because a free answer sets a precedent she cannot afford. **Note the tell: she prices things that a friend would simply tell you.**

**Never gives up:** **the registry.** Not the building, not the business, not her seat — the *book*, and her custody of it. She will trade away individual holdings, whole claims, her own money, and any single ally to remain the person who keeps the records. She would rather be poor and hold the ledger than rich and have it read.

## Contingencies

**If the party asks for a registry accounting** → immediate, cheerful, total cooperation, and the accounting takes *time to prepare properly*. She is not refusing. She is never refusing. The books will be ready, thoroughly, in a tenday or two, and in that window a number of holdings will be legally and correctly transferred to intermediaries. Nothing she does is a crime.

**If Brewbarry accepts Fang** → she has a friend and a spy inside the region's rising power and will be an excellent, genuinely helpful patron for a long time. **Do not have her betray him.** The horror is that the arrangement works, that Brewbarry benefits, and that Vukradin finds out from someone else.

**If Vukradin becomes mayor** *(the Claudien writ — `notes/claudien/fold_in.md`)* → she supports it loudly and immediately, because a mayoralty is a single office and a single office can be worked. She would far rather negotiate with one idealist than with seven councillors and a crowd.

**If Claudien arrives** → **she is the first person in Phandalin to notice, and the first to object, and her reasons are entirely selfish.** A free oracle that answers any question destroys the market for a broker of secrets. See the section below.

**If the party publicly exposes her Zhentarim membership** → she does not deny it, does not flee, and does not retaliate. She reframes: the Black Network is a *trade consortium*, she has brought carts and credit to a town with neither, and — with real feeling — *what exactly has she done to any of them?* This is her strongest ground and she knows it, because the honest answer is "nothing."

**If cornered with no exit** → she gives them the registry, in full, voluntarily, and asks to stay on as its clerk. She would rather be the one who knows where everything is written down than the one who owned it. **She does not fight and she does not run.**

## The Claudien Collision

`notes/claudien/fold_in.md` casts Linene as first petitioner, Merel Voss as Keeper, Boney as First Rememberer, Jenna Roscoe as Visiting Adjunct, Adabra as major convert, Ser Kaelen as The Unconsulted, Harbin as early endorser. **The casting has a principled objector and no self-interested one.** Halia fills it exactly.

**Why she notices first:** Claudien spreads by *successful consultation* — it answers, for free, the questions nobody present can answer. Halia's entire business is being the person who knows what you do not, priced at fifty gold. **She is not morally threatened. She is being undercut**, and a merchant notices a competitor's pricing before anyone notices a miracle.

**And her counter-move is the infection vector.** She is Zhentarim: her response to a superior competing product is not a crusade, it is *acquisition*. Find the owner. Buy it, corner it, or licence it. **But you cannot investigate an oracle that spreads by being consulted without consulting it.** Halia is uniquely equipped to detect the problem and uniquely guaranteed to accelerate it, and she will do it competently, in good faith, for money.

**The party cannot dismiss her and cannot trust her.** She is right about Claudien, earlier than anyone, for reasons that have nothing to do with the town's welfare.

## Voice samples

Calculating, warm, unhurried. **Her tic: she converts things to figures, and she answers a question with a price.** She never threatens, never raises her voice, and is never the first to break a silence.

- *"Sit down, Master Brewbarry. You've been running a business for eleven days and you've already got a shipping agent, a letter of credit and a listening device. That's not bad. That's not good either, yet — but it's not bad."*

- *"Fifty gold and I'll tell you which of them is lying to you. I know that seems steep for a neighbour. But if I gave it away, you'd have no reason to believe it was worth anything."*

- *"Your Lord Vukradin stood on that green and said the treasure belongs to the people it was taken from, and every soul in this town cheered."* — a pause, entirely pleasant — *"Somebody is going to have to work out who those people are. I keep the only book in town that could tell you. I've offered my clerks to the committee. It seemed the least I could do."*

- *(to Valphine)* *"You'll want things that Barthen won't stock and Linene won't carry. I'm not asking what for. That's rather the service."*

- **The line she would never say:** *"I want to run this town."* She has wanted that for eleven years and has never once said it aloud, to anyone, including herself in those words. She says **"the Exchange has become rather central"** instead, and believes it.

## What she would never say

She never threatens, never boasts, and never claims credit. She does not monologue, does not explain her plan, and does not enjoy having power — she enjoys having *position*, which is different and much quieter. She will not lie outright when a true statement will do the same work, because a caught lie is the beginning of being looked at. She does not sneer at Vukradin's idealism and would be genuinely puzzled to be told she should; she finds it useful and, in a way she does not examine, restful. And she will never, under any circumstance, refuse the party something in a way they will remember as a refusal.

## Open decisions (for the user)

1. **Does Halia know the UBT drafting will audit her?** The whole doc pivots on this. **If yes**, she is already moving and her subcommittee helpfulness is a counter-operation. **If no**, she is genuinely enthusiastic about UBT — she likes rules, she keeps the records, this is her element — and she walks into her own destruction cheerfully, which is a much sadder and better story. *My preference is no, and that she works it out on screen.*

2. **Has she noticed the Don-Jon discrepancy?** Determines whether the party can buy it or has to discover it. If she has noticed and sat on it for a year, that is a colder character than the rest of this doc implies.

3. **Does the party know she is Zhentarim?** I found nothing in the bible, summaries or grounding docs — she is listed as "Neutral" in `campaign_state.md` and covert everywhere else. If that is right, the reveal is unspent and it should probably be *her* choosing to disclose it, not them catching her.

4. **How large is "a startling amount of Phandalin"?** Needs a number before anyone opens the book. My instinct is that it should be shocking but not absurd — enough that the town would be angry, not so much that the town is already hers. A third of the registered claims feels right; a majority makes the last 48 chapters read differently in retrospect.

5. **Who wrote to her from above, and are they coming?** The orders-from-above pressure implies a Zhentarim superior with a timetable. `Urlam Stockspool in Triboar` is the canonical upstream contact and is currently just a name. Whether the pressure stays a letter or arrives as a person is a significant escalation choice.

6. **Is Soma's read on her available?** Soma's arc-score trigger is identifying *motivated* patterns. Halia is the most motivated pattern in Phandalin and has been in plain sight for 48 chapters. Whether Soma gets a hit on her — and when — is worth deciding deliberately rather than rolling for. **Note that Soma is now doing double duty:** she is also the only party member positioned to articulate what is wrong with the repatriation venture, which is not that it is crooked but that it is *observed*.

7. **Does Vukradin take the repatriation deal?** Not something to decide in advance — but decide **whether it stays clean if he does.** All the force comes from the deal being genuinely good and *staying* genuinely good. If it later turns out to have been a swindle, the beat collapses into an ordinary betrayal and Halia becomes a much smaller character. *My strong preference: it never goes bad. It works, for years, and the only cost is ever the ledger.*

8. **Does Ser Kaelen learn he has been out-competed by the Zhentarim?** Halia's honest provenance service directly outperforms his Margaster-compromised recovery office, on the exact axis he has been trading on with Vukradin since ch16. That is a live wound in the *"I do not know whether he loves me or is managing me"* thread, and it fires without anyone planning it.

9. **How far along is she in Leilon when the party arrives?** Neverember urgently needs the town rebuilt and cannot fully fund it. If Zhentarim capital is already underwriting part of the reconstruction, the party opens *Storm Lord's Wrath* to find their Phandalin neighbour holding paper on the town they were sent to save. That may be too fast — worth setting her starting position deliberately before the tier opens.
