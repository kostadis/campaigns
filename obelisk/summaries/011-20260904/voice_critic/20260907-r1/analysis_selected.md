## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 1

The faint smell of smoke finds us before the cave does.

We follow it up a rugged ridge on the lower slopes of Wyvern Tor. From the ridge I can see the bottom of a ravine about fifty yards away. There is a cave mouth there, and twenty yards outside it a single bugbear keeps watch beside a boulder.

One cave. One visible guard. Enough distance for my shortbow.

“So we can see only one bugbear, standing over there.”

I study the line between us, then retreat to where Pip, Sister Maela, and Veyra are waiting.

“Okay, guys, so I see a bugbear… at the ravine? I was thinking I could use my shortbow and take that guy out. But he's kind of far, and I think I'll be able to make it. What do you guys want to do? Do you have any better idea to tackle the situation?”

“Well, the first thing we should do is check to see if he's doing anything,” Pip says.

“What do you mean, is doing anything?”

“Well, is he the kind of guard that spends his time thinking hard, or is he the kind of guard that—you know, what kind of guard is he?”

“To explore that, you need to go close to him, and I think it's a bad idea going close to him.”

Pip looks at me. “You can sneak, right?”

“I can sneak, but, well, yeah, yeah, I guess you're right. Okay, I can go as close as possible. Let me try.”

I go alone.

The ground gives me enough stone and broken cover to approach without showing myself. When I get close, I see that the bugbear has one good eye. The other is covered by a milky film. He leans on a salvaged pike, a long polearm like a spear, and watches the ravine with the expression of someone who has been assigned a job he does not want.

He is not alert. He is bored.

I cannot see past him into the cave. It is too dark from here. The smoke is stronger, though, and it is coming from inside. A cookfire means someone else is there. How many, I cannot price yet.

The others begin moving closer. Something shifts behind me—stone, brush, a foot where it should not be—and the bugbear straightens. He brings the pike forward and advances south a few paces, searching for whatever he heard. He does not know where we are. He only knows that something is wrong.

I send out my Mage Hand.

A stone strikes rock behind him. He turns. I consider making another sound to the east, then another to the west, until he calls for help.

“I want him to call for help,” I tell the others when I can make myself heard. “Not everyone, like, you know, I want them to at least call another bugbear, or maybe two.”

Pip does not like this calculation. An unknown cave does not promise only one or two reinforcements because that would be convenient for us.

“You haven't led us wrong yet, Zenvon,” he says, “but it's much better to silently kill the sentry. It might be more than two.”

He is right about the number we do not have. He is also too far away to reach the bugbear quickly if we begin now. If I bring the cave out, the advantage of one isolated guard disappears.

“I mean, it makes sense, but I'm thinking if we can kill him without alerting others. Okay, yeah, I guess, I guess it doesn't matter either way. I feel using the Mage Hand is more effort. For the same output.”

The bugbear remains south of his post, pike ready, looking over his shoulder. I cannot tell whether he is frightened, cautious, or simply doing the minimum required by his assignment.

“No, he's gonna stay there, wherever he is. But he just has the back turned towards Pip.”

He is already suspicious of the south. So I revise.

“Okay, so when he suspects there is something towards the south behind the rock, I throw a stone using Mage Hand from the north, and he turns his back around, and then I would want Pip to move into the ridge, sneak, and stay in position there.”

The pike stays in front of him. His good eye searches south, then cuts back over his shoulder. I watch for some further indication of what he will do.

There is none.

“Tricky here.”
## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 2

The guard will follow something he believes he can handle. A noise is uncertain. A lost traveler is not.

“Okay, I want to use Veyra as a bait.”

Veyra looks at me.

“Because she… she looks like a… sorceress, like, you know, like a student? Right, so I want Veyra to step out into the open so that the bugbear can notice her and walk a little forward.”

I consider having her call for help. That would improve the performance and inform everyone inside the cave that we are here.

“No. I think she just stands confused, not knowing what to do. I'll attack from the back, and Pip can attack from the front.”

I have thought a lot about this.

Veyra picks up her notebook. She studies the bugbear, measures the space she will need to avoid him, and steps into view. She says nothing. She stands in the open with her book and looks like an academic who has taken one incorrect turn and is now trying to determine which fact betrayed her.

The bugbear sees her and growls. He takes a cautious step forward.

“Just a tourist,” he mutters.

He says it as though a tourist is a kind of lunch.

Veyra answers with Magic Missile.

The bolts strike him before he can react. He surges forward and finally sees the rest of the arrangement: Pip ahead, Sister Maela closing, Veyra no longer pretending to be harmless.

I loose my arrow from concealment.

It misses.

I move along the ridge toward the others and disappear behind cover again. The bugbear is still standing, and the cave is still close enough for one shout to ruin the whole plan.

“We can't let him get close—we can't let him warn the other guys,” Sister Maela shouts.

She marches directly at him and smashes him with her mace.

“This is how it's done.”

Veyra casts Magic Missile again. The second burst hits harder than the first. Pip closes with Talon and finishes the sentry before he can reach the cave or raise an alarm.

The bugbear falls beside the pike. His blind eye still has the same milky film. It had nothing to do with the fight. It is simply there.

“I feel a little bit sad for the bugbear.”

Sister Maela looks down at him. “It's always a good thing to feel sad about killing one of the creatures in this world, but—it was either us or him.”

“But… I feel sad because he was blind in one eye.”

“Yeah,” Veyra says, “but I was his lunch.”

“I get it, that's okay.”

Sister Maela studies me. “That's very… it's very noble of you to thoughtfully care about the bugbear. That is to your credit.”

This is more attention than I wanted for the observation.

“Oh, well, okay, let's move on. Like, what can we do now?”

“Well, if we leave the body here and somebody comes out, they might notice,” Sister Maela says. “All our effort to get some kind of surprise might go to waste.”

That is practical.

“Okay, let's hide the body in the bushes.”

We carry him away from the cave mouth and push him beneath the nearby brush. The pike goes with him. When we step back, the sentry post is empty and the cave has not yet noticed.
## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 3

I still have a potion of invisibility. I do not use it.

The cookfire inside the cave gives us enough light to inspect the room from the entrance. I peer around the stone and count.

Four bugbears. One ogre. One orc.

The ogre is enormous even beside the bugbears. I look for anything else—another passage, a prepared defense, some detail that makes the numbers better. I find nothing. Sister Maela takes her turn looking and finds no more than I do.

Then Pip studies the cave.

“You know what's interesting about this group?” he says. “The orc.”

The orc sits beside the fire. The others are positioned around him, and when he speaks, they listen. This is not what the sizes suggest.

“He's the weakest of all of these characters, and yet he seems to be the only one that has any authority here,” Pip says. “Like, normally, an ogre's in charge. So I don't know, but this seems like a very interesting situation.”

“How does he confirm that the orc is in authority here?”

“Look, Zenvon. In the military, sometimes most of these creatures respond to whoever's the biggest, so normally it would be the ogre in charge. An orc would be bringing them food left, right, and center. But if you look inside, he's the one sitting next to the fire. And everybody's sort of listening to him.”

I look again. The ogre has the body to control the cave. The orc has the seat by the fire.

“Okay, that's a brilliant observation.”

“So, I'm just gonna tell you: that tells me he's the brains of the operation.”

One orc changes the value of the whole group.

Pip continues carefully. “So… you know, I don't want to tell you how to do your job. My experience is not with these kinds of groups, but in the army, if you kill the leader in this kind of group—it's kind of a mob, kind of a gang—if you kill the gang boss, everybody else suddenly doesn't want to fight anymore.”

“That makes our job interesting.”

“I just wanna throw that out there, boss. Or, you know, we could run in there and try to kill them all. Last time we faced four bugbears, we had to run for our lives, so I'm not entirely certain that's something we want to do.”

Four bugbears drove us out before. This cave has four bugbears, an ogre, and the orc who has somehow made all of them listen.

“Well, you make a point—that's pretty valid. So, what do you suggest? Do you suggest to kill the orc from this far, or do you wanna confront him? Because if we confront him, like, he's gonna… how about if he alerts the bugbears and the ogre?”

Sister Maela looks carefully past us into the cave.

“Well, let me have a look at the situation.”
## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 4

We need to reach the orc before six creatures become one fight.

“Okay, I have a weird idea. I want to create an illusion of the guarding bugbear, and then all four of us walk behind him. Without being seen. To the orc.”

The dead sentry would be expected here. If the creatures inside glance toward the entrance, they see their guard. We use him twice: once as a body in the bushes, once as an explanation for why nothing is wrong.

The illusion cannot walk, which removes the convenient part of the idea. It can remain near the entrance and conceal where we gather before the charge.

“We can hide behind it, and charge.”

The image appears in the firelight: the sentry back at his post, occupying enough of the cave mouth to interrupt the view beyond it. Behind the false bugbear, we close together and prepare to rush the orc.

“Okay, perfect. Awesome. Let's do that.”

Sister Maela and I move first.

We break from behind the illusion and cross the space before the cave understands what has entered it. Pip and Veyra follow, but the sudden rush leaves them half a moment behind. The orc turns from his place beside the fire.

I strike him with my shortsword, then cut him with the scimitar. Both blades land. Sister Maela swings and misses, and for one short moment the orc remains alive with the entire cave beginning to rise around us.

Then I am on him again.

My shortsword catches Brughor Axe-Biter and knocks him off balance. While he reaches to recover, I bring the scimitar across his throat.

His hands go to his neck. Blood spills between his fingers, and he drops beside the fire.

The nearest bugbear watches him fall.

“I'm outta here.”

He turns and runs for the cave mouth.

“Why is he running, like, outside, or, like, into the cave somewhere?”

Outside. He wants no further part of this place.

“What about the other bugbears? Have they noticed this?”

They have. All four watched their boss die. The calculation Pip described is happening in front of us. The bugbears look toward the exit. Gog looks toward us.

“So, all of them, including the ogre?”

The bugbears want out. The ogre wants to kill us. I cannot find anything more complicated in their faces than that.

“Mmm… okay, I'll let the bugbears run away then.”

They are still armed and still dangerous, but attacking them buys us a larger fight for no return.

“Yeah, just fight with the ogre.”

The first bugbear runs past Pip and Veyra. They let him go. Veyra almost strikes him anyway, but Pip reminds her of the plan before she turns the retreat into another engagement.

Sister Maela points toward the exit. “If you want to live, be somewhere else.”

The remaining bugbears understand. They move around her rather than through her and flee into the ravine.

Gog calls them cowards. He does not follow. He stands over Brughor Axe-Biter’s body, furious and devastated, and condemns us for killing the orc he loved.

That explains why he stays.

The others served a boss. Gog has lost someone.
## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 5

Sister Maela calls down Sacred Flame. It falls over Gog’s head, and the ogre howls.

“Me Gog. You kill Brughor. Me eat you alive.”

Another bugbear escapes behind him. Then another. Each one reaches the cave mouth and keeps going. All four abandon an ogre who looks strong enough to break any of them in half. They should have been able to make this a much worse fight.

Something about the arrangement is still missing. We do not have time to find it.

Pip runs into the cave with Talon raised.

“Please, baby, don't let me miss.”

The sword hits Gog. Pip swings again and misses.

Gog raises his greatclub and brings it down at him. Pip gets clear. The club strikes nothing. The final bugbear decides that this is not the time or place for bravery and disappears outside.

Now it is only Gog.

Veyra sends a Fire Bolt into him. It lands with almost no effect.

“Oh, shit. Okay. It's such a huge creature, like…”

I use True Strike for the first time, choose my opening, and drive the shortsword into him. The scimitar follows. It cuts deep, but Gog remains upright. Sister Maela calls Sacred Flame again and he resists it. Pip attacks and misses. Gog swings his club and misses again.

The fight becomes a collection of almosts around one very large creature.

Veyra turns toward me. “Hey, boss, should I use my last spell?”

“Go ahead, make it count.”

Magic Missile crosses the cave and pounds into Gog. He staggers. The rage remains, but his body is near its limit.

“Before I kill him, I want to get some information from him, if I can.”

I hold my weapons ready and step close enough to make him focus on me.

“Gog, yes, yes. Hey, Gog, listen. We did not intend to hurt you or your boss, but we had to help our friend Hamun Kost. Like, and also protect the land from all the, you know, threats and the robberies that you guys have been doing. I just wanted to know before, you know, we finished the business, like, why are you doing this, and do you know anything about Hamun Kost? Tell us what has happened in the past.”

Gog looks at me.

“You killed Brughor! Brughor!”

He says the name again. The anger breaks under it.

“Brughor say… Brughor…” He pauses. “Brughor…”

Then he begins crying.

The relationship between them is plain now. This is not an ogre grieving only because his commander has fallen. Brughor Axe-Biter was his companion.

“Okay.”

“Brughor say attack.”

That is his answer. Brughor ordered the attacks, so Gog attacked. Hamun Kost’s name means nothing to him. If Gog has seen the wizard, he cannot connect the name without a description.

“Okay. Oh, okay. Interesting.”

There is no account of the raids beyond Brughor’s orders. There is no information about Hamun. There is no additional transaction available here.

“So Brughor says they attack, and they attack, that's it, okay. So this guy, he doesn't know anything. Fine, kill him. I will.”

Sister Maela nods. “Sounds good to me. I never liked ogres.”

Her next spell fails against him. Pip steps in with Talon and kills Gog.

I clap.

“Yeah, good job, Sister Maela.”

“That was Pip,” she tells me.

“Oh, that was Pip, yes, sorry.”

Gog falls near Brughor. The cave becomes quiet.

Sister Maela looks at me. “Dude, you're cold.”

“I have to be sometimes, you know?”

“You are really cold, man.”

“Did you just forget that I was feeling bad for killing the bugbear who was blind?”

She pauses.

“I didn't say you weren't kind, but that was cold. You try to negotiate with him, decide he has nothing to add, and then it's just, ‘Kill him’?”

“Yes.”

“Personally, I wouldn't have wasted time on the negotiation. I would have just killed him, but, you know…”

Veyra is pleased with how it ends.

I do not discuss it further.
## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 6

They robbed travelers. Therefore there should be treasure.

“Yeah, of course, they robbed a lot of people, so there must be a lot of treasures around.”

All four of us spread through the cave. Pip searches near the possessions around the fire. Sister Maela and Veyra take the edges. I examine the walls and find a strange charcoal drawing.

From a distance it is only a figure. Closer, it resembles a goblin with something shaped like a crystal jutting from its skull. The proportions are wrong, but I cannot tell whether the subject is wrong or the artist is bad.

“Black crystal?”

There is no color except charcoal.

“So the crystal, is that real, or is that also drawn in charcoal?”

It is all drawn. No real crystal is embedded in the wall.

“Oh, I see, okay, okay.”

I call Veyra over.

“Come look. I see a drawing here on the wall. Does this figure seem familiar from one of the books that you have read?”

Her blue crystal flares faintly.

“Ha,” she says. “This is the third such strange goblin we've seen. Remember what Sildar told us?”

“No.”

She takes out her notes, because Veyra keeps what the rest of us allow to leave our heads. Sildar’s account described a strange goblin with an elongated head during the Cragmaw ambush. The exact connection is unclear. Another line preserves what the creature said: *You're not what Ruxithid wants.*

Ruxithid the Chosen.

When Veyra wrote that name before, her crystal flared. Now it has reacted to the charcoal figure.

Before we can decide what that means, Pip calls from elsewhere in the cave.

“Well, that's interesting.”

He is not looking at the drawing. He has found an unlocked chest.

“Well, he's a warrior, so… yes, right.”

Pip opens it and looks offended.

“Well, thank God. It's a good thing Harbin Wester was gonna give us some money.”

“Yeah.”

“Look at this. This is a disgrace. What kind of bandit only has 180 silver pieces, 15 electrum pieces, and three bottles of perfume?”

The three bottles are worth five gold each. The total is not nothing. It is also not what I expect from a group that has been raiding the region.

“That seems odd for people who have robbed—for, you know, for bandits who have robbed a lot of travelers.”

“You do remember that nobody comes this way, right?” Veyra says.

“Right, but these guys, I guess they… they attack all the travelers on the highway, I suppose. This is just their hideout.”

She pauses.

“That's a really good connection! So, hear me out. I've got this crazy idea.”

“Go on.”

“It's all connected. The bugbears, the strange goblins, the crystals… my mentor…”

She takes out paper and begins drawing. One clue points to another. The goblins connect to crystals. The bugbears connect to goblins. Her mentor enters the arrangement. Arrows multiply until the diagram has more confidence than evidence.

“Oh, wow, okay. Yeah, yeah, the detective thing.”

Sister Maela examines it.

“I think you're missing an arrow.”

Veyra freezes. “Where?”

Sister Maela laughs. Veyra immediately begins searching the diagram for the missing connection.

“Oh, shit. Right.”

I study the page. I cannot determine whether I am looking at real connections or Veyra arranging unrelated things until they agree with her.

“What's the final conclusion of your connection?”

“I don't know, but it all points to somebody in charge doing something awful.”

This is not a conclusion I can use, but we have two places to ask.

“Well, I guess Hamun can give us more information about that, and I guess things are lying in Cragmaw Castle. And that's where we can find our answers.”

“Exactly! That's the arrow that's missing.”

Veyra draws a small castle and redirects all the arrows toward it.

“That's where we have to go.”

She shows Sister Maela the completed diagram with the satisfaction of someone who has recovered a lost proof. Sister Maela closes her eyes and says a small prayer. It appears to help her avoid saying what she thinks.

“So, do you wanna head back to Hamun and then get the information about Cragmaw Castle?”

We still divide the treasure before leaving. Pip killed Gog, and I am in a happy mood.

“Before we leave, Pip, since you killed the ogre, I wanna give you half of the silver pieces, and one of the vials of the perfume.”

“Boss?” Veyra says. “I thought this was a group where we split things evenly.”

“Sure, but the ogre was very big, and he got the last kill, so I thought, you know, maybe… It's not that you guys will not get anything, but the next, you know, maybe whoever gets the last kill.”

“I'm just in a happy mood, so…”

## Zenvon Forepot — Scene 7

Harbin Wester owes us one hundred and twenty gold when we return to Phandalin. Hamun Kost owes us information now.

We go first to Old Owl Well.

His zombies are still there. I call for Hamun without calling loudly enough to make all of them interested in me.

One of the undead wears the remains of a Red Wizard’s clothing. Another looks as though he served as a guard. The rest have the clothes and hands of peasants. They may have been dead for years and brought here from somewhere else. What matters is that Hamun has made servants of them.

He emerges delighted.

“Tell me, Zenvon, what news do you have? Tell me only good news.”

“Well, Hamun, the good news is the threat has been extinguished at Wyvern Tor. We killed the boss, and the ogre, and the bugbears ran away.”

“Wonderful! Wonderful, wonderful. And if I recall, I promised you a meal.”

“Yes, and also some information.”

“Yes, well, it's uncivilized to not have the meal first.”

“Sure, yeah.”

He claps his hands.

The meal is arranged beside a fire, with a proper table and good food. The expense is not only in what we eat. It is in the presentation, the serving, and the comfort Hamun has built around himself in the ruins.

The Red Wizard zombie waits on us.

“Great.”

“Please, please enjoy the food,” Hamun says. “It is very well cooked. My apprentice has been serving me well—perhaps a little better in death than he did. My apprentice Warden is serving me so much better in death than he served me in life.”

The waiter is Warden Holl, Hamun’s former apprentice.

“Who was he before? Was he your servant as well?”

“My apprentice,” Hamun reminds me.

“Right, just confirming.”

A dead apprentice serves dinner while the living wizard smiles beside the fire. I do not trust Hamun. It may be because he is a Red Wizard. It may be because one of his dead colleagues is carrying the plates.

It is still business.

He asks what happened at Wyvern Tor, so I give him the sequence.

“The party was in a cave, and then we sneakily attacked the sentry, and then took out the boss first, and then their entire group collapsed.”

I tell him about the bugbears fleeing and Gog remaining. Then I mention the thing that did not fit with the rest.

“But there was something strange that we found on one of the cave walls. There was a figure of a goblin drawn in charcoal, but there was, you know, a crystal-shaped thing on his head. That was strange to me. Do you happen to know anything about that?”

Hamun stops being cheerful.

His hands drop. The relaxed host disappears, and for a moment I see the wizard underneath the dinner.

“I want you to say exactly what you saw. Again.”

“Well… Veyra can give you the exact details. Veyra, would you like to tell what you saw on the wall?”

She explains the charcoal image and the other strange goblin signs we have encountered. Hamun listens without interrupting.

“Fascinating,” he says. “Very interesting. And you said, Veyra, you've seen this before.”

“Yes.”

“This is extremely interesting information.” He pauses. “I don't know what it means.”

I cannot decide whether this is true. His face has returned to something controlled.

“Well, it would seem that my research in this area may actually prove to be very valuable.”

“I wonder if you don't mind, could you explain me in simpler terms? Is this something we should be worried about?”

“So, what do you know about this crystal?” Hamun asks. “And these black stones?”

Veyra draws breath. She is prepared to give him every fact, every theory, and probably the whole diagram.

“No, no, actually, no.”

She stops.

“No, we are not aware of those things, but on the way to Phandalin, we saw a black crystal somewhere on the way, and then it disappeared.”

That is enough information to watch him. I see nothing I can price. Pip does. Hamun’s interest rises for a moment at the mention of the black obelisk, then he hides it again.

“And he hides it again?” I ask quietly.

Pip confirms it.

Hamun turns toward him. “Well, let me say something for you, my dear boy. I happen to be an expert archaeologist. And my expertise happens to be the Netheril Empire. I have more than a passing knowledge of Netheril. The language of the ancients.”

He points to Pip’s sword.

“For example, you see that sword you have?”

“Yes,” I say. “It's, you know, forged with diamonds and all the other precious stones.”

“It once belonged to a great knight named Aldith Tresendar, known as the Black Hawk.”

“Tresendar, the name seems familiar.”

Tresendar Manor.

“Tresendar Manor, yes. And Aldith Tresendar, okay.”

“As you can tell, I have made a bit of a habit of learning everything there is to know about this area,” Hamun says. “So, if you ever find any Netheril artifact, or need any information, just come to Hamun.”

“Sure, but why was he called the Black Hawk? I have that question. Is it because he had any of the black stones or crystals?”

“No, no, no. He was known as the Black Hawk for other reasons. He was known as the Black Hawk because he wore black armor and had a habit of wearing a cape.”

“See, okay, that's interesting.”

Hamun describes Aldith Tresendar arriving in black armor, his cape billowing, Talon in his hand.

Pip looks down at the sword. “I just hope, you know, to keep up to his name when I use this sword. In combat.”

Hamun smiles. “Yes, Pip, I hope that for you too. And as I said, anything you find related to the Netherese, consider me your expert.”

“Sure, will do.”

This offer improves nothing. Hamun was excited by the obelisk and hid it. He says he does not understand the charcoal drawing. He is an expert in Netheril who wants us to bring him Netherese artifacts.

I still do not trust him.

Veyra’s blue crystal remains concealed. Hamun does not know she carries it. She sits at the table looking as though a hundred billion questions are pressing against her teeth, but she respects the decision and says none of them.

The meal ends with Hamun knowing more about what we found than he did before.

We still do not know the route to Cragmaw Castle.
