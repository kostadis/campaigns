# Chapter 11 Look who came to dinner

Date: 2026-09-05

## Summary
The party crested the rugged ridge of Wyvern Tor with the faint smell of cookfire smoke hanging in the cool air. At the bottom of a rocky ravine, a lone bugbear leaned on a long pike beside a cave mouth, his milky, half-blind eye scanning the terrain with the bored disinterest of a sentry who had long since stopped expecting trouble. Zenvon crept silently down the slope to observe him, noting the creature's inattentiveness before slipping back to share his findings with Pip, Sister Maela, and Veyra.

The party hatched a layered plan. Zenvon used his magical hand to hurl stones from a distance, drawing the sentry's gaze away from the group's position. Then Veyra stepped into the open, clutching her notebook and doing her best impression of a bewildered student who had wandered far from home. The bugbear squinted at her and muttered something that sounded like "just a tourist" — though the tone carried all the warmth of a predator eyeing its next meal. While the creature's attention was fixed on Veyra, the rest of the party crept into striking range.

The ambush was swift. Veyra struck first, launching a volley of glowing darts that staggered the sentry. Sister Maela charged in with her mace, shouting that they couldn't let him warn the others inside. Pip closed the distance and drove his enchanted blade home, and the bugbear crumpled to the ground. The party dragged the body into the bushes and pressed on toward the cave mouth.

Peering into the firelit interior, Zenvon took stock of what waited inside: four bugbears, a hulking ogre, and a single orc seated near the fire. Pip studied the group carefully and noted something unusual — despite being the smallest and physically weakest of the lot, the orc was clearly the one in charge. The bugbears deferred to him, the ogre kept his distance, and the orc sat at the fire with the quiet authority of a gang boss. Pip advised that killing the leader first would likely shatter the group's will to fight. Zenvon agreed, and the party devised their approach.

Zenvon conjured a stationary illusion of a bugbear near the cave entrance, using it as cover while he and Sister Maela crept silently into position. The ruse worked well enough to grant them the element of surprise. Zenvon moved with deadly precision, striking the orc leader with his short sword and then drawing his scimitar across the man's throat in one fluid motion. Brughor Axe-Biter was dead before he hit the ground, and the cave erupted into chaos.

The bugbears, witnessing their boss fall, wanted no part of what came next. One by one they bolted for the cave entrance, shoving past Pip and Veyra into the open air. The party let them go. What remained was Gog — a massive, grief-stricken ogre who bellowed Brughor's name with a raw anguish that made the cave walls shake. It became clear, uncomfortably so, that the ogre and the orc had shared something far more personal than a chain of command. Gog raised his great club and swore to eat them alive.

The fight against Gog was brutal and sustained. Sister Maela called down pillars of sacred fire upon the ogre's head while Pip slashed at him with Talon. Veyra hurled fire and magical darts from the edges of the cave, and Zenvon pressed in close with both blades. Zenvon attempted to negotiate with the dying ogre, asking what he knew about their mission and the strange forces at work in the region — but Gog had nothing to offer except grief, crying out for Brughor until Pip ended the fight with a final blow. Sister Maela looked at Zenvon with weary disbelief, remarking that he was remarkably cold for someone who had just tried to open a conversation.

The party searched the cave and found a modest haul — silver coins, a handful of electrum pieces, and three vials of perfume, a disappointing reward for a band of raiders. More interesting was what Zenvon found on the cave wall: a crude charcoal drawing of a goblin with a crystal jutting from its skull. Veyra's own crystal flared faintly the moment she laid eyes on it. She pulled out her notebook and began furiously connecting threads — the strange goblins, the crystals, her missing mentor, the bugbear raids — drawing arrows between each point until the diagram sprawled across two pages. Sister Maela observed that she was missing an arrow, which sent Veyra into a frantic search for the gap. When she finally found it, she drew a small castle at the center of the web and declared that all roads led to Cragmaw Castle.

The party returned to Old Owl Well, where Hamun Kost received the news of their success with visible delight and promptly seated them at a fine dinner table — served, unsettlingly, by his undead former apprentice Warden Holl. Hamun remarked with dry amusement that Warden Holl had proven far more useful in death than in life. When Zenvon described the charcoal drawing of the crystal-headed goblin, Hamun's easy manner vanished in an instant. His hands went still, and he asked to hear every detail again, his composure only barely recovered before he revealed that his expertise lay in the ancient Netheril Empire and its lost language. He identified Pip's sword, Talon, as the blade of Aldith Tresendar — a knight known as the Black Hawk for his black armor and billowing cape — and offered himself as a resource for any Netherese artifacts the party might encounter. Pip noticed a flicker of barely concealed excitement cross Hamun's face when the black obelisk was mentioned, and Zenvon quietly signaled Veyra to say nothing about her crystal. The party left the dinner table well-fed, suspicious, and more certain than ever that the answers they were looking for waited somewhere inside Cragmaw Castle — though the evening ended without Hamun actually handing over the route there. The party still has the name and no road to it.

# Chapter 11 Look who came to dinner

Date: 2026-09-05

## Summary
The party crested the rugged ridge of Wyvern Tor with the faint smell of cookfire smoke hanging in the cool air. At the bottom of a rocky ravine, a lone bugbear leaned on a salvaged pike beside a cave mouth, his milky, half-blind eye scanning the terrain with bored disinterest. Zenvon initially proposed shooting him with a shortbow, but Pip recommended observing the guard first. A Stealth check of 25 brought Zenvon close enough to study him. The cave was too dark to inspect from that position, but the smell of cooking smoke suggested more occupants within.

The party hatched a layered plan, complicated when the companions’ approach alerted the sentry to something south of his position. Zenvon used Mage Hand to throw stones from another direction. He initially considered drawing out one or two more enemies, but Pip warned that an alarm might bring considerably more. The distance also mattered: Pip had roughly 60 feet of open ground to cover before he could attack. Veyra finally stepped into the open, clutching her notebook and impersonating a bewildered student. She remained quiet to avoid attracting the whole cave. The bugbear muttered, “Just a tourist!” with the appetite of a predator assessing its next meal.

Only Veyra gained surprise in the ensuing ambush. Her first Magic Missile dealt 6 damage; Zenvon’s shortbow shot missed, and he moved along the ridge and hid. Sister Maela charged in with her mace, determined to prevent the guard from warning the others. Another Magic Missile struck for 12 damage, and Pip finished the sentry with his enchanted sword. Zenvon expressed sadness about killing someone blind in one eye, while Maela praised his compassion and Veyra pointed out that she had been the intended lunch. At Maela’s prompting, the party hid the body in the bushes.

Inside the firelit cave waited four bugbears, a hulking ogre, and a single orc seated near the fire. Pip recognized an unusual hierarchy: the physically weakest member appeared to command everyone else. The bugbears deferred to him, the ogre kept his distance, and the orc occupied the place of authority. Drawing on his military experience, Pip suggested killing the gang boss first to break the others’ morale. He also reminded Zenvon that their previous encounter with four bugbears had forced them to flee.

Zenvon proposed concealing the party behind an illusory version of the dead sentry. After discussing Minor Illusion’s limits, they settled on a stationary image rather than a walking disguise. The cover earned advantage on the approach, and Zenvon and Sister Maela gained surprise. Zenvon’s first two hits dealt 7 damage apiece, leaving Brughor Axe-Biter barely alive; Maela missed her opening attack. Zenvon then acted again in the normal initiative order, delivering the decisive short sword and scimitar assault that cut Brughor’s throat and shattered the band’s cohesion.

The four bugbears wanted no part of the fight after their leader fell. Zenvon allowed them to escape, and Pip restrained Veyra from attacking a fleeing enemy. Sister Maela warned the survivors to get out of her way. Gog, however, remained: the ogre was furious and grief-stricken over Brughor’s death. Their attachment proved more personal than a chain of command; the DM established that they had shared a romantic relationship. Gog denounced the fleeing bugbears and threatened to eat the party alive.

The fight against Gog was sustained, with several missed attacks on both sides. Maela’s first Sacred Flame dealt 10 damage, Pip struck with Talon but missed his Action Surge follow-up, and Veyra’s Fire Bolt contributed a single point. Zenvon tried True Strike for the first time, landing a short sword attack for 12 damage and following it with a natural-20 scimitar hit for another 10. Veyra committed her last Magic Missile at his instruction and dealt 15 damage.

Before finishing Gog, Zenvon attempted a diplomatic interrogation about the raids and Hamun Kost, rolling a natural 20 for a total Persuasion result of 22. Gog cried for Brughor and explained only that Brughor told him to attack. He did not recognize Hamun’s name and would have needed a description. Zenvon decided the conversation had yielded enough and ordered the fight finished; Pip delivered the killing blow. Maela found the transition from negotiation to execution remarkably cold, even while admitting she would have skipped the negotiation herself. Veyra was pleased.

The cave yielded a modest haul of silver, electrum, and three perfume vials. More significant was a crude charcoal drawing of a goblin with a crystal jutting from its skull. Veyra’s blue crystal flared faintly, and she retrieved her notes on Sildar’s account of a strange goblin. She began connecting the goblins, crystals, bugbears, and her missing mentor in a sprawling diagram. Maela’s joke about a missing arrow sent her searching for another connection. Zenvon could not tell whether the theory held together, but his suggestion that Hamun and Cragmaw Castle might supply answers gave Veyra her missing destination: she drew a castle and pointed the arrows toward it.

Zenvon initially proposed awarding Pip half the silver and a perfume vial for killing Gog. Veyra objected to introducing kill bonuses into an evenly shared haul. They resolved the dispute when Zenvon gave Pip his own share instead, preserving the others’ shares.

At Old Owl Well, Hamun Kost greeted the victory with delight and insisted on dinner before business. His undead former apprentice, Warden Holl, served the fine meal beside a pleasant fire. Hamun’s hospitality became abruptly more serious when Zenvon described the crystal-headed goblin. He requested the details again, claimed not to know their meaning, and suggested that his research might prove valuable. Zenvon could not determine whether he was lying, but Pip caught a brief flash of excitement when the black obelisk was mentioned.

Hamun presented himself as an archaeologist specializing in Netheril and its ancient language. He identified Pip’s sword, Talon, as the blade of Aldith Tresendar, the Black Hawk, whose title came from his black armor and billowing cape. He offered to advise the party on Netherese artifacts. Zenvon remained suspicious and kept Veyra from revealing her blue crystal, despite her obvious eagerness to ask questions. The party ended the dinner well-fed and still without Hamun’s promised route to Cragmaw Castle. The session closed with advancement to level 4 and a pending choice of a feat or ability score improvement, plus one additional spell.
