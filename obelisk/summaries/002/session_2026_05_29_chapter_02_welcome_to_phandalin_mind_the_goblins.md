# Chapter 02 Welcome to Phandalin, Mind the Goblins

Date: 2026-05-29

## Summary
The party arrived at the Stonehill Tavern in the frontier town of Phandalin, weary from the road and heavy with worry for their missing patron, Gundren Rockseeker. The common room was warm but tense, filled with locals who pretended not to stare while clearly sizing up the newcomers. Toblin Stonehill, the broad-shouldered owner with sandy hair and tired blue eyes, greeted them with forced hospitality, offering rooms and hot stew while glancing nervously at the door whenever voices rose too loud.

Zenvon Forepot made his way to the bar, where the sharp-eyed dwarf bartender Elsa poured him a drink and wasted no time sharing what she knew. She spoke proudly of her scholar sister in Neverwinter and mentioned a man named Professor Orryn Voss. Veyra, recognizing the name of her mentor, explained that he had come to Phandalin searching for mysterious black stone fragments believed to be part of a great obelisk tied to minds, memory, and transformation. His final warning, which Veyra shared, had been chilling in its simplicity: never let anyone assemble the fragments. The party exchanged uneasy glances, recalling strange experiences on the road — a whisper that said "returned," a sudden chill against a holy symbol, and a fleeting vision of a shadowy obelisk. Elsa suggested they seek out Daran Edermath, a retired adventurer tending an orchard nearby, if they wanted to learn more about the region's secrets.

The locals at the tavern had no shortage of warnings to offer. A slight Gnomish woman named Freda, her fingers constantly twisting at loose threads, spoke in a small but resentful voice about the Red Brands — a gang that had taken hold of Phandalin and terrorized its people. She noted bitterly that the gang seemed to leave one woman alone: Halia, who worked at the Miner's Exchange, and that this was worth wondering about. Young Tuck Stonehill, the innkeeper's nine-year-old son with a missing front tooth and scuffed knees, tugged at the party's attention to share a secret — his friend Karp had found a tunnel leading into the woods near where the red-cloaked gang lurked. An older farmer named Narth quietly asked Sister Maela Dawnforge if she might speak with Sister Garaele, the local cleric who had returned to town limping and pale, refusing to explain what had happened to her. A loud miner named Lanar made no effort to lower his voice as he condemned the Town Master, Harbin Wester, as utterly useless in the face of bandits, goblins, and the Red Brands alike.

When Zenvon pressed Toblin for more, the innkeeper leaned in and dropped his voice low. He told them that the Red Brands had murdered a man named Thel Dendrar in broad daylight on the street, and that his wife and children had vanished shortly after. The gang drank at a rundown tap house called the Sleeping Giant at the far end of town, and Toblin made it clear he had no desire to speak any louder than he already had.

The following morning, the party paid a visit to Daran Edermath at his orchard on the edge of town. The retired adventurer, a drow who had lived for over five centuries and served as a marshal across distant lands, greeted them with genuine warmth — especially after Pip Thistlewick proudly declared the party's purpose with characteristic enthusiasm. Daran confirmed that the Red Brands' safe house lay beneath Tresendar Manor, a ruined estate on the edge of Phandalin, and that their leader went by the name Glasstaff. He also tasked the party with a separate concern: strange reports of undead chasing prospectors away from Old Owl Well, ancient ruins of a Netherese watchtower to the northwest, and he offered his prized boots as a reward for anyone willing to investigate. When he realized the party had stopped to gather information rather than immediately ride to Gundren's rescue, he fixed them with an amused but pointed look and told them, in no uncertain terms, that they really ought to go now.

Before leaving town, Zenvon led the group to the Shrine of Luck, a small temple built from stones salvaged from nearby ruins and dedicated to the goddess Tymora. The acolyte Sister Garaele, a scholarly young elf, blessed them warmly and told them she would have another task waiting when they returned. With prayers offered and information gathered, the party set out down the goblin trail, moving carefully through the dense woods. The path proved treacherous — Zenvon narrowly avoided a hidden pit, and moments later Pip Thistlewick was yanked off his feet by a concealed snare, left dangling ten feet above the ground until his companions hauled him back down.

After five miles of cautious travel, the trail ended at a cave mouth in a hillside, screened by dense briars and thickets with a shallow stream trickling out from the darkness within. Zenvon crept ahead alone and discovered a hollowed-out lookout post hidden in the briars, where two goblins and their boss lounged in bored inattention. He slipped back to the party and relayed what he had seen, and together they agreed it was the perfect moment to strike. They moved into position silently, and the ambush began — though Zenvon's opening shot sailed wide, the element of surprise still held long enough for the party to press their advantage.

The fight was scrappy but decisive. Veyra unleashed a volley of magical force that struck all three enemies at once, while Sister Maela waded in with her mace and Pip Thistlewick charged forward with his sword. The goblin boss swung back hard but failed to connect, and Zenvon, switching to his blades, delivered a breathtaking strike that cut one goblin down with brutal finality. Veyra finished the second goblin with a bolt of fire, and after Sister Maela landed a solid blow on the boss, Zenvon followed with another powerful hit before Veyra ended the fight with a final burst of flame. The lookout post fell silent.

The party searched the bodies and claimed what weapons they could find — a scimitar and a pair of javelins — before pressing into the cave mouth. Zenvon moved ahead on his own, ghosting past a pack of three wolves resting in the shadows without disturbing them. Deeper in, the passage narrowed and the darkness thickened into something absolute, the kind of black that swallowed torchlight whole. Zenvon pressed on toward a rubble-choked passage and a steep slope, and then — without warning — a massive coiled snake reared up from the debris, its hiss cutting through the silence like a blade. It had not yet struck, but it was close enough that Zenvon could feel the cold weight of its attention. He held perfectly still, then began to ease himself backward into the dark, leaving the snake and whatever lay beyond it waiting for another moment.
