He opened the bidding with a corpse, and then he opened his mouth.

"So look, I helped you, right? And, you know, I didn't — Glasstaff? Man, he's like some scary shit, and I don't want to be here anymore, and I don't really want to die for him, so I did you a solid, and you're gonna do me a solid, and let me leave, and pretend you never saw me."

For a moment I thought he had believed our story. He had not. He had simply done the same thing I do: looked at the room, counted who was standing, and made an offer. I could respect that. It did not mean I would accept it.

"There's a little bit of fairness in what you're saying, but—" I said. "We need you. Tell me more about Glasstaff and Spider, and anything else that you know, and we'll let you know."

Then, because a man like this prices in coin and not in kindness, I told him about the coin. Gold pieces, silver pieces, a share of what we had earned through our adventure. "Be a nice guy with us," I said. "I cannot guarantee anything, or you can join us."

He talked. They almost always talk.

"Glasstaff? He's a wizard, not just a muscle with a title. I've seen him drop a man with a spell, no weapon drawn, nobody messes with him twice."

Useful. Then the dungeon: multiple levels, and he had only been sent below once, to carry supplies. "I don't want to go back. Don't know what's there, but it's kinda wrong. There's something really wrong over there that messes with your head, I'm told." He said *Wick's only ever been sent* and then caught himself and said *I've only been sent*, which is a small thing, but a man who slips between himself and a character he is playing is a man I keep my eyes on.

And the chain. Glasstaff answers to somebody. He had overheard complaining about orders from town, and once about the boss's business. Somebody in Phandalin, he thought. Then he trailed off, the way a man trails off when the next word costs more than he is being paid.

So I paid the next word myself.

"Black Spider," I said.

He flinched. Not a big flinch. But I was watching his face when the name landed, and the name landed like a debt collector at the door. He knew nothing about it, I believed that part, but the name had weight in him, and weight is information too.

I pressed him. I put some edge in it. He gave me nothing but what his fear was worth: "Look, dude, I just carry crates, alright? You start asking about the boss's boss, that's how people end up carrying less."

Fine. A low man. Low men have low ceilings.

"Okay," I said. "Are you really interested to join us?"

He said yes. And while he was saying yes I was reading him: the eyes, the hands, the too-quick agreement. And the reading was clean. He would take whatever money we had and get out of Dodge at the first opportunity. Not a betrayal. He would not put a knife in our backs. He was simply looking at my gold the way I look at a door. "Of course I'm with you, boss," he said, and the *boss* was the tell. Nobody calls you boss that fast unless they are already leaving.

"In order to trust you, you're gonna tell me all the secrets about this manor?" I asked him anyway. "Like, what's upstairs?"

I stood there and totalled it. A man who knows our faces. A man who flinches at the Spider's name, which means the Spider's people can make him talk faster than I can. A man whose yes is a lie of convenience. Release him, and he runs to whoever pays next. Keep him, and he runs anyway, with our coin. It was, I told myself, a tough choice. Hold on.

It was not a tough choice. I wanted one more thing from him first, that is all. So I tied him, and I put his face into the cistern, and I held it there until he had something better to sell.

He did. One thing. There was a family, the Dendrars, that the Redbrands handled a while back.

Behind us. In the cells. The family we had just cut out of cages. *Handled.* That was the word he chose, with the water still running off his chin.

He had nothing else. I had already priced him.

"Yeah, I mean, I would prefer to attack him at this point instead of letting him run away," I said.

Nobody argued. It did not take long. He was a man who would run. Now he is a man who will not.

Then the room. "Did we get anything here? There are so many crates and barrels." The barrels held food. But a rope ran down the south wall of the cistern, and two feet under the water hung a waterproof satchel: a potion of healing and a potion of invisibility. Glasstaff's escape kit, or somebody's. People hide the things they expect to need in a hurry.

The three dead bandits, last. Sixteen silver and seven gold on the first. Twelve silver and five gold on the second. Twenty copper, eleven electrum, and two garnets on the third, ten gold each, by my eye.

I pocketed the garnets and did not look back at Wick. There was nothing there I had not already counted.
