I pocketed the garnets and did not look back at Wick. There was nothing there I had not already counted.

There was one item left on the ledger, and it had been sitting there unpaid the whole time we were killing our way through the rest of the building. The family. I had opened their cell with my own tools, told them the way out, and moved on to the next room like a man who had finished a job.

I had not finished the job. Somewhere between the cistern and the barracks it arrived in my head with the weight of a dropped coin: they had no cloaks. The skeletons let us pass because of the red cloaks. A woman and two children walking out of that cell in plain clothes would have been, to those two dead guardians, exactly what a Redbrand would want stopped. They never got out. They could not get out. They would be back in the cell, holding the door.

I had told them it was safe. It had not been safe. That is the kind of error I do not usually make, and I did not enjoy making it now.

So we went back. Down through the rooms we had already emptied, past dead men we had already priced. The two skeletal guardians watched us come with whatever passes for watching in an empty skull, saw four red cloaks, and did nothing. Good. The disguise was still holding its value.

Then the cell room. Two skeletons, poised at the door. Not wandering. Not resting. At the door, and not moving from it.

I weighed it. Draw and fight, or test the arrangement. Fighting was the expensive option — two of them, a closed room, and Veyra spent down to nothing. And the arrangement had held so far: they were never supposed to attack the ones in red. There was no reason to pay for what the cloak might buy for free.

"Okay, so, yeah, looks like they might follow my orders, or try to," I said, and walked forward, and asked them to move out of the way.

They did not follow my orders. They did not listen to me at all — I am not sure there is anything left in there to listen with. But they did not attack. They simply stood there in the manner of furniture that used to be men, and let me reach the door.

I tried the door. It did not give.

Not locked. Held. Held tight from the other side, with the particular unevenness of bodies pressed against wood — a woman's weight and two smaller weights, braced against everything outside. They had been like that for I did not want to calculate how long. In the dark, with two dead things loitering an arm's length away, holding a door shut with their own backs because the last people who promised them safety had walked off and left them with it.

That last people was me.

I put my mouth near the wood and made my voice as small as it would go.

"Hey guys, it's me. I came back for you. It's all safe now."

Nothing. Fair. I would not have let go either.

"You can let go of the door. Let me help you."

A pause. Then the sound of furniture scraping across stone — a bench, maybe, being dragged back inch by inch by hands that did not fully believe in the offer yet.

I have been paid in gold, in silver, in favours, and once in a jade frog nobody has valued yet. I am telling you plainly: that scrape was worth something. I did not stop to work out what.

The door swung open.

I stepped inside so they could see the cloak on me and not on the things behind me, and the skeletons — seeing red walk through their door — decided the matter was concluded. They withdrew, joints clicking, back to their resting positions, like guards going off shift. I let them go. There was no profit in breaking what had just worked twice.

Then it was only the walking. Mirna Dendrar and her two children, kept in the middle of four red cloaks, up through rooms full of nothing that could hurt them anymore, and out. The hideout under Tresendar Manor was cleared of Redbrands — on this level, at least, which was the level I was prepared to sign for.

At the end of it I stood in the air and totalled the day, and the total came out clean. Everyone I meant to walk out had walked out. Nothing left holding a door somewhere in the dark, waiting on my word.

No cliffhanger this time, which is a good thing.

No cliffhanger.

This time.
