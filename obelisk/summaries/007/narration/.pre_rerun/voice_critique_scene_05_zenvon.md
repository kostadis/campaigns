## Flags

### [1] Verbatim alteration — numeral spelled out inside a quote

> "And we don't have access going upstairs to the fifteen—"

**Why:** Source reads `"And we don't have access going upstairs to the 15…"`. He was reading a number off the battle map — the GM had just said *"You see where that number 15 is?"* — so the digit is what he actually said, and it is the thing on the map he is pointing at. Spelling it out turns a map reference into speech.
**Suggested rewrite:** `"And we don't have access going upstairs to the 15…"`

### [2] Cliché / on-the-nose simile

> And there it was, clean as a signed confession: murmuring.

**Why:** *Clean as a signed confession* is a workshopped simile — the kind the examples file never uses. Compare the same act in scene 02, done plainly and better: *"everything came clean — no guessing, no maybes."* Zenvon's images come from trade and money, not from courtroom drama.
**Suggested rewrite:** `And there it was, no guessing required: murmuring.`

### [3] Tell-not-show — narrated attention

> At the top I put my ear to the door and gave it my full attention — breath held, hand flat on the wood, every small sound sorted and weighed.

**Why:** *Gave it my full attention* names the state, then the appositive list demonstrates it. The demonstration alone is stronger, and the spec's model for him is observation without commentary.
**Suggested rewrite:** `At the top I put my ear to the door. Breath held, hand flat on the wood, every small sound sorted and weighed.`

## Verified, not flagged

- `"And I try to help him"`, `"So that I can pull it off"`, `"So, so we carefully move past the trap"`, `"Around it, yes, yes, and being more careful…"`, `"Avoid any other traps"`, `"Got it, got it"`, `"That's true"`, `"Oh, okay"`, `"Okay, let's follow this guy"`, `"This guy has told everybody"`, `"There's no choice"`, `"He leaves us no choice other than to attack"` — all verbatim.
- The ESL markers survive intact: doubled acknowledgement (`"yes, yes"`, `"Got it, got it"`) and the `"So,"` opener. Correct per the 2026-08-01 ruling.
- `"The floor ate Pip."` — invented opener, and the strongest line in the session. Deadpan, no sentiment, immediately self-corrected (*"That is not the professional way to describe it, but it is accurate"*). Exactly the register the spec asks for.

## Verdict

Only one real defect — the `15` → `fifteen` change inside a quoted map reference — and two stylistic softenings around the listening beat. The whip paragraph and the opening line are the best-calibrated prose of the eight scenes. Spot-edit; do not re-narrate.
