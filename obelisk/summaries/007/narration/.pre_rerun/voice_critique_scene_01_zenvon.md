## Flags

### [1] Verbatim alteration — Sister Maela's Turn Undead line

> "BE GONE! In the name of my Dwarvish god!"

**Why:** The source says **dwarven**, in both places it appears — the gm-assist summary bullet (*"invokes the power of her dwarven god… commanding: 'BE GONE! In the name of my dwarven god!'"*) and the quote block (*"In the name of my dwarven god."*). "Dwarvish" is not a word anyone at the table said, and the narration itself gets it right in scene 03 (*"solidly, without ceremony, dwarven"*). Load-bearing dialogue, silently changed.
**Suggested rewrite:** `"BE GONE! In the name of my dwarven god!"`

### [2] Register-wrong vocabulary — bureaucratic verb

> It missed spectacularly. The bolt went somewhere that was not any of us. I filed that under things that would not happen twice.

**Why:** *Filed* is clerical. Zenvon keeps a ledger, he does not keep a filing cabinet — the spec's metaphor is transactional (prices, accounts, what the last person paid), not administrative. The examples file never reaches for office vocabulary.
**Suggested rewrite:** `I priced that as a thing that would not happen twice.`

### [3] Cliché / florid interiority — the "perfect swing" pile-up

> The second was the other thing entirely — the swing where the body knows before the mind does, the edge arriving exactly where the neck bone meets the shoulder bone, clean, perfect, the kind of strike you would pay to see.

**Why:** *The body knows before the mind does* is stock combat prose, and the sentence stacks four appositives on one swing. The spec is explicit: *"Avoid florid interiority… He would not describe a crypt as 'oppressive.' He would notice that there are three sarcophagi and two exits."* This is the sentence in the scene that reads least like him.
**Suggested rewrite:** `The second landed where the neck bone meets the shoulder. Exactly where I sent it. My best work.`

### [4] Cliché simile

> To Pip. Warmth poured into him and he straightened up, good as morning.

**Why:** *Good as morning* is a workshopped fantasy simile with no relation to how this narrator sees. The beat that matters here is the one immediately after — the ledger of who got healed — and the simile softens the entry to it.
**Suggested rewrite:** `To Pip. He straightened up, whole again, and went back to work.`

## Verdict

The one genuine defect is the altered Turn Undead quote (`Dwarvish` for `dwarven`); everything else is polish. The ledger voice is at its densest here — 15 accounting tokens in 842 words, the highest of the session — and it earns most of them, but *"filed"* and the perfect-swing sentence are where the register slips. Spot-edit; not worth re-narrating.
