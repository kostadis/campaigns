## Flags

### [1] Convergence with the examples file — first of four uses of the same formula

> My fingers closed around it and for a moment I was not counting anything.

**Why:** This is lifted from `examples/zenvon.md` §*"Feeling, arriving as an interruption to the arithmetic"* — *"and for a moment I was not counting anything. / Then I was again."* That file opens with the instruction *"only the **manner** transfers, never the incidents."* The manner is *feeling arrives as an interruption to the arithmetic*; the phrasing was one demonstration of it. The narration adopted the phrasing as a catchphrase and fires it in scenes 02, 03, 04 and 08 — four times in eight scenes, twice on the same boy in adjacent scenes.
**Suggested rewrite:** Keep the beat, change the mechanism. `My fingers closed around it and the number went out of my head.`

### [2] Florid interiority — the dead woman's biography

> She had been somebody. A lady of this manor, maybe, back when the manor was a manor and the men in red cloaks upstairs were knights instead of thugs. Somebody had laid this on her wrist and meant it.

**Why:** Three sentences of invented history and sentiment about a stranger. The spec: *"He notices **prices, exits, and who is lying** — not weather, not architecture, not his own feelings"* and *"Avoid florid interiority."* The preceding sentence (*"A hundred and forty gold, by my eye, and my eye is usually within ten"*) is exactly right; this undoes it. The *"somebody… Somebody"* anaphora is house-style rhetoric, not his.
**Suggested rewrite:** `Somebody had put it on her wrist and meant it. That is not what it is worth now.`

### [3] Generic prose — writerly scene transition

> "Okay, then yes," I said, and went to the door to find out what today wanted from me.

**Why:** *To find out what today wanted from me* is a stock world-weary-narrator flourish. He goes to the door because there is a noise behind it and noises are information; the personification of "today" is a register he never uses.
**Suggested rewrite:** `"Okay, then yes," I said, and went to the door to find out what was making the noise.`

## Verdict

The dead woman's biography is the clearest voice break in the session — it hands Zenvon three sentences of sentiment about a stranger immediately after he has appraised her bracelet to within ten gold. That, plus the first firing of the *"not counting anything"* formula, makes this the scene where the narrator's own voice is loudest. Spot-edit both; the negotiation with Maela at the close is excellent and should not be touched.
