The door opened onto a long room, and I counted before I moved. Three areas, partitioned by walls of iron bars, north and south. Filthy straw on the cell floors. Hinge doors, chains, padlocks. Two human women in the cell to the south. A boy in the cell to the north.

And men in red cloaks, standing in that particular way that is supposed to look like not standing at all.

They had heard us coming — we had made noise, and noise has a price — and they had set an ambush. As ambushes go, it was a poor one. I have set ambushes. There is a craft to it, a stillness you have to practice, and these men had not practiced. They were waiting, they were trying to surprise us, and they failed. Nobody was surprised. I would say nobody on either side.

The boy in the north cell had his hands on the bars. For a moment I was not counting anything.

Then I was again.

I moved first. That is correct — the first cut belongs to whoever is fastest, and in this party that is me. I had to be careful, so I went in low, put my dagger into the nearest one, felt it bite through cheap leather and not much further. Not exactly my best work. He stayed up. So I did the other half of the trade — back, out of reach, before his friends could close the distance. In, cut, out. That is the whole arrangement. I do not renegotiate it mid-room.

One of them swung a short sword at Pip and missed, and then did something I noted with a small amount of respect: he stepped aside, deliberately, making a clean angle for the man behind him. A ruffian who makes room for his colleague. Somebody had trained these men at least one thing.

Veyra was pulling back instead of burning them, and for a heartbeat I did not understand why. Then I looked at her properly. Whatever she carried in her, she had mostly spent it already, and she was pale in the way that means one solid hit ends the conversation. Up close, with blades in her face, her work falters — she needs distance the way I need an exit. So she got herself distance.

Makes sense, yes.

Sister Maela does not need distance. Sister Maela stepped in and hit a man with her hammer, and it landed the way her work always lands — solidly, without ceremony, dwarven.

The second ruffian went for Pip and missed him too. Two swings at Pip, two misses. They were not having the fight they had planned.

Then it was Pip's moment, and I gave him the target, because I keep the ledger of who is bleeding and by how much. The guy who was recently injured — the one I had opened, the one Maela had dented. Him first. Always finish what is already paid for.

"Go on, baby."

Pip went. His sword came down and the man simply stopped, all at once, the way a bill stops mattering once it is settled. One down. Two left, and the arithmetic tilting our way.

The nearest survivor did the brave thing, which in a room like that is the same as the wrong thing — he charged Pip. And missed. Third miss at Pip. I began to suspect these men had trained the stepping-aside and nothing else.

Both of them were inside the room now, so the geometry favored me. I moved forward, used the dagger again, and this time the blade found something that mattered — I felt him sag around it. Then minimum back, yes. Always the minimum back. Reach is a resource. You do not give it away for free.

Veyra stepped up behind me — close enough now, safe enough now — and fire left her hand. It struck one of them full on, and I checked which one, because the ledger must be current: the far one, and he was swaying, scorched, almost nothing left of him. Standing out of habit rather than argument.

Sister Maela closed the account. She asked her god for fire and her god said yes. Light came down out of nowhere onto the swaying man, and he did not endure it. He burned and dropped and that was the whole of him.

The last one looked at his three colleagues on the floor and arrived at the correct valuation. He had had better days in his life, and he decided to go find one. He disengaged — carefully, properly, the one other thing he had been trained to do — and ran for it.

I counted his steps going out of the room. Six of them.

Then it was quiet, except for the straw shifting in the cells, and the boy still had his hands on the bars.
