The fight ended with Wick doing the last of the work himself. His own men, dead by his hand, because a room that is listening will take the next thing you give it, and I had given it a traitor.

For a moment I thought he had believed our story. He had not. What he believed was simpler, and he laid it out before the blood had finished spreading.

"So look, I helped you, right? And, you know, I — Iarno? Man, he's like some scary shit, and I don't want to be here anymore, and I don't really want to die for him, so I did you a solid, and you're gonna do me a solid, and let me leave, and pretend you never saw me."

A solid for a solid. That is an opening offer, and an opening offer is never the price.

"There's — I mean, there's a little bit of fairness in what you're saying," I said, "but we need you. Tell me more about Iarno and Nezznar the Spider, and anything else that you know, and we'll let you know." I told him about the gold we carried, the silver, the treasure earned through the whole of this business, and that a share of it could be his. "Be a nice guy with us."

He talked. They almost always talk, once you have shown them the money and the door in the same sentence.

"He's a wizard," Wick said, "not just a muscle with a title. I've seen him drop a man with a spell, no weapon drawn, nobody messes with him twice."

On the dungeon: multiple levels, and Wick had only ever been sent to the — "I've only been sent to the subterranean part once to carry supplies, and I don't want to go back." He caught himself slipping out of himself mid-sentence, which I noted. "Don't know what's there, but it's kinda wrong. There's something really wrong over there that messes with your head, I'm told."

On the wizard's leash: Iarno answers to somebody. Wick had overheard him complaining about orders from town, once about the boss's business — somebody in Phandalin, he thought, but the thought trailed off and did not come back.

So I gave him the name.

"The Black Spider. Nezznar."

He flinched. Not a performance — a performance comes half a beat late, and this came before he could stop it. I pressed. I did not raise my voice; I simply let him understand where he was standing and who was standing with him.

"Look, dude, I just carry crates, alright? You start asking about the boss's boss, that's how people end up carrying less."

Nothing behind that door. He was a man who carried crates, and the flinch was the whole of what he owned on the subject.

"Okay. Are you really interested to join us?"

"Of course I'm with you, boss."

"In order to trust you," I said, "you're gonna tell me all the secrets about this manor? Like, what's upstairs?"

And while he answered I watched his face the way I watch a merchant's hands, and the arithmetic came back clean. The yes was a lie. Not the dangerous kind — he was not going to put a knife in anyone's back. He was going to take whatever money we gave him and be gone the first time we all looked the same direction at once. The money was his exit, and I was being asked to fund it.

Correct. I had priced him.

*This is a tough choice.* It was not, really. But I wanted the rest of what he knew first, so I tied him up and put his face into the cistern until the last of it came loose.

It came loose. There was a family, he said, shaking now — the Dendrar family — that the Redbrands had handled a while back.

Behind us. In the cells. The family we had just pulled out of cages.

That was everything he had. Which left the question of the man himself: a man who would run, carrying our faces and our business in his head, into a town where the boss's boss lived.

I would prefer to eliminate him at this point instead of letting him run away.

So I did. It was quick. Nobody said anything, and I did not offer anything to be said.

Then the room. So many crates and barrels, and most of them held food. But two feet under the cistern's surface a rope ran taut to the south wall, and on the end of it hung a waterproof satchel: a potion of healing and a potion of invisibility. Somebody's exit fund. Iarno's, most likely. It was ours now.

The three bandits Wick had killed for us carried sixteen silver and seven gold on the first, twelve silver and five gold on the second, and on the third, twenty copper, eleven electrum, and two garnets worth ten gold each.

I counted it twice and put it away.
