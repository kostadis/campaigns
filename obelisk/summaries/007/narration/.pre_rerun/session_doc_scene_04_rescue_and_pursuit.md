The last Redbrand still breathing decided he had seen enough of us. He did not swing. He did not shout. He put his back to the fight and spent everything he had on distance — one long, careful retreat down the corridor, out of everyone's reach.

I let him go for one full breath. In that breath I counted: two cells with locks, three captives behind them, one runner, one corridor, and skeletons somewhere out past the door that answered only to red cloaks.

Pip turned to me. "Are we chasing him, boss, or not?"

"Wait, where did he run away? Like, which way?" I marked the direction. A runner is only a problem if you do not know his heading. "Okay."

You gonna chase him? The question sat there and I did not like either answer.

"Not yet," I said.

But a running man is a mouth on legs. If there was help left in this place, he would find it and bring it back, and then the arithmetic would change against us. "Quite possible. Okay, yeah, yeah. Two of us will chase them, and two of us…" Free these people. Yes. That was the shape of it.

"So, me and, Veyra will go chase them, you guys free this,"

Pip cut in before I finished. "You're the, you're the guy with the fingers who knows how to open the locks, I'm the guy with the sword that knows how to."

He was right, which is rare enough that I noted it. I carry the tools. He carries the reach. Sound division of labour costs nothing to admit.

"Yeah, okay," I said. "So, you and Veyra can go behind the Redbrands Ruffian, and Sister Maela will help."

Pip went after him the way Pip goes after anything — full speed, sword first — and I heard the blade land somewhere down the corridor, a real hit, the kind that makes a man reconsider his choices without stopping him from making more of them.

I went to the boy's cell. Iron bars, an ordinary lock, the kind a jailer buys when he expects his prisoners to stay frightened rather than clever.

*Interesting.*

The tumblers gave with a little coaxing. Routine work — focus, not effort. The door swung open and the kid came out of it like something released from a spring.

"Yay! I've been saved!"

He was very happy. I did not have anything prepared for that. His face when the bars moved was — I was not counting anything, for a moment.

Then I was again.

Down the corridor Veyra's firebolt cracked out and caught the runner across the back — a scorch, not a stop. Beside me Sister Maela worked the second cell, and she got it open too, and the women came out of it. Both cells clear. I confirmed it out loud because a thing counted out loud stays counted. "Okay, now, I've freed… me and Sister Maela have freed both the…"

The ruffian pulled away again — another clean retreat, no opening for anyone. "Shit, okay, he ran again."

Then, from down the passage, a sound. "I see, I hear some footstep." Except it was not a footstep. It was the specific sound of a floor stopping being a floor, followed by the sound of Pip arriving somewhere twenty feet lower than he had intended. A pit, hidden in the hallway, waiting for exactly one man moving at exactly that speed. By the noise of him at the bottom he had landed soft — annoyed, not broken — but he was out of the chase, and the chase was the whole reason he was there.

I looked at the freed prisoners. "I'm gonna… my job here is done."

"Wait, you're, you're gonna just leave us here?"

"Just stay here until I come back," I told them. "It's not safe yet for you guys to move out."

This was not hospitality. It was arithmetic. They had already tried the obvious door once, and the skeletons on patrol out there had moved on them the moment they saw bodies with no red cloaks — the cloaks are the pass, and these people had none. So the same bars that had held them were now, in a manner of speaking, the safest wall in the building. They said okay. They did not like it. I did not need them to like it.

Then I went to the pit. Pip at the bottom, twenty feet of dark between us, and I carry no rope. I carry a whip, which is a rope that has made something of itself. I fed it down and hauled him up, hand over hand, and he came out dusty and unimproved and ready to keep going.

Behind us, Veyra shifted for one last angle on the runner and let a second firebolt go. It went wide. The wall took it — a black scorch where a man's back should have been — and the corridor swallowed him.

One runner loose. Three captives penned behind us, safe exactly as long as they stayed put. Pip on his feet. Both locks open.

I priced the day so far at even. The runner would cost us later. The boy's face, I did not put a number on.
