I listened at the door first. Several voices behind it, murmuring, low. Men waiting for something. Waiting for us, most likely. The wounded one had run somewhere, and this was somewhere.

I opened the door anyway, and I stood in it.

Four of them that I could see. One I could not: south side, tucked out of the light. Five, then. One exit behind me, one behind them. The wounded ruffian was among them, still bleeding, still breathing hard, and pointing.

They looked at me. They looked at the red cloak on my shoulders. And they stopped.

"Wait! You're one of us!"

Then they turned on their own man. "What do you mean they attacked you, man? Are you sure these are the guys that attacked you?"

"Do I look like I'm the kind of person who would lie? That is a killing machine! Kill him!"

Killing machine. Flattering, but unhelpful. He was telling the truth, which put him at a disadvantage. The truth was all he had, and he had already spent it running. I had a room that was listening, and a room that is listening will take the next thing you give it.

So I gave it.

"We — we are one of you guys. Like, come on, we all have the same dress. And look at this guy — this guy has turned a traitor. He was trying to — I saw him letting someone in. So that's why I wanted to catch him and, you know, eliminate him."

I let it settle. Then, because a room like that wants a simple ending:

"So, he's the bad guy."

I felt it land. There is a moment in every negotiation when the other side stops weighing your offer and starts weighing each other, and I watched that moment cross four faces at once. It worked. The blood on him helped. Blood on a man makes any story about him easier to believe, which is unjust, and useful.

They approached him with their blades pulled out. He tried to defend his honor, loudly, badly, with more blood on him than credibility. It did not take.

The big one, Dosa Rook, was first to buy it, and bought it whole. "About time somebody cleaned house. I'm going to cut this rat into several pieces."

But there was a second one. There is always a second one.

"Hmm, traitor," he said. "Funny. Nobody sent word down, but it's not very convincing when he's covered in blood and you're covered in blood."

A fair point, honestly. This one had done the arithmetic I was hoping nobody would do. "I'm gonna have to go check this story, see if it's real."

I did not try to talk him out of checking. A man who wants to verify will only get more suspicious if you stand between him and the ledger. What you do instead is offer to carry the ledger for him.

"Alright, I'll — I'll join with you. Let's go check. Let's confirm your suspicions."

The route to checking ran past the stairs. Maela and Veyra were at the stairs. That was the whole plan, and it required nothing from me except walking beside him at a courteous pace and not attacking him myself. The room could still see me, and a helpful escort who suddenly puts a dagger in his companion is difficult to explain twice. So I walked, and I let my team do the accounting.

He reached the stairs. Pip's blade came out of the dark and hit him. Hard, real, but the man stayed up. Veyra's spell went wide. Maela's mace found air.

Three swings, one wound, and a live witness.

He screamed for help.

*Oh, no.*

Behind me the ruffian found his voice at last. "I told you these guys weren't to be trusted." He had been telling them the truth all evening. Nobody had listened until now, and now was too late for everyone, himself included.

The room came apart. Pip was fastest. He finished the man he had struck, then crossed the floor and planted himself beside me, ready. Good man. He hits what he aims at, and he does not require the situation explained to him.

My moment came. Dagger, close, quick. It went in the way it does when the count is right, and the second bandit dropped where he stood.

"Step back."

That left Wick. Wick, and the wounded ruffian beside him: the honest one, the one who had run to his friends and told them the truth and watched them believe me instead.

Wick looked at the room. Wick did his own arithmetic.

He drew his blade and cut the ruffian down himself, his own man, the only one in the room who had never lied, and then he threw up his hands and begged for mercy.

I have negotiated with many kinds of men. I had not, until then, watched one open the bidding with a corpse.
