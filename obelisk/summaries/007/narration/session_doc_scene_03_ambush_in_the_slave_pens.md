The door opened, and I did what I do with a room: I counted it.

One long chamber, cut into three by walls of iron bars. Filthy straw on the cell floors. Hinge doors on chains, padlocked. Two human women in the cell to the south. A human boy alone in the cell to the north.

And ruffians. Waiting for us.

That was supposed to be an ambush. It should have been an ambush — we had made enough noise coming down that corridor to warn a deaf man. They had every advantage a man can be handed for free: the room, the warning, the choice of ground. And they wasted all of it standing in the wrong places and looking at the wrong door. By the time the first one moved, we had already seen them, and the whole arrangement collapsed into an ordinary fight. Which is the kind I prefer. In an ordinary fight, you can count.

The boy had his hands on the bars.

For a moment I was not counting anything.

Then I was again. Nearest ruffian, red cloak, blade out, close enough. I went first — I usually do, when it matters — and I went in fast, the dagger already moving. It caught him, but it did not bite deep. A scratch through leather, nothing a man dies of. Not my best work. I did not stay to apologise. In, cut, out, back through the doorway before his friend could get an angle on me. You do not linger in a room like that if you are my size. You visit.

One of them stepped up and swung a shortsword at Pip. Missed him clean — and I noticed what he did next, because it was the only clever thing any of them did all day: he slid sideways as he missed, opening a lane for the man behind him. So. One of them had been in a real fight before. Good to know which one.

Veyra pulled back instead of casting, and I did not understand it at first. Then I did. She had nothing left in her — everything she carries had already been spent upstairs, and what remained were the small tricks, the kind you cannot aim properly with a blade in your face. And she was one solid hit from the floor. Standing in reach of a shortsword to throw fire at a disadvantage is a bad trade. Makes sense, yes. She stepped clear. I would have priced it the same.

Sister Maela does not step clear. Sister Maela stepped in, and her hammer came down on a ruffian with the sound of a real argument being made. He staggered. He did not enjoy it.

The second ruffian took his opening — the lane his friend had made for him — and swung at Pip anyway. Missed anyway. All that clever footwork, spent on nothing.

Then it was Pip's turn, and Pip wanted a target. There were two of them still standing, and only one of them mattered. The guy who was recently injured — the one Maela's hammer had already softened — that was the one. Number one. I pointed him at it.

"Go on, baby."

Pip went. One stroke, decisive, the way he does everything with a sword, and the man folded up and stopped being a problem. That is the arrangement between us, and it works: I choose, he finishes.

The survivor charged Pip — grief, probably, or panic, they look the same at that range — and missed him too. This crew could not hit Pip if he stood still and held their hands.

My opening came again. Both of the remaining men were inside the room now, which meant the room was mine to enter. I slipped forward, put the dagger in properly this time — and this time it bit, deep enough that I felt it change something in the way he stood — and then I went minimum back, yes, out of reach before anyone thought to answer. In, cut, out. It is not glamorous. It is arithmetic.

Veyra found what she had left. She stepped up and threw fire, and it hit the man I had just cut — hit him hard, hard enough that he was standing by habit rather than by anything you could call health. This time the sound of it matched the look of it. He swayed there, one bad breath from the floor.

Sister Maela gave him the bad breath. She spoke, and light came down on him from nowhere in particular, and he did not have it in him to get out from under it. He burned, briefly, and died. Radiant, they call that. I call it the last item on his bill.

Which left one.

He looked at the room — at Pip, at Maela, at the burnt thing that had been his friend a moment ago — and he did his own counting, and he did it correctly. He disengaged, cleanly, and ran.

I watched him go and did not spend anything stopping him. Some debts you write off.

The boy still had his hands on the bars.
