## Flags

### [1] Register-wrong vocabulary — recurrence of scene 01's defect

> We had been fighting our way through a door we could have walked through. I filed that away without comment.

**Why:** Second instance of *filed* (see scene 01 flag [3]). Clerical verb in a narrator whose entire metaphor system is money. The sentence around it is strong — the realisation that the fight was an avoidable expense is pure Zenvon — which makes the filing cabinet the only wrong note in the paragraph.
**Suggested rewrite:** `…a door we could have walked through. I noted the price of that and said nothing.`

### [2] Convergence with house style — direct address to the reader

> I will tell you what I did not say. I did not say that Gundren had not paid us to be the good guys. I did not say that I was one bad exchange from the floor. I did not say that "the good guys" is a story, like the cloaks are a story, and stories are things I sell, not things I buy.

**Why:** The *"I will tell you"* address and the three-beat anaphora are lifted from `examples/house_style.md`, which is the **global** style file — not Zenvon's. That file explicitly warns *"Only the manner transfers."* The content here is genuinely on-voice (stories as merchandise is a very good line for him); it is the rhetorical scaffolding that belongs to the house narrator. Scene 08 does the same thing (*"I am telling you plainly"*), so this is a two-scene pattern worth deciding on deliberately.
**Suggested rewrite:** Drop the frame, keep the goods. `I did not say that Gundren had not paid us to be the good guys. I did not say I was one bad exchange from the floor. "The good guys" is a story, like the cloaks are a story, and stories are things I sell, not things I buy.`

## Verified, not flagged

- `"So… the bottom… East door is where the skeleton's from."` and `"So… both of the skeletons ran through the open door at the bottom…"` — verbatim, and **correctly attributed to Zenvon** in the source.
- `"Ohh."` / `"Hey."` — verbatim, and the narrator's bridge between them (*"because they were standing there and I was standing here and nobody was doing anything"*) is a legitimate gloss, not invented speech.
- `"Storylines," … "Storylines."` — the doubled utterance preserved per the ESL ruling.
- `"Well, I'll do this for you, Sister M," … "For you."` — verbatim, and the spec's key relenting beat handled exactly as documented (*"for her, notably, not on the merits"*).
- `"I'll deal with them in the next room, but—"` — this quote now tracks the **smoothed** layer (the previous run's did not).
- The dead woman's biography that was flagged in the first run is **gone**. It has been replaced with *"Someone had loved her enough to bury her wearing it, and enough time had passed that the arithmetic had changed hands"* — one sentence, in his own metaphor. This is the single biggest improvement between runs.

## Verdict

The cleanest scene of the eight on every mechanical measure — three em-dashes, one register hit, no fabricated or altered quotes. The only structural question is the borrowed house-style address in flag [2], which is a taste call rather than a defect. Spot-edit.
