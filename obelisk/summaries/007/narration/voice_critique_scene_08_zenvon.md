## Flags

### [1] Name provenance — ✅ RESOLVED (GM ruling, 2026-08-01: *"he doesn't know the name"*)

**Applied in `.scrubbed.md`:** `Mirna Dendrar and her two children` → **`The Dendrars`**.

Zenvon keeps the *family* name, which he legitimately has — Wick gave it up under pressure in scene 07 (*"there was this family, the Dendrars, that the Redbrands handled a while back"*) and the GM confirmed on the spot that they were the captives the party had freed. He loses the first name, which he was never told. As a side effect this also retires the Nars/Nilsa age wrinkle below: the sentence no longer classifies them as "two children."

Original flag retained for the record:

> Then it was only the walking. Mirna Dendrar and her two children, kept in the middle of four red cloaks, up through rooms full of nothing that could hurt them anymore, and out.

**Why:** **Mirna Dendrar is never named in any session-7 source.** The extraction and `session-summary.md` describe the captives only as *"two women and a young boy"* / *"the Dendrar family."* The name reaches the narration through `--reflections`, which injects `world_state.md` (where she is named 6×, recorded as *"Freed from the Redbrand cells in Ch. 7"*) and `campaign_state.md`.

So this is grounded in your own grounding docs rather than invented — but it is still the narrator asserting a name **Zenvon has no on-screen moment of learning**. Two further wrinkles worth your eye: `world_state.md:48` itself flags the discrepancy (*"The Ch. 7 summary describes the captives as 'two women and a young boy'"*), and it records the children as Nars (13) and **Nilsa (18)** — an 18-year-old is one of the "two women," not one of the "two children" the narration describes.

**Resolved as (b)** — rendered without the first name. The GM's ruling settles it: Zenvon does not know it, so the narrator does not use it.

### [2] Convergence with house style — direct address, second instance

> I have been paid in gold, in silver, in favours, and once in a jade frog nobody has valued yet. I am telling you plainly: that scrape was worth something. I did not stop to work out what.

**Why:** Same pattern as scene 02 flag [2] — the *"I am telling you plainly"* address comes from `examples/house_style.md`, the global style file, not from Zenvon's. Two instances across eight scenes makes it a deliberate-or-not decision rather than a one-off. The content is otherwise excellent, and the payment list is the correct way for him to register feeling.

The **jade frog checks out** — it is a real campaign item, present in `world_state.md`, `party.md`, `planning.md`, and `campaign_state.md`. Not invented.

**Suggested rewrite:** `I have been paid in gold, in silver, in favours, and once in a jade frog nobody has valued yet. That scrape was worth something. I did not stop to work out what.`

## Verified, not flagged

- `"Okay, so, yeah, looks like they might follow my orders, or try to,"`, `"Hey guys, it's me. I came back for you. It's all safe now."`, `"You can let go of the door. Let me help you."`, `"No cliffhanger this time, which is a good thing."`, `"No cliffhanger."`, `"This time."` — all verbatim.
- **Mechanical scan B: one hit, dismissed.** *"They had been like that for I did not want to calculate how long"* — *calculate* is on the scan list, but for a narrator whose entire idiom is arithmetic it is native vocabulary, and the construction (refusing to do a sum he always does) is characterisation. Not a defect.
- The first run's florid self-portrait — *"I am not, as a rule, the voice people are glad to hear through a door. I have talked men out of rooms, into rooms, out of their money… The mouth is a tool and I keep it sharp"* — is **gone**, and the *"not composing anything / Then I was again"* formula with it. Replaced by *"I put my mouth near the wood and made my voice as small as it would go"*: one action, no self-commentary. A clear improvement.
- *"That last people was me."* — four words, no justification, and the closest thing to remorse the spec permits him. Correct.
- *"like guards going off shift"* and *"in the manner of furniture that used to be men"* — invented similes, both grounded in the transactional/labour register rather than in fantasy stock.

## Verdict

The Mirna naming is the only item requiring a decision rather than an edit, and it is a knowledge-state question of exactly the kind the entity registry cannot represent — the same structural gap that produced the Nezznar leak in scene 07, surfacing here through `--reflections` instead. The prose is otherwise the most restrained of the eight scenes and the session's closing beat lands without ornament.
