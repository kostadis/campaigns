Six of them. Three stone sarcophagi, two doors: one behind us, one behind them. That was the room, and that was the whole of the accounting before the first one picked up its shortbow and fired.

It missed by a margin that was almost insulting. The dead do not price their shots. I noted that and moved in.

Sister Maela shouted at my back: if you have a mace, now would be a good time to use it.

"I don't have a mace, I don't—" The thing was already turning toward me, so the sentence ended where it ended. "I'll use the axe."

First swing went wide. The second was the cleanest strike I have made in years. I felt it before it landed, the way you feel a deal closing. The axe found the seam between the ribs. Bone cracked. The whole cage of it shuddered.

And it stayed up.

"Wait, my best hit does nothing?"

It did something. It did not do enough. That is a distinction I have used on other people many times, and I did not enjoy having it used on me.

"Shit."

Another one loosed at Pip and the bolt skipped off his armor. Then Sister Maela walked forward. Walked, in front of all of them, like a woman stepping up to a lectern. And planted herself.

"BE GONE! In the name of my dwarven god."

"Turn on bed," I said, which was not what she had said. Whatever the correct name was, two of the six took it badly. Something went out of them, whatever passes for nerve in a thing with no flesh, and they backed off her like she was fire.

"No damage, just frightening," I said. Updating the ledger. She was worth more than I had been paying her in respect, and I made a note of it.

One of the frightened ones bolted through the far door and into the dark.

"So it's gone." Two exits had just become a problem for later. I put it on the list.

The one in front of me did not run. Its short sword came in under my guard and went deep. Deeper than anything has gone into me in a long while, the kind of blow that stops being pain and starts being information. I did the arithmetic standing up. The total was bad. I was on my feet, and one more entry like that would close the account.

Pip killed the one I had cracked open, then swung into the next without pausing for breath. Good value, Pip, always. Veyra had been spending her magic all day, and if ever there was a time for the last of it, this was it: three darts of light, one skeleton down, another staggering.

I put away the axe and drew the scimitar. "Nick, Nick is possible with the scimitar. Yes." Strike and step away: the blade is built for exactly the position I was in. The first cut missed. The second took the thing apart at the spine and it went down in pieces.

"Yay." That was the full extent of the celebration the situation warranted.

One left. It came on and I did not hold the line. "I'm gonna move back. I'm down to almost nothing, so…" There is no shame in it. A man who does not know his own remaining balance dies confused. The skeleton pivoted to Pip instead and caught him: a bruise through the armor, nothing that changed his footing.

"I will cast healing on you," Sister Maela said. To Pip. I watched the warmth go into him and stood there holding my own margin in both hands. Sound allocation. Pip was the line, I had withdrawn, the coin went where the work was. I did not say anything. I noticed that I noticed.

Far off through the door, the fled ones kept up their chatter. "The ones that ran away?" Still running, yes. Fine.

Pip hit the last one. Veyra, out of her real magic, threw fire at it: a sound like the end of the world, and behind the sound, almost nothing. To be fair to the fire, the sound was excellent.

Then it was my moment. I had to close within arm's reach, no way around that, so I closed, dagger out, and put it through the thing's neck-bones. Clean. Certain. I knew it was true before it landed. It came apart on the floor and stopped being a problem.

Afterward, the two that had fled came drifting back. And stopped. They looked at our red cloaks, and they took up posts by the wall like we belonged there. Like we were the management.

Which meant the cloaks would have held them from the start. The entire fight had been an avoidable expense.

I was too near empty to dwell on the price of that lesson. So, first we would inspect the sarcophagi. The dead stood guard, and we went through their masters' boxes.
