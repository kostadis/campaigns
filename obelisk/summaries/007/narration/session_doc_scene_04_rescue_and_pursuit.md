The boy still had his hands on the bars. I noted that, and I noted the last Redbrand at the same time — he had decided that, on balance, he had enjoyed better days in his life, and he was putting distance between himself and us with every breath, careful not to give anyone a clean opening as he went.

Pip looked at me. Are we chasing him, boss, or not?

"Wait, where did he run away? Like, which way?"

The corridor north. One exit. One man.

"Okay."

I did the arithmetic. One man running is one man. One man running to friends is a room full of friends. "Not yet," I said, and then thought better of it, because a runner who calls help is more expensive than a runner you catch. "Quite possible. Okay, yeah, yeah. Two of us will chase them, and two of us…"

Free these people. That was the other half of the sentence, and everyone understood it.

"So, me and Veyra will go chase them, you guys free this—"

Pip cut across me. "You're the guy with the fingers who knows how to open the locks. I'm the guy with the sword that knows how to."

He did not finish the sentence either. He did not need to. It was a fair repricing of the labor, and I do not argue with a fair price.

"Yeah, okay. So, you and Veyra can go behind the Redbrand Ruffian, and Sister Maela will help."

Pip went after him like money owed. I heard the sword land somewhere down the corridor — a solid hit, the kind that slows a man's mathematics considerably.

That left me and the iron bars.

"Interesting."

An iron lock on a bandit's cell. Not a good lock. A lock bought by men who did not believe anyone would ever stand on this side of it with tools. I took out my picks and the work was routine — pressure, a turn, done. It was the quietest thing I had done all day, and possibly the only honest one.

The door swung open and the boy came off the bars like a cork out of a bottle. "Yay! I've been saved!" His whole face did it — the thing a face does when a debt it did not know how to pay gets cancelled anyway. Nobody was paying me for this door. I noticed that I did not mind, which was worth noticing.

Behind me, Veyra's fire went down the corridor and clipped the runner — a graze, enough to sting, not enough to stop. Sister Maela had already moved to the second cell, working the lock with more patience than I would have credited her, and it gave. The women came out of the dark all at once.

The Redbrand slipped another attempt to pin him and sprinted on.

"Shit, okay, he ran again."

Well. He was Pip's problem now. Mine was inventory. "Okay, now, I've freed… me and Sister Maela have freed both the…" Both cells. Empty. Confirmed.

Then, from up the corridor — a wrong sound. Wood giving way, and a man dropping. "I see, I hear some footstep." That was not a footstep. That was Pip finding a hole in the floor that the Redbrands had not marked on any door, twenty feet of it, straight down into the dark. From the sound of him swearing at the bottom, the fall had cost him bruises and dignity in roughly equal amounts, and nothing worse. But he was twenty feet down and the runner was gone past him.

"I'm gonna… my job here is done," I said, moving.

The freed people did not agree. All right, wait — you're gonna just leave us here?

A fair question, and the answer was yes, but not the way they meant it. There were skeletons walking the halls out there, and skeletons do not negotiate and do not read faces — they read cloaks. We were wearing red. These people were wearing what a month in a cell leaves you. They had tried the door once already, I gathered, and the bones had come for them, and that was why they were still in this corridor instead of halfway to daylight.

"Just stay here until I come back," I told them. "It's not safe yet for you guys to move out."

They stayed. People who have just been let out of a cage will believe the man who opened it, for a while. That is a line of credit I try not to overdraw.

Then I went up the corridor to the pit. Pip was at the bottom of it, upright, annoyed. I do not carry rope. I carry a whip, which is a rope with opinions, and I fed it down to him and set my feet and hauled. He is not a small man. My arms informed me of this in detail. But he came up over the lip and that was that.

Down the far corridor, Veyra had repositioned and loosed her fire one more time at the shrinking shape of the runner. It went wide and hit the wall instead of him, and the stone came out of it blackened and the Redbrand did not.

One runner loose. One fighter out of a hole. Two cells empty, and a boy somewhere behind me still wearing that face.

I priced the day so far. It came out ahead.
