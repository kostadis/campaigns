# Chapter 07 Deception

Date: 2026-07-18

## Summary
The air in the Tresendar Crypts was thick with the smell of ancient stone and decay as the party squared off against a cluster of skeletal guardians. One of the undead creatures raised a shortbow and loosed an arrow at the group, but the shot went wide, clattering harmlessly against the far wall. Zenvon Foreput pressed forward and struck at the nearest skeleton, drawing first blood in the grim underground chamber. Then Sister Maela Dawnforge stepped to the front of the group, raised her holy symbol high, and called upon the power of her dwarven god. Her divine presence washed over the undead like a wave, and several of the skeletons recoiled in supernatural terror, fleeing through the nearest door and vanishing into the darkness beyond.

With the frightened skeletons gone, the remaining undead fought on, and one of them landed a vicious blow against Zenvon, leaving him barely standing. Pip Thistlewick and Veyra coordinated their attacks with practiced efficiency, hurling magical bolts and steel alike until the last active skeleton in the room clattered to the floor in a heap of bones. Sister Maela laid her hands on Zenvon and channeled her healing power into him, mending his wounds before the party turned their attention to the three stone sarcophagi resting in the chamber. Digging through the ancient remains, Zenvon's fingers closed around a jeweled gold bracelet nestled among the bones of a human woman — a valuable find worth keeping. As they searched, the two skeletons that had fled earlier crept back into the room, but upon noticing the red cloaks the party wore, the creatures simply stood at attention, recognizing the garments as a sign of allegiance and choosing not to attack.

It was then that Sister Maela heard it — muffled cries drifting through the northeastern door. "Please, help us!" the voices begged, desperate and frightened. The cleric turned to Zenvon with a firm look and reminded him that they were the good guys, and that the helpless deserved their protection. Zenvon relented, and the party moved toward the door. When they pushed it open, they found a long room divided by iron bars into filthy cells lined with straw. Two human women were locked in one cell, and a young boy was confined in another — all of them captives of the Redbrands. Before the party could act, however, a group of Redbrand ruffians who had been lying in wait lunged forward, attempting to catch the adventurers off guard. Their ambush failed entirely, and the fight was on.

Zenvon darted forward and drove his dagger into the nearest ruffian before slipping back out of reach. Sister Maela swung her hammer with righteous fury, cracking bone and sending one of the bandits reeling. Pip cut down a wounded ruffian with a single decisive strike, and Veyra unleashed a torrent of fire that scorched another nearly to ash. Sister Maela then called down a column of sacred radiant flame that finished the job, leaving the man a smoldering ruin. The last surviving ruffian, seeing his companions fall one by one, turned and bolted from the room.

Pip gave chase while Zenvon and Sister Maela stayed behind to free the prisoners. Zenvon worked quickly, picking the locks on the iron bars with practiced ease, and the young boy burst free with a cry of relief. Sister Maela freed the women from the adjacent cell as Pip sprinted after the fleeing bandit. The chase led him through a winding hallway — and then the floor gave way beneath Pip's feet. He plunged twenty feet into a hidden pit trap, landing hard in the darkness below. He survived, but the fall left him bruised and shaken, and the bandit had gained precious ground.

Zenvon rushed to the edge of the pit and used his whip as a makeshift rope to haul Pip back up to safety. Sister Maela mended Pip's injuries with a touch of divine magic, and the party pressed on into a large cellar below the manor. The room was lined with kegs and barrels, and a great stone cistern dominated the western wall. Stairs climbed fifteen feet to a landing above, and it was through a door up there that the fleeing ruffian had disappeared. The party investigated the cistern and discovered a waterproof satchel hidden beneath the water's surface, tied to a rope along the south wall. Inside, they found a potion of healing and a potion of invisibility — a welcome discovery.

The party climbed the stairs and pressed their ears to the door at the top. They could hear voices murmuring on the other side — several of them. The fleeing ruffian had clearly warned his companions. Zenvon considered his options for only a moment before a bold plan took shape in his mind. He threw open the door and strode in wearing his red cloak, projecting the confidence of a man who belonged there. The bandits paused, confused, and Zenvon seized the moment — pointing at the fleeing ruffian and accusing him of being a traitor who had let enemies into the hideout. His performance was utterly convincing, and the bandits turned on their own comrade with blades drawn.

Zenvon then lured one of the remaining bandits toward the stairs under the pretense of going to verify his story, and the moment the man was within reach of the waiting party, Pip struck him down. The ruse unraveled in an instant as the other bandits realized they had been deceived, and a chaotic brawl erupted. The party killed two of the bandits, including Dosa Rook, leaving Wick alone standing next to the injured but living Redbrand ruffian. Seeing an opportunity, Wick cut down the wounded ruffian himself before throwing up his hands to beg for mercy.

Wick was willing to talk, and Zenvon pressed him hard. The thug described Glasstaff as a dangerous wizard — not merely a figurehead, but a genuinely terrifying man who could drop someone with a spell before a weapon was ever drawn. Wick also mentioned that the dungeon beneath the manor had multiple levels, and that the subterranean depths were deeply unsettling in a way that went beyond ordinary danger. When Zenvon spoke the name "Black Spider," Wick visibly flinched, his face going pale with a fear he could not quite hide, though he claimed to know nothing about the figure. He did reveal one final piece of information under duress — that the Redbrands had previously "handled" a family called the Dendrars.

Zenvon weighed his options. Wick had made a show of offering to join the party in exchange for gold, but Zenvon could see through the act — the man wanted the coin and the first opportunity to disappear. There was no loyalty to be bought there, only a thug looking for an exit. Zenvon made his decision, and Wick met his end in the cellar. The party then looted the fallen bandits, recovering a modest collection of gold, silver, electrum coins, and two garnets of notable value.

With the barracks cleared and the treasure secured, the party made their way back down to the cells where the freed captives were still waiting. The two skeletal guardians from the crypts had drifted back to the doorway and stood blocking the path, but they made no move to attack — the red cloaks still held their silent authority over the mindless undead. On the other side of the door, the captives had barricaded themselves in, terrified by the skeletons just outside. Zenvon called out to them, reassuring them that it was safe, that he had come back for them as promised. The sound of furniture scraping across stone followed, and then the door swung open. The skeletons, seeing Zenvon step inside, retreated to their resting positions without a sound. The captives were free at last, and the Tresendar hideout had been swept clean of its Redbrand occupants — at least on this level.

## Locations
### Tresendar Crypts
An ancient burial site located beneath Tresendar Manor, featuring three stone sarcophagi, false columns, and a heavy atmosphere of decay where skeletons stand guard. The skeletal guardians ignore those wearing red cloaks.

### Slave Pens
A long room partitioned by iron bars into filthy cells lined with straw, used by the Redbrands to hold captives including women and a young boy.

### The Cellar
A large subterranean room featuring a stone cistern filled with water and walls lined with kegs and barrels, with stairs leading to a landing fifteen feet above the floor and doors leading to other areas of the hideout.

### The Pit Trap
A concealed twenty-foot-deep hole hidden in the hallway, designed to catch intruders. Pip fell into it while chasing a fleeing Redbrand ruffian.

## NPCs
### Pip Thistlewick
A capable fighter who accompanies the party, contributing significantly to combat by killing multiple Redbrand ruffians and pursuing a fleeing bandit, though he fell into a pit trap during the chase.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A dwarven cleric who serves as the party's healer and moral compass. She turned several skeletons with her divine power, healed party members multiple times, and urged the party to rescue the captives, reminding them they are 'the good guys.'

### Veyra
A spellcaster who supported the party with magical attacks including bolts of force and fire, though she ran low on spells during the session.

### Skeletons
Mindless undead guardians armed with shortbows and short swords that haunt the Tresendar Crypts. They are programmed to ignore those wearing red cloaks but will defend the area against intruders.

### Redbrand Ruffians
Thugs wearing red cloaks who serve as guards and enforcers within the hideout. They attempted to ambush the party in the slave pens and later in the barracks, but were ultimately defeated.

### Human Boy
A young captive held in one of the cells of the slave pens who was overjoyed upon being rescued by Zenvon.

### Dosa Rook
A bandit who was initially fooled by Zenvon's deception and was killed by the party along with another bandit once Zenvon's deception fell apart.

### Wick
A low-level Redbrand thug who, when left alone with his injured comrade, killed him to curry favor and beg the party for mercy, by sharing information about Glasstaff and the Black Spider. He revealed that the Redbrands previously 'handled' the Dendrar family. Zenvon ultimately executed him.

### Glasstaff
The wizard leader of the Redbrands, described by Wick as a dangerous magic-user who can drop a man with a spell and who takes orders from someone in Phandalin.
