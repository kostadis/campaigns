# It's Nothing Personal, You See

Date: 2026-07-16

## Summary
Deep beneath the ruins of Tresendar Manor, the party stood face to face with the Nothic — a gaunt, one-eyed creature of terrible hunger and terrible knowledge. Through Sister Maela's translation, the creature laid bare a secret it had plucked from Pip Thistlewick's mind: that Pip had once won a strange blackstone shard in a dice game near Neverwinter, only to sell it to a passing peddler bound for Phandalin. Since parting with the shard, Pip had been haunted by dreams of a broken black stone buried beneath the earth and whispers in the dark that called his name. The Nothic, unbothered by the weight of this revelation, made its terms simple — it was hungry, and it would let the party live if they were willing to give it one of their own.

Zenvon stepped forward to negotiate, asking what the party would receive in return for such a sacrifice. The Nothic's answer was blunt: it would eat only one of them and leave the rest to go about their business. The party exchanged glances and reached a cold agreement — their prisoner, the Redbrand bandit Rondar, would be the one to pay the price. But Rondar had been listening, and the moment he understood what was being decided, he bolted, screaming for help at the top of his lungs. Pip lunged to stop him, but Rondar slipped free. Zenvon moved to intercept, landing a series of precise strikes that left the fleeing man barely standing. Sister Maela delivered the final blow with her mace, looking Rondar in the eye as she did. "It's nothing personal, you see," she said quietly. Rondar's last words were a darkly logical protest — "You can't expect me to not think it's personal when you're about to kill me" — before he crumpled to the ground. The Nothic was pleased, dragging the body away into the dark with evident satisfaction, and the party was free to move on.

With the creature occupied, the group pressed westward through the hideout's stone corridors. Zenvon pressed his ear to the first door they encountered and heard the low rumble of bugbears — large, brutish creatures laughing among themselves. He moved to a second door and caught the sound of humans, their voices loose and easy, the clink of coins suggesting a gambling game in progress. Pip Thistlewick warned the group that charging in without a plan risked being caught between two hostile forces. The party settled on the humans as their first target, agreeing to strike fast and hard before anyone could raise an alarm.

They disguised themselves in the scarlet cloaks of the Redbrands and burst through the door. Four tough warriors in matching red cloaks sat around a table heaped with coins and trinkets, completely unprepared for what came through the door. Pip Thistlewick led the charge with a fierce declaration — "Never did like the crooked kind" — and the fight was on. Veyra unleashed bursts of magical force, Zenvon moved through the room with his blades, and Sister Maela swung her mace with grim efficiency. One of the ruffians managed to land a blow on Veyra and screamed toward the bugbears' door for help before Veyra silenced him with a final surge of magic. When the dust settled, all four Redbrands lay dead, and the room was theirs.

A heavy knock came at the bugbears' door almost immediately — a husky voice demanding to know if everything was alright. Without missing a beat, Zenvon pitched his voice low and gruff, calling back that all was well. The bugbear, apparently satisfied, shuffled away. The party wasted no time stacking ale barrels against the bugbears' door to buy themselves time, then turned their attention to a door to the north.

Beyond it lay a wizard's workshop — a cluttered, bubbling space filled with alchemical devices, distillation coils, and shelves crowded with strange tomes and parchment. Veyra's eyes lit up as she moved through the room, identifying three bottles of rare reagents: Mercury, Dragon Bile, and Powdered Nightshade, each of considerable value. Sister Maela, meanwhile, pulled a dusty journal from the shelves — the account of an adventurer named Urman, written in Dwarvish. It described the history of the Lost Mine of Phandelver, the Forge of Spells, and a legendary magic mace called Lightbringer, commissioned by the priests of Lathander and lost when the Wave Echo Cave vanished from history.

Pressing further east, the party entered what was clearly a personal chamber — draped in scarlet cloth, furnished with a writing desk, a comfortable bed, and a wooden chest. More importantly, a secret door in the wall had been left ajar, as if someone had fled through it in a hurry. The party gave chase immediately, pouring through the hidden passage in pursuit of whoever had just escaped. Veyra spotted the figure first — a wizard moving fast through the dark — and struck him with a bolt of fire on Zenvon's orders to stop him without killing him. Pip hurled a javelin that found its mark, and the wounded wizard stumbled but did not fall. Zenvon called out a demand to surrender, but the man was already too far ahead. With a shout directed at the Nothic to guard the entrance, the wizard — whom the party recognized from Sildar's description as Iarno Albrek — vanished in an instant, teleporting himself to safety and leaving the party grasping at empty air.

Back in Iarno's quarters, the party searched what he had left behind. On his desk they found a letter sealed in wax with the shape of a spider — the symbol of the mysterious villain known as The Spider, who controlled a network of bandit gangs across the region. The letter ordered Iarno to capture the party if possible, kill them if necessary, and deliver any dwarven maps in their possession without delay. It was damning proof of Iarno's betrayal and his connection to a conspiracy far larger than the Redbrands alone. The chest at the foot of the bed yielded gold and gemstones — carnelians and peridots among them — and the party divided the spoils before moving on.

A nearby armory offered further rewards: racks of spears, swords, crossbows, and bolts lined the walls, along with a dozen dirty red cloaks hanging from hooks by the door. The sheer volume of weapons suggested the Redbrands had been preparing for something significant. Zenvon traded his blade for a finely crafted short sword decorated with semi-precious stones, and Pip followed suit, swapping his crossbow for a more ornate version. Sister Maela rolled her eyes at the aesthetic priorities on display, but said nothing.

The party continued south through a secret door they had discovered in the cavern walls, eventually arriving at a heavy door that opened into a desolate crypt. Three large stone sarcophagi dominated the chamber, and carved columns along the walls were shaped to resemble spreading oak trees. Six skeletons in rusty mail stood propped against the walls, utterly motionless. Veyra warned that they might be animated — capable of attacking if disturbed — while Sister Maela identified them as undead. Zenvon drew on his knowledge of history and recognized the chamber as the Tresendar crypts, the ancient burial place of a noble family that had once called the manor home. The information was reassuring, but only briefly.

The moment Zenvon moved toward the northeast door, the skeletons stirred. One by one, the ancient guardians pulled themselves upright, hollow eye sockets turning toward the intruders who had dared to disturb their rest. Sister Maela's voice was steady as she named what they were facing — undead, risen to defend the crypt. The party braced for the fight of their lives as six sets of bony hands reached for rusted weapons, and the silence of the tomb was shattered at last.

## Locations
### Tresendar Manor Basement
A subterranean complex beneath the manor ruins, featuring natural caverns and finished stone rooms used as a hideout for the Redbrands. The Nothic lurks in a crevasse within the cavern.

### Redbrand Barracks
A large room furnished with worn tables, chairs, and ale kegs, decorated with brown and red draperies. It served as a gathering place for the scarlet-cloaked ruffians, who were found gambling when the party burst in.

### Wizard's Workshop
A laboratory filled with bubbling alchemical devices, distillation coils, alembics, and bookshelves crowded with strange tomes and parchment. Iarno was attempting to brew a potion of invisibility here.

### Iarno's Quarters
A chamber draped in scarlet cloth containing a writing desk, a comfortable bed, and a wooden chest. A secret door in the wall provided Iarno with a hidden escape route.

### Redbrand Armory
A chamber lined with racks of weapons including spears, swords, crossbows, and bolts, with hooks holding a dozen dirty red cloaks — suggesting the Redbrands were building up their forces.

### Tresendar Crypts
A desolate burial chamber containing three large stone sarcophagi and columns carved to look like spreading oak trees, guarded by six skeletons in rusty mail. Identified as the final resting place of the elders of the Tresendar family.

## NPCs
### Nothic
A telepathic, one-eyed creature with long fangs and claws that lurks in the caverns beneath Tresendar Manor. It hungers for flesh and possesses the ability to extract secrets from the minds of those nearby. It agreed to let the party pass in exchange for Rondar's body, and later demanded more food after Iarno's escape.

### Rondar
A Redbrand prisoner being held by the party. When he overheard the plan to sacrifice him to the Nothic, he attempted a desperate escape by screaming for help, but was ultimately struck down by Zenvon and executed by Sister Maela.

### Pip Thistlewick
A brave companion who assists the party in combat and offers tactical advice, such as warning about being trapped between two enemy groups and suggesting barricading doors with barrels. He charges into battle with a sense of justice.

### Sister Maela Dawnforge
A stern companion who serves as the party's translator for Undercommon and is willing to take decisive, sometimes harsh, action against criminals. She provides religious and historical insight and attempts to call down divine radiance on the fleeing Iarno.

### Veyra
An impulsive magic-user who provides long-range support with spells and tends toward lethal force, much to Zenvon's frustration. She is fascinated by alchemical equipment and identifies the rare reagents in Iarno's workshop.

### Redbrand Ruffians
Tough human warriors wearing scarlet cloaks who were found gambling in the barracks. They attempted to retaliate and call for help from nearby bugbears before being cut down by the party.

### Iarno 'Glasstaff' Albrek
A wizard who was overseeing the Redbrand operations and working for a mysterious figure known as The Spider. He was spotted fleeing through a secret door and escaped the party using teleportation magic after shouting a command to the Nothic.

### The Spider
A mysterious figure who sent orders to Iarno via a sealed letter with a wax spider symbol, directing him to capture the party and retrieve dwarven maps.

### Skeletons
Six undead guardians clad in rusty mail that stood motionless against the crypt walls until the party attempted to move deeper into the chamber, at which point they suddenly animated.
