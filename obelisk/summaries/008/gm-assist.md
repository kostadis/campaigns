# Session 2026-08-02

Date: 2026-08-02

## Summary
The session opened with the party reaching 3rd level — a level-up owed since Chapter 3 — and Zenvon committing to the Arcane Trickster archetype, which handed him the *Mage Hand* and *Minor Illusion* he would lean on for the rest of the night. No long rest had been taken.

The party returned to the prison cells, where the Dendrar family had barricaded themselves in terror at the sight of the skeletal guardians standing watch outside. Zenvon called out to them, and the sound of furniture scraping across stone was followed by the door swinging open. The skeletons, recognizing the red cloaks, stepped aside and retreated to their resting positions without a sound, and the captives were free at last.

Mirna Dendrar, a human woman with two teenage children — thirteen-year-old Nars and eighteen-year-old Nilsa — was overjoyed to see her rescuers. She explained that the Redbrands had murdered her husband, Thel, and had intended to kill the rest of the family as well. She had bought time by lying about wealthy relatives in Neverwinter who would pay a ransom, hoping someone would come. When she pressed for news of Thel, Zenvon hedged rather than describe what the party had seen — a deception that held — and she was left with the closure of knowing where her husband lay, and a request that they bring his body back if they ever could. In gratitude, Mirna told the party of a valuable emerald necklace hidden behind a section of storage shelves in her family's old herb and alchemy shop in the ruined town of Thundertree, insisting they take it as their own if they ever passed that way.

The party escorted the Dendrar family through the ruins of Tresendar Manor and out through the front of the building, delivering them safely to the outskirts of Phandalin. With the family secure, the adventurers turned back toward the cellars to deal with the remaining threats lurking below. Moving stealthily through the apothecary and into the cavern beyond, they could hear the distant, wet sounds of the Nothic feeding on the bodies of the fallen. Zenvon used *Minor Illusion* to conjure the convincing sound of Glasstaff working in his workshop, and the creature fell silent and crept away to investigate, leaving the crevasse unguarded.

Zenvon descended into the steep fissure and discovered a hidden chest resting in the darkness below. Using *Mage Hand* to open it from a safe distance — wary of traps — he found 160 silver pieces and 120 gold, five malachite gems, three potions of healing, climbing and growth, and a finely crafted magical longsword — Talon, the blade of the long-dead knight Aldith Tresendar, though nobody in the party recognised it or learned its name. Pip was delighted, and the party quietly agreed the sword belonged in the hands of their most capable fighter. With the treasure secured, they turned their attention to the bugbear barracks.

The party crept to the barracks door and listened, hearing the deep, guttural voices of bugbears barking orders at a goblin in the goblin tongue. Zenvon used *Mage Hand* to ease the door open, and the party stepped inside wearing their red cloaks. A miserable goblin named Droop promptly fainted from terror at the sight of them. The lead bugbear, a brute named Nosk, laughed at Zenvon's attempt to assert authority, sneering that it was just like a Redbrand to think he was in charge.

Zenvon quickly realized that the bugbears served the Black Spider directly rather than Glasstaff, answering to an entirely different chain of command. Rather than back down, he leaned into the confrontation, rage-baiting Nosk with pointed questions about what the Black Spider would think of his behavior. The gambit worked almost too well — Nosk, furious and flustered, blurted out that the Black Spider was at **Cragmaw Castle** before the color drained from his face as he realized what he had done. "It don't matter that now you know," Nosk snarled, "because now I kill you." The room erupted into violence.

The fight was brutal and desperate. Zenvon cast *Thunderwave*, and the blast slammed into two of the bugbears, but the creatures were powerful and resilient, and Nosk answered with a crushing blow from his morning star that left Zenvon barely standing. Sister Maela channeled divine energy to keep him on his feet, but another bugbear landed a second vicious strike that nearly finished the job. The party was outmatched, and the realization came quickly — they needed to run.

Pip slammed the barracks door shut to buy precious seconds while Zenvon poured flask after flask of oil across the threshold and down the hallway. Using *Mage Hand*, he ignited the oil just as the bugbears smashed through the shattered door, and the hallway erupted in flames. The fire scorched the brutes and turned the slick, oil-soaked floor into treacherous terrain, slowing their pursuit to a crawl. The party dashed through the tunnels toward the manor entrance, Sister Maela dragging a struggling Veyra behind her.

As they sprinted past the crevasse, the Nothic emerged from the shadows and watched them flee. It called itself Ssarnak, and as Veyra ran past, it leaned close and whispered something that stopped her cold — her teacher was not dead; he was not in a place where dead is easy. Veyra went pale and tried to turn back, desperate to speak with the creature, but Sister Maela grappled her and held her fast. The door at the manor entrance was slammed shut and locked behind them just as the bugbears began hammering on the other side, and the party collapsed into the cool night air of Phandalin.

In the quiet aftermath, Zenvon made a choice he had been deferring since the road: he told Veyra about the black stone in his pocket and produced it. Her blue crystal flared even brighter in response, the two objects resonating with some unseen magical force. The strange, mutated goblins they had seen in the crevasse, the glowing stones, the Nothic's whispered revelation — it was all beginning to feel connected, lending new weight to the theories of Veyra's missing mentor, Orryn Voss. Zenvon kept his growing suspicions to himself, but he trusted her more than he had before.

The party returned to the Stonehill Inn, bloodied and exhausted, where Toblen Stonehill offered them a free night's stay in gratitude for ridding the town of the Redbrands. After a long and much-needed rest, they met with Sildar Hallwinter, presenting him with the Black Spider's letter — sealed with a wax spider, recovered from Glasstaff's desk back in Chapter 6 — which confirmed Iarno Albrek had been working for the Black Spider all along. Sildar was appalled by the betrayal and paid the party the remainder of their agreed reward. He then offered a new bounty for Iarno's capture, preferring him brought back alive to face trial in Neverwinter, though he admitted quietly that he would not be upset if the man came back dead. Zenvon argued that capturing the Black Spider was the more important prize, and Sildar conceded the point, noting that Iarno had likely fled straight to his master.

The party then paid a visit to the Shrine of Luck, where a scholarly acolyte named Sister Garaele praised them for their heroics before making a request of her own. She had previously attempted to approach a powerful undead banshee named Agatha to ask about the location of a legendary spellbook belonging to the mage Bowgentle, but had barely escaped with her life. She asked the party to bring Agatha a jeweled silver comb as a gift to win her favor, offering three potions of healing in return. Veyra confirmed that Bowgentle appeared in her research notes, and Zenvon accepted the task, though he made clear there were other pressing matters to attend to first.

Their final stop was the Miner's Exchange, where the Guildmaster Halia Thornton greeted them with a smile and congratulations on defeating the Redbrands. Her expression cooled noticeably when she learned Glasstaff had escaped, and she quickly pivoted to asking whether the party had found any correspondence among his personal effects, claiming she wanted to ensure local trade would no longer be disrupted. She offered a hundred gold pieces for any letters they had recovered. Zenvon rebuffed her, suggesting the information was worth far more, and the two parted on carefully polite terms — but the party walked away convinced that Halia Thornton knew far more about the Redbrands than she was letting on.
