# Chapter 01 Phandalin: The Threshold Awaits

Date: 2026-05-22

## Summary
The road to Phandalin began simply enough — a contract, a wagon full of provisions, and the promise of easy coin. Zenvon Foreput, a rogue whose work in Neverwinter had grown dangerously complicated, had signed on to escort a delivery for a dwarf named Gundren Rockseeker, who had already ridden ahead with his warrior escort, Sildar Hallwinter. Joining Zenvon Foreput on the slow wagon were three unlikely companions: Pip, a traveler drawn to frontier towns; Sister Maela, a cleric answering a troubling letter from an old contact; and Veyra of the Blue Candle, a tiefling searching for her missing mentor, who had vanished while investigating fragments of an ancient stone. Veyra carried with her a charcoal map covered in frantic, conspiratorial annotations, a blue candle wrapped in cloth, and a half-burned note that read: "Was in Phandalin. The town is not a prize. It is the threshold."

The journey took an unsettling turn when the wagon's wheel struck a black stone half-buried in the road, which shattered beneath it with a sound like breaking glass. In that same instant, Zenvon Foreput glimpsed something that made his blood run cold — the shadow of a tall, broken obelisk stretching across the road, though nothing stood there to cast it. Veyra's blue candle pulsed with a single, silent flicker of light, as if responding to something ancient and invisible. When Zenvon Foreput pressed Veyra about it, she admitted she didn't fully understand the candle's behavior, only that it was somehow connected to her mentor's disappearance — and that he had been investigating fragments of exactly such an obelisk before he vanished. Zenvon Foreput quietly pocketed the cracked black stone and said nothing more of it.

The party had barely turned onto the Triboar Trail when they came around a bend and found the road in disarray. Two horses wandered riderless, sniffing at ransacked belongings scattered across the dirt. Before anyone could make sense of the scene, four goblins erupted from the dense thickets on either side of the road. The fight was desperate and chaotic — arrows flew wide, blades glanced off leather, and Sister Maela was struck twice, left barely clinging to life. Veyra, terrified and crying that she was too young to die, nonetheless crawled out from beneath the wagon long enough to unleash bolts of magical force that felled one of the attackers. Pip charged into the fray with reckless enthusiasm, shouting that he was made for this, and ultimately delivered the killing blow on the last goblin standing. Zenvon Foreput, after several frustrating misses, finally closed the distance on one of the creatures and cut it down with a flurry of shortsword and dagger, while a final arrow from his shortbow ended the skirmish entirely.

In the aftermath, the truth of what had happened on that road became grimly clear. The horses were identified as belonging to Gundren Rockseeker and Sildar Hallwinter. Their saddlebags had been looted, and an empty leather map case lay discarded in the dirt — whatever map it had contained was gone. Pip spotted a trail heading north through the thickets, and Zenvon Foreput followed it carefully, finding signs that about a dozen goblins had come and gone along the path — and that two human-sized bodies had been dragged away from the ambush site. Gundren and Sildar had been taken alive.

Zenvon Foreput made the call to press on to Phandalin first, complete the delivery, and return for Gundren with a clear head and a full night's rest. The town emerged from the wooded hillside as a modest collection of log buildings set among the crumbling stone ruins of a far older settlement — a place that had once been much grander. At Barthen's Provisions, the largest trading post in town, a young woman named Elmina Barthen paid out the promised coin and immediately asked after Gundren with genuine worry in her eyes. Zenvon Foreput held his tongue at first, unsure who could be trusted, but Sister Maela had no such patience — she stepped forward and told Elmina plainly that they had found the horses, the ransacked camp, and the signs of two people dragged away, though they believed both men had been alive when taken. Elmina's face fell, and she urged the party desperately to go find him.

It was Veyra who then shed light on why Gundren's disappearance might be about far more than a simple robbery. Elmina revealed that Gundren had been thrilled in recent weeks, convinced he had discovered the lost mine of the Phandelver Pact in the nearby hills. Veyra knew the history well: more than five hundred years ago, dwarves, gnomes, and human spellcasters had forged an alliance to share a wondrous cavern called Wave Echo Cave, home to a magical forge where powerful artifacts could be crafted. The alliance had been shattered when bandits and evil wizards attacked the cave in a catastrophic battle that destroyed much of the cavern and buried its location forever. For centuries, treasure seekers had searched for it in vain — and now Gundren believed he had found it. The empty map case suddenly felt like a very heavy absence.

The party settled in for the night at the Stonehill Inn, a sturdy roadhouse at the center of town where locals nursed their drinks and eyed the newcomers with quiet curiosity. Two humans and a gnome sat among the patrons, glancing over before returning to their own conversations. Zenvon Foreput told Elmina they would rest and set out at first light to follow the goblin trail north. The warmth of the common room and the smell of ale offered a brief reprieve, but the weight of what lay ahead was already settling over the table — a missing employer, an ancient lost mine, a cryptic note about thresholds, and a black stone sitting in Zenvon Foreput's pocket that had made the shadow of a broken obelisk appear from nowhere.
