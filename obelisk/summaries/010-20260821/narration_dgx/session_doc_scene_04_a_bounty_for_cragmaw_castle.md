Morning arrived the way mornings do — too soon, and with the taste of last night's ale still on my tongue. We came down the stairs at the Stonehill Inn with our packs half-fastened and our minds already on the road, and there was Sildar Hallwinter sitting at a table, looking like a man who had placed himself in our path on purpose.

He wanted us to sit with him. We had things to do, places to be, and I could feel the whole party leaning toward the door like hounds on a scent. Zenvon said something about hurrying. Sildar just wanted breakfast. There was a whole round of back-and-forth where we tried to figure out whether he wanted something from us or simply wanted company, and the answer, as it turned out, was both.

"I just want you to know something," he said, settling back. "I want you to find the castle. The Lords' Alliance would like you to find Cragmaw Castle."

And then, the part that matters: "I think that there would be a good payout for somebody who were to find it."

Zenvon asked if just finding it was enough. Sildar shook his head. We'd need to locate it, defeat its leaders, drive them off. The whole thing. Zenvon said we'd talked about this assignment before — and Sildar pointed out, gently, that we'd never actually settled on a price. The only bounty we'd discussed was Iarno's, and that was a separate matter. A different purse.

"Oh, okay — so this looks like more money, then. Cool."

That was Zenvon, doing the arithmetic in his head. And then he said we could talk about it once we were back from Conyberry, and Sildar leaned forward and let that hang in the air for a moment before he delivered the hook: a large amount of money for someone who was going to take care of it.

I nudged Zenvon. "Can we at least find out how much?"

He gave me a look that said he was absolutely not going to be the one to ask, which meant I was going to have to be the one to ask. I stood up. I could feel the whole room — well, the four of us plus Sildar — watching me, and I decided that the best approach was formality. Respect. A man who had been addressed properly was a man who could be talked into a number.

"Mr. Sildar?"

*Obviously, Pip is trying to suck up to him.*

"Mr. Sildar, sir—"

"Yes, Pip! You don't have to call me *Mr.* You can just call me Sildar."

"Oh, thank you, thank you, Sildar. Well — how much money would there be?"

He let the question sit there, enjoying it, the way a fisherman enjoys the tug before he sets the hook. Then he said the words I will remember for the rest of my life.

"I think that the Lords' Alliance thinks this is an important thing. So if you were to deal with everything there, 500 gold pieces would definitely enter your pockets."

Five hundred. I heard the number and my brain stopped doing anything except turning it over like a coin in the light. Five hundred gold. I've never held five hundred gold in my life. I've never been in the same room as five hundred gold. I turned to Zenvon, and I'm sure the grin on my face was visible from the next county over.

"Boss, are we gonna go do that? But, I mean—"

I caught myself. I was going to say the wrong thing. I could feel it coming out of my mouth before it fully formed. So I turned back to Sildar, and tried to put some dignity back in my voice.

"Well, I think that's very generous of you, Mr. Sildar."

He corrected me again — just Sildar, just Sildar — and I nodded along, not really hearing it, because in my head I was already spending the money. New armour. A better shield. Maybe that horse I'd had my eye on, the chestnut one with the attitude problem.

Sildar stretched back in his chair and said, with the careful casualness of a man who knows exactly what he's doing, that the Lords' Alliance would look very kindly on someone who took care of Cragmaw Castle.

I did not fully understand what that meant at the time. Favors, opportunities — I had a dim sense that it was good, that being on the good side of an organisation that could put five hundred gold on a table was worth having. What I did not understand was that he was talking about me. Specifically. When he looked at Cragmaw Castle, he was looking at a path that ended with a slightly-too-eager fighter in the service of the Lords' Alliance.

Zenvon pulled me aside. Not far, just a step or two, but it felt like being yanked out of a warm fire and into the cold.

"I'm going to pull Pip a little aside and ask him to contain his excitement. Because — just think about it. Five hundred gold pieces divided by four of us is how much? You're just going to get 125 gold pieces."

He said it the way you'd talk to a child who had just seen a candy shop and was about to run inside with no plan. "Just don't be too excited, man."

I blinked. One hundred and twenty-five gold. Still a fortune. Still more money than I'd held at any one time in my life. But it wasn't five hundred. It wasn't the armour and the horse and the comfort. It was a share. A stake. The difference between winning the purse and being one of the hands that carried it.

"That's… that's good advice, boss."

I turned back to Sildar, and I tried to dial it down. I really did. I've never been good at hiding what I'm feeling — it's a failing I've had since I was a boy, and it has cost me at cards, at dice, and at just about every negotiation I've ever tried to conduct. But I gave it my best attempt.

"Well, I mean, you know, it's a chunk of change, but we're… you know, we'll keep that under advisement, Sildar."

That was me at my most businessman. That was me performing the role of a man who had been offered a job and was considering it carefully, rather than a man who had to physically restrain himself from grabbing Sildar's hand and shaking it until his arm came off.

Sildar nodded, satisfied. Good enough. We discussed where we were headed next, and Zenvon held the line — he'd given his word to Daran, so Old Owl Well came first. Cragmaw Castle would wait. The money would wait. Everything would still be there when we got back.

The meal wound down. Toblen came to the door to wish us well, and we started gathering our gear. I was still half-floating on that number when Sildar called out one last thing.

"However — the last thing, as Sildar says, he looks at the magical sword you guys collected from Tresendar Manor."

He looked at Talon — the blade I'd been carrying since the manor, the one I'd missed with on my first swing. He gave it a long, appraising look, and then he said, "my, my, my — that is a fine, noble sword, Pip."

Zenvon answered first — something about earning it, bravery — and then realised Sildar was talking to me, not to him. Sildar had addressed me by name. He had looked at the sword in my hands and called it noble.

And I — I missed it.

I was still thinking about the gold. I was still turning over the number, dividing it, re-dividing it, wondering if there was some way to get a bigger share. Sildar's compliment went right past me like an arrow I never saw coming. I did not hear the door he was opening. I did not hear the words he was really saying beneath the words he was actually saying.

The others heard it. I know now that Zenvon heard it — he looked at Sildar, looked at me, made a calculation of his own, and chose to say nothing. He knew. He sat there with the knowledge that Sildar Hallwinter was not just praising me, that the sword was a test and a signal all at once, that the Lords' Alliance had an open spot and a candidate in mind — and he decided, in that moment, to keep it to himself.

I walked out of the inn with a fine noble sword at my hip, five hundred gold pieces glittering in my head, and not the faintest idea that I had just been offered something that could not be split four ways.
