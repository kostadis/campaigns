The Miner's Exchange had a table in the back, and someone had left a map on it. That was all we needed. We stood around it like it was a ledger that owed us money, which, in a way, it did.

I counted the markers. Old Owl Well. Wyvern Tor — already accepted, a hundred and twenty gold from Harbin Wester, and Pip had done the negotiating on that one, so I trusted the number. Agatha's Lair. Thundertree. Cragmaw Castle.

Cragmaw Hideout was still on there, and I said as much — that one we'd already cleared. The goblin cave we walked through on the way in. Not a quest. A memory.

Then there was Cragmaw Castle, sitting in the forest with no road to it and no one in town who knew the way. That was the main line. I could see it the way I'd see a bill I couldn't pay yet. The side jobs were the income. Power, magic items, information — the DM said it plainly enough. Travel happened on the road, on foot, thirty miles a day if we pushed, and things could find us between here and there.

I did the math on that the way I do. Old Owl Well was a pair of boots from Daran Edermath, and I had recently come into magic I did not fully understand — the trick with the hand, the trick with the sound. A man who is learning to do something new should go somewhere that tests it. That is just sense.

Also, I was curious about the bugbears.

Nobody said that part. The three of them were loose somewhere east of town, wounded, having watched their friends burn. Tresendar Manor was not finished. I chose to leave it unfinished. I wanted to see what they did with the time.

"Old Owl Well," I said, and pointed.

That was the whole strategic discussion, really. The rest was feet. The Triboar Trail runs north and east, and there was an orchard on the way out of town. I decided we would stop there first.
