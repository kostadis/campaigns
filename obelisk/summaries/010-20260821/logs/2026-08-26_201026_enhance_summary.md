# Chapter 24 The Council at Sporedome

Date: 2026-08-23

## Summary
The party arrived at Sporedome, the great fungal amphitheater that served as the staging ground for the coalition's march on Maerimydra. The cavern buzzed with a myriad of voices — secretive Drow mingling with tall mushroom men, stout dwarves conversing with red-feathered humans, and faction leaders locked in furious debate. Among the assembled faces were Solom Ned'razak, the cryptic Drow Archmage; Sovereign Agaricusof the Myconids, who communicated through drifting spores; a Mind Flayer representative watching from the shadows; and the newly appointed First Lord of Hillsfar, a man the party recognized as the very guard they had once met at the city's gates. Perched atop a tall mushroom, a little girl in a dirty dress sat alone with her arm around a blind black goat — the mysterious Elisande, who had a habit of appearing at the most critical junctures of their journey.

Sovereign Agaricusconveyed through his connecting spores that a fierce battle had recently taken place between the demon queen of fungi and his colony, a conflict that had only been resolved with the help of outside adventurers and the demon Juiblex. The revelation reminded the party of Alder's grand design — her "Plan A" had been another adventuring party working to collapse the demonic incursion from within, while the party themselves were her "Plan B," tasked with intercepting the demon lord Graz'zt when he inevitably manifested in Maerimydra. Solom Ned'razak, for his part, announced that his experiment to purge fiendish taint from a fire giant had succeeded, though the process required the subject to be subdued for ten full days, making it useless as a battlefield solution. The news sparked excited debate among the assembled factions, but the hard truth was clear: there was no shortcut. The city would have to be taken by force.

Eleniel Alasindale, known as the Moonsilver Herald, raised her arms and a magical hush fell over the amphitheater. She addressed the crowd with calm authority, outlining the plan: three coalition forces would deploy to advanced positions and establish bases for a coordinated assault on Maerimydra, while two smaller teams would scout alternate approaches and gather local allies. The primary objective, she made clear, was not to seize the city but to kill Helde Helsfon, the fiend-born fire giant who commanded the demonic forces. Without their leader, the fire giants would descend into chaos and begin tearing each other apart. When Akritas asked bluntly whether they could actually take the city, Eleniel answered with equal bluntness — they had enough to make the enemy look the wrong way for a while, and that had been enough before.

Knowing what the rest of the coalition did not, the party pulled Eleniel aside to warn her about Graz'zt. At first, she dismissed the warning as one of many dire predictions she heard before every campaign. But when Bramgrim produced Alder's autograph — written on his sword during their legendary encounter with the Harper hero — Eleniel's expression shifted from dismissal to bitter recognition. She accused Alder of engineering the entire situation, sending them to die cleaning up a mess the Harpers had made. After a tense exchange, however, she acknowledged that nothing about the plan could change: the fire giants had to be defeated regardless, as it was the only path to Graz'zt. She told the party plainly that once they were inside Maerimydra, they would be on their own — and when Daein pointed out they always had been, she offered no argument.

The party then turned to the Mind Flayer, hoping to gain intelligence on Graz'zt before the battle. The Mind Flayer initially claimed that all debts between them were settled, having already provided the party with two powerful magical items. Bramgrim and Felkur appealed to the creature's pride, arguing that the demonic incursion was a threat worth finishing, and the Mind Flayer — eyeing Bramgrim's brain with unsettling interest — eventually agreed to provide a detailed accounting of Graz'zt's known abilities. Akritas kept her bow quietly at the ready throughout the entire negotiation, just in case the Mind Flayer's interest in Bramgrim's brain became more than academic.

No sooner had the Mind Flayer departed than a short halfling with the look of a seasoned rogue pulled Bramgrim aside and delivered a grim warning. The Drow alliance, she explained, was held together by a single thread: fear of Solom Ned'razak. Drow society operated on a brutal principle — if any faction attempted to kill another and failed to eliminate every last member, the remaining factions were honor-bound to destroy the attacker. This was what kept the coalition from collapsing into civil war. The danger, however, was that Dorina T'sarran — a vampire Drow cleric and the former ruler of Szith Morcane — had been spotted in the area and had sworn revenge against Solom. Daein recognized the name immediately: Dorina shared a surname with Velasta Tsaran, and both were clerics of the same dark goddess. Dorina was Velasta's aunt, and if she found Solom alive and told her niece, the lie that had kept Velasta focused on Maerimydra would unravel, and the entire coalition could come apart before a single blow was struck.

With the political landscape mapped and the dangers catalogued, the party turned to the practical work of assigning their allied factions to the battle's objectives. The Stone Giants, well-matched against fire giants, were sent to the Northern Forward Base. The Hidden, a faction of skilled thieves, were assigned to a scouting mission deep in the Underdark. The leaderless Never Answers were paired with the friendly undead forces, while Rilira's former slaves and Grundar's Duergar were sent to the Eastern Base. Quilbrin's Drow were kept in reserve as a tactical backup — a decision Rilira objected to loudly until Daein's persuasive reasoning won her over. Solom Ned'razak watched the entire arrangement with a quiet, cryptic smile.

As the factions were organized, two more parties approached with private requests. A representative of the Order of the Gauntlet told the party of the Cave of Black Whisperers, where a dwarven leader had risen as a powerful mummy and held his kin in undeath — a mission brought to them by Glenna Moonsmith, who begged for her ancestor to be laid to rest. Then, in a far darker corner of the preparations, a Zhentarim operative approached Felkur with an expensive bottle of rare wine and a proposition: in exchange for cementing a smuggling partnership, the Black Network wanted the party to retrieve newborn children of Graz'zt from Castle Maerimydra, to be raised as demonic operatives. Felkur listened with apparent interest, privately planning to give the Zhentarim minor items as a decoy while keeping the real prize — a powerful ledger — for himself, ensuring that any magical investigation would lead to the Black Network rather than him. As the armies began to march out of Sporedome to the sound of cheering, Eleniel shared a final somber moment with Daein, confessing her fear of what awaited them. Daein reassured her by mentioning the extremely strong alcohol he had been brewing — enough to knock someone out if they were captured. Eleniel smiled despite herself, put on her helmet, and headed off to lead her forces into the dark.

Bramgrim also produced a written quest log of all the party's open obligations, of which Daein disputed exactly one entry: "We don't owe anything to Pipyap."

The revelation reminded the party of Alder's grand design — her "Plan A" had been another adventuring party working to collapse the demonic incursion from within, using a magical device to draw all the demons to one place so they would slaughter each other, while the party themselves were her "Plan B," tasked with intercepting the demon lord Graz'zt when he inevitably manifested in Maerimydra after his own counter-plan. Solom Ned'razak, for his part, announced that his experiment to purge fiendish taint from a fire giant had succeeded, though the process required the subject to be subdued for ten full days, making it useless as a battlefield solution — "It's unfortunately not a mass solution, but what it does mean… is that it offers us a path." The news sparked excited debate among the assembled factions, but the hard truth was clear: there was no shortcut. The city would have to be taken by force.

Eleniel Alasindale, known as the Moonsilver Herald, raised her arms and a magical hush fell over the amphitheater. She addressed the crowd with calm authority — "We are about to undertake the largest military operation in recent memory" — outlining the plan: three coalition forces would deploy to advanced positions and establish bases for a coordinated assault on Maerimydra, while two smaller teams would scout alternate approaches and gather local allies. The primary objective, she made clear, was not to seize the city but to kill Helde Helsfon, the fiend-born fire giant who commanded the demonic forces and was in the city preparing an invasion of the Sunlight Realms. Without their leader, the fire giants would descend into chaos and begin tearing each other apart. When Akritas asked bluntly whether they could actually take the city, Eleniel answered with equal bluntness — "We have 7 contingents, three which have never held a line, and Maerimydra has a demon lord in the courtyard. We have enough to make them look the wrong way for a while. That has been enough before. Ask me about Flan sometime." She closed the address without room for doubt: "There is no going back now. We mobilize in an hour."

Knowing what the rest of the coalition did not — the GM was emphatic that nobody else in the room suspected Graz'zt, from Vuhm who doesn't think in demonic terms, to Quil'bryn who thinks only in pointy objects, to Solom who "is a wizard, and you never know what the wizards want" — the party pulled Eleniel aside to warn her about Graz'zt. At first, she dismissed the warning as one of many dire predictions she heard before every campaign: "I have an army to lead to war, and you're telling me a few minutes before I'm about to lead them to war that I have to change my plans about some hypothetical outcome that may occur based on information I have no possibility of verifying." But when Bramgrim produced Alder's autograph — written on his sword during their legendary encounter with the Harper hero — Eleniel's expression shifted from laughter ("Alder? You spoke to Alder?") to bitter recognition. She accused Alder of engineering the entire situation, sending them to die cleaning up a mess the Harpers had made. After a tense exchange, however, she acknowledged that nothing about the plan could change: "Oh god, I'm really gonna say this.Our only hope is if the… five of you manage to make it to Graz'zt. The only way you're going to make it to Graz'zt is if we make it into Maerimydra and defeat the fire giants." She told the party plainly that once they were inside Maerimydra, they would be on their own — Daein drew the boundary first ("Yes, we're gonna get you to Maerimydra, but from there on, you're alone"), and when he pointed out they always had been, she offered no argument.

The party then turned to the Mind Flayer, hoping to gain intelligence on Graz'zt before the battle. The creature greeted them as "the ones that helped us with the rogue thought," but claimed all debts between them were settled, having already provided the party with two magical items of significant power and wealth — "as I see it, the ledger is clean." Worse, the news of Graz'zt made it waver entirely: "This forces us to reconsider our involvement in this war… Why would we risk ourselves? The Mind Flayers have survived demonic incursions. Probably engineered a few." The Drow alliance, she explained, was held together by a single thread: fear of Solom Ned'razak. Drow society operated on a brutal principle — if any faction attempted to kill another and failed to eliminate every last member, the remaining factions were honor-bound to destroy the attacker, as in the Menzoberranzan story of a noble house exterminated for leaving a single child of its rivals alive. This was what kept the coalition from collapsing into civil war. The danger, however, was that Dorina T'sarran — a vampire Drow cleric and the former ruler of Szith Morcane — had been spotted in the area and had sworn revenge against Solom. Daein recognized the name immediately: Dorina shared a surname with Velasta Tsaran, and both were clerics of the same dark goddess. Dorina was Velasta's aunt, and if she found Solom alive and told her niece, the lie that had kept Velasta focused on Maerimydra would unravel — for Velasta still believed the fire giants had killed Solom, and as the GM observed, "at this point in time, you can't even keep track of all of the lies you've told everybody."

With the political landscape mapped and the dangers catalogued, the party turned to the practical work of assigning their allied factions to the battle's objectives. The Stone Giants, well-matched against fire giants, were sent to the Northern Forward Base. The Hidden, a faction of skilled thieves, were assigned to a scouting mission deep in the Underdark. The leaderless Never Answers were paired with the friendly undead forces, while Rilira's former slaves and Grundar's Duergar were sent to the Eastern Base. The party claimed the Western Forward Base for themselves. Quilbrin's Drow — whose leader pointedly reminded Daein, "let us not forget our arrangement" — were kept in reserve as a tactical backup, a decision Rilira objected to loudly until Daein's persuasive reasoning (a 25) won her over. Solom Ned'razak watched the entire arrangement with a quiet, cryptic smile — one Daein found so infuriating he asked whether he could hit him.

As the factions were organized, two more parties approached with private requests. A representative of the Order of the Gauntlet, Zern Xerkstil, told the party of the Cave of Black Whisperers, where a dwarven leader had risen as a powerful mummy and held his kin in undeath — a mission brought to them by Glenna Moonsmith, who begged for her ancestor to be laid to rest. Then, in a far darker corner of the preparations, a Zhentarim operative approached Felkur with an expensive bottle of rare wine and a proposition: addressing him by his real name, Oldenwood, praising his "masterful team's ability to move goods from one location to another," and citing rumors that an arcanaloth tower had been breached after the party left Maerimydra and that the Cambions had withdrawn support from the fire giants ("Perhaps," said Felkur, twice), the Black Network offered to move any goods out of the Underdark "for a small shipping fee" — and asked, in exchange for cementing the partnership, that the party retrieve newborn children of Graz'zt from Castle Maerimydra, to be raised as demonic operatives. Felkur listened with apparent interest, privately planning to give the Zhentarim minor items as a decoy while keeping the real prize — a powerful ledger — for himself, ensuring that any magical investigation would lead to the Black Network rather than him. As the armies began to march out of Sporedome to the sound of cheering, Eleniel shared a final somber moment with Daein, confessing her fear of what awaited them: "Daein, I remember when we first met. I didn't think we would be here, rushing into death or to glory… Oh, if the Demon Lord captures us, I really do hope we die." Daein reassured her by mentioning the extremely strong alcohol he had been brewing — enough to knock someone out if they were captured. Eleniel smiled despite herself, put on her helmet, and headed off to lead her forces into the dark.

## Locations
### Sporedome
A massive fungal amphitheater serving as the staging ground for the coalition forces. It is filled with a diverse mix of Drow, Myconids, humans, dwarves, and other factions preparing for the march on Maerimydra — "secretive drow mixed with tall mushroom men, while stout dwarves talk with humans wearing helmets capped with red feathers," with excitement warring against panic in a dozen furious conversations. Sovereign Agaricus knows everything that happens inside Sporedome and pays zero attention to anything outside it. The session's planning and negotiations all take place here, and the armies mobilize from it within the hour.

### Szith Morcane
A Drow outpost where political tensions recently boiled over, resulting in the disintegration of Alasta by a clone of Solom Ned'razak, and where Felkur's commanding speech united the fractious coalition. Its allied force provided the coalition with the location and avenues of approach to Maerimydra. Its former ruler, the vampire Dorina T'sarran, has resurfaced nearby with a vendetta. Who rules it is explicitly the *second* problem — after the fire giants.

### Maerimydra
The target of the upcoming military operation, a city deep in the Underdark currently occupied by fire giants and demonic forces under the command of Helde Helsfon, who is present in the city preparing an invasion of the Sunlight Realms — and where Graz'zt is expected to manifest as his escape route, a fact known only to the party. The assault plan involves three forward bases (Northern, Eastern, Western), an underdoor scouting route that may feed the lake of blood, and gates that will be contested once the forward positions fall. Solom previously tried to make a deal with its fire giants, and rumors say its Cambions have since withdrawn their support.

### Hillsfar
A city in the sunlight realms whose newly appointed First Lord, formerly a simple guard the party once met — the very first guard they encountered at its gates — attends the war council and publicly praises the party as its heroes and defenders. He has risen to power "somehow, with all of the chaos and mayhem of Hillsfar," and arrives pre-flattered by Isthana Rao's "glorious words" about the party.

### Cave of Black Whisperers
A haunted cavern where a group of dwarves was once slaughtered by the Drow of Szith Morcane — surprised, then massacred. Their leader has since risen as a powerful mummy, "and it is [his] evil forces that [cause] the dwarves to languish in undeath." Glenna Moonsmith has begged the Order of the Gauntlet to lay her ancestor to rest permanently. The party plans to go there first, before taking their position at the Western Forward Base.

### Castle Maerimydra
The seat of power within the occupied city, rumored by the Zhentarim to be the location where the children of the demon lord Graz'zt — including the newborns the Black Network covets — can be found somewhere in or near its grounds.

## NPCs

He leads a Drow faction that is ultimately kept in reserve as a tactical backup force, per an arrangement with the party ("let us not forget our arrangement" — everyone else goes in first to soften the targets).

### Helde Helsfon
The leader of the fire giant and demonic forces in Maerimydra. He is a fiend-born fire giant — one of the giants Graz'zt infused with demonic power — and the primary assassination target of the coalition's military operation. He is present in the city preparing the invasion of the Sunlight Realms, and his death is expected to throw the chaotic, demon-infused fire giants into disorganized infighting.

### Alder
A legendary Harper hero described as a 21st-level rogue who was involved in trapping Lolth in a dimensional prison, working an arrangement with Vhaeraun — the event that triggered all the madness in the Underdark as Lolth fights to escape. She is famous throughout the north: she helped defeat the Drow Empire's incursion, and her son saved the world from the dragons. She has the personality of an irritated middle-aged mom and "this annoying habit… of showing up wherever she wants, making an announcement, and then leaving." She previously tasked the party with being her 'Plan B' to intercept Graz'zt in Maerimydra, after Graz'zt discovered her Plan A. Bramgrim obtained her autograph on his sword — and the "short halfling who looks like a middle-aged rogue" who pulled Bramgrim aside with the Dorina warning is, by Bramgrim's own account ("Alder explained to me…"), Alder herself.

### Dorina T'sarran
A vampire Drow cleric of Kiaransalee and the former ruler of Szith Morcane. She is the aunt of Velasta Tsaran — the two are emphatically *not* "good and happy" family — and has been seen in the area, having vowed revenge against Solom Ned'razak. If she discovers Solom alive, she will tell Velasta, unraveling the party's lie that the fire giants killed him. Her potential interference poses a serious threat to the coalition's stability: "We cannot afford her meddling."

### Rilira
Leader of the former Drow slaves — the mob the party hired — who vociferously objects to keeping forces in reserve, arguing that the Drow knights should be on the front lines. She is ultimately persuaded by Daein's strategic reasoning (a 25) to accept the plan, a moment that draws a smile from Solom Ned'razak. Her contingent, alongside Grundar's Duergar, is assigned to the Eastern Base.

### Glenna Moonsmith
The owner of the Pudding Plow and The Stop, a familiar contact of the party — and, as the GM reminds the table, Charles' girlfriend. She has approached the Order of the Gauntlet and begged them to lay her dwarven ancestor — now risen as a powerful mummy in the Cave of Black Whisperers — to rest permanently.

### Mind Flayer
A Mind Flayer representative present at the Sporedome war council, from the colony the party once helped "with the rogue thought." He initially claims all debts are settled after providing the party with two magical items of significant power and wealth ("as I see it, the ledger is clean"), and news of Graz'zt nearly drives his colony out of the war entirely — mind flayer doctrine being to be "anywhere but near the demonic incursion," having survived several and "probably engineered a few." He is persuaded to provide a detailed list of Graz'zt's known abilities. He eyes Bramgrim's brain with unsettling interest ("What a tasty brain"), expresses a wish to see Felkur drunk, and reserves the right to re-evaluate further involvement as the situation unfolds.

### Zern Xerkstil
A member of the Order of the Gauntlet who approaches the party at Sporedome. He acknowledges they have not sworn fealty to his order — "we've seen how you've been focused for good" — but brings them Glenna Moonsmith's plea regarding the Cave of Black Whisperers anyway, a mission with "all the stench" of Gauntlet work. Declining would earn his very big disapproval. He knows nothing of the Zhentarim's competing offer happening steps away.

### Hamadh
The representative of the cult of Vhaeraun among the Szith Morcane factions, present at the war council and acknowledged by Eleniel alongside Solom, Rilira, and Quilbrin. He was one of the parties in the political standoff that erupted back in Szith Morcane before Felkur's ballista speech.

## Items
### Hellfire Projectors
Powerful ballistic weapons extracted from the fire giant forge after the death of King Dengor. The party managed to secure three finished projectors and components for four more before escaping the rampaging fire salamanders.

### Rare Metals
Valuable materials worth roughly a thousand gold pieces salvaged from the crumbling forge during the party's narrow escape from the fire salamanders.

### Alder's Autograph
The signature of the legendary Harper hero Alder, written on Bramgrim's sword. Bramgrim excitedly obtained it when meeting the famous figure. During the session, it is presented to Eleniel Alasindale as proof of the party's intelligence source, causing her to recognize its authenticity and take their warnings about Graz'zt seriously — though with bitter frustration. Bramgrim himself has quietly realized that carrying the signature of the hero who banished Lolth into a dimensional prison, while working alongside followers of Lolth, "is not a really good plan" — saved only by the fact that the banishment is not commonly known.

### The Ledger
A significant document that Felkur intends to steal from a tower during the invasion of Maerimydra. His plan is to get blackout drunk before taking it so he has no memory of the theft, making magical scrying useless — "Because I won't know that I stole it. I'll just know that I have it." He also plans to give the Zhentarim minor items as a decoy so any investigation would lead to them rather than him. In a newly canonized twist, the ledger's record of Felkur's own surname is misspelled — and Felkur, dyslexic and indifferent, never noticed, having signed his name in a variety of different ways over the years, all of which he believed were correct. This is the in-world reason no one has ever been able to track him down.

### Rare Bottle of Wine
An expensive and rare vintage offered by a Zhentarim operative to Felkur as a gesture of good faith during their secret negotiations, as the operative attempted to wine and dine him into a smuggling partnership — sitting him down away from the eyes of the Emerald Enclave, the Harpers, and the Order of the Gauntlet.

### Bramgrim's Quest Log
A written log, kept in Bramgrim's voice, describing all of the party's open quests and situating them in the context of the adventure. Because it is Bramgrim's record, "it's entirely possible that he got something wrong" — and Daein has already disputed one entry, insisting the party owes nothing to Pipyap. It also records Felkur's goal of "FU money" and the party's ambition to run their own business.
