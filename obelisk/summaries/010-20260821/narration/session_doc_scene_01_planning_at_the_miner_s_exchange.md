The map gave us five places and only one route we did not know.

Old Owl Well. Wyvern Tor. Agatha’s Lair. Thundertree. Cragmaw Castle.

The list I had copied out made it look cleaner than it was. Names in lines, promises beside them, payments where there were payments. A good list should reduce uncertainty. This one only arranged it.

Wyvern Tor had already been agreed. Harbin Wester would pay one hundred and twenty gold, after Pip corrected his first number. That made it a proper contract. Agatha’s Lair meant carrying the jeweled comb to the banshee and asking about Bowgentle’s spellbook. Thundertree had Mirna Dendrar’s necklace, although calling that a quest was generous. She had told us where her family left it. We wanted it.

There was also the Cragmaw Hideout marked on the map. We had already cleared it. Goblin cave, dead end, finished business. Someone had put a marker where the work used to be.

I understood the error. It is comforting when a mark on a map means there is something waiting to be solved. Less comforting when it means we have forgotten to remove the mark.

Cragmaw Castle was different.

Gundren Rockseeker was there, or had been taken there. The Spider was connected to it. The Cragmaws knew it. We knew it was somewhere inside the forest.

Somewhere is not a location.

That made the castle the main business and also the one place we could not simply walk toward. We needed a road, a guide, a prisoner who knew the route, or information from someone local. At present we had none of these.

The other places offered something more immediate. Experience. Information. Magic items. Power, if that was the correct word for it. We could travel to one, come back better prepared, and perhaps find the missing piece that would turn somewhere in the forest into an actual destination.

Travel itself would take time. The marks on the map sat close together because the Sword Coast had been reduced to a sheet of paper. On the ground there would be miles between them, weather, camps, and whatever chose the road on the same day we did. No one was going to lift us from the Miner's Exchange and put us down beside a well.

I looked at the routes again.

There were local matters too. Halia Thornton had wanted the Spider’s correspondence and had offered too little for it. She knew enough to want the letters, but not enough to name their proper value—or she believed we did not. Either possibility remained open.

Then there were the bugbears.

We had left Tresendar Manor through fire. They had not followed us through it. That did not mean they were dead. It meant we did not know where they were.

Three wounded bugbears, loose near Phandalin, with knowledge of Cragmaw Castle.

Useful, if found.

Dangerous, if not.

I could have proposed going back immediately. We could search the manor, find what the fire had left, recover Thel Dendrar’s body, and determine whether the bugbears had escaped. That would close several accounts.

I did not propose it.

I wanted to see what they would do.

A creature running from a fire makes choices quickly. Where it goes, whom it warns, what it takes with it—these things can tell more than questions. If the bugbears remained near the manor, we could deal with them later. If they moved, they might show us something useful.

This was curiosity. I priced it as patience.

Old Owl Well stayed under my hand on the map.

I had magic now. Not borrowed light, not Veyra’s knowledge explained to me afterwards. Mine. A hand that could reach where I could not. A sound and force I could push into a room. An image that could make someone act on the wrong information.

Old Owl Well was ancient. There was magic there, or enough evidence of it that Daran Edermath wanted the place investigated. He had offered Boots of Striding and Springing for the work. The payment was useful. The well itself was more useful.

If something there could teach me what these new powers were worth, then it was not a side matter. It was preparation.

So I chose Old Owl Well.

The Triboar Trail would carry us most of the way. We could cover roughly thirty miles in a day if the road allowed it, less if it did not. There would be no shortcut except good planning, and good planning was what the map was for.

The other names remained on the page. Wyvern Tor. Agatha’s Lair. Thundertree. Cragmaw Castle.

Unfinished business does not disappear when you choose something else.

It waits.
