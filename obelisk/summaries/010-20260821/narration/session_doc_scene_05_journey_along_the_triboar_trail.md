We had discussed Conyberry. We did not go to Conyberry.

The road toward Old Owl Well was shorter, and Toblen’s warning had improved the price of delay. Every extra mile in this country cost food, daylight, and another chance for something to find us. So we took the direct road northeast.

I went ahead.

At first the Triboar Trail still carried signs of use: wheel ruts pressed over older wheel ruts, boot marks, hooves. Enough traffic to call it a road. Not enough to see another wagon anywhere on it.

The High Road carried the proper trade north and south. Neverwinter had walls, guards, ships, markets, and people who could complain about prices while believing the roads would remain open. The Triboar Trail went east and west into country where such beliefs became expensive.

We covered near thirty miles the first day.

Nothing attacked us.

This was good. It was not reassuring.

The abandoned caravan told us more than an uneventful road could. It had been attacked and left where it stopped. Whatever could be carried had been carried. What remained was the evidence of interruption: a journey planned, paid for, and ended without reaching its destination.

I inspected it for an answer.

There was none.

The attackers might have come down from Wyvern Tor. That would agree with what we had heard. It might also have been goblins, Redbrands, or someone whose name had not yet reached Phandalin. The remains did not identify anyone. Evidence does not become better because one answer would be convenient.

Still, it confirmed one thing. The reports were not only reports. Someone was taking caravans on the Triboar Trail, and nobody had cleared them out.

No wagon passed us that day. No traveller appeared around the next bend. But the trail was not dead. Fresh marks crossed old ones, and the ground had not yet softened enough to erase them. People were still using the road.

They were simply using it while knowing this could happen.

That is the practical boundary of civilization. Not a gate. Not a sign. It is the place where a robbed caravan can remain on a road still in use, and each new traveller studies it before continuing.

Neverwinter and the land close around it were the last reliable part. Beyond that, every ruin had several owners and none of them living. Barbarians had passed through. Orcs had passed through. Goblins had passed through. Armies had fought over the same ground, then left it poorer than they found it. The Spellplague had broken what war missed. Gods had interfered. Wizards who should have known better had interfered more.

The country had been charged for all of it.

Night came without an attack. We kept our places and our watch. Maela and Veyra gave us what light we needed; Pip stayed close enough to answer trouble; I watched the dark beyond them.

There are roads where silence means safety. This was not one.

Morning found us intact. We continued northeast.

The ground became rougher beneath us as the day went on. The easier stretches narrowed. Hills rose around the trail, broken and uneven, limiting what I could see ahead and offering too many places for someone else to watch us approach.

Old Owl Well was somewhere among them.

I counted the ridges, the blind approaches, and the distance back to open ground.

Then I kept walking.
