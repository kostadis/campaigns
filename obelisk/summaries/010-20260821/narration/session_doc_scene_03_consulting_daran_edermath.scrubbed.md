Zenvon had already decided we were going back to Daran Edermath before we went to the Stonehill Inn. He wanted to tell him we were leaving for Old Owl Well in the morning, ask what else we needed to know, and then return to town.

It was a sensible plan. Zenvon’s plans often begin sensibly. Trouble starts when someone gives him a writing implement.

We found Daran beside his cottage, clearing underbrush from the edge of the orchard. He was a retired drow adventurer, though the stoop of his back and the careful pace of his work suggested that the adventuring had retired before the rest of him did. He planted his tool in the earth and beamed at us.

“Well, well, well, well, well. I… the heroes of Phandalin!”

I checked behind us. No other heroes. Apparently he meant us.

“Well, I have to say, I'm very proud to hear that you dealt with the Redbrands.”

“Yeah, we walked… it happened as an accident. We didn't intend to, but it was, I guess, God's plan.”

There are several ways to describe our campaign against the Redbrands. Accident is defensible. Divine plan seems unfair to the gods.

“That is the best news I have,” Daran said. “Now, I just have to ask — have you been able to deal with the Old Owl Well problem?”

“No, and that's why we are here. Me and my friends, we were thinking to start tomorrow for the Old Owl Well.”

“Fabulous, fabulous, fabulous.”

“And we are here to talk to you about whether we can get any other details — or whether we have to be careful with anything on the way.”

Daran sat back and considered the eastern horizon.

“Well, if you stick to the Triboar Trail — now, an important thing to realize is, as you're going east on the Triboar Trail…” He paused, gathering enough history to bury us with. “Well, you have to understand. A large chunk of the North — in particular the more you go east — was torn apart by wars, demons, gods, elemental catastrophes. And so things become less and less settled. In fact, Conyberry has been a wreck for years.”

I looked east. Wars, demons, gods, catastrophes, ruins. Professional road security generally prefers roads where the list ends at potholes.

“Do you recommend any shorter or easier paths to get to the Old Owl Well?” Zenvon asked.

“Well, there are two ways you could go. You can take the road, which should be safer — you might even meet the odd occasional traveler. Or you could go through the hills. Of course, when you're going through the hills…”

He let that trail away. The hills contained creatures inclined to regard stretches of wilderness as private property. I understood the principle. I have a shield, and I become territorial when anyone touches it without asking.

“Plus, there are some bandits, according to the mayor — the Lord Mayor.” Daran frowned at the title. “Harbin.”

“At Conyberry?”

“No, he's been saying — and I have to be honest with you, I haven't been following all the details, but let me just see what he says. I don't want to get it wrong… Well, he's been saying there have been several attacks near Wyvern Tor, of caravans. And so I don't know where they'd be acting from, so you might want to be careful there.”

We had already taken Harbin Wester’s money to deal with that problem. More accurately, we had taken the amount I persuaded him the problem was worth. It was comforting to learn that bandits continued to respect my valuation.

“Well, Daran, looks like you know more about Old Owl Well and the best ways to lead us there — but would you like to join us?”

“Well, you know, it's been a long time, and my back hurts, and I've got all the…” Daran stopped and straightened as far as his back allowed. “You know, one of the things about being an old adventurer is: you're an old adventurer because you choose to stop adventuring.”

That was the best advice he gave us.

“Oh, that makes sense. Well, in that case — is there anything you have that you think will be useful to us on the way?”

“Well, I've…” Daran drew himself up. “Keep your eyes up. Make sure your sword is sharpened.”

“Wait, what? Eyes up?”

“Yes.”

Zenvon took out his notes.

I bit the inside of my cheek.

“Yeah, I was going to make notes of it, actually.”

Daran brightened. The advice expanded immediately: back straight, eyes open, head turning, weapons maintained. Each point reminded him of an expedition. Each expedition reminded him of a companion, a narrow escape, a poor campsite, or a buckle that had not been inspected properly.

“Okay, no.”

Too late.

For the next long while, Zenvon stood solemnly recording the accumulated wisdom of keeping watch and not letting equipment rust. I fixed my eyes on a tree behind his shoulder. My mouth hurt. At one point he glanced at me, and I had to disguise the sound trying to escape as a cough.

Eventually he closed the notes.

“Cool. Alright, then, we'll head back.”

He had endured it, though, and somewhere in Daran’s reminiscences there were a few useful scraps about reading tracks and noticing disturbed ground. Zenvon had written those down too. Of course he had.

Then he reopened the notes.

“Okay, I have… oh, Daran, I just recalled a question I wanted to ask you. Tell me more about Netheril. What kind of magic should we expect over there?”

Daran’s pleasure faded.

“Why would you be asking that?”

“Because you mentioned it earlier.”

There was a brief dispute over whether he had. Zenvon consulted the page.

“I… oh, I see, okay. No, actually — the note says Daran called it an old watchtower of Netheril, so…”

The notes had struck back.

“Well! Well, you have to understand, this is an old story. A long time ago, there was an ancient empire — called Netheril, the Netherese Empire. And that Netherese Empire had ships that could fly in the sky. And they had access to magic that mortals no longer have access to. The highest-powered wizard spells nowadays are at the ninth level, but in Netheril it was said that they could reach the twelfth level of power.”

I stopped smiling.

“So there were magical artifacts they could create that had never been created before. One of their leaders, Karsus, I believe is the story — I'm not a scholar on the topic — did something which caused the Weave itself to break. And that, in turn, almost destroyed magic itself. And then, as a result, the Netherese Empire fell apart.”

Daran rubbed the earth from his palms.

“But, you know — let me just tell you something.” He paused. “Those are stories. Right? It's like a big ancient empire that dies because of hubris. I mean, come on. It sounds like a morality play.”

“Well, I believe all the stories are built on some real-life incidents, so…”

“Yeah — well, you know, Zenvon, that's a good… that's not something to… I've got to give you credit.”

Daran told us the vanished empire had left powerful magical trinkets scattered across the North. Rumour of one was enough to draw magic-users, scavengers, and anyone hungry for power.

“And what happens to them?” Zenvon asked.

“Well, if they're acquired by people who care about the goodness of our society, I like to think that they're used for good. And if they're acquired by other people — well, that's why we have adventurers like you.”

“That makes me think — do you think any of those trinkets could be cursed?”

“Well, the word *cursed* is a strong word.”

“Well, I mean, think of it like — somebody picks it up, and they get bad luck.”

“No, no, no — that's just, again, old wives' tales. People have been saying those things. I think it's just that… You know, there are these powerful magical things.”

“Oh, okay.”

“And with any powerful magical thing, you never really know why, who created it, for what reason, and to what purpose.”

“Can't agree more with you.”

I could.

I knew about black stone, dreams beneath the earth, and whispers that knew my name. I also knew what it felt like to sell something strange to a peddler because coin seemed more useful than questions. I kept both opinions behind my teeth.

“And so I would say that those old wives' tales about the corrupting influence of Netheril and magic are just that — old wives' tales. Meant to scare people and keep them away from those kinds of magical things — which,” Daran paused, “isn't a bad thing. Isn't a bad thing.”

The orchard had become very quiet.

“There's a lot of magical things out there that, in the wrong hands — or even worse, in hands that are not capable of handling those magical things — bad things can happen.”

“Wisely said, Daran. Wisely said.”

“So I just… but I've got to be honest with you, Zenvon, there's a lot of stories about the North…”

What Daran wanted was simpler than the stories. He wanted us to go to Old Owl Well, look carefully, and return with an answer: was something there becoming a threat to Phandalin?

“Okay, okay. Well, I'll see you when I'm back from the Old Owl Well.”

“Oh, excellent, excellent.”

“Thank you for your time.”

Daran returned to his underbrush. Zenvon put away his notes.

I managed not to laugh until his back was turned.
