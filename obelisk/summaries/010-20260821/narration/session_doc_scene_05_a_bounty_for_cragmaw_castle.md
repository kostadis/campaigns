Morning found Sildar Hallwinter waiting over breakfast.

Zenvon nearly walked past him. We had promises to keep, roads to cover, and several dangerous places competing for the privilege of killing us. Breakfast did not appear urgent.

Sildar made it urgent.

"I just want you to know something."

He had my attention. Also eggs. But mostly my attention.

"I want you to find the castle. The Lords' Alliance would like you to find Cragmaw Castle."

"And I think that there would be a good payout for somebody who were to find it."

"Just find the castle — that's it?" Zenvon asked.

"Well… locate, defeat, and drive off the band leaders."

That was considerably more work than finding it. I can find a wasp nest. Removing the wasps is where pricing becomes important.

"Yeah, we've talked about this assignment earlier, haven't we?" Zenvon said.

"Yes, but we didn't negotiate on a price."

"No, did we not?"

Zenvon frowned.

"No, I think we did. You know — for alive, some prize; for dead, some prize."

"Yes, but that's only for Iarno. It's not for the whole of Cragmaw Castle."

"Oh, okay — so this looks like more money, then. Cool."

"Alright, well, I guess we can talk about it once we're back."

"From Conyberry?"

Sildar paused. "Well, I just want you to know that there is a large amount of money for someone who's going to—"

Large is a word that should never be allowed to pass unmeasured. A large breakfast is pleasant. A large goblin is not. A large amount of money, however, deserves immediate professional attention.

I nudged Zenvon and asked whether we might at least learn the amount.

"Oh, I'm not going to ask — but you ask."

So much for leadership.

I rose. Slowly. Respectfully. Talon hung at my side, and I adjusted the belt so the sword would sit where Sildar could see it. This was not ingratiation. It was presentation.

"Mr. Sildar?"

He looked up.

"Mr. Sildar, sir—"

"Yes, Pip! You don't have to call me *Mr.* You can just call me Sildar."

"Oh, thank you, thank you, Sildar. Well — how much money would there be?"

"Well, Pip."

Another pause. A good pause. The sort that fattens a purse before the coins arrive.

"I think that the Lords' Alliance thinks this is an important thing. So if you were to deal with everything there, 500 gold pieces would definitely enter your pockets."

For a moment I could feel every one of them.

Better armour. Properly fitted. Thick across the ribs and shoulders, without somebody else’s dents hammered flat and sold as character. Repairs for my shield. A scabbard worthy of Talon. Enough left over to eat somewhere the spoons did not bend.

"Boss, are we gonna go do that? But, I mean—"

I caught myself and turned back to Sildar.

"Well, I think that's very generous of you, Mr. Sildar."

He corrected me again, then leaned back and let the other part of the offer settle between us. The Lords’ Alliance would look kindly on whoever cleared Cragmaw Castle. Favours. Opportunities. Doors opened by hands that normally kept them shut.

I heard *kindly* and thought it meant they might pay promptly.

Zenvon pulled me aside. He divided the reward among the whole party and quietly reduced my shining mountain of gold to one respectable hill. Still gold. Still armour. But apparently one must not look too pleased, or the person offering it may discover they could have offered less.

"Just don't be too excited, man."

"That's… that's good advice, boss."

I returned to the table with my face arranged into what I hoped was mild financial interest.

"Well, I mean, you know, it's a chunk of change, but we're… you know, we'll keep that under advisement, Sildar."

Under advisement was excellent. Five hundred gold had already purchased several things in my head, but outwardly it remained under advisement.

Zenvon did not change our course. He had given Daran Edermath his word, and Old Owl Well remained our immediate destination. I respected that. A promise is a promise, even when another road has considerably better armour waiting at the end of it.

Before we left, Sildar’s eye fell upon Talon. He called it a fine, noble sword and addressed the compliment to me.

Zenvon answered first.

"So, well, yeah, we got it. I think I earned it — for my bravery."

Sildar’s attention remained on me.

I put a hand on Talon’s pommel. Fine, certainly. Noble sounded like something a sword became after surviving long enough for a song to be written about it. Mine had so far survived my ownership, which was an encouraging beginning.

Zenvon went still for a moment, studying Sildar. Then his expression cleared, and he kept whatever conclusion he had reached to himself.

I assumed he was reconsidering the division of the reward.

There was also Mirna Dendrar’s treasure in Thundertree—the emerald necklace behind the shop shelves. I had not forgotten it. I am capable of remembering several future improvements to my armour at once.

Toblen Stonehill wished us well at the door. I settled my shield across my back, checked Talon at my hip, and stepped out of Phandalin with the Lords’ Alliance looking kindly upon me.

Whatever that meant.
