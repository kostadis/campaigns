I came downstairs hungry enough to consider breakfast a serious appointment. Sildar Hallwinter was already seated, and he invited us to join him.

Zenvon hovered beside the table. “Yeah, we've got to hurry a little bit, but I thought I'd just—”

“He didn't want to talk with us?”

Sildar did. He also wanted breakfast. Apparently both could happen at once.

“Oh, I see. Okay.”

We sat. There are worse ways to receive dangerous employment than over hot food, although there are few better ways to forget that the employment is dangerous.

“I just want you to know something,” Sildar said. “I want you to find the castle. The Lords' Alliance would like you to find Cragmaw Castle.”

He let that settle between us.

“And I think that there would be a good payout for somebody who were to find it.”

“Just find the castle — that's it?” Zenvon asked.

“Well… locate, defeat, and drive off the band leaders.”

That was not just finding it. That was finding it, entering it, fighting everyone important inside, and being alive afterwards to collect. Important distinction.

“Yeah, we've talked about this assignment earlier, haven't we?”

“Yes, but we didn't negotiate on a price.”

“No, did we not?” Zenvon frowned. “No, I think we did. You know — for alive, some prize; for dead, some prize.”

“Yes, but that's only for Iarno. It's not for the whole of Cragmaw Castle.”

“Oh, okay — so this looks like more money, then. Cool.”

Cool was one word. I had several others prepared, but none of them were useful until Sildar stopped calling the payment good and started calling it an amount.

“Alright, well, I guess we can talk about it once we're back.”

“From Conyberry?”

Sildar paused. “Well, I just want you to know that there is a large amount of money for someone who's going to—”

I nudged Zenvon. “Can we at least find out how much?”

“Oh, I'm not going to ask — but you ask.”

Delegation. A vital leadership skill.

I stood and straightened everything that could be straightened. My coat resisted, but I had already risen and there was no withdrawing with dignity.

“Mr. Sildar?”

“Mr. Sildar, sir—”

“Yes, Pip! You don't have to call me *Mr.* You can just call me Sildar.”

“Oh, thank you, thank you, Sildar. Well — how much money would there be?”

“Well, Pip.”

He knew my name. Promising.

“I think that the Lords' Alliance thinks this is an important thing. So if you were to deal with everything there, 500 gold pieces would definitely enter your pockets.”

Five hundred gold pieces.

I have tried, since then, to remember whether I breathed. I believe I did, because I remained standing. My mind had already bought better armour, repaired my shield, replaced my boots, and put something aside for emergencies, by which I meant more armour.

Zenvon went silent and left me in charge.

“Boss, are we gonna go do that? But, I mean—”

Sildar was still there.

“Well, I think that's very generous of you, Mr. Sildar.”

He corrected me again, then leaned back and told me the Lords’ Alliance would look very kindly upon whoever dealt with Cragmaw Castle. I heard *five hundred*. I heard *kindly*. The second word seemed pleasant but materially less important.

Zenvon drew me aside. He divided the reward among the four of us and arrived at a much smaller sum. He also pointed out that castles full of goblins presented certain expenses, chiefly the possibility of dying in one.

“Just don't be too excited, man.”

He was right. Negotiations depend upon appearing willing to walk away, even when the money has entered your imagination and furnished it.

“That's… that's good advice, boss.”

I returned to Sildar with my shoulders lowered and my face arranged into what I hoped was financial indifference.

“Well, I mean, you know, it's a chunk of change, but we're… you know, we'll keep that under advisement, Sildar.”

Excellent. Very restrained. Nobody could possibly have guessed that I was still spending it.

Zenvon kept his word to Daran Edermath. Old Owl Well remained our immediate destination, reward or no reward. I approved. A promise ought to survive breakfast.

As we prepared to leave, Sildar’s attention settled on Talon at my side. The sword rode more naturally there every time I wore it, though I had not forgotten my first attempt to use it. A noble blade deserves a noble beginning. It got me missing.

Sildar admired it as a fine, noble sword.

Zenvon answered before I could. “So, well, yeah, we got it. I think I earned it — for my bravery.”

Sildar’s eyes stayed on me.

“Nice work, yeah, yeah,” Zenvon said.

There was a pause. Zenvon looked from Sildar to the sword and then to me. Something had clicked cleanly into place behind his eyes, but he said nothing.

I put a hand on Talon’s pommel. Fine. Noble. Both accurate. If Sildar meant anything beyond that, he had chosen an unnecessarily indirect route to say it.

Before we stepped outside, I remembered Mirna Dendrar’s treasure waiting in Thundertree. Another destination, another promise, and—if her description held—another contribution to the armour fund.

Toblen Stonehill wished us well at the door. I adjusted my battered shield, checked Talon, and walked out of Phandalin still thinking about five hundred gold.
