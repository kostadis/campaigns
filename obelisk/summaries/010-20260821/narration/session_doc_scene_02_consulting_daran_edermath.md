Zenvon did not intend to make it wait much longer. Before we returned to the Stonehill Inn, he turned toward Edermath Orchard. Daran knew Old Owl Well, or at least knew enough to send us there, and Zenvon wanted every scrap of advice available before we set out.

We found Daran beside his cottage, clearing underbrush from the edge of the orchard. He straightened at our approach, dirt on his hands and delight already spreading across his face.

“Well, well, well, well, well. I… the heroes of Phandalin!”

There are worse things to be called. Some of them have outstanding warrants attached.

“Well, I have to say, I'm very proud to hear that you dealt with the Redbrands.”

“Yeah, we walked… it happened as an accident. We didn't intend to, but it was, I guess, God's plan.”

I kept my face straight. We had not so much walked into the Redbrands as fallen through them, occasionally from a considerable height. Still, if a god wished to claim responsibility, that god could also explain the pit.

“That is the best news I have,” Daran said. “Now, I just have to ask — have you been able to deal with the Old Owl Well problem?”

“No, and that's why we are here. Me and my friends, we were thinking to start tomorrow for the Old Owl Well.”

“Fabulous, fabulous, fabulous.”

“And we are here to talk to you about whether we can get any other details — or whether we have to be careful with anything on the way.”

“Well, if you stick to the Triboar Trail — now, an important thing to realize is, as you're going east on the Triboar Trail…”

Daran sat back. The underbrush had lost him entirely.

“Well, you have to understand. A large chunk of the North — in particular the more you go east — was torn apart by wars, demons, gods, elemental catastrophes. And so things become less and less settled. In fact, Conyberry has been a wreck for years.”

That was a lot of catastrophe for one direction. West had sea. East had demons, gods, and ruined villages. North probably had weather.

“Do you recommend any shorter or easier paths to get to the Old Owl Well?” Zenvon asked.

“Well, there are two ways you could go. You can take the road, which should be safer — you might even meet the odd occasional traveler. Or you could go through the hills. Of course, when you're going through the hills…”

He let that hang. I understood the unfinished portion to mean that the hills contained things with strong territorial opinions and teeth sufficient to enforce them.

“Plus, there are some bandits, according to the mayor — the Lord Mayor. Harbin.”

“At Conyberry?”

“No, he's been saying — and I have to be honest with you, I haven't been following all the details, but let me just see what he says. I don't want to get it wrong…” Daran paused to gather the report from wherever he had stored it. “Well, he's been saying there have been several attacks near Wyvern Tor, of caravans. And so I don't know where they'd be acting from, so you might want to be careful there.”

“Well, Daran, looks like you know more about Old Owl Well and the best ways to lead us there — but would you like to join us?”

“Well, you know, it's been a long time, and my back hurts, and I've got all the…”

Daran reconsidered the list of failing parts.

“You know, one of the things about being an old adventurer is: you're an old adventurer because you choose to stop adventuring.”

That was sound advice. It was also the first advice he gave us that afternoon which could be applied without further explanation.

“Oh, that makes sense. Well, in that case — is there anything you have that you think will be useful to us on the way?”

“Well, I've…” Daran began. “Keep your eyes up. Make sure your sword is sharpened.”

“Wait, what? Eyes up?”

Daran added keeping our backs straight, watching for danger, and turning our heads frequently enough to observe it. Zenvon took out his notes.

“Yeah, I was going to make notes of it, actually.”

Daran brightened.

“Okay, no.”

Too late.

For the next several minutes, Zenvon solemnly recorded the accumulated wisdom of an adventuring career: look around, stand properly, maintain your equipment. Daran supplied stories to support each point. One involved poor posture at a river crossing. Another began with a blunt sword and took a very long route to the conclusion that sharp ones were preferable.

I looked at Zenvon. He looked at his notes.

I pressed my lips together until they hurt. Professional road security does not laugh when its commander is trapped by stationery.

Then Zenvon found the exit.

“Okay, I have… oh, Daran, I just recalled a question I wanted to ask you. Tell me more about Netheril. What kind of magic should we expect over there?”

Daran’s pleasure faded into caution.

“Why would you be asking that?”

“Because you mentioned it earlier.”

There was a brief dispute over whether he had. Zenvon consulted his notes and found the reference to an old Netherese watchtower. The notes had saved him from Daran’s memory after first delivering him into Daran’s stories. A balanced instrument.

“Well! Well, you have to understand, this is an old story,” Daran said. “A long time ago, there was an ancient empire — called Netheril, the Netherese Empire. And that Netherese Empire had ships that could fly in the sky. And they had access to magic that mortals no longer have access to.”

Veyra had gone very still. I stopped smiling.

“The highest-powered wizard spells nowadays are at the ninth level, but in Netheril it was said that they could reach the twelfth level of power. So there were magical artifacts they could create that had never been created before.”

The difference meant little to me as arithmetic. As a warning, it was clear enough. Modern magic could burn, deceive, kill, and tear a room apart. Netheril had looked at those limits and continued upward.

“One of their leaders, Karsus, I believe is the story — I'm not a scholar on the topic — did something which caused the Weave itself to break. And that, in turn, almost destroyed magic itself. And then, as a result, the Netherese Empire fell apart.”

Daran paused.

“But, you know, that's [unclear] — but let me just tell you something. Those are stories. Right? It's like a big ancient empire that dies because of hubris. I mean, come on. It sounds like a morality play.”

“Well, I believe all the stories are built on some real-life incidents, so…”

“Yeah — well, you know, Zenvon, that's a good… that's not something to… I've got to give you credit.”

Daran brushed dirt from one palm with the other.

“Anyway, so the Empire mysteriously disappeared. But what it did was leave a large amount of its powerful magical trinkets all across the North. And so, when people find one of those trinkets, they're very desperate. When people hear that there's a trinket around, every magic user and power-hungry and…”

“And what happens to them?”

“Well, if they're acquired by people who care about the goodness of our society, I like to think that they're used for good. And if they're acquired by other people — well, that's why we have adventurers like you.”

“That makes me think — do you think any of those trinkets could be cursed?”

“Well, the word *cursed* is a strong word.”

“Well, I mean, think of it like — somebody picks it up, and they get bad luck.”

“No, no, no — that's just, again, old wives' tales. People have been saying those things. I think it's just that… You know, there are these powerful magical things.”

“Oh, okay.”

I said nothing. I had once held a piece of black stone. Then I had sold it, started dreaming of broken stone beneath the earth, and heard whispers speaking my name.

Bad luck was a small phrase. Conveniently small. You could put it in your pocket and pretend it weighed nothing.

“And with any powerful magical thing,” Daran continued, “you never really know why, who created it, for what reason, and to what purpose.”

“Can't agree more with you.”

“And so I would say that those old wives' tales about the corrupting influence of Netheril and magic are just that — old wives' tales. Meant to scare people and keep them away from those kinds of magical things — which”—Daran paused—“isn't a bad thing. Isn't a bad thing.”

Fear keeping the incapable away from danger sounded entirely sensible to me. That is why shields exist. A shield says there is danger over there and I would prefer it to remain over there.

“There's a lot of magical things out there that, in the wrong hands — or even worse, in hands that are not capable of handling those magical things — bad things can happen.”

“Wisely said, Daran. Wisely said.”

“So I just… but I've got to be honest with you, Zenvon, there's a lot of stories about the North…”

In the end, Daran wanted something simpler than relics, curses, or the breaking of magic. He wanted us to go to Old Owl Well and learn whether anything there threatened Phandalin.

“Okay, okay,” Zenvon said. “Well, I'll see you when I'm back from the Old Owl Well.”

“Oh, excellent, excellent.”

“Thank you for your time.”

Daran returned to his underbrush. Zenvon closed his notes. I waited until my face was turned away before I smiled.
