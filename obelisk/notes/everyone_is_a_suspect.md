## What I understood going in (catch me if I'm wrong)

The party is standing outside the holding cells with Mirna, Nars, and Nilsa Dendrar, at the end of a long day, no long rest since the crypts. They believe the hideout is finished. It is not: **R9 (four bugbears + Droop)**, the **crevasse floor**, and **R7's storeroom** are all untouched, and four scenes are waiting in town. Zenvon is the only PC; Veyra, Maela, and Pip are DM-run sidekicks going to level 3 this session.

Two corrections from your review that this doc is built on:

1. **There is no lower level.** `(module)` The Redbrands' Hideout is R1–R12, one floor. Wick's "the dungeon has multiple levels… the subterranean part messes with your head" `(table)` is now read as **Wick describing the natural caverns** — R7's unfinished cavern mouth, the crevasse, the side passages, the unnatural cold at the bottom. "Messes with your head" is the nothic's telepathy.

   **The module hands you this retcon for free.** In "What the Redbrands Know" it lists, as a thing any Redbrand can tell you: *"**Complex Guardian.** The lower part of the complex is guarded by a hideous 'eye monster' (see area R8)."* `(module)` The book itself calls R8 **the lower part of the complex**, and the thing down there is a telepath that reads minds. Wick was repeating standard Redbrand gossip, accurately. Nothing was invented and nothing needs walking back.
2. **Nobody in Phandalin is Iarno's boss.** Your ruling — and the module backs it flatly. `(module)` *"Iarno 'Glasstaff' Albrek, the leader of the Redbrands, is taking his orders from someone called the Spider"* — directly. **"Only the Spider knows Glasstaff's true identity as Iarno Albrek."** Nezznar operates out of Wave Echo Cave, not town. There is no handler, no cut-out, no contact. Halia isn't it. Harbin isn't it. Sildar isn't it.

> **GM-only, load-bearing:** The players are going to spend this session building a conspiracy theory out of Wick's line, and they are going to be wrong. Play every suspect straight. Halia should behave *exactly* as suspiciously as she already has, because she is a genuinely shady operator who is also, on this specific question, innocent.
>
> **The elegant part:** the party's suspicion of Halia is *correct in spirit and wrong in fact.* `(module)` She is a **Zhentarim agent** working to bring Phandalin under her control. She wants Glasstaff captured and his correspondence delivered to her **so she can take over the Redbrands herself**, and in chapter 4 she smuggles Nezznar the Spider out of town into Zhentarim custody, letting the campaign's villain escape justice. She is absolutely playing her own game and she will absolutely betray them later. She is just not Iarno's boss. Every instinct the party has about her is right except the one they're testing.
>
> What Wick's "orders from town" actually was is an open decision at the end of this doc. **Do not answer it this session.** The party should leave town more suspicious than they arrived.

## The Win Condition

**The long rest and the level-up happen on screen** — that's the precondition, not the prize, and the sequencing note in Stage Directions explains why it has to come first.

Then three things must be true when you stop. **Sildar knows** his missing agent is the man who ran the Redbrands, and it landed as a scene rather than a line of loot-sorting. **Mirna has her answer** about Thel — which means somebody went into the crevasse. And **the party has said the obelisk out loud**: Pip's shard, Zenvon's stone, Veyra's candle, the dead elongated-skull goblins, in one conversation, with all four of them present.

Everything else — the bugbears, Talon, the beaver pelts, Halia — is optional and cuttable.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party just walks out and goes to bed)

- **The Dendrars** get delivered to town and Mirna asks her question anyway, in the street, in front of people, and the party has to say "we don't know." That's a worse scene, not a skipped one.
- **The bugbears** `(module)` eventually push the ale barrels aside, find their employer gone and eight dead Redbrands, and leave. With them goes the only in-building route to **Cragmaw Castle and Wave Echo Cave** — and Nosk's **iron key to every door in the hideout**.
- **Ssarnak** gets hungry. It has been fed twice by the party and it now associates them with food. The south tunnel is a hundred feet of unguarded pipe into Phandalin.
- **Talon, the hoard, and Thel's body** stay at the bottom of a crevasse the party has now crossed three times without looking down.
- **Iarno** spends the night getting further away, poorer and angrier.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Long rest first, in the cleared hideout.** They hold the ground, the doors, and the skeletons. Level-up happens here. This also means Veyra walks into the crevasse with **Candlelight Reading** already in hand — see the sequencing note in Stage Directions, it matters.
2. **Down the crevasse before the bugbears.** `(module)` The walls are "easily climbed without a check" — no roll, no rope, no cost. It is the single highest-value thing in the dungeon and it is free.
3. **Deal with R9 in scarlet cloaks, not steel.** `(module)` The bugbears "assume they serve Glasstaff" and the module explicitly invites the con: *"Clever characters might even persuade the bugbears to help deal with 'traitors' or 'impostors' elsewhere in the dungeon."* Four bugbears is a real fight for a level-2 rogue and three level-3 sidekicks.
4. **Escort the Dendrars up personally**, in daylight, cloaks off. Mirna walking free through Phandalin is the party's public receipt.
5. **Sildar before Halia.** Whoever they tell first shapes the whole town arc. Sildar pays and mobilizes; Halia banks it.

## The Fork — three doors, and what order costs

The party thinks they're finished. They're standing at a junction and don't know it. **Don't present this as a menu.** Let them declare a direction and then charge them for the order they picked.

| Door | Table time | Scene | Cuttable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Up and out** — the Dendrars, daylight, town | 60–90 min | Sildar, Halia, Harbin, Mirna | No (Sildar) |
| **The barrels** — R9, the 4+1 | 20–40 min | Con, fight, or walk away | Yes |
| **The crevasse** — R8 floor | 20–30 min | Thel, Talon, the goblin corpses | No (Mirna's answer) |
| *(R7 storeroom)* | 2 min | Pelts, the cavern mouth | Yes |

**What changes with order — this is the actual prep:**

- **Bugbears before the long rest** is a hard fight: Veyra has no slots, Zenvon is coming off 4 HP, sidekicks are still level 1. **After** the rest it's a manageable fight and a much better con, because a rested party can afford patience. If they go for the barrels first, *let them*, and let it be genuinely dangerous. Don't soften it.
- **Crevasse before town** means Zenvon walks into the Sildar scene with a dead man's pendant in his pocket and a decision about when to produce it. **Crevasse after town** means Mirna has already asked in the street and been told "we don't know," and going back down is penance rather than discovery. Both are good. The second is sadder.
- **Town before the basement** means they come up in scarlet cloaks with three civilians and no answers, and every unfinished thing down there stays unfinished while they're being congratulated. If they then go back down, the bugbears have had hours.
- **Bugbears before the crevasse** and Ssarnak is unfed and watching them fight. **After**, and the party has four fresh bodies to pay a nothic with — which Maela will notice, out loud, having done this once already.
- **The obelisk conversation** attaches to whichever of these has the longest quiet stretch. Default is the long rest. Backup is the crevasse floor, standing over the goblin corpses.

> **GM-only:** There is no wrong order and no wrong number of doors. If they take one and leave, the Default Trajectory above tells you what it costs them. Say none of that out loud.

## Scene 1 — The Long Rest They Haven't Taken

### Setup

Immediately where Chapter 7 stopped: outside the cells, three civilians, Veyra with no first-level slots, Zenvon healed off 4 HP earlier. The nearest defensible ground is the crypts — which they own, because the skeletons stand down for red cloaks `(table)`.

There is no wandering-monster pressure here. Let them rest. The only thing in the hideout that could interrupt is R9, and the bugbears have already been talked into going back to bed once `(table)`.

### The level-up, on screen

Sidekicks to **3**. Per `characters/sidekick_progressions.md`, with the level-3 obelisk signature features **approved by you this session**:

- **Pip** — 26 HP. **"Returned."** When Pip uses Protective Instinct against an attack by an Aberration or a psionic creature, the attack misses outright instead of having disadvantage.
- **Maela** — 22 HP. **Consecrating Touch.** When she restores hit points with a spell, she can also end one charm, fear, or psionic-domination effect imposed by an Aberration.
- **Veyra** — 18 HP. **Candlelight Reading.** She holds the blue candle to an obelisk fragment, aberrant residue, or psionically-touched object and asks one yes/no question; the candle answers as *augury* allows.

Zenvon levels on his own — that's Nikhil's call, not yours.

> **GM-only:** All three features are aberration-and-psionics-shaped and do nothing against bandits. That's deliberate, and the party will notice. **Let them notice.** Three companions simultaneously develop powers that only work on a threat they haven't met yet is not a mechanical accident — in-fiction, it's the obelisk reaching for them. Maela should be unsettled by hers. Veyra should be delighted by hers and then quiet about what that implies.

### The obelisk conversation (must-hit #3)

This is the scene the campaign has been owed since Chapter 5. The nothic broadcast Pip's secret into everyone's heads, combat started, and **nobody has mentioned it since** `(table)`.

Who opens it: **Veyra**. Her candle is the only object in the party that behaves like evidence, and she is one conversation from assembling the whole thing. Give her the line:

> "Pip. The stone you sold. Describe it to me. Not what it looked like — what it felt like to hold."

What she can assemble if the conversation runs: Pip won a blackstone shard near Neverwinter and sold it to a peddler **bound for Phandalin** `(table)`; her candle flared when she wrote **"Ruxithid"** `(table)`; her mentor **Orryn Voss** vanished saying the fragments must never be assembled `(table)`; and a goblin on the Triboar Trail said *"You're not what Ruxithid wants."*

**The pressure point is Zenvon.** He has a black stone in his pocket, taken on the road, and he has told nobody `(table)`. Veyra is now openly asking the table who has touched black stone. Nikhil chooses.

### Branch — Zenvon comes clean

Veyra's candle reacts to the stone in his hand. Play it as relief, not accusation — Maela is the one who'd be angry, and she's more interested in the stone. This buys real goodwill and the party moves as a unit for the rest of the session.

### Branch — Zenvon keeps it

Fine, and better for the long arc. But **the crevasse is coming**, and there are two psionic goblin corpses down there. If he's carrying the stone within 60 feet of them and Veyra has her candle out, the candle pulses and she does not know why. Don't resolve it. Let him sweat.

### Branch — the party skips the conversation entirely

They can. If they do, **Ssarnak is your backstop.** It has an unused Weird Insight and two prepared lines from prep that were never used `(proposed, from notes/redbrand_hideout.md)`:

> *"Your teacher is not dead. He is not in a place where dead is easy."* (Veyra)
>
> *"You took something on the road. It knows it was moved."* (Zenvon)

Use the second one. It makes the reveal involuntary and public, exactly as Pip's was — which is thematically correct and also fair.

### NPC notes
- **Mirna Dendrar** — Will not cry in front of strangers. Wants one thing: Thel. She will ask tonight, and she will push. She has two children watching her.
- **Nars (13)** — Frightened, quiet, keeps close to Nilsa. Small beat: he stares at the red cloaks the party is wearing. He knows that colour.
- **Nilsa (18)** — Old enough to be angry rather than scared. She's the one who'd ask what the party intends to do about it.

## Scene 2 — The Crevasse (must-hit #2)

### Setup

R8, the cavern they've now crossed twice. `(module)` The crevasse is 5–10 ft wide, 20 ft deep, and **its rough walls are easily climbed without a check.** Nobody needs to roll to go down. Say that plainly if they hesitate — the cost of this scene is zero and the party has been treating it as a hazard.

The bottom **feels unnaturally cold.** Under *detect magic* it emanates a faint **necromantic aura** that makes organic matter decompose at half rate. `(module)` That's why there's anything left of Thel to find, and it's the honest answer to "why is this place wrong."

### What's down there `(all module)`

- **Thel Dendrar's skeleton**, picked clean by Ssarnak, wearing a **carved wooden pendant shaped like a unicorn's head.** Mirna can identify both.
- **Two psionic goblin corpses** under the southern bridge — elongated skulls, filthy rags, killed by a pair of vicious claws. Different from the Cragmaw goblins. *(The module says the characters learn more about these in later chapters. You do not have to answer anything here.)*
- **Ssarnak's hoard**, in a battered chest in a cubbyhole under the *northern* bridge — invisible from the rim, obvious once you're down. 160 sp, 120 gp, five malachite gems (12 gp each), a *potion of healing*, a *potion of climbing*, a *potion of growth*.
- **Talon** — a *+1 longsword* in a silver-chased scabbard, hilt worked as a bird of prey. DC 15 Intelligence (History) recalls Sir Aldith Tresendar, the Black Hawk, who died fighting bandits that came up through these caverns.

### The hazard nobody has triggered yet

`(module)` **The southern bridge is rigged to collapse under more than 50 pounds.** DC 10 Perception from beside it spots the rigging. The party has crossed twice without incident — either they used the northern bridge or it hasn't come up. Either way it's still armed.

Comedic and material: if **Pip** goes over the southern bridge carrying the hoard chest, that's the second twenty-foot fall in two sessions, and this time the chest goes with him. 160 sp scatter into a necromantic cold spot. Nobody dies. Everybody has to pick up coins in the dark while Maela says nothing very loudly.

### Branch — they bring the pendant to Mirna

`(from notes/redbrand_hideout.md — still live, never played)` She takes it, holds it, and does not speak for several seconds. **Do not fill the silence.** Do not have another NPC speak. Let Nikhil decide whether Zenvon says anything.

Afterward she offers what she has: an **emerald necklace** hidden beneath storage shelves in her family's old herb-and-alchemy shop in the southeast of **Thundertree** `(module)`, which she has never dared return to. That's a Chapter 3 hook handed over by someone who owes them.

### Branch — they take Talon and leave Thel

Playable and in character for this party. Mirna asks anyway. Somebody has to answer her holding a magic sword they found ten feet from her husband's body.

### Branch — they never go down

Then Mirna's question has no answer this session and **must-hit #2 fails.** If you want to force it without railroading: **Ssarnak** will trade the location of "the meat with the wooden animal around its neck" for food. It has been dealing with this party all campaign and it knows exactly what it has.

### NPC notes
- **Ssarnak** — Wants meat. That is the entirety of it. `(module)` It "knows everything the Redbrands know," which now includes the layout, the secret doors, and the skeleton password **"Illefarn"** — none of which the party ever asked for. It will still sell all of it.
- **Ssarnak's leverage on the party:** it is the only creature in the dungeon that has been consistently honest with them.

## Scene 3 — The Barrels (R9: the 4+1)

### Setup

`(module)` Four **bugbears** and one **goblin**, Droop. Leader: **Nosk**. Behind a door the party barricaded with ale barrels in Chapter 6 `(table)` after Zenvon faked a bandit's voice through it and sent one of them back to bed.

Listening at the door: DC 10 Perception hears gruff voices issuing cruel commands in Goblin.

### What they're worth `(module)`

- They are **the only ones in the hideout who know the location of Wave Echo Cave.**
- They **also know where Cragmaw Castle is.**
- **Nosk carries an iron key that locks and unlocks every door in the hideout**, plus a belt pouch with 13 sp and semiprecious stones worth 50 gp.
- They will not divulge the locations willingly — they fear the Spider more than they fear the party. `(module)` The module's stated bypass is *charm person*.

> **GM-only — the door that is actually closed:** Veyra does not have *charm person* and does not get it at level 3 (`sidekick_progressions.md` gives her the obelisk feature, no new spells; second-level slots aren't until 5). **So the module's intended solution is unavailable to this party.** Don't dangle it. The realistic outcomes are: kill them, con them, or leave them — and Cragmaw Castle's location comes from somewhere else entirely (see below).

### Approach — the third con

This party wins by lying, and the module hands you the opening: *"If the characters are wearing scarlet cloaks, the bugbears assume they serve Glasstaff. Clever characters might even persuade the bugbears to help deal with 'traitors' or 'impostors' elsewhere in the dungeon."* `(module)`

Zenvon has now run this con twice and both times it worked. **Make the third one cost something.** Nosk is not a drunk bandit — he's a professional the Spider posted here to keep Glasstaff in line, and he is contemptuous of the Redbrands. Suggested opening for Nosk, dismissive rather than hostile:

> "You're the loud ones. Somebody was screaming about traitors half the night. Glasstaff sort it out, or is he still hiding behind his glass?"

That question is a trap the party can walk into two ways. Glasstaff is gone and Nosk doesn't know it.

### Branch — the con lands

They point the bugbears at "impostors" elsewhere and buy an exit, or walk them into the crypt skeletons, or simply get the door open and the barrels moved without a fight. **Nosk's iron key is the material prize** — it opens R6 and every locked door they've been picking all campaign.

### Branch — the con fails

Comedic and material: Nosk doesn't attack immediately. He asks the "Redbrands" to prove it by fetching Glasstaff. When that unravels — **he takes the cloaks.** Four bugbears stripping scarlet cloaks off four humiliated adventurers is funnier and worse than a fight, because the cloaks are load-bearing: **they are the only reason the crypt skeletons stand down** `(table)`. Losing them means the route back through R4 is suddenly six hostile skeletons again, and the party has to remember the password they never asked Ssarnak for.

### Branch — they fight

`(module)` Four bugbears is a genuine threat to a level-2 rogue and three level-3 sidekicks, especially with no second long rest available. Let it be scary. Droop faints on sight and stays down 1d10 minutes.

### Branch — they leave the barrels alone

Entirely reasonable. Note it in the Default Trajectory above and let the bugbears walk out of the campaign carrying the map coordinates. That's a real loss, and the party should eventually feel it.

### NPC notes
- **Nosk** — Professional, contemptuous, bored. Works for the Spider, not Glasstaff, and resents being loaned out as muscle to a wizard who won't come out of his room. Wants: to go home. Worries: the Spider does not tolerate failure and Nosk has been sitting in a basement for weeks producing nothing.
- **Droop** — `(module)` A **Scraptop** goblin — scavengers and tinkerers, not Cragmaw. Wandered in looking for wagon parts and got press-ganged. Knows the hideout layout, secret doors, and traps, and shares freely if asked kindly. **Does not** remember Cragmaw Castle's location, only that it's north, in the forest. If the bugbears die he asks for an escort out. Small beat. Don't oversell him — he is a warm body the party saved, not a party member.

## Scene 5 — Up Into the Light

### Setup

Daylight, Phandalin, the party coming up out of a tunnel or the manor ruins **wearing dead men's cloaks** with three freed civilians behind them.

> **Stage direction — play this once, early, with someone harmless.** `(proposed)` Tuck Stonehill sees Zenvon in a scarlet cloak from across the street and does not run to him. That's the whole beat. Two seconds. Then let Zenvon take the cloak off or not.

### The order of operations is the party's choice — and it matters

Whoever they tell first shapes the town arc. **Do not steer them.** But know what each one does with it:

### Sildar Hallwinter — the emotional peak (must-hit #1)

> **GM-only staging, from `notes/redbrand_hideout.md`, never played:** Do not describe his face. Let him say Iarno's name once, quietly, like he's checking whether the word still works. Then: *"Lord's Alliance business. I'll arrange the transport."* He does not shout, weep, or lecture. He is too tired and too angry. **Do not rush this and do not let it get compressed into loot-sorting.** If the party heads for town, put Sildar in their path before anyone else gets a chance at them.

He pays the **200 gp** module reward for eliminating the Redbrand threat `(module)`, and — once he knows the truth — wants Iarno **captured and transported to Neverwinter to face the judgment of a higher authority** `(module)`. Pay the gold in full; the gang is destroyed. Let him be visibly unsatisfied anyway, because Iarno walked.

> **GM-only — the knife you already have:** `(module)` *"Iarno Albrek, a human wizard, was Sildar's contact in Phandalin."* Not a colleague. **His contact.** The man Sildar was supposed to report to when he arrived, the reason the Lords' Alliance thought this town was covered. Sildar has spent this entire campaign — the ambush, the cell in the Cragmaw hideout, the recovery — trying to reach the one person here who was always going to be the problem. You do not need to have him say any of that. You need to know it while you play the silence.

What he does with the Spider's letter: reads it twice. Confirms the handwriting is Iarno's. The line that lands hardest is not the order to kill the party — it's *"I'm counting on you, Iarno. Don't disappoint me."* `(module, verbatim)` Somebody else got loyalty out of him.

> **If they offer him the Lords' Alliance angle:** `(module)` Once Iarno's treachery is uncovered, Sildar privately approaches whoever in the group has shown a commitment to protecting vulnerable folk and urges them to join — title of **"Cloak,"** and a pin. That is **Maela**, unambiguously. She is the one who said "we're the good guys, remember?" and made them open the cell door. Offering it to her, in the same session where she is quietly carrying what she did to Rondar, is the best scene available to you that nobody has scheduled.

**If they tell him Wick's line — "Glasstaff answers to somebody in town" — Sildar takes it seriously.** He is the one NPC with no reason to be suspected and every reason to worry. He will start looking. He will look in the wrong places, because there is nothing to find.

### Halia Thornton — the innocent suspect

She has a standing invitation out to Zenvon `(table)` and she was in the room when Glasstaff was unmasked.

`(module)` Her standing offer is **100 gp** to capture the Redbrands' leader **and** bring her any correspondence from his quarters. The party has half of that: the correspondence, plus a name nobody in Phandalin has ever had attached to Glasstaff. **Glasstaff himself got away.** Whether she pays full, pays partial, or pays full *because the letter is worth more to her than the man* is yours — the third option is the most in character and the most alarming, and Zenvon is a good enough reader of rooms to notice she didn't haggle.

> **GM-only — she is a rival, not a handler.** `(module)` Halia is an agent of the **Zhentarim**, working to make the Miner's Exchange the closest thing Phandalin has to a government. She wants Glasstaff removed **so she can take the Redbrands over herself**, and she does not reveal that. A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check tells a character she has ulterior motives for wanting the leader gone. `(module)` In chapter 4, if the party jails Nezznar in Phandalin without posting guards, **Halia breaks him out and delivers him to the Zhentarim** — the campaign villain escapes justice through her.
>
> So: she is a genuine future antagonist whom the party is about to hand a dossier. Play her warmly. Take the letter. Pay the hundred. **Do not tip any of it.**
>
> **Give her one moment that reads as a tell and is not one:** she goes very still when they say "the Spider." She has heard that name before and it frightens her — she is a middle-manager for a criminal syndicate contemplating a rival with a regional network. That's fear. The party will read it as guilt. Say nothing afterward.

> **If Zenvon impresses her:** `(module)` She quietly assesses whether anyone in the party is Zhentarim material — she's looking for people focused on wealth, power, and influence. Zenvon, who has talked his way through three rooms and executed a prisoner for being unreliable, qualifies. If she extends the offer he gets the title **"Fang"** and a pin. That's a real fork for Nikhil and it costs the campaign nothing to open.

### Harbin Wester

Owes them publicly. Has the **Wyvern Tor** contract live at 120 gp `(table)`, holds their surrendered Redbrand prisoner, and is now the man who has to explain what happens to Tresendar Manor.

> **GM-only:** Also not the handler. He is a coward and a banker, and the reason he looks guilty under questioning is that he hid behind a locked door while four people fought in the street outside his hall and he has not stopped being ashamed of it.

If they have the pendant, this is where it happens — or in the hideout, your call. If they don't, she asks anyway, in public, with her children present.

## Anticipated player questions

**"Who's the boss in town, then?"**
→ Nobody. `(module)` Iarno reports to the Spider directly, and the Spider is at Wave Echo Cave. Give them honest, non-committal responses from every suspect and let them build the theory unopposed. The one thing you must not do is have an NPC *deny* it convincingly — a clean denial reads as elimination and collapses the board. Let everyone be a little evasive about something unrelated.
