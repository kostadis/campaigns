# Hamun Kost — Strategy Doc

GM-only working doc. Drafted 2026-08-22, against campaign state through Chapter 8.

**Source rulings folded in (GM, 2026-08-22):**
1. **Agenda:** Thayan obelisk-surveyor *and* Forge-waiter, blended.
2. **Price:** nothing paid on credit. He pays on delivery, never before.
3. **Geography:** Old Owl Well is **northeast of Phandalin**. The "northwest" direction spoken in Ch. 1 was a verbal error; the route intended and used at the table was northeast. *(The module contradicts itself: Ch. 1 p. 30 also says "a couple of days' march northeast of Phandalin," while Ch. 3 p. 49 says eighteen miles southeast of Conyberry. Campaign table canon is northeast.)*

---

## The Frame (Hamun from inside his own head)

He is a careful man in a dangerous place with insufficient resources, and he has been for four years. He is not a warlord and has no ambition to be one; the men who become warlords in Thay die in their forties with their organs in jars belonging to someone else. Hamun Kost intends to die old, indoors, having been correct about something important. What he has out here is a tent, twelve labourers who do not need feeding, a very good chair he had shipped at absurd expense, and a hypothesis nobody else in the world has had. He did not bring the labourers. Most of them are the tower's own garrison, dug out of the shell where they fell however many centuries ago and put back to work. **One of them is his acolyte, and still wearing the robe he died in.**

The hypothesis is this: the tower at Old Owl Well is not a watchtower. It was built to look at something, and the thing it was built to look at is still there, in pieces, under the ground near a mining village fifty-odd miles off. He worked this out from window placement and a survey of sightlines, and it is the best work he has ever done. He has told nobody, because the moment he tells anybody it stops being his.

And here is the shape of his problem, which is why he is not simply digging: **finding it is not the same as being able to do anything with it.** Netherese work of that grade cannot be handled with a chisel and good intentions. It needs an apparatus — a place built to channel and bind raw magical energy — and there is exactly one such place within five hundred miles, and it has been buried since a Thayan war-band helped bury it. He knows where it is because his own people wrote down where they'd been. He cannot take it, because a drow with an army of bandits is sitting on it and Hamun Kost has twelve zombies and a scroll of *darkness*.

So he waits. He is very good at waiting. He has been waiting for four years and would happily wait another two, except that lately the waiting has developed a deadline, and now four adventurers have walked up his hill, and one of them is carrying something that his instruments should not be able to feel from this distance.

## His Current Position

- **Where:** Old Owl Well, in the hills **northeast of Phandalin**. A colourful tent in the courtyard of a ruined Netherese watchtower. Twelve zombies stationed inside the tower's shell, out of sight, as labour and as a fence. He is alone otherwise.
- **What he has:** a Netherese ring of green glass — his one real find in four years, and he knows it is a trinket compared to what he is actually looking for. A scroll of *darkness*, a potion, about 90 gp in mixed coin and stones. Notes. A great many notes.
- **What he does not have:** muscle, allies, a line of retreat, or time.
- **The twelve** *(per `docs/background/monsters_phase2_descriptions.md` §4.1, "the Kost Wardens")*: **Warden Dreth**, a former sentinel of the tower with half his face peeled by sun, leads them. **Warden Holl** is a Red Wizard acolyte in a singed robe — *"the Kost Warden who never learned he was dead."* Holl is the single most useful thing on this hill for a party that examines rather than attacks: he is proof Hamun did not arrive alone, and proof of what happened to the person who came with him. Hamun will not discuss it.

**Three clocks, all running:**

1. **The marauders.** The mixed band at Wyvern Tor—orc chief Brughor Axe-Biter, four bugbears, and the ogre Gog—has scouted his camp and gone away to think about it. He read that visit correctly. They will come back with numbers, and twelve zombies will buy him about four minutes. *This is the pressure that makes him deal with strangers at all.*
2. **The dig has gone dry.** He has taken the site as far as stonework and sightlines will take him. To go further he needs to ask the woman who built it, and to ask her he needs her name, and the only creature in the region who knows it is a banshee who has already nearly killed one visitor this season.
3. **Someone is expecting a report.** He does not discuss this. Four years is a long time to be funded on a hypothesis. He has been sending back enough to justify the next quarter, and he is running out of enough.

## His Secret

**The obelisk is his. He found it first, he found it by reasoning rather than by being told, and he does not know what it is.**

He has worked out that a shattered Netherese artifact of enormous power lies scattered around Phandalin, and that the tower he is camped in was built by people who were watching it. That is more than the Lords' Alliance knows, more than the Black Spider knows, and more than Veyra has assembled. On that axis he is ahead of everyone at the table.

**And he is wrong in the way that gets people killed.** He thinks he has found a Netherese power source. He has no idea about the Far Realm, no idea about Ilvaash, no idea that something is using the shards to reach through, and no idea that a goblin called Ruxithid is being spoken to by things that want Phandalin turned inside out. He is a competent man who has correctly identified the box and has no conception of what is in it.

This is the lever, and it cuts both ways. He is the single best source of hard information the party can reach about *what the obelisk is and who made it* — and he is the last person who should be allowed to touch one.

**The sub-secret, held tighter:** Thay's archive holds the account of the mercenary wizards who sacked Wave Echo Cave five hundred years ago. That is how a man in a tent knows the location of a mine that has defeated every prospector since. He did not find it. **He inherited the receipt.**

## The Optimal Play (For Him)

Played as smart as he actually is, here is his week.

1. **Never fight. Talk on the first syllable.** The instant anyone calls out, the zombies stand down. A fight he wins still costs him irreplaceable labour — there is no fresh garrison to dig up — and a fight he loses costs him everything. He opens with irritation, not menace — a scholar interrupted — because irritation reads as harmless and menace invites a crossbow.
2. **Be boring.** Establish immediately that he is a tenured bore excavating masonry. Complain about the labour. Complain about the climate. Offer them the chair. Nothing about this man should look like it is worth robbing, which is why the ring stays in the box and the box stays shut.
3. **Find out what they want before offering anything.** He asks more questions than he answers and he does it by seeming to make conversation. Within ten minutes he will know they are looking for a castle, a dwarf, and a drow, and that two of them are carrying black stone.
4. **Discover, reluctantly, that he can help.** Never eager. He should appear to be talked into it.
5. **Charge in labour, and take payment on delivery.** *(GM ruling.)* No information leaves the tent on credit. He has been paid in promises before; the promises were sincere and worthless. Both jobs he wants are **east** — Wyvern Tor and the banshee at Conyberry — which conveniently sends four armed strangers a long way from his dig for a fortnight.
6. **Do not lead with Cragmaw Castle — he knows it is worthless.** Reidoth is fifty miles up the same road the party is already walking, and any druid in Neverwinter Wood can point at a goblin fort. If he opens with the castle he gets undercut and looks cheap. **His only real currency is the mine**, so the castle is the free sample and the mine is what he is actually selling. He will let them believe the castle is the deal for as long as they'll believe it.
7. **Get Dresina's name without being seen.** He will not go to Agatha. Not because she would kill him — because she is a *diviner*, and a diviner who looks at him gets his employer, his hypothesis, and four years of work for free. He needs a courier, and couriers who don't know what they're carrying are the best kind.
8. **Let someone else open the mine.** He cannot take Wave Echo Cave from the Spider. Four adventurers who have already dismantled the Redbrands can. So he points them at the castle, and at the Spider, and waits, and walks in afterwards through a door somebody else broke.
9. **If the crystal flares, tear up the plan and start buying.** See Contingencies. This is the one thing that makes him move fast, and moving fast is not his skill.

## The Optimal Play (Against Him)

The party's actual best line, whether or not they find it.

1. **Talk to him. He is soft to conversation and hard to force.** Every hostile approach costs them the single most informed person about the thing they're all standing on. A greeting shouted across the courtyard is worth more than a surprise round.
2. **Ask him what the tower was watching.** He is not braced for a party that asks the *right* question instead of a greedy one. He talks about his work more than he means to — it is the one subject where his discipline slips, because nobody has ever been interested before.
3. **Know that Reidoth undercuts him.** The party's real leverage is that they don't need him for Cragmaw Castle. If anyone says the word "druid" out loud in that courtyard, his asking price collapses and he has to reach for the mine to stay in the conversation.
4. **Do not sell him the black stone.** He will pay very well. It is the worst trade available in the campaign.
5. **Spend Agatha's one question on *him*, not on the castle.** The nastiest, cleverest move on the board: they already know the banshee answers once and they're already fighting over whether it goes to Garaele's spellbook or the castle. A third option — *"who is the wizard at Old Owl Well and who does he serve?"* — costs them a lead and buys them the truth about a man who is about to become important. Do not signal this. If a player reaches for it, give it full weight.
6. **Take the ring by trade.** He will part with it for the right consideration and it costs him nothing he cares about. Stealing it makes an enemy of a man who writes letters home.
7. **Get the Wyvern Tor job paid twice.** Harbin already owes 120 gp for clearing those marauders. Hamun wants the same marauders gone. Any party with Pip in it should work out that one march collects from two employers, and Pip is exactly the character who will.

## Negotiation Stances

- **Concedes readily:** the coin, the agates, the potion, the scroll, the chair. Objects. He will hand over the ring in a trade or a hostage situation without much argument — it is four years of consolation prize, not the prize. He will also concede, if pressed and if they've been decent, that the party is in more danger than they think.
- **Trades:** the route to Cragmaw Castle (cheap, and he knows it). Safe passage and water. Identification of anything Netherese they put in front of him — *this is his most underrated offering and the party's best use of him*; he could identify **Talon** in thirty seconds, and read Urmon's journal, and tell them exactly what the black stone is not. Eventually, at a high price and never on the first meeting: **Wave Echo Cave.**
- **Never gives up:** why he is really here. Who funds him. His notes — he would burn them first, and he has thought about the order in which to burn them. And once he has it, **Dresina's name**, which he will not repeat aloud to anyone for reasons that are entirely correct.

## Contingencies

- **If the party attacks on sight:** zombies screen, he does not. He is out of the courtyard inside two rounds, and he does not come back that day. He comes back later, having learned four faces and lost four years of assumptions about how safe this hill is. The party has now made an enemy of Thay for a tent, and the ring they took is *identifiable*.
- **If the party is polite and curious:** he over-shares about the architecture and under-shares about everything else, and by the end of the conversation he has decided they are useful. He offers the second job before they have finished the first.
- **If Veyra's crystal flares in the courtyard** *(see Open Decisions — this is your biggest lever)*: he sees it. He knows exactly what it means, which is more than Veyra does. Everything reprices in that instant — he stops selling and starts buying, and what he wants to buy is the stone in Zenvon's pocket and the tiefling who can find more. His offer will be generous, specific, and immediate, and it is the most dangerous moment in the scene.
- **If they say "Ruxithid," "obelisk," or "elongated skulls":** he goes very quiet and asks them to say it again. This is the first outside confirmation his hypothesis has ever had. He will trade well above market to hear the rest, and he will not explain why he wants it.
- **If the marauders hit his camp first:** the price drops through the floor. A Hamun Kost who has just lost his zombies and his tent is a Hamun Kost who will pay in locations, and he will find the party rather than wait.
- **If the party brings him Dresina's name:** he pays generously and immediately, and then he starts a ritual he should not start, alone, at night, with a banshee's word and no idea what is listening. That is a scene, and it is available to you whenever you want the obelisk plot to get loud.
- **If the Spider learns he exists:** Nezznar sends people. Hamun does not survive that unaided, and knows it — which is his standing reason to want the party alive and between him and everything else.
- **If cornered with no exit:** he bargains with the only thing he has that cannot be taken off his body. *"Wave Echo Cave. I know where it is. You will not find it without me and you cannot get it out of me by hurting me, because I am not brave and I will simply lie."* It has the additional virtue of being true.

## Voice samples

- *"You have come a long way up a steep hill to interrupt a man who is not doing anything to anyone. Put that down and I will find you a chair. There is one chair. We will have to be adult about it."*
- *"Undead. You keep saying undead as if it were one thing. It is a category. It is like saying 'animal' and expecting me to know whether you mean a finch. Precision is free — you should try spending some."*
- *"No. You will do the thing, and then I will tell you. I have been paid in promises twice. Both men meant it sincerely at the time. Sincerity is not a currency I can spend at an inn."*
- *"It is not a watchtower for orcs. Look at where the windows are. Look at what they are pointed at — no, not the road, the road is fifty years old and this is not. Go on. Look."* **(He does not mean to say this. He says it anyway, every time, because nobody has ever looked.)**
- *"Twelve. Eight came out of the tower — the garrison, still where they fell, which ought to tell you something about how this place ended. The others I found nearer to hand. They cannot read and they will not lift anything above shoulder height."* **(If asked whether he named them:)** *"No. Some of them arrived with names."*
- *(Buying, after the crystal flares — flat, fast, and completely without the earlier dryness:)* *"Name a number. I am not going to haggle and I am not going to explain, and if you are clever you will notice that I have just told you what it is worth by refusing to do either."*

## What he would never say

He does not threaten. Not once, not even cornered — a threat is a promise of violence he cannot deliver, and issuing one converts a conversation he might survive into a fight he definitely won't. He does not boast, does not mention Thay unprompted, does not name his employer, and does not say the word *obelisk* out loud in front of strangers. He never claims a power he doesn't have; his whole safety is in being underestimated, and a bluff that gets called ends with him dead in a tent.

And he does not lie about anything checkable. He **omits** — precisely, professionally, and with a clear conscience. If a player asks him a direct question and gets a real answer, that answer is true. It is simply never the whole of it. Playing him as a liar wastes him; the pleasure of this NPC is a man who tells the truth in slices and lets you choose the size.

## Open decisions (for you)

1. **Does Veyra's blue crystal flare at Old Owl Well?** The largest single lever in this doc. If the tower is genuinely a node on the obelisk network, the party's detector goes off in Hamun's courtyard, in front of Hamun. It hands you the scene's hinge and it welds this side quest onto the campaign spine permanently. If it stays dark, Hamun remains a merchant with a good secret and the obelisk thread waits for Wyvern Tor. **Decide before they crest the ridge, not during.**
2. **Does Daran know he's there?** Daran Edermath is 500-plus years old, a drow, an information broker, and he just sent the party to a Netherese ruin two days' march from his orchard while worrying aloud about "dangerous magic." Either he genuinely doesn't know a Red Wizard has been camped there for four years — which is a little hard to credit — or he does, and sending four armed strangers to go and look at him is a Harper move he didn't explain. Both are good. They are very different NPCs.
3. **Who is he reporting to, and what happens when the reports stop?** You need a name in your head even if it never reaches the table. It is also the cleanest way to keep him relevant after Chapter 4.
4. **Is Dresina reachable, and what does she say?** The module gives you the name and nothing else. If Agatha's answer is a live thread rather than a trophy, decide now what a Netherese architect's shade knows about what her tower was pointed at.
5. **The marauder clock.** How many sessions before Wyvern Tor comes back to Old Owl Well in force? A number in your head makes his urgency real and gives you a way to make the party's delay cost something.
6. **The ring.** Loot, or plot? A Netherese artifact in a campaign about a Netherese artifact is a temptation. It is also fine for it to be a very nice ring.
7. **Warden Holl.** Who was he, how did he die, and does Hamun know he is still standing in the tower? A man who raised his own dead apprentice and then declined to name him is a different man from one who did not notice. Both are playable; they are not the same NPC.
8. **Does he survive Chapter 4?** He is written to walk into a mine somebody else has just cleared. Whether he gets there, and whether the party is still in it when he does, is the best cliff you have available at the end of the Wave Echo Cave arc.
