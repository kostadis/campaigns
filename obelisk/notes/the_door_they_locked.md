# The Door They Locked Behind Them

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging for Chapter 9.

Tags: `(module)` = verified against `docs/background/obelisk.md` this session. `(table)` = happened at your table. `(proposed)` = mine, yours to accept or bin.

## What I understood going in (catch me if I'm wrong)

Chapter 8 ended with the party running. They took the crevasse chest and Talon, freed the Dendrars, told Mirna the truth about Thel and then *didn't go get his body*, walked into the bugbear barracks in red cloaks, rage-baited Nosk into naming Cragmaw Castle, lost the fight, burned a hallway to cover a retreat, and locked a door behind them with three wounded bugbears on the other side. Then they slept in an inn, got paid, took a quest, and were polite to a woman they don't trust.

You've ruled: **there is no lower level under Tresendar Manor**, and **nobody in Phandalin is Iarno's handler** — both of which the module backs flatly, and both of which were already settled in `notes/everyone_is_a_suspect.md`. Nothing in this doc reopens either.

This session forks. You don't know whether they'll stay in town or head for the castle, so both wings are prepped below — and they share one hinge event that fires either way.

## The hinge (read this before anything else)

> **GM-only — Nosk has the key.** `(module)` *"He also has an iron key that locks and unlocks all the doors in the Redbrands' hideout."* The party locked the manor door behind them. If Nosk survived the fire, **that door was never locked** — it was closed. You told the table it would take three rounds to break through `(table)`. It took none.
>
> Which means the bugbears have been loose since the moment the party turned their backs, and they were loose for the entire long rest at the Stonehill Inn. Chapter 9 does not open on a sealed door. It opens on an empty manor and a question nobody has asked yet: **where did they go?**

This is the best structural gift Chapter 8 handed you, because it resolves the same way in both wings:

- **The bugbears know the route to Cragmaw Castle.** `(module — R9: "The bugbears also know the location of Cragmaw Castle, but again, they won't share this information willingly."*)
- **The party needs that route and has no other easy one.** They left Droop behind.
- So the three creatures that beat them in a fight are also the map they're missing.

You get to decide where the bugbears went (see Open Decisions). But whatever you pick, the party's best lead and their worst enemy are currently the same three wounded brutes.

## The Win Condition

Chapter 9 lands if the party leaves it **committed to a direction with a real price attached.** They currently have five open jobs, a rescue, a bounty, a promise to a widow, and no route north. That's not a plan, it's a menu. The session's job is to make them spend something — time, the necklace, Agatha's one question, Reidoth's goodwill, or Sildar's patience — to buy a single thread and start pulling it.

Secondary, and nearly as important: **the party should learn that the door they locked was never locked.** Not as a punishment. As the moment they stop being the ones who set the terms.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing)

- **The three bugbears** report to Cragmaw Castle that a party in scarlet cloaks knows the name of the place and killed their brother. King Grol starts posting sentries. The stealth approach in `notes/cragmaw_castle.md` gets harder every day they wait.
- **Mirna Dendrar** stops asking about Thel. She doesn't make a scene about it. She just stops, and that's worse.
- **Halia Thornton** decides the letter isn't for sale and starts working out how to take it. `(module)` She is Zhentarim and she wants the Redbrands for herself.
- **Sildar** starts spending Lords' Alliance money on his own manhunt for Iarno, and the party's 200 gp bounty evaporates into somebody else's win.
- **Gundren** — no hard module clock, but `notes/cragmaw_castle.md` sets your mental deadline: a full in-fiction week of side quests and Vyerith has left the castle with him.
- **Wyvern Tor** raiders keep hitting caravans. Harbin keeps not mentioning that he already paid.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Ask Sildar about Cragmaw Castle before leaving town.** `(module)` He offers **500 gp** to locate the castle and defeat or drive off the band's leader — a number nobody at the table has heard yet, and far larger than the 200 gp Iarno bounty they're currently excited about. He also suggests the method: *search the lands around the Triboar Trail for raiding parties.*
2. **Go back down for Thel before leaving town.** It's one scene, it closes a promise, and the crevasse is now unguarded — Ssarnak has no reason to stop them and the bugbears are gone.
3. **Split the routes correctly.** Campaign table canon places Old Owl Well northeast of Phandalin. Conyberry (Agatha) and Wyvern Tor remain on the eastern Triboar Trail loop. `(module conflict)` Chapter 1 also places Old Owl Well northeast, while Chapter 3 places it eighteen miles southeast of Conyberry; use the campaign's northeast ruling.
4. **Spend Agatha's one question on Cragmaw Castle, not Bowgentle.** `(module)` She answers exactly one, and she can answer almost anything in the adventure. Garaele's spellbook question is worth three potions of healing. The castle's location is worth 500 gp and Gundren's life.
5. **Don't fight the hobgoblins.** See the road wing. That encounter is a warning, not a fight, and it is not tuned to be fair.

# WING A — TOWN

*If they stay: the register is a grateful town that is not safe, and a party discovering their own leftovers.*

## Scene T1 — The Morning Bill

### Setup
Morning, Stonehill Inn. Toblen's free night is over and he's a little awkward about mentioning it. Sildar is at his table. `(table)` Elsa, Tuck, Freda and Narth are around.

The town is *delighted*. That's the texture — everyone the party passes is pleased with them, and the party is carrying a promise to a widow, a letter that exactly one other living person in the world knows the contents of, and three wounded bugbears they think are behind a door.

### The two pins

Both factions can court Zenvon **on the same morning**, and neither knows about the other. This is the best material in the town wing.

> **GM-only — Sildar's offer is now live.** `(module)` *"If the party eliminates the threat from Cragmaw Castle or uncovers Iarno's treachery, Sildar Hallwinter privately approaches certain members of the group to urge them to join the Lords' Alliance… the title of 'Cloak' and hands them a pin."* They uncovered Iarno's treachery last session. The condition is met. He speaks to *"those who exemplify a commitment to protecting vulnerable folk"* — which is Maela first and Zenvon second, and Sildar will notice that and be a little troubled by it.

> **GM-only — Halia's offer is still open.** `(module)` She quietly assesses whether anyone is Zhentarim material — people focused on wealth, power, and influence. Title: **"Fang."** Zenvon qualifies on the evidence: three rooms talked through, one prisoner executed for unreliability, and a man who told a Lords' Alliance agent that information was worth more than a hundred gold.

**Play them as the same offer in two accents.** Sildar's is delivered with the backhand he can't help — `(table)` *"for someone of your background."* Halia's is delivered with no backhand at all, which is the seduction. She's the only person in Phandalin who has never once been surprised that Zenvon is good at this.

### Sildar's actual business
- Pays attention to Talon. `(proposed)` He doesn't know what it is either, but a Lords' Alliance man knows a nobleman's blade when a halfling's fighter is carrying one, and Tresendar is a name he recognizes.
- **The 500 gp.** `(module)` Locate Cragmaw Castle, defeat or drive off the band's leader. Let him say the number and then let it sit.
- His suggested method `(module)`: search the lands around the Triboar Trail for raiding parties. He does not know where the castle is. Nobody in town does. `(module)` *"no one in town knows the exact location of Cragmaw Castle."*

### Branch — the party mentions Nosk named Cragmaw Castle
Sildar goes still. This is the first hard confirmation he's had that Gundren is somewhere findable. His reward offer gets more urgent, not more generous — he's not a haggler, he's a man who just found out his friend has an address.

### Branch — the party asks Sildar who Glasstaff answers to
> Hold the line from Chapter 8: **do not let anyone deny it convincingly.** `(module — there is no handler)` Sildar's honest answer is *"the Spider, presumably — that's what the letter says."* He has no reason to be evasive, so make his evasion about something else: he is embarrassed that he vouched for Iarno to Gundren personally.

### NPC notes
- **Sildar Hallwinter** — wants the castle found and Gundren alive; increasingly wants to be *useful* rather than the man who gets rescued. Voice: courtly, class-blind in the way that means he says the quiet part. Wants from this scene: to hand the party a bigger job than they think they're taking.
- **Toblen Stonehill** — wants to know if it's over. It isn't, and he'd rather not be told. `(proposed)` If the manor comes up, he mentions someone saw lights at the ruin last night. He assumes it was the party.

## Scene T2 — Halia's Second Approach

### Setup
The Miner's Exchange, or wherever she chooses to intercept them. She was refused a hundred gold for the letter last session `(table)` and she is not a person who asks twice at the same price.

### The honest doorway
The **jade frog statuette** has been unappraised since Chapter 3. The Exchange is where you get things appraised. This gets Zenvon through her door for a reason that isn't the letter, which is exactly how she prefers to be visited.

### Approach — what she actually does
She does not raise her offer immediately. `(proposed)` She does three things, in this order, and you can stop at whichever one lands:

1. **She buys something else.** The reagents, the malachite, the frog — at a fair price, cheerfully, no haggling. Establishing that she is a good counterparty.
2. **She asks about Cragmaw Castle.** Not the letter. She's heard a name. She wants to know whether the party is leaving town, and when. `(proposed — GM-only: she wants to know how long the manor will be unattended.)
3. **She tells them something true and useful, for free.** `(proposed)` Pick one: that the Sleeping Giant's orc bartender has been drinking alone since the Redbrands fell and will talk; that a caravan hand came in off the Triboar road three days ago with a spider-marked satchel; that Harbin has quietly written to Neverwinter about who owns Tresendar Manor now. Free information is the most expensive thing she can give them.

### Branch — they sell her the letter
She pays, she is warm about it, and `(module)` she now has the one document tying Iarno to the Spider. Downstream: in chapter 4 she is the reason Nezznar walks. Selling her the letter doesn't cause that — but it tells her the party is purchasable, which changes how she plays them later. Don't telegraph it. Let it be a good deal.

### Branch — they keep refusing
> **GM-only:** She stops asking and starts arranging. `(proposed)` Not a theft this session — a *watcher*. Someone notes when the party leaves town. If the party goes on the road and comes back to find their room at the Stonehill searched but nothing taken, that's her, and it's unprovable.

### Branch — they accuse her outright
She is delighted. Genuinely. `(module — DC 15 Insight reveals she has ulterior motives for wanting the leader gone.) Let the Insight land if they roll it. What it tells them is *she wanted Glasstaff gone for her own reasons* — which is true, and which they will absolutely misread as confirmation she's the handler. **That misreading is the good outcome.**

### NPC notes
- **Halia Thornton** — wants the Redbrands' apparatus, not their reputation. Voice: warm, unhurried, never defensive, asks questions instead of answering them. Worry: that the party is more principled than useful. Wants from this scene: to find out when Phandalin will be empty of them.

### Branch — they bring Mirna the pendant
`(table)` She asked. They said they'd try. Stage direction from last session's doc still stands and I'm repeating it because it's the best thirty seconds available to you: **when she takes it, nobody speaks. No NPC line, no description of her face. Wait for a player.**

## Scene R1 — Twenty-Five Gold Pieces for This One

### Setup
First or second night on the road, whichever direction they picked. `(module — Triboar Trail random encounters, Hobgoblin Squad: 1d4+3 hobgoblins.)

> `(module)` *"This squad seeks the adventurers, hoping to collect a bounty set by the Spider. One of the hobgoblins carries a crudely drawn sketch of one party member, with '25 gold pieces for this one' and a symbol of a spider scrawled beneath it."*

This is the Chapter 6 letter arriving in person. The party has been carrying written proof that the Spider ordered their capture; now it has a price and a picture.

### The thing to decide before the session
**Whose face is on the sketch.** It changes what the scene means:
- **Zenvon** — obvious, and the least interesting. He's been the visible one.
- **Pip** `(proposed, recommended)` — the sketch is of a man nobody would describe as the leader. Which means the Spider's information is *bad*, or it's about the shard, and either reading is a live wire. Pip's secret is public in the party and unexamined since Chapter 8.
- **Veyra** — points at Orryn Voss and the candle, and Ssarnak's whisper is one night old.

### Approach
Four to seven hobgoblins against a level-3 party is not a fair fight. **Do not tune it down.** Stage it so the party sees them before contact — hobgoblins are disciplined, they move in formation, they make noise like soldiers rather than raiders. Zenvon has Cunning Action and now has *Minor Illusion*. Let the tools they've been using in corridors get tested in open country where there are no doors to close.

### Branch — they fight and it goes badly
They have Talon, three potions of healing from the chest, and the oil trick is not repeatable in a field. If someone goes down, **let them go down.** The party ran last session and survived by running; the lesson should be that running is a tool, not a failure. If they run, the hobgoblins do not pursue indefinitely — they're paid per head, not per corpse.

### Branch — they take the sketch
The best outcome. Now they're carrying a portrait of one of themselves with a bounty on it, and the question of *who told the Spider what they look like* has no comfortable answer. `(proposed — GM-only: nobody told anyone. Iarno saw all four of them in a lit corridor in Chapter 6 and he is the only person who both escaped and reports to the Spider. That is a clean, findable answer and it makes the Iarno bounty personal in the other direction.)

### Branch — they avoid the encounter entirely
Fine. Let them. Then the sketch turns up later, nailed to a tree, weather-stained, at the edge of Neverwinter Wood. Same beat, colder.

## Scene R2B — Thundertree and the Terse Druid *(if they go northwest)*

`(module)` Fifty miles northwest, two days. Ruins overrun by ash zombies, mutated plants, spiders at the base of the hill, cultists in black masks on the eastern side — and a **young green dragon in the tower.**

`(module)` An elderly human woman with long white hair *"who doesn't use two words when one word will do."* Emerald Enclave. Reasonably hospitable. She warns them about everything in the ruins and suggests they leave before they're killed.

> **GM-only — she does not just hand over the directions.** `(module)` *"Reidoth hesitates… she doesn't want to rile the goblins without good cause. > Note the irony available to you: the party is carrying two obelisk shards and a woman whose crystal lights up around them. "Rooting out unnatural threats" is *literally their situation* and none of them know how to say so.

**A young green dragon will kill this party.** If they take the Venomfang bargain, "chase off" is the operative phrase and the module means it — bluff, threat, fire, noise, anything but a stand-up fight. Zenvon has *Minor Illusion* and has already used it to fake a wizard at work. That's the tool. If they roll initiative against Venomfang, you are running a TPK.

`(module)` If they do chase it off, Reidoth privately offers Emerald Enclave membership — title **"Springwarden."** Third faction pin in two sessions, if you're counting.

### NPC notes
- **Reidoth** — wants the ruins left alone and the dragon gone, in that order, and resents that those two wants conflict. Voice: one word where two would do. Never elaborates unless asked twice. Wants from this scene: to find out whether these people are a threat to the wood or to what's in it.

## Stage directions

- **The open door gets no music.** No sound cue, no monster, no ambush. Describe it flat: the door stands open, the burn is a smear, there are three sets of prints walking out through it. Then stop talking.
- **Say the 500 gp once.** Sildar is not selling. He states a number and waits. If a player asks him to repeat it, that's the scene working.
- **Halia never gets defensive.** Not once, not even accused. The moment she defends herself she becomes readable, and her whole function is that she isn't.
- **If Mirna gets the pendant: nobody speaks.** Repeating this from last session's doc because it still hasn't happened.
- **The hobgoblins should be seen before they're fought.** Formation, discipline, a marching noise. Give the party the chance to choose not to fight, so choosing to fight is a choice.
- **Reidoth's silences are the character.** Ask a question, get one word, wait. Don't fill it with flavor text. She is the first NPC in this campaign who is harder to talk to than a monster.
- **Do not let Agatha be safe.** She is cordial and she is a banshee, and the party should feel the comb doing work.

## Open decisions (yours, not mine)

- **Did Nosk survive the fire?** The one bugbear that died was unspecified `(table)`.
