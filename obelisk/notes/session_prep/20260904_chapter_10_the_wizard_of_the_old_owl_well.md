Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging for Chapter 11 (and probably 12).
**Party:** all four at **Level 3**. Zenvon Forepot (Nikhil, Halfling Rogue 3 / Arcane Trickster) is the only player-run PC. Veyra of the Blue Candle, Sister Maela Dawnforge and Pip Thistlewick are sidekicks voiced by Kostadis *and* Nikhil.
**Register:** three movements, in order — **a military problem**, then **horror**, then **the payoff**. Do not blur them. The tonal shift at the cave wall is the session.

> **Scope:** opens on the ridge where Chapter 10 stopped — fifty yards out, one bugbear sentry, cave mouth in the ravine. Closes in Hamun Kost's tent at Old Owl Well with the route to Cragmaw Castle in hand.
>
> **Expect this to run two sessions.** The natural break is the end of Movement 2 — the cave taken, the drawing found, the party sitting in a hole in the ground with something they can't explain. That is a better cliff than any fight ending. If pacing runs long, break there and open the next session on the walk back to Kost.

## The Win Condition (state it plainly to yourself)

**The session lands if the party takes a cave they cannot win a fight in, and comes out of it holding two things: the route to Cragmaw Castle, and a picture they cannot explain.**

The fight is not the point but it is not a formality either, and here is the arithmetic you need in your head:

> **This party lost to four bugbears at Level 3.** That was Chapter 8, in the barracks, and they only got out by pouring seven pounds of oil across a hallway and lighting it. `[TABLE]`
>
> **Wyvern Tor is four bugbears, plus an orc chief, plus an ogre.** Adjusted encounter XP is roughly **2,900 against a deadly threshold of 1,600.** `[OVERLAY: my math, check it if you like]`
>
> **A stand-up fight in the open cave kills this party.** Not "goes badly." Kills it.

So the encounter has a correct solution and the module hands it to you in two sentences:

- **`[MODULE]` If the characters quietly take out the lone bugbear, they have a chance to surprise the raiders in the cave.** If the sentry spots them, or an attack fails to drop him, he shouts and falls back — and then it is all six at once.
- **`[MODULE]` Gog fights until slain. The rest flee if Brughor is killed.** Brughor has **18 hit points.**

That is the whole tactical puzzle, and it is a good one: **the sentry is the encounter.** Everything after it is either a surprise round on an 18-hp target, or a funeral.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing clever)

- Zenvon rolls Stealth, gets a middling result, and Hul — half-blind on one side, bored, but a trail-ranger — hears the scree. He shouts.
- Six creatures come out of a cave mouth into a ravine the party is standing at the bottom of. Gorra knows these caves by smell and flanks. Gog closes.
- The party's escape route is **uphill, over rugged terrain, in the open**, and two of them have no darkvision.
- Somebody dies. Realistically Pip, who has a shield and a habit of taking point when danger is obvious.
- If they run: Brughor's band now knows four faces, and they are between the party and Old Owl Well.

**If the party never enters the cave** — takes the sentry, sees six shapes inside, and withdraws — that is a legitimate outcome and you should let it be one. They go back to Hamun having failed, and Hamun does not pay, because *nothing leaves the tent on credit*. `[TABLE]` That is a real and interesting session too. Don't rescue them from it.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

Not a rail. This is what the optimal party does, written down so you can recognise a partial version of it and let it work.

1. **Watch the sentry before touching him.** Ten minutes of observation buys: he is alone, he is bored, he checks the cave mouth every so often, and **his milky eye faces the party's approach if they come around the north side of the boulder.** Give this to a Perception check, DC 12. `[OVERLAY]`
2. **Take Hul silently and completely.** Zenvon's **Sneak Attack** on a surprised target is the tool. The **potion of invisibility** is the other tool, and this is the single best use of it in the campaign so far. A failed attempt is not a setback — it is the bad ending of the whole encounter.
3. **Do not walk in the front.** *Minor Illusion* has carried two sessions for this player — the Glasstaff workshop sounds, the cellar con. A noise at the far end of the ravine pulls one or two out of a cave that currently holds six.
4. **Kill Brughor first, and kill him fast.** 18 hp. A surprise-round Sneak Attack plus a *Firebolt* from Veyra does it on an average roll. **Four bugbears then break and run** — that is module canon, not a mercy.
5. **Then it is four of them against one ogre**, which is a fight at Level 3 rather than an execution.
6. **Read the walls before leaving.** This is the one they will forget. See Stage Directions.
7. **Shout before entering Hamun's courtyard.** He asked. Twelve zombies are the consequence of forgetting.

**The thing nobody has noticed yet, and Pip is the one who should:** `[TABLE]` **this job is already paid for twice.** Harbin Wester's 120 gp contract on these exact marauders has been sitting unstarted since Phandalin, and neither the party nor Hamun remarked on it in Chapter 10. One march, two employers. Pip is a character who counts money out loud. Let him get there himself.

# MOVEMENT ONE — The Military Problem

## Scene 1 — The Boulder

### Setup
Late afternoon, second day out from Old Owl Well. Lower slopes of Wyvern Tor, on a rugged ridge. Fifty yards below, a cave mouth at the bottom of a ravine. Faint smoke. Twenty yards outside the cave, **Hul**, hunkered behind a boulder.

The party is out of his sight and knows it — you confirmed that at the end of Chapter 10. `[TABLE]`

Present. Offstage: everyone else, in the cave.

### Approach
This is a planning scene and it should be allowed to be slow. Resist filling silence. The party has never once been given a fortified position and time to think about it — every fight so far has started by surprise, and half of them were surprises *on* them.

Things they can establish, and what each costs:

| They try | Give them |
|---|---|
| Watch him for ten minutes | Alone; bored; the milky eye; the cave-mouth check; **no relief coming soon** |
| Count the cave | Nothing from out here. They cannot see in. **Say so plainly** — the not-knowing is the tension |
| Circle for a second entrance | There isn't one. Don't invent one. Let the answer be "this is the door" `[OVERLAY]` |
| Smell the smoke | Cookfire, inside, and something greasy. More than one creature `[OVERLAY]` |
| The Redbrand cloaks | **This trick is burned.** A bugbear laughed at it in Ch. 8 — *"leave it to a Redbrand to think he's in charge"* `[TABLE]` |

### Branch — the sentry goes down clean
Surprise. The cave does not know. Go to Scene 2 with the party choosing their moment. This is the good outcome and it should feel earned, not lucky.

### Branch — the sentry survives the attempt, or spots them
He shouts and retreats to the cave. **`[MODULE]`** All six now. Do not soften this — but do not railroad it into a TPK either: the ravine is fifty yards of open ground and the party's line of retreat is real. Let them run. A party that runs from Wyvern Tor and comes back the next day with a better plan is a *better* session than one that wins the first time.

If they run: Brughor moves the camp's watch outward and doubles it. Coming back is now genuinely harder, and the tor knows their faces.

### Branch — they try to talk to him
Unexpected, in character for Zenvon, and you should reward the audacity. Hul is not a negotiator and Brughor is terrifying to him; his instinct is to shout. But he is also half-blind, alone, and bored — a party that opens with *"we're not here for you"* gets one round of him deciding.

**What Hul actually knows:** the camp raids the trail; there is a chief who hits people; there are goblins that come and go and *he does not like them*. He does not know why they come. `[OVERLAY]`

### NPC notes
- **Hul** — wants his shift to end. Voice tic: talks to himself, low, to stay awake. Wants from this scene: nothing. That is what makes him killable and what makes killing him land.

## Scene 2 — The Cave

### Setup
Map 3.3. Cookfire, plunder, an unlocked chest, and six creatures who are not expecting anyone.

### The fight, if it is a fight
- **Brughor** — 18 hp. The hinge. He is a chief because he hits harder than the bugbears and they are terrified of him, which is exactly why his death routs them.
- **Skarn, Gorra, Muk** + one more — CR 1 each. **Surprise Attack** matters: if *they* get surprise, it is +2d6 on the first hit and that is how Chapter 8 went wrong.
- **Gog** — CR 2, fights until slain. He is the reason the fight does not simply end when Brughor drops.

**Play the rout honestly and visibly.** When Brughor falls, the bugbears break — and the party should *see* it happen, because it teaches them something true about this world that they learned the hard way from the wrong side in Chapter 8. Gorra is the one who runs first; she knows the caves.

### Branch — they take Brughor in the surprise round
Best case. Four bugbears scatter into the hills. Party vs. Gog. Then Movement Two, in a cave that is suddenly very quiet.

### Branch — a straight fight develops
Watch HP honestly and give them the outs the room contains: the cookfire, the ravine, the chest as cover, the fact that bugbears are *lazy and cruel* and will chase a fleeing halfling rather than hold formation. If it turns, Maela's Turn Undead is not relevant here but her **mace to Rondar** and her **grapple on Veyra** establish that she supplies violence when asked to be a conscience — she is the one who covers a retreat.

### Branch — the bugbears surrender or are captured
Live option, and worth more than the treasure. **A captured Axe-Biter knows the road to Cragmaw Castle** — the module never says so but the campaign has already established it for the Tresendar three `[TABLE]`, and this band raids the same trail. That undercuts Hamun's price entirely and is a *great* problem to hand yourself. `[OVERLAY]`

### Treasure `[MODULE]`
Unlocked chest: **180 sp, 15 ep, three vials of perfume (5 gp each).** Plundered from homesteads farther north — say so; someone owned this.

# MOVEMENT TWO — The Horror

**This is the reason the session has a title. Change your voice here.**

## Scene 3 — The Northwest Wall

### Setup
The fight is over. The cave is warm and smells wrong. Somebody is looting the chest.

### The trigger `[MODULE]`
> *If the characters examine the cave walls, they find a strange, scrawled drawing on the northwest wall. The drawing crudely depicts a lanky goblin with a crystal jutting from its skull. The characters can tell this isn't a Cragmaw goblin, but they can't learn anything more just yet.*

The module says the characters can't learn more **yet**. That is written for a party that has seen nothing. **This party is not that party**, and the difference is the entire scene:

- Two dead goblins with **elongated skulls** under Tresendar Manor, Ch. 6 `[TABLE]`
- A live one on the Triboar Trail: **"You're not what Ruxithid wants."** `[TABLE]`
- **Veyra's blue crystal flares when she writes the name "Ruxithid."** `[TABLE]`
- Zenvon's black stone. Pip's shard. Ch. 8 put three of the four in one conversation about it. `[TABLE]`

So they *can* learn more. Not facts — **recognition.** They have seen this thing's face before and they came here for a completely unrelated reason, chasing a bounty on some orcs. **The horror is not that it followed them. It is that it was already here.**

### How to run it
Do not announce the drawing. Wait for someone to examine, or for the second pass over the room. If nobody looks — see Stage Directions; there is a way to give it to them that does not cheat.

Give it in the order a person actually sees it. Charcoal or something like charcoal. A figure, badly drawn, lanky. Then the head. Then the thing coming out of the head.

**Then stop talking.**

### Branch — Veyra's crystal `[OVERLAY — and it is the biggest lever in the doc]`
`notes/hamun_kost_strategy.md` Open Decision #1 asked whether the crystal flares in Hamun's courtyard, and noted that if it stays dark *"the obelisk thread waits for Wyvern Tor."*

**It stayed dark.** `[TABLE — Ch. 10 as played]` So it waits for here, and here is better: a hole in a hill with a picture of Ruxithid on the wall and no wizard watching her face.

**My recommendation: let it flare.** Not brightly. Once, faintly, the way it did at the name. Veyra notices before anyone else and has to decide whether to say so — and she is the character who *always* says so, which makes the pause before she does it mean something.

Cut this freely if you want to hold the thread. Nothing downstream breaks; the drawing alone carries the scene.

### Branch — Maela
`docs/party.md`: *"The new unaddressed thing is Maela."* She said nothing during the Chapter 8 obelisk conversation and wore a face the party rolled to read and failed.

**A cave with that drawing on the wall is where you make her speak, or conspicuously not.** She is the one who names things — *"zombies are mindless, someone else raised them"* was her, ten minutes into Chapter 10. If she looks at a goblin with a crystal in its skull and has nothing to say, that is now a fact the party has observed twice, and Zenvon is a rogue who notices patterns.

You do not have to resolve it. You have to make it visible.

### Branch — they miss it entirely
Acceptable. Do not force it. But see Stage Directions for the honest second chance.

### Branch — a psionic goblin is present `[OPTIONAL — Open Decisions #3]`
If you want Movement Two to have a heartbeat: one is here, off-task, and it does not fight. It watches from the dark at the back of the cave and it is not afraid of them, and when it goes it says something short. It should not be a combat. It should be worse than a combat.

### NPC notes
- **The drawing** is an NPC tonight. Treat it that way: it has a stake (it wants to be recognised), a worry, and a small goal.

# MOVEMENT THREE — The Payoff

## Scene 4 — The Courtyard, Again

### Setup
Old Owl Well, two days back northeast. `[TABLE — campaign canon is northeast; the Ch. 1 "northwest" was a verbal error.]` Crumbling watchtower, mounds of rubble, the colourful tent. No one in sight.

### The thing that will ambush you `[MODULE]`
**The twelve are inside the tower shell and cannot be seen from outside.** If the party walks up without calling out, they shamble. Hamun told them in Chapter 10: *don't walk into someone's home without calling out first* — and Zenvon argued that the tent was technically the home. `[TABLE]`

**If Zenvon walks in on that argument, let the zombies come out.** He was being clever and he was wrong, and Hamun will find this extremely funny in a dry way that is not quite an apology.

### The meal `[TABLE]`
He promised one. Give it to them. It is a very good meal, incongruously — the man shipped a chair out here at absurd expense. `[TABLE — strategy doc]` Warden Holl serves. Nobody comments on this.

### The payment
He pays on delivery, never before, and delivery has now happened. `[TABLE]` He gives up **the route to Cragmaw Castle**, and — per the strategy doc — he knows perfectly well it is the cheap thing he's selling. He hands it over without much theatre because **his real currency is Wave Echo Cave** and he is not spending that tonight.

### The hinge — and this is why the doc is built this way
`notes/hamun_kost_strategy.md`, Contingencies:

> **If they say "Ruxithid," "obelisk," or "elongated skulls": he goes very quiet and asks them to say it again.** This is the first outside confirmation his hypothesis has ever had. He will trade well above market to hear the rest, and he will not explain why he wants it.

**The party has just spent an entire movement staring at exactly that.** Movement Two loaded the gun; this is where it goes off. If anyone describes the drawing — and Veyra will, she is constitutionally incapable of not describing it — the negotiation inverts. He stops selling and starts buying.

He does not explain. He should not explain. He asks them to describe the skull again, precisely, and then he asks *where on the wall*, and then he is quiet for slightly too long.

### Branch — they ask him the right question
*"What was the tower watching?"* He is not braced for it. `[TABLE]` His discipline slips on this one subject because nobody has ever been interested. He will show them the window placement. He will make them look.

### Branch — they offer him the black stone
**The worst trade in the campaign.** `[TABLE]` He will pay very well. Do not stop them; do let the payment be *generous, specific and immediate*, which is the tell.

### Branch — they mention a druid, or a captured bugbear
His price collapses `[TABLE]`, and he has to reach for the mine to stay in the conversation. That is an early, cheap reveal of Wave Echo Cave — good if you want the campaign to accelerate, expensive if you don't.

### Also available, if they ask `[TABLE — strategy doc, "Trades"]`
- Identify **Talon** in thirty seconds (still unidentified since Ch. 8)
- Read **Urmon's journal** (Dwarvish — the Lost Mine, the Forge of Spells, *Lightbringer*)
- The **Netherese ring of green glass** — he will part with it in a fair trade; it costs him nothing he cares about
- The **second job**: ask Agatha the name of the tower's builder. *(She knows it: **Dresina**.)*

### NPC notes
- **Hamun Kost** — *"My dear boy, if I wanted my secrets to be safe, I would kill you."* `[TABLE — Ch. 10]` He does not threaten, does not boast, and **does not lie about anything checkable.** He omits. Playing him as a liar wastes him. Read `notes/hamun_kost_strategy.md` before the session; it is better than anything I would write here.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Can we take the sentry without killing him?"** → Yes. Grapple, knock out, tie up. Harder than killing, and a live bugbear is worth more (he can be questioned about the road to Cragmaw). Say yes and make them pay for it in rolls.
- **"How many are in the cave?"** → **You cannot see in.** Hold this. Do not give them a count from outside; the not-knowing is the entire tension of Scene 1.
- **"Do the Redbrand cloaks work on these ones?"** → No, and tell them why: a bugbear already laughed at it in Chapter 8.
- **"Isn't this the same job Harbin already paid us for?"** → **Yes. 120 gp, sitting since Phandalin.** Confirm it enthusiastically the moment anyone works it out. Do not hand it to them.
- **"Is that goblin one of the ones we saw dead?"** → Not the same individual. Same *kind*. That is the answer and it is worse than yes.
- **"What does the crystal in its head mean?"** → Nothing they can determine here. Veyra can theorise; the theory is not knowledge. **Do not let a check produce the answer** — this is Ruxithid and Chapter 5's problem, and it is not for sale tonight.
- **"Can we just ask Hamun about the drawing?"** → **Yes, and this is the best thing they can do.** See Scene 4's hinge. What they will not get is why he cares.
- **"How far is Cragmaw Castle?"** → He gives them the route. He does not go with them. *(And see Already in Motion #1 — you know what's on that road.)*

## Stage directions (timing and silence)

- **Scene 1 is silent.** Once you have described the sentry, stop offering. Let the planning discussion run without GM input. This party has never had a fortified position and time; find out what they do with it.
- **When the sentry drops, do not immediately cut to the cave.** One beat. The ravine is quiet and nothing has come out. That beat is the whole military movement paying off.
- **When Brughor falls and the bugbears break — narrate the running.** Slowly. This party ran from bugbears eight chapters ago and nobody has said it out loud since.
- **The drawing:** describe it in the order a person sees it. Charcoal. A figure. The head. Then the crystal. **Then say nothing at all and wait.** If the silence gets uncomfortable, you have run it correctly.
- **If nobody examines the walls,** the honest second chance is *light*: Maela or Veyra carries the light and the party has no darkvision on two members. When the light moves across the northwest wall on the way out, the drawing is simply in the beam. That is not a cheat — it is how caves work.
- **In Hamun's tent, when someone says the word:** he asks them to say it again. Then let the pause be **too long.** Longer than is comfortable. He is a man who has just heard his life's work confirmed by four people who do not know what they are holding, and the only thing on the table is a very good dinner.
- **Do not have Hamun explain anything at the end.** The session should close on him being quiet, not on him being informative.

## Open decisions (yours — I am not deciding these)

1. **Does Veyra's crystal flare at the drawing?** The big one. `hamun_kost_strategy.md` #1 deferred it here and here is where it pays. **I recommend yes, faintly, once.** Decide before they enter the cave, not during.
2. **Does Maela react to the drawing?** She has now been silent about the obelisk twice. Silent a third time makes it a pattern the players will start hunting. Speaking makes it a scene. Both are good; they are different campaigns.
3. **Is a psionic goblin physically present at Wyvern Tor?** The module permits it. It converts Movement Two from a static discovery into something with a pulse — and it risks turning a horror beat into a combat beat, which is the failure mode.
4. **Do any Axe-Biters survive as prisoners, and do they know the road to Cragmaw?** If yes, Hamun's price collapses and the party's leverage changes shape entirely. Decide before the rout.
5. **Does Brughor's band know about Ruxithid at all, or just tolerate the goblins?** The module says the raiders "never learn much." I have run with *tolerate and dislike*. If you want them to know something, decide what before someone interrogates one.
6. **Does the party get a level here?** Wyvern Tor is a substantial clear and they are due-ish. Sidekick parity held last time; if you bump, bump all four (`characters/sidekick_progressions.md`).
7. **How many sessions until the Tresendar ambush lands?** You have banked it on the Cragmaw road. A number in your head makes the next two sessions tighter.
8. **Does Hamun mention Wave Echo Cave tonight?** Cheap if he does, and the campaign accelerates. My read: **not tonight** — he just got safer (Already in Motion #2) and has less reason to overpay.
