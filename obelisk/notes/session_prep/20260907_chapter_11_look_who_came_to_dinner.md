Co-GM working doc, written 2026-09-07. Staging after **Chapter 11 Look who came to dinner**; proposed scenes are not played history.

[TABLE] The party ended over dinner at Old Owl Well after clearing Wyvern Tor. Hamun still owes the promised directions to Cragmaw Castle, and the party has kept Veyra’s crystal secret from him. [TABLE — GM ruling, this prep] The surviving manor bugbears are hunting them: returning to Phandalin and staying the night brings a hostage confrontation with Toblen’s family the next morning; heading straight for Cragmaw brings an interception on the Phandalin road off the Triboar Trail.

**Party [TABLE]:** Level 4. Zenvon Forepot is the player-run rogue; Pip Thistlewick, Sister Maela Dawnforge, and Veyra of the Blue Candle are sidekicks, voiced at the table by GM and player. > **Scope [OVERLAY]:** Finish Hamun’s dinner → let the party choose castle, banshee, or town → resolve the hunters where that route places them → continue toward the chosen objective. The intended stopping points are the castle approach, the conclusion of Agatha’s audience, or the family’s rescue. If play moves quickly, the castle reference below supports continuing to Gundren. Do not require all three destinations or pull the mine expedition into this session.

## The Win Condition [OVERLAY]

The party chooses its next lead with enough information to own that choice, and the consequences of the manor escape become tangible. On the road, success means stopping or redirecting the hunt before it reaches civilians. In town, it means separating the family from the hunters’ control. Agatha’s audience or reaching the castle is meaningful forward progress; neither must be sacrificed to force the other.

## The Default Trajectory [OVERLAY]

- Hamun pays the directions he owes, then tries to retain access to the party’s discoveries.
- The party returns to an operating inn and can spend the evening on ordinary business. The hunters locate their lodging, seize the family overnight or at dawn, and make their demand in the morning. If the party detects and disrupts the approach, resolve that intervention instead.
- Sildar contains the surrounding street and seeks an opening; he does not launch an assault through hostages.
- Agatha remains available. Garaele’s request stays unfinished until the correct question is answered and reported.
- At the castle, Grol continues bargaining. His visitor has a separate agenda; neither waits for the party’s preferred entrance.

## The Clever Play [OVERLAY]

## Scene 2A — Before There Are Hostages

**Setup [TABLE]:** Use on the direct castle route. The party meets the hunters on the Phandalin road just off the Triboar Trail. They are heading toward town, not carrying the family away from it.

**Approach [OVERLAY]:** Place the hunters just south of the junction, visible or audible from the trail. A goblin scout watches the junction while the bugbears argue about finding the party at its usual inn. Give the party a chance to notice that scout before mutual recognition. The burned fur or scorched equipment identifies the manor survivors; do not rely on recognizing an unconfirmed named leader.

Suggested hunt-leader line, after recognition: “There you are. Saves us asking at your inn.”

> **NPC interiority [OVERLAY]:** The leader wants to undo the humiliation of the fire in front of his own troops. The other two want a victory they can survive. The goblins were assigned to a hunt; they have no personal grievance and do not want to be first into a heroic sword.

**Branch — ambush or direct attack [OVERLAY]:** Give goblins cover and a route away; use the existing bugbear blocks. A goblin can lose its nerve when the hunters’ confidence breaks. Do not repeat the Wyvern Tor result automatically: killing one leader does not predetermine everyone else’s morale.

**Branch — talk, threaten, or bargain [OVERLAY]:** Their orders and their pride are different handles. Offer a believable exit in return for abandoning the hunt, information, or guiding the party toward the castle. A captive can confirm the route and describe an entrance personally used; knowing the road does not mean knowing every room or Vyerith’s nature.

**Branch — evade or let them pass [OVERLAY]:** The road to the castle remains open. First make their destination intelligible through the inn remark, overheard instructions, or visible southward movement. If they are knowingly allowed onward, retain the town hostage branch for a later return. No teleporting reinforcements.

**Failure with a material cost [OVERLAY]:** A failed bluff convinces a goblin that the party carries a fortune; it runs for the castle to spread that exaggeration. Track its journey and warn of the escape. The joke is an absurd price on the heroes; the cost is a potential warning reaching the garrison, not immediate omniscient alertness.

## Scene 2B — Breakfast Is Cancelled

**Setup [TABLE — revised GM ruling]:** The party returns to Phandalin and can stay the night. The trouble develops overnight; the hostage demand lands the next morning. Toblen and his family are not already prisoners when the party arrives.

**The evening [OVERLAY]:** Let them collect rewards, talk about the road, eat, and arrange beds. Put one small sign of the hunt into the scene: a watcher steps out of the inn’s lamplight when noticed. If the party investigates, let them find the scouting goblin and potentially prevent the takeover. Do not force the morning tableau after they have disrupted the plan.

**Overnight into dawn [OVERLAY]:** The hunters wait until the family begins opening the inn, then take the common room and kitchen. Their goal is leverage over armed adventurers, not a silent attack on every bedroom. Resolve any watch, alarms, or precautions the party established. A normal completed night’s sleep grants its normal rest benefits; use the dawn seizure to open the next scene after that rest.

**The morning [OVERLAY]:** There is no breakfast noise, then a heavy voice calls for the party. If they lodged upstairs, open with them in their rooms or on the landing, with the family held below. If they slept elsewhere, the demand reaches them from the inn’s entrance as they approach. Establish their actual sleeping location rather than relocating them for the scene.

**Branch — they leave town before staying [OVERLAY]:** Do not trigger a morning crisis immediately just to preserve the encounter. Track the hunters’ arrival and pursuit from the positions already established. The overnight stay is this scene’s intended trigger, not a requirement that the party sleep here.

**Proposed staging [OVERLAY]:** Use a common room, kitchen exit, and upstairs landing as a simple encounter sketch; these are staging, not a published inn map. One bugbear makes the demand beside Toblen. One guards the other family members. One watches the back. Goblins watch the street and stairs. Choose their number before running the scene.

Give the party proof of life from its actual position—upstairs, at another lodging, or outside the inn. Sildar responds when the morning disturbance becomes public, clearing bystanders and looking for a safe opening. He can hold an escape route or protect rescued hostages once the party creates one; he has not watched the takeover unopposed all night.

Suggested hunt-leader demand: “The ones from the manor. Show yourselves. Your innkeeper is waiting.”

> **NPC interiority [OVERLAY]:** The leader wants the party exposed and disarmed. He is afraid that a second escape will make him ridiculous at Cragmaw. Toblen wants his family together and alive. Play him carefully watching his captor’s hands rather than making defiant speeches. Sildar wants a rescue he can support without provoking a killing.

**Branch — negotiate in front, extract behind [OVERLAY]:** Talking creates time but does not automatically disable guards. Let the party learn the family’s position, distract one watcher, and open one exit. Bring any discovered hostage movement into the visible scene. Success can mean an evacuation while the leader is still delivering his ultimatum.

**Branch — offer an exchange [OVERLAY]:** The hunters are interested because the party is their actual target. Let negotiation establish staged releases rather than assuming all heroes surrender before anyone goes free. A false surrender can work, but its risk is real: separated allies and surrendered weapons.

**Branch — attack immediately [OVERLAY]:** Make the geometry and threatened hostages clear before resolving attacks. Guards may drag a hostage into cover or threaten harm; telegraph a deliberate execution attempt so the party has a chance to interrupt. Do not promise that reckless attacks are harmless, or make a single missed attack automatically kill a family member.

**Branch — withdraw or refuse [OVERLAY]:** The hunters initially wait, then prepare to move the family. Show it: bundles packed, a shutter opens, a guard orders everyone downstairs. Leave rescue on the road possible. Decide destination and elapsed time before moving them.

**Failure with a material cost [OVERLAY]:** A distraction wrecks the inn’s best furniture and closes the planned back exit. Toblen’s eventual bill is painfully specific. The cost is repairs and a harder rescue position; the family’s suffering is not the punchline.

**Afterward [OVERLAY]:** Give the family a quiet moment before gratitude or accounting. Rescuing them does not force Toblen to endorse every tactic. Let the party recover, collect existing rewards if sought, and choose its next road.
