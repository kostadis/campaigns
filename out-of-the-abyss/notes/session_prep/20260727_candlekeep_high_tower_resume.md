## ⭐⭐ A'lai's secret objective — the spine of the whole scene

**He is trying to get arrested, and he cannot say so.**

He knows Plan B exists. He knows Manshoon is coming tonight regardless, and that
using the bypass will cost Manshoon a weapon he wanted to keep. He knows what
happens to the man who made that necessary.

**A Candlekeep cell is the only place he sees morning from.** Free is worse —
free means going back. Dead-by-Watcher is arguably better than free.

> ⭐ **The proof is already on the table: he still has the `dimension door`
> scroll.** Scrolls don't need slots. **He has had a working exit in his pocket
> this entire fight and has not used it.** If a player thinks to ask why the
> cornered wizard isn't running, that is the whole mystery, and they've earned it.

### But he can't surrender in front of witnesses

**One Zhentarim walking out of this tower is a report that A'lai turned** — and
then the cell doesn't protect him, it just tells Manshoon the address.

**So the last thug has to die, and A'lai has to not be seen doing it.**

---

## BEAT 1 — The last thug (this round)

A'lai has cantrips and deniability. That is the only toolkit he has ever been
good with. Two ways to play it:

**The precise version — and the maths is clean.** ⭐ **One Arcane Burst kills the
thug outright:** 32 HP, already 2 down, against 27 average force damage. He has
four attacks. **He spends one of them on his own man**, played as a shot at the
party that goes wide.

That solves the witness problem in a single action and costs him a quarter of
his output — and the remaining three go wide, spread thin, or into cover. He has
spent thirty-one years being exact about things; let him be exact about this.
**Insight if anyone calls it** — against a man who is *more* fluent when lying.

**The colder version.** He does nothing at all and lets Grygum's acid finish it
(2d4 at the start of its turn, 30 to go — slow, and he may not have the time).
The tell is free to anyone paying attention: ⭐ **the wizard never once tried to
save his own men.** Grygum notices exactly this kind of thing.

> **⚠️ Failure state worth leaving open — if the party captures the thug or it
> escapes**, A'lai's plan collapses and he has to improvise in front of them.
> He'd have to kill it openly, which burns him, or fight on with cantrips, which
> he loses. **The party can wreck his night without ever understanding what they
> did.** Don't steer them off it.

## BEAT 2 — The stop

Once the witnesses are gone, he comes out. **Hands open, nothing in them.**

> **A'lai:** "I'd like to stop. Not surrender — I'm aware how that sounds, and
> I'm aware you have no reason. Stop."
>
> *(a beat; he is listening to the stairwell, not looking at them)*
>
> "I have nothing left. You will have counted; I'd be insulted if you hadn't.
> But I know which two lines of that cryptogram matter, I know where the room
> under the keep is, and I know the name of the person in Menzoberranzan who has
> been paying to keep your drow breathing."
>
> "I would very much like to tell all of it to somebody with a locked door."

**He is auditioning.** Everything he gave them tonight — Manshoon, the metal
hand, the eleven years — was the opening bid. It was never a monologue. It was
a man pricing himself.

**If Thorin asks why he's suddenly helpful**, the true answer is the one he
can't give, so he says something almost true:

> **A'lai:** "Because in about four minutes the people coming up those stairs
> will be very disappointed in me, and you strike me as the more reasonable
> disappointment."

### The capture must read as defeat

**Not a negotiation.** He needs to be visibly **overpowered**, ideally with a
hand on him — Thorin grabbing him is perfect. He resists just enough to make it
true. **He does not say thank you.**

That staging is the whole point: when the assassin arrives, what it sees is a
Great Reader who was taken, not one who folded.

## BEAT 3 — The assassin, and the scene A'lai has already written

He gets his line out first, before it can decide anything — fast, aggrieved,
completely in command of his voice:

> **A'lai:** "Took your time. They have had me since the landing." *(a small nod
> at his own bound hands — evidence)* "It's not a loss. It's a position. I've
> been in the room. **I know exactly who is carrying what.**"

Captivity reframed as fieldcraft. His alibi and his re-hire in one breath.

### ⭐⭐ And he is wrong

**A'lai does not know Grygum has it.** He watched Daz take the sapphire and
watched Daz run with it. He never saw the handoff — Sleight of Hand 19, while
A'lai was busy being counterspelled and grappled.

**So the only intelligence he has to sell is that the drow is carrying the key
and the sapphire.** He will say it with total confidence, because he is a
precise man who saw it with his own eyes.

**He points the assassin at Daz.**

> ⚠️ **Run the numbers before you do this.** The assassin's opener against a
> **surprised** target is roughly **62 damage on a single attack** — crit
> shortsword (2d6+3) + crit Sneak Attack (8d6) + 7d6 poison on a failed DC 15
> Con save — **and it gets two attacks.** Daz took 26 this session. **If that
> lands on a surprised Daz, it kills him outright.**
>
> **This is not an imposed danger.** It is the direct consequence of Daz's own
> misdirection working *too well* — and the party has a real answer, because
> they know raiders are in the building. **Surprise is the entire block.** Roll
> Stealth +9 against their passives honestly; don't assume it.

**The irony is the payload.** Daz's best play of the night is the reason the
knife comes for him. And A'lai — who has spent the evening insisting on the
difference between one thing and another — sells his employer a lie he believes.

## Open decisions

1. **Does A'lai ever drop the act?** Cornered, with the assassin's boots on the
   stair, does he plainly ask for the cell — or go to it still pretending it was
   their idea? The second is more in character; the first is the better scene.
2. **Does Manshoon get him anyway?** A'lai dead in a warded cell behind a locked
   door, a right-hand glove set neatly beside him. It was an option back in the
   original decision 2 and it is a great deal more earned now.
3. **Does A'lai see the trade before the party does?** He is a precise man
   watching Tadric — the boy whose survival cost Manshoon the whole night — stand
   in a room an assassin is about to enter. A'lai has no reason to protect him
   and one very good reason to let it happen. **Whether he says anything is the
   measure of what's left of him.**
4. **Does anyone notice the unused scroll?** Free tell, no roll needed — a
   cornered wizard with a working exit who does not take it. Available whenever
   you want a player to feel clever.

## ⚠️ The clock — he is at 62, and that is the whole tension

**The party can kill him in about two rounds.** Zalthir alone did 22 before going
down. **Thorin has not landed a single hit on him** — he went for the thugs — and
Thorin with Dawnbringer and superiority dice is the biggest gun on the board.

**And dead is not arrested.**

So this is not a man choosing his moment. **It is a man running out of time to
stop**, in front of players who have every reason to finish him and no idea he
wants to be taken alive. He has to get the words out before Thorin gets a full
turn on him.

> ### ⭐ Leave the failure state live
> If they kill him they lose the two cryptogram lines, the lava-chamber route,
> the location of the bypass, and **the third erasure piece on Daz's patron.**
> *(Yvenne's fourth tick and Vizeran's Stage 4 remain the failsafes — expensive,
> not fatal.)* And the assassin arrives to find its asset already dead, which is
> a different scene entirely. **Do not rescue him from this.** The party earned
> the position, and A'lai earned the position, and neither of them knows what
> the other is doing.

**Meanwhile he outguns them and won't use it.** Four Arcane Bursts at 27 average
is ~108 a round at 150 ft, free. He put Zalthir down **while rolling two natural
1s** — he could drop a second PC next round and will not.

⭐ **A man with 108 damage a round who is not killing anyone is the loudest tell
in the room.** Let a player notice before you explain anything.
