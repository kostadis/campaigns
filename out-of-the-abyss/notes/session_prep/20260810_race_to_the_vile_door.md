## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

**The Book of Vile Darkness does not leave Candlekeep in Manshoon's hands.**

There are **two ways to win and both are real:** reach the riddle door first,
or put him on the floor. The race is the *cheaper* win — tempo costs less than
hit points — and a sharp party will find that out. But **killing him is a
legitimate victory and it is on the table.** Don't steer them off it.

Not "the party clears the Vault" — the Echoes are the real prize and they are
*free* if the party stops swinging. The session lands if the players end it
knowing they had a choice about how to win, and made one.

Secondary landing: **A'lai's Baenre guess goes into Mike's head** and stays
there. He must never learn it's wrong tonight.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing)

- **Manshoon** works the cryptogram from the control-room records he just took.
  He is roughly six hours from the riddle door and **not in a hurry.** By
  mid-morning he is in the lava chamber; by afternoon he has the Book and
  `teleport`s out. Candlekeep never recovers the loss.
- **A'lai** is taken by the surviving Avowed, put in the null cells, and is
  dead within a tenday — Manshoon can reach him there and A'lai knows it. His
  Baenre intuition dies with him.
- **Sylvira** holds the Inner Ward with `counterspell` and dies doing it. The
  plague was going to take her anyway; she would rather it be this.
- **Tadric** keeps the wards at 30% by hand until he collapses. He is the only
  reason the keep is still lit.
- **Kalan** stays gone. The redundancy doctrine that saved the keep goes
  uncredited, and the man who wrote it is remembered as the one who ran.
- **The Echoes of Alaundo are never heard.** Echo 3 is Eldeth's call to
  Gauntlgrym. Without it, the next arc opens on a letter with no prophecy
  behind it — survivable, much weaker.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Trade with A'lai before anything else.** He is terrified and cheap right
   now. Two cryptogram lines and the secret-room location for a witnessed
   promise of the null cell. Twenty minutes of table time, two hours off the
   clock.
2. **Take Sylvira's freebies and don't ask why she's helping.** She hands over
   Vydykyq and Limniz without being asked twice.
3. **Send Tadric for Kalan.** Tadric is the one person Kalan will answer — and
   sending him **also gets Tadric out of the keep**, which quietly holds the
   Sylvira fuse for you. Two problems, one errand. If they don't think of it,
   Kalan turns up on his own anyway (below); the party just doesn't get the
   credit, and you have to manage Tadric by hand.
4. **Skip clue scenes they can buy.** Every clue run at the table is a box on
   the Manshoon Track. The party that races *fastest* is the one that does
   the *least*.
5. **In B2: go straight for the Echoes cabinet, then the riddle door.**
   Manshoon genuinely does not care about the cabinet. Fighting him is the
   trap; the correct play is to rob him of the only thing he wants while he
   is busy being menacing.
6. **Answer the riddle door with "candle" and get inside.** That's the win.
   Everything after is denouement.

> **The nudge, if they're grinding him *badly*:** have him *say it.* "You are
> spending rounds. I am spending slots. Only one of us is running out of the
> thing that matters." Then let them work it out.
>
> ⚠️ **But only if they're losing.** If they're actually landing hits and
> burning his resistances, **shut up and let them cook.** Killing him is a real
> win and the nudge above will read as you steering them off it. The line is
> for a party that is flailing, not a party that is winning the hard way.

## The Manshoon Track (the race clock)

Six boxes. **He starts on box 1** — he has the control-room records and a head
start. Mark it where the players can see it. Do not explain it.

**Advance +1 when:**
- A clue scene is run at the table (not narrated/bought)
- The party engages Manshoon in B2 instead of moving past him
- A PC loses a turn to **Ward-Eater**'s speed-0 (this is why that rider exists)
- The party takes a short rest
- The party stops to fully interrogate A'lai *after* the first trade

**Reduce −1 for each:**
- A'lai's two cryptogram lines (bought)
- Sylvira's two freebies (Vydykyq + Limniz)
- Kalan's ward-route shortcut (if he returns)
- Glabbagool's banked Whispering Dome boon, spent here

| Track | Where he is when the party arrives |
|---|---|
| **≤3** | Still in B2, browsing. **Party can reach the riddle door first.** ⭐ |
| **4–5** | In B2 and moving. Riddle door contested — footrace, one round of grace |
| **6** | **Inside B3.** Party arrives as he lifts the Book. Hardest ending |

> **Do not let it hit 6 by accident.** If the dice are running cold, eat a box.
> This clock exists to create pressure, not to punish a bad night.

# Manshoon, Depleted

**Custom block, budgeted at ≈8,400 XP (CR 12 equivalent), for four L9 PCs
+ Glabbagool.** Hard for that table is ~6,400 (≈8,000 counting Glabbagool);
deadly is ~9,600 (≈12,000). He sits at the top of *hard*.

> ⭐ **Canonical Manshoon is CR 13. He is one step below it, and the gap is the
> depletion.** If a player does the arithmetic and works out they're fighting
> a nearly-full-strength archwizard because they cost him a night's slots —
> confirm it. They earned that.

> **GM-only — the design contract, restated.** Two *independent* dials:
> **he is genuinely dangerous, and he is genuinely killable.** What stays
> engineered out is **lethality against the PCs** — nothing here one-shots a
> full-HP L9 character, and he never finishes a downed one. What is emphatically
> *not* engineered out is his own mortality. **The party can kill him tonight.**

**AC** 19 (*robe of the archmagi* + *staff of power*)
**HP** 190
**Speed** 30 ft.
**Saves** INT +13, WIS +9, CON +7 · **Spell save DC** 17, **+9** to hit
**Magic Resistance.** Advantage on saves against spells and magical effects.
**Legendary Resistance 3/day.**
**Staff of power:** 2 charges left. ⛔ **Retributive Strike is cut** — it's a
suicide-TPK button and he is a clone who very much intends to keep living.

### Legendary actions — 2/round, and deliberately near-harmless

The action economy is the difficulty. The damage is not.

- **Turn the Page** (1) — moves 20 ft without provoking.
- **Ward-Pull** (1) — one creature within 30 ft, **DC 17 Str save** or dragged
  15 ft toward him and speed halved until the end of its next turn. **No damage.**
- **Counter-Read** (2, once per round) — casts `shield` or `counterspell`
  **without expending a slot.**

> These make him feel like an archwizard — he repositions you, he denies your
> spells, he is never where you left him — while adding **almost no damage.**
> That is the whole trick. ⛔ **Do not swap any of these for a damaging option.**

### Spell slots (post-breach)
`4 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 2` (1st–6th), plus **one 7th.**
**No 8th or 9th** — he burned them getting through the wards last night, in
front of the party. Diegetic, not a fudge; say so if asked.

- **1st** `shield`, `mage armor`
- **2nd** `mirror image`, `misty step`
- **3rd** `counterspell`, `dispel magic`, `fireball`
- **4th** `dimension door`, `greater invisibility`, `banishment`
- **5th** `wall of force`, `hold monster`, `fireball` **(5th max — see cut list)**
- **6th** `globe of invulnerability`, `true seeing`
- **7th** `teleport` — **the exit, and it is now something he must *spend a turn* on**

> **Cut list — deliberately absent.** `cone of cold`, `chain lightning`,
> `disintegrate`, `power word` anything, `meteor swarm`, and `fireball` above
> 5th. A 5th-level `fireball` averages 35 — it can drop a L9 wizard from full
> but cannot kill outright. Everything on the cut list *can.* That's the line.

### Actions

***Metal Fist.*** Melee, +8 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 6 (1d4+3) bludgeoning,
and the target is **grappled** (escape DC 16) if Manshoon has a free hand.

> ⭐ **This is the courier's hand.** The glove over metal that A'lai described
> for eleven years, and the prophecy's *"his metal hand."* When it lands,
> **describe the glove and skip the damage number.** Six points is nothing;
> the recognition is everything. Also his **Daz-capture vector** — he grapples
> the visible drow, he does not stab him.

***Ward-Eater (Recharge 5–6).*** He pulls on the keep's failing lattice. Each
creature within 30 ft: **DC 17 Con save.** Fail — 22 (5d8) force damage and
**speed 0 until the end of its next turn.** Success — half, no speed loss.
**Manshoon regains 25 HP.**

> The signature. Twenty-two damage kills nobody at L9; the **speed 0** costs
> them a round of the race and the self-heal makes him *read* as unkillable —
> which is now a bluff the party can call. Every PC who fails **advances the
> Manshoon Track by 1.**

### Tactics

- **R1** — `globe of invulnerability`, then talk. He does not open with
  violence. The globe shuts off Daz and Grygum at range and deals zero damage:
  maximum menace, zero risk. **Let them discover they have to walk into it.**
- **R2** — `mirror image`. Now Zalthir's flurry eats duplicates.
- **R3** — `wall of force` to cut the party off from the riddle door, *or*
  `fireball` if they've clumped. **Prefer the wall** — it costs tempo, not HP.
- **R4+** — Ward-Eater on recharge; `Metal Fist` grapple on Daz; `banishment`
  on whoever is hurting him most (it removes a PC **safely** — no damage, they
  come back); `counterspell` anything that would actually stop him.
- **`shield` freely**, and via Counter-Read when the slot is better spent
  elsewhere. Making Thorin miss is better theatre than eating the hit.

### Exits — and every one of them is now *deniable*

He wants out by whichever comes first. **None of these are automatic.**

1. **HP ≤ 50** → he casts `teleport`. ⚠️ **This is an action on his turn, in
   the open.** `Counterspell` (DC 17 check vs 7th level), a stun, a readied
   action, or `Silence` all stop it. **If they stop it, he does not get another
   chance that round — and he is at ≤50 HP.**
2. **Counterspelled twice** → professional insult; he leaves if he can.
3. ⭐ **The party reaches the riddle door** → nothing here is worth the slot
   any more. He goes. **Cheapest win for the party.**

> **He does not kill tonight — but he can absolutely be killed tonight.**
> A dropped PC is face-down and stable; he steps over them. If the table needs
> to hear it: *"I am not here for your friends. I have never once been here for
> your friends."* That is a statement about **his** priorities, not plot armour
> for him.

### On Legendary Resistance

**Three.** Zalthir's Stunning Strike is the party's win button against a caster,
and the fight's whole shape is him burning all three and then **the fourth stun
landing.** Let it land. A stunned Manshoon at ≤50 HP **cannot teleport out** —
that is the party's kill window and it should feel exactly that decisive.
**Do not add a fourth resistance because the fight is going well.**

## If they kill him — run this

Do not soften it, do not walk it back, and do not have a contingency fire.
**He dies.** Let the table have the full weight of having killed Manshoon.

- The body goes **waxy, not bloody** — too smooth, too even, like a candle that
  cooled wrong. Nobody at Candlekeep can say why.
- **The metal hand comes away clean at the wrist.** No scarring, no join, no
  surgery. It was never attached; it was *fitted.* ⭐ **Guaranteed trophy** —
  the prophecy's *"his metal hand"*, now literal.
- On the inside of the wrist-cup, in tiny engraved Thorass: **a number.**
  Not a name, not a rune. A number. Do not explain it. Do not let an NPC
  explain it. **This is the entire clone plant** and it costs one sentence.
- **The Book is safe.** Candlekeep owes them a debt it cannot discharge, and
  the Zhentarim lose their Candlekeep operation permanently.
- **A'lai's reaction is the scene.** Eleven years of a gloved courier, and the
  man on the floor has a hand that unscrews. He starts laughing and can't stop.
  He will not explain why either, because he doesn't know — he only knows he
  was never talking to a person.

> **The clone return — how to play it as a beat, not a takeback.** Manshoon is
> canonically a chain of clones (WDH runs on exactly this; your own session
> record already has him as *"the man who died more than once and kept
> returning"*). So do **not** bring him back soon, and never as a "gotcha."
> Bank it. He surfaces at Gauntlgrym or later, and the reveal lands as
> *recognition* — the number on the wrist, and a second hand with a different
> number — rather than as the GM undoing a win. **They still killed him.**
> They killed a Manshoon. That is what killing Manshoon has always meant, and
> the party is now one of the very few groups in the Realms who knows it.

### If they kill him *and* he never cast `teleport`
He still had the slot. He chose to spend the round on `banishment` or the
grapple instead, because he did not believe he was going to lose. **Say that
out loud in the aftermath.** Arrogance is a better cause of death than
arithmetic.

# SESSION A — THE RACE

## Scene 1 — The Cold Open: A'lai in the Dark

### Setup
Pre-dawn, Deneir's Sanctum or wherever they've stashed him. A'lai bound.
Somewhere above, faintly, the sound of a very old man browsing a library at
his leisure. **Tadric** is holding the wards together by hand and has been
awake for a day and a half.

### Approach
Open on the sound, not the man. Manshoon is audible before he is relevant —
a page turning, unhurried, from three floors up.

A'lai talks before he's asked. He is not brave and has stopped pretending.

> *"He is not looking for me. That is the part you are not understanding.
> He has never once looked for me. He sent a courier for eleven years and the
> courier wore a glove, and I never saw the face, and I told myself that was
> discretion."*
>
> *"It was not discretion. It was that I was not worth a face."*

**What he trades, and what he actually has:**

| He offers | Real? |
|---|---|
| Location of the secret room beneath the keep | ✅ True and useful |
| **The two cryptogram lines that matter** | ✅ True — **−1 Track each** |
| A house in Menzoberranzan paying to keep a drow breathing | ⚠️ **A guess. Wrong.** |
| What's *really* in the depository | 🔒 **Withheld** — his only card |

**The Baenre line — deliver it exactly once, and do not oversell it:**

> *"I could not trace the payments. I could only trace the patience. Eleven
> years, and never once a demand — nobody is that patient who is not that
> safe. There is one house in Menzoberranzan that can afford to be patient."*
>
> *"Baenre. It must be Baenre. Nobody else can afford to wait that long for
> nothing."*

> **GM-only.** He is **wrong.** The patron is **Vizeran DeVir**, through
> cutouts, and A'lai has no idea Vizeran exists. But the guess is *productively*
> wrong: Baenre is canonically the house that **will** receive the party in
> Menzoberranzan — Quenthel doesn't obstruct adventurers hunting demon lords
> (OOTA Ch.15) — so the false lead walks them toward the one door that opens.
>
> ⛔ **The name DeVir is not spoken at Candlekeep. Not tonight, not by anyone.**
> Hard guardrail — see `docs/daz_devir_narrative_arc.md`. Vizeran gets that
> line, in a safe room, at Stage 4. Tonight Mike gets a wrong house and his
> own paranoia, which is exactly the arc's Act I design ("use at least one
> false lead to prevent a linear reveal").

### Branch — the party refuses to deal
Fine. They run all six clues at the table. **Track +2 over the session.** A'lai
goes to the null cells with everything still in his mouth and becomes a
Gauntlgrym-era asset instead. Nothing breaks; the night just gets longer.

### Branch — the party threatens instead of trading
He gives up the cryptogram lines immediately and **lies about the house** —
names **Mizzrym**, because Ilvara is a name these people visibly react to and
he is buying goodwill with something he thinks they want. Now the false lead
is a *worse* false lead, and Baenre stays unsaid until Menzoberranzan.

### NPC notes
- **A'lai** — wants a null cell and to be too boring to kill. Tic: narrates his
  own failures in the third person, like a review he's already written. Wants
  from this scene: to be *worth keeping alive.*
- **Tadric** — grey with exhaustion, still doing the job. Tic: apologises before
  delivering bad news. Wants: someone to relieve him. Does not ask.

## Scene 2 — The Cryptogram Race

### Setup
Investigators' Office. Wards at 30% on Tadric's manual reroute, straining
visibly at the edge of every lit chamber. Six clues on the table.
**Read them verbatim from `candlekeep_day_four.md` Beat 2 / arc file.**

### Sylvira arrives
She lets herself in without knocking and starts working before anyone greets
her. She has Vydykyq and Limniz already annotated.

> **GM-only — the bomb under the table.** **She does not know Moziqodo is dead**,
> and she does not know the party did it. Play her *warm.* Play her grateful.
> Let her thank Thorin for something small. **Do not foreshadow it. Do not have
> her ask where her son is.** If a player asks after Moziqodo, she says he's
> resting and moves on.
>
> ⏳ **Fuse length: banked for Gauntlgrym** (ruling 2026-08-10). She goes the
> whole Candlekeep arc without knowing. Every kind thing she does across both
> sessions becomes retroactively awful when it lands. **You have to keep a
> straight face for two sessions** — that's the cost, and it's worth it.

> ⚠️ **The leak risk is Tadric, and you must manage him deliberately.**
> `planning.md`: *Tadric identified Moziqodo as Sylvira's son.* He is alive, he
> is in Scene 1, and he is the **only** surviving person who both knows the
> relationship and would think to tell her. If you play him naturally he will
> walk into her chambers and say it.
>
> **Three ways to hold the fuse, pick one and commit:**
> - **He's too busy.** He has not slept in a day and a half and is hand-holding
>   the ward lattice. He genuinely does not have a free hour. *(Simplest.)*
> - **He asks the party to do it,** and they don't. Now the silence is theirs,
>   which makes the Gauntlgrym detonation land on the players rather than on
>   an NPC's oversight. *(Best — but only if someone actually agrees to it.)*
> - **He assumes she's been told.** The keep is in chaos; four bodies came out
>   of the High Tower. He never thinks to check. *(Most fragile — a single
>   player question breaks it.)*
>
> **The bodies themselves are not a problem.** Moziqodo, Bookwyrm and the
> Zhentarim dead all came out of the same fight in the same hour, and the
> surviving Avowed are handling them badly and in a hurry. Nobody is doing
> forensics tonight.

> **If somebody at the table decides to tell her anyway:** stop the scene, let
> it go quiet, and play it straight. That's a real choice and it deserves the
> air. She still helps — but she stops speaking to whoever said it, and the
> Gauntlgrym payload is spent here instead. **Don't punish the honesty.**

**Clue allocation with the buys in:**

| # | Answer | Location | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vydykyq | Drakonoikos / catacombs | **Sylvira freebie** — narrate |
| 2 | Limniz | Astronomicon Orrery | **Sylvira freebie** — narrate ⚠️ *see below* |
| 3 | Fustilugs | Philosopher's Court | **A'lai's line** (if bought) |
| 4 | F-A-D-E | Melodrome / Jook's Box | ⭐ **RUN** — doppelganger + 1 assassin |
| 5 | Chop Chop to the Rock | Jewel of the Styx | ⭐ **RUN** — midnight wights |
| 6 | Bow | School of Drama (Batbayar) | **A'lai's line** (if bought) |

**Run two. Narrate four.** That is what makes this a one-session race instead
of two.

> ⚠️ **Clue 2 is narrated, but don't narrate it flat.** Sylvira hands them
> Limniz — and the Orrery is where **Manshoon's mage and two thugs are
> looting.** Say so. *"She already has it. She also mentions, without any
> particular emphasis, that when she went past the Astronomicon there were
> three people in it who did not work here."* The party skips a fight and
> **learns the race is real** in the same sentence. That's the cheapest
> tension in the session.

**The two run at the table are 4 and 5 — one fight, one dread:**
- **4 · Melodrome / Jook's Box** — his **doppelganger + assassin**, caught
  mid-theft. This is the only time before the Vault that the players see
  Manshoon's operation with their own eyes. Make them *working*, not waiting.
- **5 · Jewel of the Styx** — **midnight wights.** A hazard, not an ambush.
  The best atmosphere of the six; nothing here should feel aimed at them.

> **If the party doesn't buy from A'lai,** clues 3 and 6 have to run too —
> four scenes instead of two, **Track +2**, and the session almost certainly
> breaks at the lava chamber instead of the riddle door. That is a legitimate
> outcome of refusing the deal. Let it cost what it costs.

### Branch — Kalan comes back (ruling: mid-race, ~clue 5)

**He returns during the race, not before it** — the party is already running
when he turns up, so there is no time to hold a tribunal about it. That is
deliberate: it makes this a redemption rather than a negotiation, and it stops
the session stalling on an argument nobody can win.

- **If the party sent Tadric** (Clever Play #3) — Kalan arrives *with* him,
  slightly earlier, and Tadric is conveniently out of the keep for an hour.
  ⏳ **This holds the Sylvira fuse for free.** Reward it.
- **If they didn't** — he turns up on his own at the Jewel of the Styx, having
  worked out where they'd have to be. Same scene, less credit.

He is filthy, has not slept, and does not apologise first.

> *"I used you. It worked. I am not going to stand here and pretend that was
> justice — it was arithmetic, and you were the cheaper number."*
>
> *"The lattice has a second route. I built it because I do not trust anything
> that only works one way. You can be angry at me on the stairs."*

**−1 Track.** He does not ask to be forgiven and he does not come into the
Vault. He goes and holds the wards with Tadric, which is the job.

> ⭐ **The thematic close is his.** They lost the case — Bookwyrm died before
> anyone could pin it on her, and now she's dead and unconvicted. What they
> kept is the *standard.* The methodical man and his redundancy doctrine are
> still standing, and the keep is still lit because of it. Land that in Beat 7
> of `candlekeep_vault_session.md`, reinstated-Gatewarden version.

### Failure is material, not numeric
A blown clue scene costs a **Track box**, not hit points. If they fumble the
Jewel of the Styx, the midnight wights don't kill anyone — they cost the party
*twenty minutes*, and Manshoon is twenty minutes better off. Say it out loud
when it happens: **"You hear a page turn."**

## Scene 4 — B2, The Vault: The Roadblock

### Setup
~100 glyph-warded tomes (`glyph of warding` + `suggestion`: *"Put it back.
Leave. Forget."* — 4d8 force, Wis DC 15; **Zalthir's Watcher's Stillness gives
advantage**). **Plant the Echoes cabinet clearly** — far wall, locked velvet
tray, ten prismatic gemstones, brass plaque: *"The Final Voices of the Seer."*

**Sylvira stops at the threshold and does not come in.**
> *"I am one archmage to his one archmage. I cannot be inside that room.
> I will hold the door."*
**Don't argue her in.** Let it read two ways: professional judgement, and a
woman with nothing left making very sure this one doesn't get past her.

### Manshoon, in the room
He does not look up immediately. When he does:

> *"By all means, browse. The trick is leaving with what you came for.
> Did you come for the book, or did you come for me?"*

🟣 **Three choices — and only one of them is a win:**

1. **Engage him.** Real fight, block above. **Track +1 per round engaged.**
   Expensive — and *he is fine with it*, because every round is a round nobody
   is racing and he started ahead. **But it can end with him dead**, and that
   ends the race permanently. High risk, highest ceiling.
2. **Race him to B3 directly.** Worse position — he `wall of force`s the
   approach and they take the long way. **Track +1.**
3. ⭐ **Take the Echoes, then go.** Cabinet, DC 18 thieves' tools. **Manshoon
   genuinely does not care** — he'll comment drily while they crack it. **The
   cheapest win**, and it costs zero Track.

> **If they fight, commit to it.** Don't hedge and don't telegraph an escape.
> Let Thorin land the crit, let Zalthir burn all three Legendary Resistances,
> and if they get him to ≤50 with a stun up — **let them take him.** The fight
> is built to be winnable at L9 by a party that plays it well; the only thing
> it is built to prevent is a PC dying.

### Branch — they try to talk to him
Excellent, and he'll oblige at length. He is genuinely, unhurriedly curious
about Daz — a drow, at Candlekeep, with a Menzoberranzan problem.

> *"You are a long way from a city that says you are dead. Somebody paid for
> that. I would like very much to know who — professionally. I collect people
> other people have already invested in."*

⛔ **He does not know about Vizeran either.** He knows *someone* is protecting
Daz and he finds it interesting. That's all. **No DeVir. No name.**

### Branch — they go for the Echoes and he lets them
He does. He's telling the truth. He wants one book and it is not in this room.
**Let this be a real win with no catch** — the party correctly read an
antagonist's priorities and got paid for it. Don't add a twist.

### NPC notes
- **Manshoon** — wants the Book, unhurried, and is faintly enjoying the
  company. Tic: talks about the party in the past tense, as a completed
  transaction. Wants from this scene: to not have to spend the 7th-level slot.
- **Sylvira** — wants this to have cost somebody something. Tic: gives orders
  as though they're observations. Wants: to still be useful before the plague
  finishes.

## Scene 6 — The Riddle Door (the finish line)

Answer: **"candle."**

Beyond it, in cold rancid air, the Book on a cracked stone pedestal. Touching
it as a non-evil creature: **8d6 necrotic.**

**Resolve by Track:**

- **≤3 — they beat him.** The door is shut and the Book is on the pedestal.
  🟣 Take / leave / destroy (Grygum: `dispel evil and good` → `holy aura` →
  holy fire). **Don't push.** Player choice, full stop. Manshoon arrives to
  find the room already decided and **exits by trigger 3** — no fight needed,
  and the loss should visibly cost him.
- **4–5 — contested.** Footrace to the door, one round of grace. Whoever
  answers "candle" first gets in. If it's the party, as above. If it's
  Manshoon, they arrive as he's reaching.
- **6 — he's inside.** He has the Book — **and he is at his most vulnerable.**
  Hands full, `wall of force` spent, the party between him and the stairs, and
  he now has to *get out carrying something.* This is the best kill window in
  the session for a party willing to take the risk. If they don't take it:
  he escapes with the Book and a wound, and **drops the metal-fingered glove**
  in the scramble. ⭐ Trophy either way.

> **All three are good endings, and each has a kill branch inside it.** Track 6
> is not a failure state — it's the canonical mid-case from
> `candlekeep_murders_arc.md`, it makes the Manshoon-pursuit thread burn hotter
> into Gauntlgrym, *and* it's the round where he's most catchable. Don't grimace
> at the table if it lands there.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Can we just kill him?"** → **Yes.** Say so plainly if asked out of
  character — he is hard, he is not invulnerable, and there is no scripted
  escape. He'll *try* to leave at ≤50 HP and they can stop him. A table that
  knows the rules commits properly; a table that suspects a fudge disengages.
- **"Wait, doesn't he just come back? Isn't he a clone guy?"** → If a player
  knows the lore, **confirm it and don't deflate the win.** *"You'd be killing
  a Manshoon. Nobody in the Realms has ever managed more than that."* That's
  true, it's cool, and it protects the beat for later.
- **"Why did we bother with the keys?"** → They bought twelve hours of his
  depletion. Without that race he'd have arrived with 8th- and 9th-level slots
  and this would be an obituary. **Let them feel cheated for a beat, then give
  them that.**
- **"Is A'lai telling the truth about Baenre?"** → He believes it completely.
  Nothing at Candlekeep can confirm or deny it. That's the honest answer and
  it's also the trap.
- **"Where's Moziqodo?"** (from Sylvira, or about her) → She thinks he's
  resting. **You** know. Don't blink.
- **"Can we take the Book and use it?"** → Yes. 8d6 necrotic to touch it as a
  non-evil creature, and it becomes a permanent Manshoon-pursuit magnet. Say
  the cost plainly and let them choose.
- **"Can Sylvira just come in and fight him?"** → She won't, and her reason is
  good. If they push hard, she comes in and it goes badly for her — which is a
  legitimate outcome, but make sure they chose it with open eyes.

## Stage directions

- **Open on the page turning.** Before any exposition, before initiative — one
  sound, three floors up, unhurried. Come back to it between scenes.
- **When A'lai says "Baenre," stop.** Do not explain, qualify, or let another
  NPC react. Move to the next thing. Mike will do the rest for free.
- **When the Metal Fist first lands,** describe the glove and skip the damage
  number entirely. Let somebody at the table make the connection out loud.
- **When Sylvira thanks them,** don't cut away fast. Let it be warm and let it
  sit a beat too long.
- **After Echo 3, count to five before saying anything.**
- **If Manshoon leaves empty-handed,** he doesn't threaten. He looks at the
  Book, looks at the party, and says nothing at all. That's the scarier exit.
- **If Manshoon dies,** do not narrate over it. No last words, no monologue,
  no contingency shimmer. He falls, the room is quiet, and the next thing
  anyone hears is A'lai starting to laugh. **Let the number on the wrist be
  found by a player, not offered by you** — and when it is, describe it and
  move on without comment.
