## ⭐⭐ The correction — A'lai was never desperate. He was *wrong*.

The old doc had him as a terrified man buying walls. **Replace that.** He is a
precise, cowardly man who ran the numbers, reached a conclusion, and had exactly
one input wrong.

> **His model:** No key #2 → the orb can't be commanded → the wards stay up →
> **Manshoon cannot come to Candlekeep.** The operation aborts. Manshoon writes
> off an asset and regroups somewhere else.
>
> **Therefore Candlekeep is the safest place on Faerûn**, and a Candlekeep cell
> is not a hedge or a hiding place. It is the *optimal play*.

He isn't scrambling for the least-bad option. He's selecting the best one. That
changes the register of everything he says: **cool, transactional, a man
arranging terms** — not a man begging for a door.

### ⭐ Where the model is wrong

He knows the Vault route — he memorised the cryptogram. He knows his own breach
exists; he opened it. What he does not believe is that **Manshoon will walk the
slow, loud way in for one book.** No one of that stature enters a warded keep
full of Watchers for a job that's already gone noisy.

**He is not ignorant of the road. He is wrong about the man.** Eleven years of
service and he mispriced the only actor that mattered.

## Why the monologue happened (retroactive, and it works)

The full Doctor-Evil speech after Zalthir goes down is not indulgence. **It is
the plan.**

| He needs | The monologue delivers |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Every slot is gone. Talking is the only action he has that isn't losing. |
| **Everyone in the room, visible** | He is about to crush the sapphire. Whichever way it resolves, he needs eyes on all four of them at the moment it does. |
| **A frame for the stone** | ⭐ He tells them it summons Manshoon. **This is a lie.** |

### ⭐⭐ The sapphire bluff — and how it costs him everything

The stone is `instant summons`, keyed to the High Tower key. It is **not** a
Manshoon signal. That was rhetoric — a man making a small object sound like a
countdown so nobody would reach for it.

**Crushing it is a no-lose instrument, and this is why he's still in the fight
at 62 hit points instead of reading his scroll:**

- Key stashed unattended → **it teleports into his hand.** He already holds key
  #1. That's both. Wall of force, into the chamber, command the orb, wards down.
  **The arc ends tonight, in his favour, in about twelve seconds.**
- Key carried by a PC → recall fails, and the stone **names the carrier and
  their location.** He hands that to the professionals and lets them do the
  killing.

He assumes the second is unlikely — the party would be fools to carry it into a
tower assault, and Kalan would never have allowed it. **He is playing for the
first branch.**

> ⭐ **And his own bluff is what loses it.** Nobody spends an action stealing a
> paperweight. By dressing the stone up as a doomsday trigger, he made it worth
> a *telekinesis*. Daz rips it out of his hand mid-crush — to stop a summons
> that was never going to happen — and takes the one instrument that would have
> found the key. **A man undone by being too fluent.**

### What the theft tells him: nothing

He knows the summon story is fiction, so he knows exactly why Daz grabbed it,
and that reason is uninformative. He does not learn the key is elsewhere. He
does not learn it's in the room. **He is left with zero information and falls
back on eyewitness memory — the exact faculty a Sleight of Hand 19 exists to
beat.** He watched Daz's hand take the stone. He never saw the handoff to
Grygum.

## ⭐ BEAT — The assassin arrives (REPLACES old Beat 3 entirely)

**⛔ Cut the old framing.** "The scene A'lai has already written" is backwards.
He has written nothing. **The assassin's arrival is a surprise, and it is the
moment his entire model collapses in front of him.**

### What the arrival proves

The operation **did not abort.** Manshoon is coming anyway, the hard way,
tonight. Which means A'lai is not a failed asset who gets written off — he is
**the man who made the hard way necessary.** That is precisely the profile that
gets killed, and he works it out in real time, hands empty, in front of four
witnesses.

### The panic blurt

Not an audition. Not a cool sale. **He is buying seconds with the only currency
he has, and the currency is counterfeit.**

> **A'lai:** *(too fast, pitched wrong for him)* "Did you kill the wizard? The
> drow — the one who ran. **Did you kill him?** He has the stone, he has the
> key, he went down the stair—"

⭐ **He is wrong.** Grygum has both. And he'd know what he'd just done roughly
one heartbeat after saying it: he sicced a professional on the party in front of
the party — the same people whose custody was his entire plan. **The first
imprecise thing he has done all night, and it's the one that counts.**

### Why the assassin didn't kill Daz on the way in

Because a lone wizard fleeing *down* and *away* from the objective is not the
job. The assassin came for the tower and whatever is in it. **Daz's misdirection
worked perfectly and nobody even noticed it working.**

### ⭐⭐ The assassin has had enough

It does not negotiate, does not report, does not ask A'lai a single question. It
answers the only thing that matters and then closes the file.

> **Assassin:** "He couldn't wait."

**Then it kills him.** Mid-sentence if you can manage it.

> **Stage directions.** No flourish, no speech, no acknowledgement that A'lai is
> anyone in particular. He has spent eleven years being the inside man and the
> organisation he served puts him down as an **administrative matter**. Let the
> party watch it happen and let nobody in the room say anything for a beat.

### ⭐ The thematic payload — this is the arc's thesis landing

The murderer they have chased for the entire Candlekeep arc is executed in front
of them by someone who doesn't care who he is. **No trial, no conviction, no
confession entered into any record.** Bookwyrm died before conviction. Janussi
is dead. Alkrist confessed to a case nobody is left to prosecute. Now A'lai dies
mid-sentence.

> *"The methodology survived. The case is still lost."* — this is where that
> lands, and it lands harder than a cell ever would have.

## Open decisions

1. **Does A'lai get the sentence out?** Killed mid-blurt is crueller and faster.
   Letting him finish means the party hears the whole wrong accusation and *then*
   watches him die for it. Either works; the second gives Daz something to carry.
2. **Does anyone try to save him?** Grygum has the range and the theology for it.
   Whether anyone spends a reaction on the man who murdered Janussi — after he
   just pointed a knife at Daz — is a real character question. Don't prompt it.
3. **Does the party understand what they watched?** A'lai's whole calculation is
   invisible from outside. Without the unused `dimension door` scroll being
   noticed, they may read this as "the bad guy got betrayed" and miss that they
   watched a man discover he'd been wrong about one thing for eleven years.
   ⭐ **The scroll on the body is the free tell** — no roll needed.
4. **Tadric.** Still in the room, still no plot armour, and now there's an
   assassin in it too.
