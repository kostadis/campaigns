## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

Tonight closes with **Kalan alive** — the case against Bookwyrm was never
enough, and that's *fine*, because catching her was never the actual point.
The tangible win on the table is **the Tadric race**: reach him in time (or
heroically fail) and either deny Manshoon the real key or lose it under
circumstances the party earned rather than one they slept through. Bookwyrm
is already dead by the time this session opens in earnest — not a thing
anyone can fix. The party leaves knowing the murder case is moot and a
bigger, still-unnamed war just announced itself.

## The Default Trajectory (if the party coasts)

- They stand in the wrecked Sanctum congratulating themselves on the horror
  fight and never ask *why* three CR4 constructs died to the last body for
  a key that read as nothing under detect magic.
- Word of Bookwyrm's death arrives and they treat it as a second, unrelated
  crisis instead of the same one.
- They never find Kalan. Tadric dies off-stage, alone. They learn afterward,
  secondhand, that their key was fake and Manshoon now holds both. Kalan
  still lives — the floor holds even here — but the night reads as a loss.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. Someone asks the question the fight just handed them for free: *why did
   a construct die to the last body for a key that's worth nothing?*
2. Recall Kalan's exact words at the handoff: *"I have given it now to
   someone who is not me."* Decoy to them, real one to someone else.
3. Realize they don't know **who or where** — the information wall — and
   that Kalan himself is the only way through it.
4. Remember his breadcrumb: *"do not look for me at my post."* His post is
   the Gatehouse. He isn't there on purpose.
5. Reach him fast enough to get the shortcut, and treat the race to the
   Echoing Hall as the real fight of the night — not mop-up after the
   horrors.

## Scene 1 — Aftermath in the Wreckage

### Setup
Deneir's Sanctum, seconds after the last horror collapses into rust and
scrap. Daz at 3 HP. Food from earlier still on the table, untouched.
**Fembris Lancer** is still locked in with them — he witnessed the key
exchange earlier and is the party's only standing person-of-interest.
Daral, freshly cured, mid-monologue about sentient-sword semiotics before
the horrors interrupted — now silent, badly shaken.

### Approach
Let the party breathe for a beat before anything else happens. Play the
room: shattered windows, a door in splinters, rust-dust settling. No new
threat yet — this is the calm the pivot needs room to land in.

> **GM-only.** Fembris is innocent and **not a thread** — just another guy
> caught up in the mess. If a player presses him, he confirms what he
> already witnessed and nothing more; don't dress it up as a lead. Don't
> spend table time here — if the party doesn't bring him up, don't either.

### Branch — they search the wreckage / tend to Daz first
Nothing of note in the rust piles (constructs, not treasure). Healing Daz
buys no story cost — let it happen. This is where the session's energy
should actually go.

### NPC notes
- **Fembris Lancer** — rattled, honest, innocent, forgettable. Answer
  questions if asked; don't prompt them.
- **Daral** — cured, shaken silent by violence; not a target tonight.

## Scene 2 — The Pivot ("Nothing Under Detect Magic")

### Setup
Same room, minutes later, as the adrenaline fades. This resolves Gates 1–2
from the source doc's Seam structure.

### Approach
**Do not announce the fake-key fact.** If the table stalls, prompt once:
*"Someone recast — or remembers casting — detect magic on the key. What did
it show?"* That's a free recall, no roll. Once it lands, let a player
connect Kalan's exact words from the earlier handoff: *"I have given it now
to someone who is not me."* The room should tilt: decoy to them, a real one
to somebody else.

### Branch — they connect it fast
Move straight into Scene 3 — word of Bookwyrm reaches them (or they go
looking for Kalan directly, if word hasn't arrived yet).

### Branch — they're slow to connect it
Let Bookwyrm's death (Scene 3) arrive first and supply the urgency instead;
the pivot can land a beat later, in the panic — *"wait, why did they want
an empty key that badly?"*

### NPC notes
None — this scene is the party's own reasoning. Your job is patience, not
exposition.

## Scene 3 — Word Reaches Them (Bookwyrm Is Dead)

### Setup
**A Watcher's alarm bell** starts tolling wrong — not the hour, an alarm
pattern. Impersonal, institutional: the keep itself reacting, not one NPC
delivering news. Bigger than any single messenger could make it feel.

> **GM-only.** Bookwyrm is already dead — throat torn (not the heart this
> time), key #1 gone for good, her First Reader pendant missing as a
> trophy. A half-finished note on her desk: *"He is using the Beast to—"*
> cut off. **Do not let this note, or anything else tonight, name Manshoon.**
> Keep the reveal atmospheric: the wards should already read as *wrong* —
> a flickering torch, a structural groan, a hum that isn't supposed to be
> there — something a PC with the right sense (Daz's arcane instincts,
> Zalthir's Underdark-honed ear) can clock as "the wards are failing,"
> without anyone naming why.

### Branch — they go see the body/scene themselves
**Free beat, no clock cost.** Grim payoff, just confirmation and
atmosphere — glimpsing the body on the way to Kalan doesn't meaningfully
delay them. Don't punish the curiosity.

### Branch — they skip it and move straight to finding Kalan
Same clock either way, per above — this isn't the "reward speed" fork
Scene 4 is. Let them choose based on what they want to see, not on cost.

### NPC notes
- **Bookwyrm / Skoda Vanaster** — already dead; a fact now, not a scene
  partner. No interiority needed here.

## Scene 4 — The Seam: Finding Kalan

*(Gates 3–4 of the source doc's four-gate structure — 1–2 are handled by
Scene 2.)*

### Setup
The party needs Kalan and only Kalan. He never named Tadric or a location
to them directly — that's the information wall (Gate 3). The only way
through it is finding the man himself (Gate 4).

### Approach
His own breadcrumb, from earlier: *"If I do not see you tomorrow, do not
look for me at my post."* His post is the Gatehouse. He isn't there on
purpose — he's gone to ground at the **Sea Warden's Tower**, lost among his
own Watchers.

**Gate this through Daz.** If a player just *remembers* the "do not look
for me at my post" line unprompted, that's free — no roll, they go straight
to the Sea Warden's Tower. Otherwise, this is a **Daz Investigation check**
(DC 13–15, your call in the moment) to work out that "his post" means the
Gatehouse and that the line was Kalan telling them, in advance, not to look
there.

Found, he gives them everything at once: *"Tadric. A Watcher — loyal,
alone, and he does not know what he carries or what's coming for it. The
Echoing Hall, in the north galleries — I posted him away from the barracks
on purpose. Run."* He can hand over a shortcut — a Watcher's stair / key to
a barred north-gallery door — as a reward for reaching him fast; it turns
the long way (down through the central wards and back up) into a straight
line.

### Branch — Daz succeeds (or a player recalls the line free)
Straight to the Sea Warden's Tower, no detour. They get the shortcut and
arrive **during** Moziqodo's attack (round 1–2) — the fight is still
winnable.

### Branch — Daz fails
They check the Gatehouse first — the obvious wrong answer. A Watcher there
redirects them ("he's not been at his post all evening") — a real but
recoverable delay before they correct course to the Tower. Costly, not
fatal.

### Branch — they never think to involve Daz / never search for Kalan at all
They're guessing Tadric's location blind. Treat any recovery here (a
scrying spell, a lucky divination) as a stretch goal, not something you
manufacture for them.

### NPC notes
- **Kalan Strongbranch** — alive on purpose, hiding out of doctrine, not
  cowardice: never bet everything on one holder, including himself. Relief
  and grief hit him at once when the party reaches him — relieved they
  lived, grieving Bookwyrm with no time to show it. He won't fight beside
  them (sidelined, watched) but he aims them and gives the shortcut.

## Scene 5 — The Race (Front 2: Moziqodo & Tadric)

### Setup
The **Echoing Hall** — a domed rotunda where footsteps echo long after a
scholar has stopped moving. Tadric is alone, running, not dueling — he has
no idea what's hunting him.

### The clock (compact — full block in Appendix A3 of the source doc)
**Moziqodo** — CR5, AC 15, HP 85, climb 40/Shadow Leap. ~2-round kill clock
on a lone Watcher: Pounce (DC 15 STR save or prone) → grapple → Throat-Tear
(bonus action vs. prone/grappled, 18 dmg). If the party arrives via Kalan's
shortcut, they land mid-fight (round 1–2) — winnable. Without it: round 3+,
they find him already dead, the Beast one Shadow Leap from gone.

### Branch — Tadric saved (rare, per the locked "near-impossible" default)
The real key is denied to Manshoon. The High Tower Library stays sealed a
while longer. Earned, and it should feel huge.

### Branch — Tadric dies, real key lost (the expected outcome tonight)
Manshoon ends the night holding both keys. The wards fail faster. The High
Tower becomes the next session's battlefield. **Kalan still lives** — the
floor holds regardless.

### NPC notes
- **Moziqodo** — a clock, not a boss. Carries the new banked backstory
  (A'lai's long game against Sylvira) — doesn't come up tonight.
- **Tadric** — decent, loyal, unaware. Dies bravely or lives gratefully
  depending on how fast the party moves.

## Scene 6 — The Cliffhanger

Bookwyrm dead — no rescue, no conviction, ever. Key #1 gone to Manshoon,
for certain. The wards visibly/atmospherically failing — no name spoken
tonight. Kalan alive — land his line soft and quiet, not triumphant:

> **Kalan:** *"You couldn't prove it. Neither could I. But you kept the
> door shut one more night — and they didn't get what they came for."*

**Milestone level-up to 9,** at the very end, after the dust settles.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Is our key even real?"** → Confirm once asked. Don't volunteer it.
- **"Can we save Bookwyrm?"** → No — she's already dead by the time word
  reaches them.
- **"Can we save Tadric?"** → Yes, brutal odds, priced honestly.
- **"Why would Kalan give us a fake?"** → His redundancy doctrine, not a
  betrayal — the sting is the hook, not a wound to litigate mid-fight.
- **"Who's actually behind this?"** → Not tonight. Bank "someone bigger is
  behind A'lai" for the High Tower session.

## Stage directions (timing/silence cues)

- Let "why would a construct die for a worthless key" hang in silence
  before anyone answers it themselves.
- When Bookwyrm's death is announced, don't editorialize — let the party
  supply the irony.
- Once the race is live, cut between it and any lingering Sanctum business
  every round — the crosscut *is* the clock.
- Let Kalan's closing line land soft. Don't let the table cheer over it.
