# Candlekeep — The Party's Side (what they think they won)

Co-GM working doc. **Companion to `20260728_candlekeep_alai_the_safest_place.md`**
— that file is the villain's interior; this one is the table's. Read them
together; they describe the same ninety seconds from opposite sides of an
information gap.

Board state as in the resume doc. Nothing here changes what happened; it tracks
**what the party believes happened**, which is a different thing, and the
distance between the two is the entire scene.

---

## ⭐⭐ The core asymmetry

| What the party believes | What is actually true |
|---|---|
| The sapphire summons Manshoon | ⭐ **It doesn't.** It recalls the High Tower key, or names whoever carries it. The summons was A'lai's bluff. |
| Daz stopped Manshoon arriving | ⭐ **Daz blinded A'lai.** He stopped a *detection*, not a summons — and it was the decisive act of the night. |
| They're in a fight with a cornered wizard | They're watching a man discover he mispriced his employer for eleven years |
| A'lai is fighting to win | He stopped fighting to win the moment the stone left his hand |
| They hold a doomsday trigger | ⭐ **They hold a paperweight.** See "The fake ace" below. |
| A'lai got betrayed by his own side | He got *filed* by his own side. It wasn't personal. That's the horror. |

> **Nobody at that table knows they were one gesture from losing the arc.** If
> A'lai had crushed that stone with the key stashed anywhere unattended, it
> teleports to a man already holding key #1, and the wards come down before
> anyone rolls initiative again.

## Per-character — what they did vs. what they think they did

### ⭐⭐ Daz — the decisive player, twice, invisibly

**What he thinks he did:** spent an action stopping a Manshoon summons, then ran
a misdirection to protect the stone.

**What he actually did:**

1. **Took away the only instrument that could have found key #2.** A'lai walks
   out of that exchange with *zero* information — not partial, zero — which is
   why he later has to guess, and guesses wrong.
2. **Painted a target on himself and got away with it.** The show of fleeing
   worked so well that A'lai names him to the assassin with total confidence.
   And then the assassin **ignores him anyway**, because a lone wizard running
   *away* from the objective isn't the job. The misdirection worked twice, in
   opposite directions, and nobody noticed either time.

> ### ⚠️ The cost, and it is his specifically
> A'lai's audition included *"I know the name of the person in Menzoberranzan
> who has been paying to keep your drow breathing."* **He dies before delivering
> it.**
>
> ⭐ **Daz's best play of the night destroys the answer to Daz's own question.**
> He saved the party's key and murdered his own mystery, and he will never be
> able to prove the trade happened. Do not soften this and do not explain it to
> him. *(Failsafes per the arc file: Yvenne's fourth tick, Vizeran's Stage 4 —
> expensive, not fatal.)*

Mike plays Daz as the running audit. **This is a line in the ledger he can't
close, entered by his own hand.**

### Grygum — holding everything, seen by no one

Holds **real key #2 and the sapphire**. Concentrating on *Tasha's Caustic Brew*;
killed Thug 1 outright; Thug 2 is dissolving on the clock.

**A'lai has no idea he exists as a threat.** The handoff landed while A'lai was
counterspelled and grappled, and the whole back half of the night runs on that
blind spot.

⭐ **He is also the one most likely to catch the tells.** WIS 20, Insight +8,
passive 18, and a voice file built on reading people before places. Two things
are sitting in front of him for free:

- **A'lai never once tried to save his own men.** Grygum notices exactly this
  kind of thing.
- **A man with ~108 damage a round who is not killing anyone.** He put Zalthir
  down while rolling two natural 1s and hasn't pressed since.

### Thorin — the biggest gun, still unfired at the man who matters

Engaged with the thugs. **Has not landed a single hit on A'lai all fight.**

The old staging had him grabbing A'lai for the capture — *"the capture must read
as defeat."* ⛔ **That scene no longer happens.** A'lai dies before anyone takes
him.

⭐ **Worth playing for:** Thorin's whole thing is radical transparency and
naming what's true. He is the one who will say the quiet part out loud after the
assassin leaves — that they chased a murderer for an entire arc and watched
somebody else close the file, mid-sentence, without asking his name.

### Zalthir — down, and his player wasn't there

Unconscious; Glabbagool positioned to stabilise. Did **22** to A'lai before going
down — the single largest contribution to the fight, and he isn't awake for the
payoff.

> ⚠️ **Gabe was absent and you ran him.** Consider whether Zalthir comes up
> before the assassin arrives. If he stays down through the climax, the
> character misses the arc's biggest beat *and* the player missed the session —
> a double erasure worth avoiding if there's a clean way to have Glabbagool get
> him upright.

### Tadric — still in the room

Flew them up the outer face under crossbow fire. The party said yes, so the tab
is theirs; **no plot armour, death genuinely available** per the standing
ruling. There is now an assassin in the room with him.

⭐ He is the boy whose survival cost Manshoon the entire night. Nobody in the
room has said that out loud yet.

## ⭐ The fake ace — the sapphire in Grygum's pocket

The end-of-session board says the party holds *"a sapphire that calls Manshoon
whenever they decide."* **They believe that. It is false.**

What it actually is:

| Property | Real? |
|---|---|
| **The third pillar of the Threefold Proof** | ✅ **Fully real.** The star-cut stone missing from Janussi's safe, taken from A'lai's hand before four deputised Watchers. Kalan's own inventory line. |
| A 1,000 gp gem | ✅ |
| `instant summons` component keyed to the High Tower key | ✅ |
| **A trigger that summons Manshoon** | ⛔ **A'lai made that up.** |

> ### ⭐ The payoff, whenever they choose to play it
> They spend a session or more treating this as their ace — a summons they can
> fire at a time and place of their choosing, into a prepared ambush. Then they
> crush it.
>
> Key #2 is **carried**, by Grygum. The recall fails. The stone helpfully informs
> them that **Grygum has the key.**
>
> Nothing arrives. Nothing happens. It is the most anticlimactic possible
> outcome for the thing they've been hoarding — and it is a lesson about taking
> A'lai at his word, delivered a full session after he said it. **Let them
> choose the moment. Don't warn them.**

## The tells — what can be noticed, free or cheap

Ordered by how much they give away. **Let players find these; don't narrate the
conclusion.**

1. ⭐ **The unused `dimension door` scroll on the body.** Free, no roll. A
   cornered wizard with a working exit in his pocket who never took it. This is
   the single key that unlocks everything else about him.
2. **"He couldn't wait."** The assassin's only line. It implies a schedule that
   existed independently of tonight — Manshoon was always coming.
3. **A'lai spent an attack on his own thug.** Insight against a man more fluent
   when lying, but Grygum has the numbers.
4. **The assassin walked past Daz.** If Daz thinks about why, he learns the
   professionals weren't hunting *him* — which means A'lai's accusation didn't
   even register as intelligence. He was that unimportant to them.
5. **A'lai died mid-sentence, arguing.** He wasn't executed as a traitor. He was
   interrupted as an inconvenience.

## Open decisions

1. **Do they realise the sapphire was a bluff?** There is no clean in-fiction way
   to learn it tonight — A'lai is the only one who knew and he's dead. Leaving
   them wrong is the better story; just be sure *you* track it so you don't
   accidentally validate the false belief later.
2. **Does anyone try to save A'lai?** Grygum has range and theology; Thorin has
   the grab. Whether anyone spends a reaction on Janussi's murderer, seconds
   after he pointed a knife at Daz, is a real character question. **Don't
   prompt it.**
3. **Who tells Sylvira, and when?** It is overdue, and if she hears it from
   Tadric or from a body carried past her door, her first line to the party
   changes permanently.
4. **Does the table connect Daz's win to Daz's loss?** That the theft saved the
   key and killed the answer about his patron. If nobody says it, **leave it
   unsaid** — it's better as something Mike works out on his own three sessions
   from now.
