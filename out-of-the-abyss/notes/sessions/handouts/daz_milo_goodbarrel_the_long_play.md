# HANDOUT — *Small Company*, Chapter Nine: "The Long Play"

Daz's own copy. Third printing, cheap rag-stock, bought on the surface
before Velkynvelve and carried through everything since — swollen along
the spine from the Darklake crossing, the last forty pages fused solid
and unreadable. Chapter Nine still opens because Daz has opened it many
times.

The marginalia in the margins is Daz's. It is **years old.** He wrote it
when this chapter was a boring one.

> **GM note:** hand this to Mike physically, at the moment the History
> check lands — or, better, at the top of next session, before combat
> resumes, so he reads it in silence with the table waiting.
>
> The horror is **structural, not descriptive.** Milo Goodbarrel is the
> optimist. He is the halfling who fought the Cult of the Dragon and
> came out of it convinced that doing the right thing tends to work.
> `daz_voice.md`: *"Milo is everything Daz is not."* This is the one
> chapter where the optimist stops. Nothing in it is gory. The fear is
> that **Milo is afraid**, and Milo is never afraid.
>
> Do not read this aloud in a spooky voice. Read it — if you read it at
> all — in Milo's warm, chatty, slightly rambling register, and let the
> warmth curdle on its own.
>
> **Tone for read-aloud:** a kind man telling you something over dinner
> that he has decided you need to hear, and being cheerful about it for
> exactly as long as he can manage.

---

```
                        SMALL COMPANY
        An Account of the Years Against the Cult
                     of the Dragon

           Milo Goodbarrel of Amphail
              Order of the Gauntlet
                   Third Printing


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CHAPTER NINE — THE LONG PLAY
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  I have put off this chapter for two years and my
  publisher has stopped being polite about it.

  So. The Moonsea.

  Everyone wants the dragons. Everyone wants the
  Well, and the mask, and Severin, and the part
  where the sky opened. I will get to all of it. I
  have written about the sky opening four times and
  every time it comes out sounding like a song, and
  every time I let it, because it was a bit like a
  song and I am not going to apologise for that.

  This chapter is not about dragons. This chapter is
  about a man in a good coat who never raised his
  voice at us once.

  We were in Zhentil Keep for nine days. We were
  there because the Cult was buying from the Black
  Network — mules, roads, silence — and we thought,
  in our great cleverness, that if we cut the supply
  we would slow the Cult down.

  We did slow the Cult down. I want that on the
  page. It worked.

  It also introduced us.
                                                    ╱
        [in Daz's hand, small, in the margin:]     ╱
                                                  ╱
             sentimental. skip.                  ╱


  His name is Manshoon.

  I have thought a long while about whether to write
  it. My friends and I made an agreement, in a room
  in Berdusk, that we would not write it or say it,
  and we kept that agreement for eleven years, and I
  am breaking it here, on purpose, and I have told
  them I am breaking it.

  I am breaking it because the agreement was
  cowardice dressed as caution, and because a name
  that nobody writes down is a name that gets to
  keep working.

  Here is what I know, plainly, and then I will
  stop.

  He founded the Black Network. Not "was involved
  in the founding of." Founded. It is his. Every
  caravan charter on the Moonsea, every road-toll
  on the Dales frontier, every quiet arrangement
  by which a village decides it is easier to pay
  than to ask — his. People think of the Zhentarim
  as a company. It is not a company. It is one
  man's estate, and it has been collecting rent
  for longer than anyone alive can account for.

  He has died. This is the part I need you to hold.

  He has died more than once, and I do not mean
  *he was reported dead* and I do not mean *he
  nearly died.* I mean buried. I mean, on one
  occasion, by us.

  We were careful. We were, I think, the most
  careful we have ever been about anything. Nadia
  checked. Nadia checked twice, and Nadia was the
  sort of person who checked. It counted for
  nothing at all. There is a kind of magic — I
  have had it explained to me three times by
  people cleverer than I am and I still do not
  properly understand it — where the man is not
  in the body. The body is a coat. He keeps
  coats. Nobody knows how many. He may not know
  how many.

  So when I say we killed him, understand that I
  am not describing a victory. I am describing an
  administrative delay.
                                                    ╱
        [in Daz's hand, same old ink:]             ╱
                                                  ╱
             halfling exaggeration. he is         ╱
             building to a moral.                ╱


  Now the part I actually sat down to write.

  He never came after us.

  I want to be very clear, because I know how this
  reads. There was no hunt. No assassins in the
  night, no burned inn, none of the things you are
  bracing for. In eleven years he has not once
  raised a hand toward me and I do not expect he
  ever will.

  What happened instead was smaller than that, and
  I have never got warm again since I understood
  it.

  Our banker changed. Our banker did not tell us
  our banker had changed.

  The Order's factor in Elturel retired early, very
  comfortably, and was replaced by a pleasant man
  whose paperwork was flawless.

  The magistrate who was going to hear our petition
  took an appointment in the Vast instead. Better
  post. Everyone agreed it was better.

  Kessa's brother was offered work. Good work.
  Honest work, so far as I can tell, and he does it
  still, and he is happy, and he has never once done
  anything to us, and I have never been able to look
  at him the same way.

  He does not chase you. He buys the chair you are
  sitting in, and the room it is in, and the road
  you would take out, and then he waits for you to
  need something. And you will need something,
  because everyone needs something eventually, and
  when you do, the only person selling will be
  someone whose wages you cannot trace.

  That is what I called, at the time, the long play,
  and my friends laughed at me for the phrase, and I
  have kept it because nothing better has come
  along.

  Nadia died six years after the Well. It was not
  him. I have looked. I have looked in the way a man
  looks when he badly wants an answer and would
  honestly prefer the ugly one, because the ugly one
  at least is an answer. It was not him. There is
  nothing there. I have found nothing, and I will go
  on finding nothing for the rest of my life, and
  that — that exactly — is the thing he does to you.

  He does not need to have done it. He only needs
  you to be unable to prove he didn't.

  I said I would stop, so I will stop.

  Only this. If you are reading this book because
  you are young and you are going out to do
  something brave, and you were hoping for the
  chapter where I tell you how we won —

  you have already read it. It was the sky opening.
  It was lovely. Go back and read it again.

  This chapter is the other one. This is the one
  where I tell you that we won, completely and
  properly and on the day, and that the man in the
  good coat was not there, because he had never
  been in the room, because he is never in the
  room, and that eleven years later I still open
  my correspondence with a knife held the wrong
  way round.

  Be brave anyway. Bravery is still the correct
  answer. I simply want you to know what it costs
  to be brave at a man who has decided not to
  attend.

                                — M.G., Amphail
```

---

> **What this reveals to Daz (player-side):**
>
> - **You cannot solve this by killing him.** *"I am not describing a victory. I am describing an administrative delay."* Milo's crew — the crew that beat Tiamat — killed Manshoon, carefully, and it changed nothing. The party's entire method does not apply.
> - **The Zhentarim is not an organisation, it is one man's property.** *"It is not a company. It is one man's estate, and it has been collecting rent for longer than anyone alive can account for."* A'lai's eleven years of theft was rent collection.
> - **He does not retaliate. He acquires.** *"He does not chase you. He buys the chair you are sitting in."* Daz has spent this whole campaign auditing who owes whom. This is the one player who cannot be audited, because the transactions never touch you directly.
> - **The small detail that should land hardest — Daz's own marginalia.** *"sentimental. skip."* He read this chapter years ago and dismissed it, twice, in his own handwriting. He has been carrying the answer since before Velkynvelve. That is not a fact about Manshoon; it is a fact about Daz, and it is the one he will actually feel.

> **What this reveals (GM-side):**
>
> - **The patron resonance is deliberate and deliberately unproven.** *"Our banker changed. Our banker did not tell us our banker had changed."* / *"He only needs you to be unable to prove he didn't."* sits directly on top of Daz's central mystery — someone in Menzoberranzan is paying to keep him alive and he cannot name them. The handout **does not claim** Manshoon is Daz's patron. It gives Mike the shape and lets him arrive there himself. You keep both doors: confirm it later, or let Daz be wrong in a way that costs him.
> - **It pre-loads the correct emotional read of this session's win.** Your prep names the beat as *"the turn from we caught him to he was the small one."* Milo does that turn for you, in a book, in advance, so you don't have to say it in GM voice.
> - **It installs "the long play" as a phrase the table now shares** — Daz already uses it (`daz_voice.md`: *"He called it 'the long play.' I always assumed he was rationalizing."*). After this chapter, when Daz says it, it means something different, and Mike will know it does.
> - **If Daz ever tries to negotiate with Manshoon:** Milo has pre-refuted it. The man does not take meetings; he takes positions. Any offer Daz makes will be accepted, honoured, and irrelevant.

> **GM use:**
>
> - **Introduce it** at the top of next session, before initiative resumes — Daz has been holding a sapphire and a name for a whole week of in-world minutes. Let Mike read it cold while the table waits.
> - **Do not introduce it** mid-combat. It is four minutes of silent reading and it will kill the fight's momentum stone dead.
> - **Silence cue:** after he finishes, say nothing. The marginalia does the work. If Mike goes back to check when he wrote *"sentimental. skip."* — let him, and do not answer. He'll settle on "before Velkynvelve," which is correct and worse.
> - **The late callback — the line Milo would say if the party ever met him:**
>   > *"Oh — you've read Nine. I'm sorry. I did think about leaving it out. I decided that a name nobody writes down is a name that gets to keep working, and then I spent about a year being frightened of my own book. Have you eaten?"*
