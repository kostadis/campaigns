# HANDOUT — Grygum's First Ledger: The East Road

The oldest thing Grygum owns. A child's ledger — twelve leaves of
scraped hide sewn with sinew, the kind an orc camp makes for tallying
stores, kept by a boy who had not yet been taught letters properly and
was learning them by copying shapes he did not understand.

He has carried it through Velkynvelve, the Darklake, Gracklstugh,
Blingdenstone and Candlekeep. He has never mentioned it. It is the
reason he takes notes.

The first hand is his, aged about nine. The second hand — the neat one,
in Candlekeep ink — is also his, from three tendays ago.

> **GM note:** hand this to Ben physically. It is short; he can read it
> in under two minutes.
>
> This handout does two jobs at once. The first is Manshoon: on the
> frontier the man is not a wizard, not a villain, not a *person* — he
> is **weather**, and an orc camp reorganised its year around him for
> generations without anyone ever saying the word aloud. That is a
> different horror from the one Daz gets, and it is the worse one for a
> cleric of Bahamut, whose entire theology is *choice over
> determinism.* The east road was never a choice. Nobody in that camp
> ever made one.
>
> The second job is a gift: this is the **origin of Grygum's
> note-taking**, the trait Ben has been playing for sixty chapters. His
> mother wrote a name in ash and swept it flat, and Grygum decided
> nothing would be swept flat again. *"I took notes. It's what I do."*
> starts here.
>
> **In-fiction spelling note (GM only):** the child's miscopy **MAN
> SHUNE** is texture, not a transcription variant. Do **not** let it
> into `entity_registry.yaml` as an alias — it is a nine-year-old
> copying ash-strokes he cannot read, which is the entire point of the
> beat.
>
> **Tone for read-aloud:** flat, short, factual, a child recording
> things because recording them is the only available action. No fear
> in the voice. The fear is in the arithmetic.

---

```
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [First hand — child's, uneven, pressed hard
   enough to score the hide]
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Stores. 4 baskets dried root. 1 basket bad.
  Told Mother. She said do not write the bad one.
  I wrote it.


  Chief Ublakk will not cross the east road.
  Asked why. Was struck for asking.

  Asked Mother. She said the road is leased.
  Asked from whom. She would not say it aloud.
  She wrote it in the ash with one finger and
  then swept the ash flat.

  I copied it before she swept it.

       M A N   S H U N E

  I do not know if it is one word or two.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  We move off the east road 11 times in a year.
  Not 12. I have counted 3 years now. Always 11.

  On those nights we camp at the dry ground.
  There is no water at the dry ground.
  Ublakk says it is tradition.

  Asked Mother if it was tradition.
  She said yes.
  She said it the way she says things that are
  not true.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  On the 11th night I stayed awake.
  Something went along the east road. Wagons.
  I counted 9. There were men walking beside
  them who did not carry torches and did not
  need to.

  Nobody in our camp looked at the road.
  Not one of them. I watched our own people not
  look at a thing for the whole night. That was
  harder to watch than the wagons.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  TODAY I DID THE EXPERIMENT.

  Ublakk would not give me the second knife.
  He struck me the first time I asked.

  I said the ash-name to him. I said it plain.
  I said, MAN SHUNE would want me to have a
  knife. I do not know if that is true. I do
  not know who he is. I said it to see what
  the name does.

  Ublakk gave me the knife.
  He did not strike me.
  He did not speak to me again for a year.

  A name did that. Nobody had to come.
  Nobody had to be told. There is no one
  standing behind me. The name works on its
  own, in an empty room, on a chief who could
  break my arm.

  I want to write down that this frightened me
  because I do not think I will remember later
  that it frightened me. I think I will
  remember that it worked.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Asked the old woman how long we have leased
  the east road.

  She said always.
  I asked how long always is.
  She said her mother moved off the road too.
  I asked about her mother's mother.
  She got angry. She said stop counting.

  So it is at least 4 old women long.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A caravan man read my ledger over my shoulder
  at the trade meet and laughed at the spelling
  and told me the right way and I wrote it down
  the right way and then he stopped laughing.

  He looked at the page for a while.
  Then he asked me to scratch it out.
  I said no.
  He gave me a copper not to say where I had
  learned it. I took the copper. I did not
  say. I am writing it here instead, because
  a thing you were paid to not-say is a thing
  worth keeping.

  He was not frightened of me. He was
  frightened of having said it out loud
  himself.


  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [Second hand — Candlekeep ink, neat, recent]
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Checked at last. Twenty-nine years late.

  The Black Network's frontier road-leases are a
  matter of public record here — they are filed as
  ordinary commercial instruments and nobody in
  this building has ever thought them interesting.
  Eleven collections a year is standard. It is not
  eleven for any reason. It is eleven because the
  Moonsea calendar runs a short month and nobody
  ever renegotiated it.

  My tribe went thirsty eleven nights a year, for
  four old women, because of a clerical convenience
  on a coast none of us had seen.

  The signature on the standing instrument has not
  changed hands. Not once. There is no succession
  recorded because there has been no succession.

  I want to set down clearly what troubles me,
  because I do not trust myself to be honest about
  it later.

  It is not that he is powerful. Ilvara was
  powerful. Themberchaud was powerful. Powerful
  things can be argued with, bought, or dropped a
  hole onto.

  It is that nobody in my camp ever made a choice.
  Not one, in four generations. They did not submit
  — submitting is a choice. They arranged their
  year around him the way you arrange it around
  winter. Bahamut's whole claim is that a creature
  is what it chooses. My people were never offered
  the ground to stand on and do that. He took it
  before they were born and he did not even have to
  visit.

  And there is the other thing, which I will write
  once and not discuss.

  He has been signing for longer than a man signs.

  I do not know what that means yet. I know what it
  cannot mean.

  She swept the ash flat. She was trying to protect
  me and I have never once been angry at her for
  it.

  I have not let anything be swept flat since.
```

---

> **What this reveals to Grygum (player-side):**
>
> - **He has known the name his whole life.** Not as history, not as lore — as the thing his mother wouldn't say and his chief wouldn't cross. The History roll of 22 was not scholarship. It was recognition.
> - **On the frontier, Manshoon is not a person. He is weather.** Four generations of an orc tribe reorganised their year around him without anyone ever needing to be threatened, visited, or told. *"Nobody in our camp looked at the road… I watched our own people not look at a thing for the whole night."*
> - **The name is a weapon that works with nobody holding it.** *"A name did that. Nobody had to come… The name works on its own, in an empty room, on a chief who could break my arm."* This is the origin of a trick Grygum has used his entire adult life, and he has never before known where he learned it.
> - **The theological problem, stated plainly and only once.** *"Bahamut's whole claim is that a creature is what it chooses. My people were never offered the ground to stand on and do that."* Grygum's faith is built on choice over determinism. Manshoon is the man who removes the choice before anyone is born to make it.
> - **The small detail that lands hardest.** *"He has been signing for longer than a man signs. I do not know what that means yet. I know what it cannot mean."* Grygum has the same fact Daz has — that Manshoon has died more than once — and he arrives at it from paperwork, and he refuses to finish the sentence.

> **What this reveals (GM-side):**
>
> - **It makes the note-taking load-bearing.** Ben has played the documentarian for sixty chapters as a bit. This makes it a wound: *"She swept the ash flat… I have not let anything be swept flat since."* Every time Grygum takes notes from here on, it means something it did not mean before, and it costs you nothing to have arranged that in retrospect.
> - **It hands you a lever on Grygum specifically.** He has *used* the name-as-weapon trick — on Themberchaud, on drow guards, on the Grygumite school. He learned it from Manshoon's shadow at age nine. If you ever want Grygum to be genuinely shaken, have someone use it on *him*.
> - **It sets up a frontier the party can go back to.** The tribe is still moving off that road eleven nights a year, right now, while Grygum is at Candlekeep. That is a rescue nobody has asked for yet.
> - **It does not name Manshoon's goal, only his tenure.** Keep the Vault, the cryptogram, and the one book he actually wants entirely out of this document — Grygum learns *what kind of thing* Manshoon is, not what he's doing here. The two handouts together give the party *he cannot be killed* (Daz) and *he already owns the ground you stand on* (Grygum), and neither gives them the plot.
> - **If Grygum dies before this ever surfaces:** the ledger is on his body, and Ben's replacement character — or Daz, who reads everything — finds a nine-year-old's handwriting doing the arithmetic that four generations of adults refused to do.

> **GM use:**
>
> - **Introduce it** at the same moment as Daz's chapter, top of next session. They are designed to be read simultaneously and compared — Ben and Mike should be allowed to trade them across the table.
> - **Do not introduce it** as a discovery. He has had it the whole time. The revelation is that he never went back and read his own ledger, which is the one failure of documentation in his life.
> - **Silence cue:** the last line is *"I have not let anything be swept flat since."* Do not follow it with anything. If Ben wants to say something, it will be better than whatever you had.
> - **The late callback — the line Grygum would give if a PC ever asks him about it:**
>   > *"My mother wrote a name in the ash and swept it flat before I could finish looking at it. She was protecting me. It was the correct decision and it did not work. Well — that's what I have instead of a childhood. Notes."*
