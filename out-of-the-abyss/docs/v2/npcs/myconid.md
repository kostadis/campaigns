### Myconid

**Current status:** Alive  
**Current location:** Unknown (last observed in proximity to Zalthir and the party during Chapter 27)  
**Allegiance/faction:** Likely part of a myconid circle; loyal to Rump-a-dump and the “Lady” (spore-associated entity)  
**Current possessions / notable items, spells, feats, wounds:** None specified  
**Current assignment / role and key relationships:**  
- Advocates for Rump-a-dump, a sprout from their circle who is unhappy and resistant to zombie conversion.  
- Believes Rump-a-dump is connected to ape-like creatures and spore phenomena.  
- Seeks to reunite with Rump-a-dump and prevent violence against myconids.  
- Now references “the Lady” as a spiritual or cosmic force tied to spore rhythms and a hidden, mind-altering underground realm — suggesting a shift toward mystic or ecstatic myconid worship.  
- Last known interaction: Addressing Zalthir and the party with psychedelic, trance-like language about “the Lady” and the “underground rave” of spore vibrations (Ch. 27).  

**Defining recent actions:**  
- Repeatedly demanded Zalthir rescue Rump-a-dump from potential harm, emphasizing nonviolence and kinship among myconids (Ch. 25).  
- Shifted tone in Chapter 27 from frustrated advocate to ecstatic mystic, introducing the concept of “the Lady” and a transcendent spore realm — implying spiritual evolution or exposure to deeper myconid lore.  

**Revealed motivations or secrets:**  
- Myconids do not kill their own; they convert dead *enemies* into zombies via a ritual process.  
- Rump-a-dump opposes this conversion process and may be a dissident or sacred figure within myconid society.  
- The “Lady” is a central spiritual entity tied to spore resonance — possibly a collective consciousness, deity, or ancient myconid hive-mind manifestation.  
- The “hidden trip” described is not a physical location but a psychic or spiritual experience induced by spores — suggesting the Myconid may now be a conduit or priest of this phenomenon.

## Uncertainty
- The identity of “the Lady” is unclear — is she a myconid elder, a fungal deity, a collective consciousness, or a hallucination?  
- Whether Rump-a-dump is still alive or where they are located is unknown — last known only through the Myconid’s claims.  
- The Myconid’s shift in speech (Ch. 27) may indicate possession, spore-induced psychosis, enlightenment, or a new role — the cause is unconfirmed.  
- It is unclear whether the Myconid is still traveling with Zalthir or has departed after Chapter 27.  
- The ape-like creatures’ exact nature and relationship to Rump-a-dump remain unexplained.
