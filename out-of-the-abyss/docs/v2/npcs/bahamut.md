### Bahamut

- **Current status**: Alive (as a deity; no evidence of death or dormancy).  
- **Current location**: Not physically present; worshipped across realms, with active clerics in the field (Grygum, Tarvis Ulain, unnamed priest).  
- **Allegiance/faction**: Champion of life, free will, and moral choice; opposed to Zuggtmoy’s corruption and chaos.  
- **Current possessions / notable items, spells, feats, wounds**: None (as a deity, no physical possessions or wounds are recorded).  
- **Current assignment / role**: Divine patron of clerics and champions who uphold free will and ethical choice; source of divine magic (e.g., radiant damage of Guiding Bolt) and moral teachings.  
- **Key relationships**:  
  - Served by Grygum (cleric), Tarvis Ulain (former servant), and an unnamed priest.  
  - Opposed to Zuggtmoy.  
  - Previously implicated in the chess game between Protanther and Stroud (whether actively intervened is uncertain).  
- **Defining recent actions**:  
  - Granted radiant power to Grygum’s Guiding Bolt (ch51).  
  - Remains a moral compass for clerics, whose teachings emphasize agency: “The true wyrm's form is not etched by fate's first breath... It is the choices that twist the spirit, or set it soaring.” (ch23).  
  - Revered as the Platinum Dragon who fosters life against corruption (ch27).  
- **Revealed motivations or secrets**:  
  - Values agency over passive obedience; disfavors spectatorship in battle (ch51).  
  - Teaches reciprocal benevolence: “When you do a good turn for someone, they may do a good turn for you.” (ch14).  
  - Recognizes the black dragon as a holy creature that breathes acid (ch31) — suggesting a non-binary view of alignment within divine creation.  
  - May have subtly influenced the outcome of Protanther and Stroud’s game — but this is belief, not confirmed fact (ch13).

## Uncertainty
- Whether Bahamut actively moved Stroud’s hand in the chess game — stated as a belief, not confirmed (ch13).  
- Whether Bahamut was watching during the battle referenced in ch49 — explicitly uncertain (“may or may not”).  
- Contradiction between Grygum calling Bahamut “boring” (ch24) and his deep, active devotion — is this personal opinion or doctrinal tension?  
- The claim that Bahamut teaches the black dragon is a holy creature (ch31) conflicts with traditional D&D alignment lore — is this a unique doctrinal deviation by Grygum’s temple, or a core tenet of Bahamut’s current teachings?  
- Whether Tarvis Ulain still actively worships Bahamut — only his past service is confirmed (ch55).  
- Whether Bahamut’s magic failing against the stone giant (ch15) reflects a limitation of the god’s power, or merely the cleric’s invocation.
