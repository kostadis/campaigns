## Alkrist

Alkrist does not appear in person in this chunk — he is being held in the Candlekeep prisons — but he is central to the events. Under Kalan Strongbranch's Zone of Truth, Alkrist had confessed out loud, in the room, to killing Janussi; this was the confession the party had been digging for. He is revealed to be Bookwyrm's nephew, and Bookwyrm has been covering for him. The evidence against him consists of his confession, the extracted heart, the cleaver, the stolen poison, and a book he possessed. Alkrist also named Bookwyrm as the mastermind. During the party's discussion, Kalan noted Alkrist is "finished" — the confession, cleaver, and blood damn him. His safe was opened during this chapter: it held a potion of flying, a potion of superior healing, a purse of gold, and a joke red herring coin (a gold coin stamped with a herring, colored red), but nothing further implicating him. He will face trial "after the naming, by the new head of the Avowed."

## Kalan Strongbranch

Kalan Strongbranch, recently fired as warden/investigator, is the man the party decides to hand the solved case to so he can "save face." He kept his Zone of Truth active in the room throughout the confession. Kalan explained the limits of the case using his own academic paper: a single spell-compelled confession is the "weakest channel"; his standard requires three different kinds of truth, and by that standard Bookwyrm walks free — the very protocol he authored now shelters their target. He revealed his real motive when he refused to force a Zone of Truth on Bookwyrm without her permission — Grygum read this as cowardice dressed as principle, a man measuring who might soon sign his paycheck. Kalan proposed the patient play: wait for tomorrow's naming ceremony, predict the vote will not name Bookwyrm (nor A'lai), and expect Fembris Lancer to be elected and then remove Bookwyrm herself. He declared "I will not sneak this time. We will walk in and boldly demand" a Zone of Truth — a change from his prior successful method of casting it from outside the room. He warmed enthusiastically to Thorin's plan to obtain an incriminating book after the party collects its reward, promising "I will come up with such a book before you leave." Bookwyrm reinstated him to his position at chapter's end ("it was unwise of me not to keep you in charge"), which he accepted while insisting it would not change his report. He guarded Alkrist's safe overnight and escorted the party to Deneir's Sanctum, full of himself about his vindicated methodology.

## Bookwyrm (First Reader)

Bookwyrm, current acting head of Candlekeep, is the party's true target — revealed as Alkrist's aunt who covered up his murder of Janussi by extracting the victim's heart and placing it in the chalice of a statue. She summoned the party to her office, where tea was already poured. Both Daz and Thorin refused to drink; Zalthir covertly fed his tea to Glabbagool rather than refuse openly. She drank hers pointedly while watching them. She dismissed their confession as compelled by "a borrowed spell," citing Kalan's own paper that a single working proves nothing. When told Alkrist confessed, she collapsed into theatrical grief ("Alkrist. I could never have imagined it") — but Zalthir and Daz both read that underneath she was relieved, "the one who has already decided they've won," her hands too relaxed, sincerely glad to have the party boxed up and out of the way. She praised Kalan's methodology, reinstated him, and had the safe opened and declared of no interest. She insisted the party be escorted to Deneir's Sanctum "to protect you" from "other criminal elements" — Zalthir read genuine worry mixed with genuine delight at containing them. Zalthir's Detect Object search for a sapphire on her person found nothing; she felt the spell brush past and ended the spectacle. The party's plan is to convict her not of murder (she only concealed a crime) but of removal/embarrassment — losing her position being "far more damning than any crime." Kalan predicts the naming vote will not name her.

## A'lai Aivenmore

A'lai does not appear in this chunk but is discussed as one of the three "doors" out of the situation — the party considered going quietly to A'lai to let him know they knew things. A thread of the conspiracy runs from the murder back to A'lai. Kalan predicts that at tomorrow's naming ceremony, "A'lai won't be named either," alongside Bookwyrm.

## Janussi

Janussi does not appear (he is the murder victim) but is discussed throughout. Alkrist confessed to killing him; the confession the party extracted concerned Janussi's death. His heart was extracted and hidden in a statue's chalice as part of the cover-up. Notably, Janussi was the person who declined Kalan's research proposal — and Kalan pointed out, without softening, that "the person who declined it is dead," which now creates the opportunity for Kalan's methodology to be vindicated. Bookwyrm melodramatically called him "such a great man… beloved by all." Grygum noted the dead keep changing whose case they strengthen.

## Fembris Lancer

Fembris Lancer stood silently off to one side while the party argued, which Thorin respected, then announced Bookwyrm wished to speak with them. Kalan predicts Fembris will "probably get elected the new avowed" and that her first act will be to remove Bookwyrm. Later, Fembris served out a punishment by keeping the party company (under guard) in Deneir's Sanctum — "his penance was us." When food arrived that no one trusted, Fembris ate eagerly and without suspicion; the party used him as an unwitting food-taster (Daz cryptically rated him "118," Daral called it "a new best practice"). Fembris did not die. During the helmed horror ambush, Fembris stood in shock. Zalthir later worked out that Fembris was present and watching when Grygum handed Daz the key the assassins were tracking.

## Daral Yashenti

Daral, the Candlekeep scholar afflicted with an ailment, spent this chunk pleading to be cured. He repeatedly begged for "the curing of me" and asked Dawnbringer directly ("Mr. Don— Mrs. Dawnbringer?"). He had stopped bragging and begun complimenting the party — for Daral, a kind of surrender. The party debated his fate: Thorin judged he had nothing to do with the murder and nothing left to tell, and argued to simply let him go rather than extort him. Grygum was quietly ready to cast Lesser Restoration but held back to let the room reach mercy on its own. Ultimately Dawnbringer cured him — the blade poured light into him and took the sickness. Ecstatic and healed, Daral immediately launched into a five-minute rhapsody about launching a new field of active research into sentient magical swords, their thematics and semiotics, using Dawnbringer as his subject — which Dawnbringer visibly drank in. During the helmed horror ambush, Daral came apart, insisting "it wasn't possible, these creatures can't be here." He found the door still locked and could not leave.

## Dawnbringer

Dawnbringer, the sentient sword carried by Thorin, spoke in his head throughout, insisting "I'm not leaving here without the therapy. That first session was actually very productive," and asking for more sessions with a sword-whisperer — the very thing Thorin has been hunting for her since the road south. She made a click "like a palm slapping stone" inside Thorin's skull. Her gender was briefly debated (Thorin: "I thought Dawnbringer was a she… I'm cool either way"; settled on "she"). She performed the cure of Daral Yashenti, lighting up and drawing the sickness out of him, then basked in Daral's lengthy praise of sentient swords — Grygum watched "the whole demeanor of the thing open up." In the helmed horror combat she was Thorin's weapon: her "sap" ability slowed a horror's movements after he struck it.

## Glabbagool

Glabbagool, the sentient ooze fused along Zalthir's forearm, was used covertly to dispose of Bookwyrm's tea — Zalthir tilted the cup and let the tea run into Glabbagool, who burned it away with a faint acid singe and, notably, stayed completely silent because Zalthir had asked him beforehand for "a moment of quiet reflection." Zalthir marked this restraint as "discipline, or the beginning of it." Glabbagool's arm covertly swiped the potion of flying from Alkrist's safe while the room was distracted — clean, unnoticed. Glabbagool was pleased: "I like the flying skill. That was a good skill." In the helmed horror fight, Glabbagool's acid trick ate through the horrors' plate armor while Zalthir grappled them.

## Brother Quellin

Brother Quellin does not appear and is only referenced in passing by Zalthir, who uncertainly attributed a saying — that the most dangerous person in a room is the one who has already decided they've won — to either the Garden Shadow or "possibly Brother Quellin," admitting he doesn't remember which.

## Bahamut

Bahamut does not appear and is referenced only by Grygum, who mused that "Bahamut may or may not have been reading over my shoulder" as he took notes on the party's decision to convict Bookwyrm of embarrassment rather than murder — "I wrote it down either way."

## Elminster

Elminster does not appear and is referenced only by Thorin as a figure of speech: opening Alkrist's safe, Thorin asked "Is it, like, an Elminster type of thing, where it's a big letdown?"
