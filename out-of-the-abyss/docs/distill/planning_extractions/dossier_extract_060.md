## Daral Yashenti

Daral is the avowed who gave Janussi the poisoned Deadwinter gift book and now fears he is being framed for the resulting murder. He had glued himself to Daz and Thorin, having concluded they were the only people in Candlekeep who did not want him dead. He held the poison book and worked out that someone wanted him to take the fall. He was anxious that no one mention to Zalthir the disparaging things he had said about dragonborn while drunk — barroom talk he insisted was not meant seriously.

He revealed the timeline of the book: he dropped it under the evergreen tree next to the other gifts around 11am on Deadwinter's Eve, before rushing to the Keeper's Tower for his end-of-year meeting, and never personally handed it to anyone. It was picked up by Janussi at dinner, and at the time it was wrapped. When asked about a magical seal, he was baffled — "Why would I put a magical seal on the wrapping? It's a gift!" He repeatedly promised that if he survived the night he would institute best practices: warded wrappings on every gift.

Daral pointed the party to the Oak Tree Apothecary and the satyr druid Leuwin, and later, in the Southern Dining Hall, recognized the significance of the lead-lined Milil chalice (used for the Midsummer Wine), leading to the discovery of Janussi's heart. He named A'lai Aivenmore as the man with motive to both kill Janussi and frame Daral, since A'lai wanted the keep and saw Daral's love of poetry and fiction as a threat. Thorin read him as genuinely terrified, not conniving, and crossed him off the suspect list. Last seen accompanying Thorin to the kitchen to interview staff.

## Janussi

Janussi is the dead scholar and great reader at the center of the investigation — poisoned by the gift book Daral gave him, which contained Midnight Tears. At the Deadwinter supper he praised Alkrist's project as "the kind of knowledge that could benefit civilization" and reminded the room that deciding which branches of knowledge to further was their privileged responsibility. When asked for solidarity, Daral muttered under his breath, "Oh, you've been right behind one of us all night." Janussi took his gift unwrapped, thanked his secret donor, and excused himself, dropping the wrapping paper in a wicker basket by the door. The string tied around his gift had been cut and retied — evidence that the tampering happened under the tree, in the room. His heart (and a blood-stained meat cleaver) was found hidden in the lead-lined Milil chalice, the lead lining blinding far-seeing magic; Daral surmised the killer stole the heart so no one could resurrect Janussi and question him. His locket, worn around his neck, was taken, presumed to have held a key.

## Kalan Strongbranch

Strongbranch does not act in this chunk but his belongings and caution are central. A key that once hung around his neck came into Grygum's possession; Grygum handed it to Daz to make it his problem. The reason for Strongbranch's nerves became clear: the stolen sapphire carried a summoning working that could pull an item to you, but only an item not in anyone's possession — and a dead man possesses nothing, while an undead can. So the cleanest way to free a man's key from his possession was to make him a corpse. Strongbranch, having carried the key for years, handed it off rather than wear it — paranoid, but the caution of a man who understood the danger.

## Brother Vareth

Vareth found Grygum and, with "the look of a man with a paper to publish," took Grygum's answer to the Question of the Age (the triangle, three options built half out of Stroud's Gambit) and turned it into a fourth corner — a new theology — telling everyone. He triggered the entire Avowed community's desire to drop everything and debate it in the Grand Hall, effectively emptying the keep of watchful eyes despite an active murder investigation.

## Fembris Lancer

Fembris is the avowed minder assigned to ensure the four armed strangers didn't help themselves to the library's contents. He asked to leave in order to attend Grygum's debate in the Oval Theater — a disputation on Grygum's new theology. Daz did not stop him, silently noting that his departure handed the party an unsupervised hour.

## Bookwyrm (First Reader)

Bookwyrm is referenced but does not directly act. She was politically unable to afford missing Grygum's debate. She had told the avowed to take the Deadwinter leftovers to their dorms. Zalthir lists her among the eight suspects: eager to fire the old boss and seat a new one. Her chambers took half the top floor of the Readers' Tower — comfortable, neatly arranged, no extravagance; Zalthir searched them and found nothing, and Glabbagool detected no poison. A clean room.

## Leuwin

Leuwin is the elderly, absent-minded satyr druid who runs the Oak Tree Apothecary — a treehouse grown into the roots of an evergreen oak between the Sea Warden's Tower and the House of Mechanus, with a pet squirrel (Nibbles). He can identify poisons definitively. He gave Daz a clean timeline of visitors: at 2:30 the gnome Fheminor came for cold medicine (horehound tincture, licorice); at 3:00 A'lai arrived asking for fennel for indigestion, and Leuwin took him down through the rear exit to the greenhouse below; at 3:15 the trouble occurred. He was shocked and called it unacceptable when Daz pointed out a broken vial in his "Dangerous Toxins" shelf, and suggested Nibbles might know something. Crucially, he was in the greenhouse with A'lai while the theft happened upstairs, so he witnessed nothing.

## Nibbles

Nibbles is Leuwin's pet squirrel, who fetched implements one at a time and served as the key witness to the apothecary theft. Daz used a spell to loosen the squirrel's tongue and bribed it one acorn at a time. Nibbles reported seeing: the unfriendly gnome girl (Fheminor); a big, clumsy, dark-skinned elf (A'lai); and a huge lizard-skinned figure (a dragonborn) who made a big mess, made something go *bling*, moved things around, and left. Critically, Nibbles saw the person because Leuwin wasn't in the room — Leuwin was in the greenhouse helping the elf while the dragonborn broke the vial upstairs and took the toxin. Nibbles could not identify any distinguishing marks. The interview ended when its cheeks were stuffed full of nuts.

## A'lai Aivenmore

A'lai is a leading murder suspect — the drow who wanted the keep and was about to be dismissed, who saw Daral's love of poetry and fiction as a threat to his approach to the universe. Daral named him as the one man with motive to both kill Janussi and frame Daral, clearing the council of anyone able to prevent his ascension. At the apothecary he arrived at 3:00 asking for fennel for indigestion and was taken by Leuwin down to the greenhouse — which Daz deduced was a deliberate diversion, occupying the druid while a dragonborn accomplice broke into the "Dangerous Toxins" shelf upstairs and took the poison. A'lai kept his own hands clean. In Zalthir's search of his chambers (sparse, tasteful — fine glassware, good ceramics, a large mirror, a self-portrait), he found a dovecote built for five with only two message-doves remaining, the perches and troughs showing that three messages had recently been sent somewhere.

## Fheminor Scrivenbark

Fheminor is the gnome who had wanted the head job but otherwise left no mark, listed among Zalthir's eight suspects. At the apothecary she visited at 2:30 for cold medicine (horehound tincture and licorice); Nibbles remembered her as unpleasant, "always unpleasant," but she had come and gone before the theft. Teles scoffed at Alkrist's project as being as likely to succeed as "some Fheminor Scrivenbark program," a remark Irony didn't understand.

## Sylvira Savikas

Sylvira, a tiefling, stormed out of the Deadwinter supper before the gifts were handed out. Both Irony and Sprig independently reported seeing her searching by candlelight around 1am — she pointed at her throat, too hoarse to speak, and was helped to find a pot of honey. Thorin concluded this was likely someone wearing her face: she used a lantern, but a tiefling doesn't need light to see, and the honey was probably a cover story for having been discovered. Zalthir listed her as the obvious suspect everyone pointed at, and deliberately skipped her chambers first on the reasoning that a name that obvious is rarely the answer.

## Kazryn Nyantani

Kazryn ("Tani"), the chief cleric, took it on herself to hand out the Deadwinter gifts from under the evergreen tree after Sylvira stormed out, giving each to the matching great reader (Secret Santa style). Listed among Zalthir's eight suspects.

## Alkrist

Alkrist is a new great reader nobody could tell Zalthir much about. At the supper he was the only cheerful one, pitching a project — *Tales of War by the Generals That Waged Them*, first-hand strategy from warlords and commanders. Thorin initially dismissed him as not a suspect ("the cheerful one's not usually the killer"), but evidence mounted: Orrin saw him feeding a small scrap of paper to a candle flame and dropping it when junior avowed walked in. In Zalthir's search of his lavish chambers (expensive furniture and cloth, fashionable art, spellbook on the desk), he found sealed glass jars of dead rats with blistered, pustuled skin — someone studying poison — and, on the bedside table, a black leather copy of *1001 Tashalian Nights* bookmarked to a tale of a military commander poisoned by a gift of surrender: a magic horn whose mouthpiece was coated in Midnight Tears. A locked safe beside it could not be opened. Alkrist rose sharply as a suspect.

## Teles Ahvoste

Teles is a great reader who scoffed at Alkrist's supper project, comparing it to "some Fheminor Scrivenbark program." Orrin revealed that Teles had been making unwanted advances on the junior avowed Irony — the "too interested in the junior avowed" gossip both Irony and Orrin hinted at. Listed among the eight suspects as one who had wanted the top chair.

## Irony

Irony is a junior avowed who served at the Deadwinter supper and is compelled to always tell the truth (she hates the obvious joke about her name). Thorin valued her as a witness. She described the supper: it began at the 5PM bell with twelve shared dishes (beetroot soup, fried carp, smoked mackerel, fruit compotes, spiced wines and ales, honey, vodka, green tea) served by her, Orrin, and Sprig, all passed around communally. She reported everyone tense except cheerful Alkrist, and that she encountered Sylvira Savikas searching by candlelight around 1am and helped her find honey. She hinted at avowed being "too interested in the junior avowed." Zalthir later noted her tell: she can't lie, but she isn't compelled to answer — she steered the party to Sprig rather than speak of Teles's advances.

## Sprig Summerfoot

Sprig is a junior avowed and the voluntary poison taster who insisted on tasting all twelve supper dishes despite the chefs saying it wasn't necessary — initiative Daral praised as a best practice. Thorin reasoned that if Janussi were the only target, Sprig would have been unwitnessed collateral, so whoever spared the food (using the book instead) may have cared about protecting her. She confirmed the gifts under the tree were all books, wands, or magical artifacts, and independently corroborated the 1am Sylvira/honey encounter. Orrin's testimony reframed the "too interested" gossip as being about Irony, not Sprig.

## Orrin Glass

Orrin is the third waiter, deaf in one ear and described as a bit of an oddball but a man who notices everything. He revealed that Teles Ahvoste had been making passes at Irony (offering the information because Irony "threw me under the bus"). He reported seeing Alkrist feed a scrap of paper to a candle and drop it when junior avowed entered, and — most importantly — that the string tied around Janussi's Deadwinter gift had clearly been cut and retied, confirming the tampering happened under the tree with everyone present.

## Glabbagool

Glabbagool, the awakened ooze companion, was eager and cheerful throughout — declaring "we're going on a secret mission!" He repeatedly tried to help: surfacing an eye to read the bright key/gem in Daz's hand (unsuccessfully, sinking back "dolefully"), announcing "I have learned silence" and asking whether it was "good silence or too loud silence," and attempting to grow a nose to smell the poison. He partnered with Zalthir in searching the Readers' Tower, flowing where Zalthir couldn't and detecting the absence of poison in Bookwyrm's chambers, though he couldn't thin himself into A'lai's finger-width safe. Zalthir privately valued that Glabbagool — having survived Blingdenstone, the ooze crisis, and the bag — still reaches toward every problem.

## Milil

Milil appears as a statue in an alcove of the Southern Dining Hall — the god of bards, depicted with open hands holding a brass chalice like an offering. The chalice, used for the Midsummer Wine, is lead-lined (lead blinds far-seeing magic). Daral recognized this and Thorin retrieved the cup, finding Janussi's heart and a blood-stained meat cleaver hidden inside — the lead lining having concealed them from every scrying working in the keep.

## Dawnbringer

Dawnbringer is Thorin's blade, referenced when Thorin warned Daral that if something bad happened to Thorin, "I'm not sure that Dawnbringer's gonna take care of you." Daral was noted eyeing Dawnbringer nervously, as if wanting to beg the blade off him.

## Kelemvor

Kelemvor does not appear but is discussed regarding the summoning mechanic: the followers of Kelemvor have repeatedly made clear, through their god's usual cryptic incomprehensible phrasing, that an undead possesses things while a dead man possesses nothing — the theological point underlying why killing Strongbranch would free his key for summoning.

## Bahamut

Bahamut does not appear but is referenced by Grygum, who mused "Bahamut may or may not have been watching. I took notes either way" as the keep emptied for the theological debate.

## Shar

Shar, goddess of darkness, is named in the footnotes of the Midnight Tears poison treatise (*On the Punctual Tears*), alongside Selûne. Her presence in the text helped Grygum grasp the "Underdark conundrum" — that in the Underdark no one can see either the moon or the dark, complicating when "midnight" occurs.

## Selûne

Selûne, the goddess of the moon, is named alongside Shar in the footnotes of the Midnight Tears poison treatise. The pairing of the moon-goddess and the darkness-goddess in a poison's documentation explained to Grygum why the poison's midnight trigger is a place (the far side of the globe from the sun) rather than a clock.

## Brother Heslin

Brother Heslin (Tervin) does not appear but is recalled by Zalthir as a possible teacher of a monastery class on "reading what an object meant rather than what it was" — "Brother Heslin, maybe, or the tall one who smelled of incense." Zalthir admits he wasn't listening then but is applying the skill now while reading Alkrist's bookmarked poison-tale.

## Whistler

Whistler is referenced but absent — invoked by Daz as the standard the party fails to meet: "we are not the famed Whistler, no safecracker of that old generation among us," explaining why A'lai's locked safe stayed shut.
