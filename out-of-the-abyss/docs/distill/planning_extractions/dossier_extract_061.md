## Daral Yashenti

Daral appears at the reunion, alive "technically" but dying — poisoned by the same book that had been used to deliver poison to Janussi. In reclaiming the poisoned book, Daral had licked his finger, licked the book, then licked his finger again, dosing himself with midnight tears through the very care he'd taken delivering it to someone else. He arrived pouring out desperate, excessive flattery — praising Thorin as a "fantastic investigator" and Dawnbringer as "stunning" — which Grygum read as a dying man spending everything he had left. Grygum holds the Lesser Restoration but deliberately withholds it ("In due time, my dear"), the party wary that once healed, Daral would find "pressing business elsewhere" and vanish, having already shared his most valuable information. In Daz's interview, Sylvira named Daral as a possible suspect — "a drunk who is bitter," selling off his worthless poetry to fund a new library — but dismissed him as too much of a drunk to manage the planning required. Alkrist's confession revealed it was Daral's own gift that had been unwrapped, coated in midnight tears, and re-wrapped as the murder weapon. Current status: alive but poisoned, awaiting restoration Grygum is not yet ready to grant.

## Fembris Lancer

Fembris arrives at the reunion "like a man who'd been rehearsing in the corridor," effusive over Grygum's colloquium ("Grygum, that was fantastic!") and eager to present his theories and notes. Grygum directs him to deposit the notes with his "secretary" — Glabbagool — who declares them "not nutritious" and "not food." Undeterred, Fembris proposes collaborating on a monograph; Grygum corrects the terminology, and they negotiate down through "duograph," "autograph with acknowledgment," to a promised draft by tomorrow. Fembris lingers at the door, asks pointedly "Where are the four of you? Who were the rest of you? I didn't see you at the talk," gets no real answer, and leaves. Later, filling the air with urgent noise ("My friends, time is a-wasting"), he takes on the errand of hunting down Kalan Strongbranch so the Alkrist interview can proceed with the truth spell in the room — departing with total, near-monastic commitment ("At some point I'll return with Kalan"). Zalthir observes his frictionless dispatch of urgent errands with admiration.

## Glabbagool

The gelatinous cube travels with the party as Grygum's self-styled "secretary." At the reunion, Fembris's notes are handed to him; he evaluates them for nutritional content and pronounces "This is not nutritious... This is not food," then expresses genuine longing: "I remember when there was cheese. Perhaps if we had some cheese." Grygum reflects that Glabbagool doesn't perform emotions — he has them, and the grief over cheese is real. During the safe discussion, Glabbagool volunteers that he could have eaten through Alkrist's locked safe ("It would have taken a while"), and reminds Zalthir with quiet authority: "Walls don't stop us. I can eat through walls." He accepts Zalthir's point that punching or eating the safe would have ruined the sneaky approach ("Good point, good point, good point"), his dignity intact. He is present in the room during Alkrist's interrogation, having simply chosen to pay attention without being asked.

## Elian

Elian pushes Thorin to co-author a monograph on investigative methodology, praising the value of Kalan's proposal and the pushback it faced. Thorin corrects him that a co-authored work isn't a monograph; Elian retreats to proposing he write the monograph while Thorin contributes a preface — Thorin insists on writing "the introduction" instead, which frames the argument rather than decorates it. Elian argues that even a counterfactual endorsement ("these techniques *would have* helped") would win academic approval, and that someone of Thorin's "stature" would do wonders for criminal investigation across Faerûn. He delivers the investigation's core intellectual content: that magic like Speak with Dead cannot be trusted because the dead are still themselves — the "myth of the person" — and that one must collect physical, testimonial, and procedural facts and find where they converge. He lights up when Grygum offers "Once you have eliminated the impossible..." and asks to use it. He handed the party a written document articulating that magic doesn't close contradictions but creates them — which underpinned Zalthir's realization about the polymorph timeline gap.

## Janussi

Janussi does not appear alive — he is the murder victim being investigated. On his last day, budget/review day, he delivered career-destroying verdicts to multiple people in a single afternoon: he blocked Kalan Strongbranch's proposal for new criminal-investigation methodologies, characterizing the entire framework as "solipsism" and a vanity project; he told Alkrist his germ-warfare research was dangerous and should stop, and that he showed no leadership potential; and he confronted A'lai. Grygum found a note in Janussi's own hand on the third floor, opening with a cutting academic insult, that documented his contemptuous rejection of Kalan's work. Sylvira revealed she and Janussi had been lovers (on and off before her, previously involved with Kazryn Nyantani), that their final end-of-year meeting at 11:30 PM was spent planning a joint library dedicated to the Great Wheel of Planes, and that it ended in an argument over a private matter — her last words with him. His heart was ripped out and his safe/chest looted after the poisoning, an act separate from the poisoned book. Grygum's verdict: "high intelligence, but not high wisdom." Janussi's third-floor rooms revealed private "habits" the party had already heard rumor of.

## A'lai Aivenmore

A'lai is identified as the one who set the murder in motion. On the day of the murder at 2:30 PM, A'lai appeared at the Oak Tree Apothecary asking Leuwin for fresh fennel (citing indigestion), drawing Leuwin into the garden while a dragonborn (Alkrist) entered and collected the poison. Alkrist's confession under the zone of truth placed a baths meeting at 11:45 PM between A'lai and Alkrist to discuss "their mutual problem" — Janussi — and the plan to deal with him using the midnight-tears method from 1001 Tashalian Nights. A'lai is a drow, which becomes central to Daz's private calculation: Daz sees A'lai as a possible lead on the unknown patron paying "Menzoberranzan rates" to protect him, and proposes that A'lai pay not in gold or books but in information about who is behind that arrangement. A'lai treats blackmail "as a kind of career" and would construct an administrative narrative for the killing. Not physically confronted in this chunk.

## Leuwin

Leuwin runs the Oak Tree Apothecary. On the day of the murder, A'lai arrived at 2:30 PM asking for fresh fennel, and while Leuwin was helping A'lai in the garden, a dragonborn (Alkrist) entered and stole the poison vial, spilling peppermint on the counter floor. Leuwin missed the intrusion — the details were supplied instead by Nibbles the squirrel.

## Nibbles

Nibbles is a squirrel who witnessed the apothecary intrusion that Leuwin missed. Per Nibbles's testimony, a huge lizard-skinned dragonborn figure arrived while Leuwin was in the garden with A'lai, and the poison was collected amid a mess.

## Alkrist

Alkrist is a bronze-scaled dragonborn in his thirties, dressed with opinions about proper attire. He entered Janussi's chambers deploying rehearsed, performative grief — declaring a "fantastic, superb relationship" with Janussi — and missed the edge of Thorin's ironic remark about remuneration, taking it as sincere (Zalthir noting his self-absorption). Unaware that Kalan Strongbranch's zone of truth was active outside the door, Alkrist came apart under interrogation: he confessed his aunt Bookwyrm was distraught over the succession; that Janussi had dismissed his germ-warfare research as dangerous and denied him leadership potential; and that the 11:45 PM baths meeting with A'lai concerned dealing with Janussi. Zalthir had already secretly read Alkrist's bookmarked copy of 1001 Tashalian Nights, marked at the midnight-tears passage. Alkrist confessed the full mechanics: stealing the vial from the Oak Tree Apothecary, coating Daral's gift book with midnight tears and re-wrapping it, tearing the vial's label and tying it to the Deadwinter tree as decoration, then burning the label when Irony and Orrin Glass arrived with a basin — washing his hands in it just in case. He insisted "I didn't actually kill him. I just put some poison on a book," then absurdly tried to claim he was under mind control while asserting everything he said was true. He recounted the midnight visit to Bookwyrm with two bottles of fine wine, announcing their Janussi problem was solved; she was appalled, they argued loudly, she stormed off, and he went home. He was genuinely confused to learn Janussi's heart had been ripped out — an act Zalthir attributed to Bookwyrm. Last known: still seated, still bound by the truth spell, fully confessed.

## Kalan Strongbranch

Kalan Strongbranch, the First Reader, is the author of the blocked proposal for new criminal-investigation methodologies (the Threefold Proof / Threefold framework) that the party had been handed as their operating toolkit. His truth-seeking spell (zone of truth) anchored the investigation and was needed for the Alkrist interview; Fembris went to hunt him down for that purpose, and Kalan stood just outside the door throughout Alkrist's confession, hearing everything. Grygum's discovery of Janussi's note revealed that Janussi had blocked Kalan's proposal as "solipsism" and a vanity project, handing Kalan a clear motive — Grygum noting this "points to Kalan pretty strongly" while acknowledging it doesn't make him a murderer, only a man who built his methodology in the wreckage of the one who told him he was wrong. Sylvira independently confirmed Kalan had begged her to intercede with Janussi to save his job (she agreed but never did), and dismissed his research as "third-rate." At the conclusion, the party resolves to hand Kalan — the wronged party with standing and motivation — the collected evidence to "clean house." Kalan poses the final question: what to do with the evidence collected.

## Sylvira Savikas

Daz interviews Sylvira in her quarters, where she is bedridden with what she calls winter flu — actually an abyssal plague contracted from her son, born in the Abyss, whom she keeps in a Mordenkainen's Mansion she built for his protection. A tiefling (technically abyssal spawn) with copper-red skin, blue-black hair, and magnetic golden eyes, she directed her familiar Jezebel to arrange seating and ran a mage hand despite her fever — Daz filing her as "not incapacitated." She was Janussi's lover and was being framed for the murder, her likeness seen impersonating her; she was shocked to learn of the framing and briefly suspected Daz of a drow mind-trick before accepting the truth. She named suspects: Daral Yashenti (dismissed as too drunk to plan it), Kazryn Nyantani (Janussi's bitter ex, "that bitter old hag"), and Teles Ahvoste (passed over for Keeper of Tomes for twenty years, "jealousy is a powerful motivator"). She recounted her timeline: the 11:30 PM meeting planning the Great Wheel library, ending in a private argument (insisting she never called Janussi a "genus traitor"), leaving the feast early as the flu came on, sneaking out to visit her son and returning around 12:30 AM, alone thereafter. She reacted with genuine offense to the suggestion she drank honey ("I would never drink honey"), providing an alibi against the kitchen sighting. She noted Bookwyrm had pushed hardest against Kalan's investigative proposal, calling it insane. She expressed relief and delight that the investigation was proceeding, grieving her last argument with Janussi.

## Kazryn Nyantani

Kazryn does not appear but is named by Sylvira as a prime suspect. She was Janussi's lover before Sylvira — "on and off" — and, per Sylvira, "always resented Janussi for ditching her in favor of me." Sylvira described her as "that bitter old hag" and referenced a "frumpy old boar." Daz enters her on the tally as resentment credible enough to survive scrutiny — long-standing grudge, apparent capability.

## Teles Ahvoste

Teles does not appear but is named by Sylvira as a suspect, one of the Great Readers among the avowed hostile to her. Sylvira reports Teles had remained hostile toward Janussi for nearly twenty years after being passed over for Keeper of Tomes despite being older and considered the more skilled practitioner — "Jealousy is a powerful motivator. For revenge. Murder." Daz files two decades of cold institutional hostility as a live grievance.

## Bookwyrm (First Reader)

Bookwyrm is Alkrist's beloved aunt, distraught that the succession was not going her way. Per Alkrist's confession, he visited her at midnight with two bottles of fine wine and two glasses to announce their Janussi problem was solved; she was appalled by the poisoning, argued loudly with him, and stormed off. Zalthir concluded Bookwyrm was the one who ripped out Janussi's heart and looted the chest/chalice afterward — someone who knew about the chalice, what it hid, and why no one would look there. Sylvira separately identified Bookwyrm as the one who felt Kalan's investigative proposal was absurd and pushed hardest against it. Daz "opened an account" on Bookwyrm as a suspect at the edge of the conspiracy. Not physically present in this chunk.

## Dawnbringer

Dawnbringer is referenced as Thorin's "charming partner" — praised by the flattering, dying Daral as "a stunning, absolutely stunning—" before Grygum cut him off. Does not act independently in this chunk beyond being named as Thorin's companion.

## Irony

Irony does not appear directly but is named in Alkrist's confession: Irony and Orrin Glass arrived with a basin of water for supper while Alkrist was burning the poison-vial label, and Alkrist washed his hands in that basin to remove any midnight tears.

## Orrin Glass

Orrin Glass does not appear directly but is named in Alkrist's confession as arriving alongside Irony with the basin of water for supper — the water Alkrist used to wash the poison from his hands after burning the vial's label.

## Jezebel

Jezebel is Sylvira Savikas's familiar. Sylvira commanded "Jezebel! Organize the chamber's seats in front of me!" and the familiar moved with practiced efficiency, indicating Sylvira routinely directs operations from her sickbed.

## Kaelira Duskryn

Kaelira does not appear but is referenced in Daz's internal accounting: she had told him bluntly that someone powerful was paying to protect a man (Daz) she couldn't understand, laying out the patron problem plainly. Her disclosure is the origin of the single unnamed entry in Daz's ledger — the unknown patron paying "Menzoberranzan rates" — which drives his interest in leveraging A'lai for information.

## Bahamut

Bahamut, Grygum's deity, is referenced repeatedly. Grygum keeps notes "whether Bahamut reads them or not," reflecting that Bahamut's domain includes the preservation of truth — which he ties to using the Mending cantrip to restore the cut wrapping paper and reveal what it once concealed. Fembris invokes his own standing "as a cleric of Bahamut" while flattering Grygum. Does not act; referenced as divine framework.

## Brother Heslin

Brother Heslin does not appear but is referenced in Zalthir's recollection: he credits either "Brother Heslin, or possibly Brother Varath" (he is genuinely uncertain which) with having devoted a season to teaching his cohort the art of the urgent errand and clean dispatch — a lesson Zalthir admits he learned poorly, having been looking out the window.

## Vareth

Vareth (Brother Varath) does not appear but is referenced alongside Brother Heslin as the possible teacher, in Zalthir's uncertain memory, of the monastery lesson on urgent errands and clean dispatch.

## Brother Quellin

Brother Quellin does not appear but is referenced in Zalthir's recollection during the Alkrist interrogation: he attributes — uncertainly, "or possibly Brother Harren" — the maxim that the most dangerous person in a room is the one who has already decided they've won, which Zalthir applies to Janussi's fatal overconfidence.

## Brother Harren

Brother Harren does not appear but is named alongside Brother Quellin as the possible source, in Zalthir's uncertain attribution, of the lesson about the most dangerous person being the one who believes they've already won.
