## Daral Yashenti

Present in the sanctum after the animated armor attack, pounding on the (already smashed-open) door and shouting to be let out until he realized it was open. Turned to the party and asked, "What do we do next, gentlemen?" A Candlekeep scholar who, like others, carries scrolls on his person. He confirmed the two keys that open Candlekeep's High Tower are "completely magical" — not cheap locksmith keys — then cast Detect Magic from a scroll, lighting up Dawnbringer, Grygum's armor, and himself, while the key stayed dark. He concluded emphatically, "This is not the key," confirming the party held a decoy. Later he helped bring the party to Bookwyrm's ruined office, could not identify "the beast" from her dying note, and explained Candlekeep's defenses: a great shield preventing teleportation in or out, and that fire does not work in the keep (a "burning bush" effect). He realized aloud that this fire-suppressing ward lives in the single room locked by the two high tower keys. He found the suggestion that the teleport protection had failed inconceivable, but was pressed by Grygum to check. He named the Sea Warden's Tower as Kalan's likely location. Last seen assisting the party's investigation.

## Fembris Lancer

Initially the party's prime suspect: Fembris had watched the key pass from Grygum's hand to Daz's, and shortly after three mindless constructs came through the sanctum windows knowing exactly which hand held the key — implying someone with knowledge had directed them. Later, at the Sea Warden's Tower, Fembris helped bring the party to Bookwyrm's office. Under Thorin's courteous but firm questioning, Fembris admitted that when he reported the key to Bookwyrm, **A'lai Aivenmore had been in the room** — redirecting suspicion away from himself and onto A'lai. Kalan counted this confession as one of the "pillars of the thrice-fold proof." Cleared as an active suspect by chapter's end.

## Bookwyrm (First Reader)

Killed during this chapter — the alarm bell that tolled "wrong" (a flat institutional pattern) announced her death. The party had previously proven her guilty (of Janussi's murder / taking his heart and key) but left her unaccused on Kalan's patient schedule, awaiting the completed thrice-fold proof; Daz had been treating her as "an asset to collect from later." An eighteenth-level magic user whose high-level slots ran to scholarly magic (Daz imagines Validate Citation / Identify False Citation rather than combat spells). Her killer struck her at rest in her own tower, exploiting foreknowledge of how relaxed and confident she'd be — information Daz judged had to have been sold to someone. Her **throat was torn, not cut** (marking an animal, not surgical, hand — distinct from Janussi's murder), and her **heart was left present and intact**, unlike Janussi's. Her office was left a ruin. On her desk, in her own hand, was a note stopped mid-sentence: "*He is using the beast to—*". Bookwyrm had been first into Janussi's chamber and had taken Janussi's heart and key; her own key was torn from her by her killer. Deceased.

## Kalan Strongbranch

Found buckling on his armor at the Sea Warden's Tower, already moving toward the party at a half-run, having heard the alarm bell. His first question on learning of Bookwyrm's death was not who or how but "Does she have the key?" — and he was relieved to learn no key was found on her body, initially framing her death as an accident, "a maddening attack by Sylvira Savikas's son," noting "I told Bookwyrm that creature would be the death of us." When told about the three armor constructs hunting a key in the sanctum, his composure drained: "Gadzooks. Someone is definitely after the keys." He deputized the party as **Watchers of Candlekeep** (offering a "small honorarium" when Daz asked about pay). Critically, he revealed his deception: the key he had earlier pressed on Grygum — with the strange phrasing "*I have given it now to someone who is not me*" — was a **decoy from the first hour**; the genuine key had been posted on **Tadric**, a young Watcher, in the North Galleries, away from the barracks on purpose. He justified this by reasoning that if enemies saw the party with a fake key, both the party and the real key would be safer. He gave Thorin a **Watcher's Stair key** for a shortcut to the North Gallery, swearing "On my honor" that this one was genuine. He tried to run with the party but, carrying extra weight and years ("Little Rotund," per Thorin), fell behind and planted, hands on knees, done. Last seen breathing hard and out of the pursuit; Tadric's anxious repeated question was whether Kalan is still alive — the party's honest answer was that he was alive when they left him.

## Janussi

Does not appear in this chapter; referenced as a prior murder victim used for comparison. Janussi's heart was removed "with instruments and intent" (surgical), distinguishing that killing from Bookwyrm's animal throat-tearing. Bookwyrm had been the first into Janussi's chamber and took both Janussi's heart and key. The party sorts the two murders into separate columns — different hands, different purposes.

## Sylvira Savikas

Does not appear directly; discussed as the key to the chapter's mystery. Daz recalls a months-old fact: Sylvira Savikas has a son, Abyss-born — she is a tiefling who was in the Abyss, where "bad things happen." She keeps the son locked in a magical sanctuary, either to protect him or to protect everyone else from him. Kalan blames her son (Moziqodo) for Bookwyrm's death and had warned Bookwyrm "that creature would be the death of us." Not physically present.

## Moziqodo

The massive Abyssal pit fiend the party fights in the domed rotunda — revealed by Tadric to be **Sylvira Savikas's son**. It beat the party to the North Gallery, had Tadric grappled flat to the floor, and was methodically opening him with its claws when the party arrived, so absorbed in its kill that it never looked up. Its entire plan was singular: kill Tadric, take the second key, disengage, and fly off into the dark before anyone could cross the floor. It never got to execute — the party took its whole turn away. Thorin engaged first with Dawnbringer (radiant, not fire), landing three strikes and frightening it (settling Thorin's earlier boast that a pit fiend *could* be frightened). Zalthir then crossed forty-five feet, struck it, and stunned it with a precisely placed monk strike ("Even better than frightened"). Grygum's saved lightning javelin missed; Daz finished with three darts of plain force (declining fire, to which the fiend is immune); Thorin delivered the killing blows. It went down without ever landing an attack. It is the "beast" of Bookwyrm's dying note and the tool of whoever is "using the beast." Deceased on the rotunda floor.

## Tadric

The young human Watcher who had earlier guarded Janussi's door; revealed to be the secret bearer of the genuine High Tower key, posted by Kalan in the North Galleries away from the barracks — loyal, alone, and unaware of what he carried or what was coming for it. Thorin summed him up: "Short human, young, Tadric. His role is Tadric." The party raced via the Watcher's Stair shortcut but arrived to find Moziqodo already tearing him apart on the rotunda floor. After the fiend was killed, Grygum healed him closed; Tadric came around grabbing for the key before his eyes focused, then identified the corpse: "Moziqodo. Sylvira Savikas's son." His immediate, cracked question — asked twice — was "Is Kalan alive?" Thorin chose radical transparency and told him the whole situation. Last seen alive, key in fist, color returning.

## A'lai Aivenmore

Does not appear directly but emerges as the prime suspect and antagonist by chapter's end. Fembris Lancer confessed that A'lai had been in the room when the key was reported to Bookwyrm — the one person with knowledge nobody else had. The party assembles the case: two attacks, two keys, one buyer. Kalan counts this as a second "pillar of the thrice-fold proof." The party concludes A'lai now holds the real key torn from Bookwyrm and is almost certainly sitting in Candlekeep's High Tower, waiting for Moziqodo to deliver the second key — not knowing his courier (the pit fiend) is dead, and not knowing the party holds both the real second key and Kalan's decoy. The party plans to confront him in the High Tower.

## Dawnbringer

Thorin's radiant sentient sword, active throughout the chapter. It lit up under Daral's Detect Magic in the sanctum. In the rotunda fight, Thorin had to be reminded — and the sword required an apology before the fight, which it got ("You are Radiant Sword!") — that it deals radiant damage, not fire (relevant against the fire-immune pit fiend). It landed three opening strikes on Moziqodo and delivered the killing blows. Still humming in Thorin's grip after the fight.
