## Fembris Lancer
The adjutant attached himself to the party throughout this chapter, "vibrating" with eagerness and insisting they return to the chapter house to give Bookwyrm a summary of events, reminding them that a summary was exactly what she had asked for. Bookwyrm designated him as the party's runner — he would fetch any witness or suspect the investigators named. He escorted Grygum down to research, walking "like someone who believed noise had consequences," and clarified that there was no dedicated poison section but rather the Flora Theca. He confirmed Grygum would have laboratory access if needed later. He also carried the message that Daral Yashenti refused to come to Deneir's Sanctum and that the party must instead go to the Hearth. Thorin noted Bookwyrm's slip of "catch… fetch any suspects," reinforcing suspicion that Fembris's helpfulness might be a leash.

## Bookwyrm (First Reader)
Bookwyrm received the party at the chapter house, where they found her already in private conference with Kei Tigersteel behind a closed door. She waved off the moon-elf scribes to usher the party in herself, "all warmth," and introduced Kei, then dismissed him to take Janussi's body to the Shrine of Oghma for purification — leaving herself alone with the investigators, which Daz noted. She pressed them repeatedly for their conclusions and suspects ("Is there any suspects in particular?" — a half-step too fast), and asked pointedly "Was there nobody that you wished to start your investigation with?" The party read this as a question wanting a name back to measure where they were pointed; they gave her only Ms. Hollypocket as a harmless answer. Daz and Zalthir concluded she was steering them toward Sylvira Savikas without ever saying the name — an unstated but strong preference, ambiguous between wanting the case solved and putting a thumb on the scale away from herself. She offered Deneir's Sanctum as their office and Vooshadi Moonriver as their doorkeeper, and warned they should not move through Candlekeep without an escort — generosity the party read as "rarely free" and possibly a bugged room. Thorin wanted her whereabouts before nine that morning to keep her on the suspect list. Kalan Strongbranch suspects Bookwyrm is either protecting one of the Great Readers or was manipulated into firing him to shield the real killer. The party filed her as "not above suspicion" while acknowledging accusing her would require hard facts. She had officially barred Kalan from the case. Last seen at the chapter house after the Council of Great Readers dispersed.

## Kei Tigersteel
Introduced by Bookwyrm as the Lorekeeper of Candlekeep, priest of the Temple of Oghma, and "the library's spiritual leader." A slender half-elf of perhaps fifty, bald-scalped with black side whiskers chiseled into a precise goatee. The party found him already in private conference with Bookwyrm behind a closed door when they arrived; Daz would have liked to overhear it but his page-reading tools were the wrong shape for a closed door. Bookwyrm dismissed him easily, granting him permission to take Janussi's body to the Shrine of Oghma for purification. He left with the body.

## Vooshadi Moonriver
Assigned by Bookwyrm to stand the door of Deneir's Sanctum, controlling who could and could not enter the party's makeshift investigation office — "we wouldn't want anyone the investigators don't want to come in, to come in." Does not otherwise act in this chunk.

## Miss Hollypocket
Referenced only. Named by Daz to Bookwyrm as the one harmless witness the party had already spoken with — offered deliberately as "a name that costs nothing" to test what Bookwyrm would do with a name, rather than as a genuine lead.

## Sylvira Savikas
Does not appear in this chunk; discussed heavily. Someone impersonated Sylvira, walking the halls in the dead of night with a lantern on the night of Janussi's murder. She was absent from the Council of Great Readers, prompting Daral to ask at the Hearth "why isn't Sylvira Savikas here?" Bookwyrm seemed to want the party to start with her without saying so. The party reasoned that because someone wore her face and carried the lantern, Sylvira herself was probably not the killer, though "she might know something" — possibly who would want to impersonate her. Kalan Strongbranch reported visiting her at quarter-past-ten on a cosmological question; he was unaware anyone had impersonated her, which Zalthir revealed to him. Daral disparaged her as Janussi's "horned tart," blaming her for Janussi wanting to fund a tome about demonic planes. Thorin assigned himself to interview her.

## Fheminor Scrivenbark
A dowdy halfling Great Reader. At the shouting council over Janussi's body, she asked whether Janussi had named a successor. Grygum noted that in the party's notes she was flagged as "somebody who seems to have the same priorities that we do," a potential ally to interview for tips — though oddly her notes entry had "no name on it," only her name. Daz chose to interview her, reasoning that witnesses with the least time often hold the most insight. Zalthir gave Daz specific questions for her: establish when Fheminor was beaten up (beaten at eight tells a different story than beaten when Janussi died — "he was beat up before he was dead"), whether she noticed anything missing, and whether she might have poisoned Janussi during a visit. Daral warned "don't be fooled by her stature, she has a steely nature," listing her among those who wanted the top job.

## A'lai Aivenmore
At the Council of Great Readers held over Janussi's body, A'lai called for a vote and new leadership. Daral, at the Hearth, listed A'lai among the suspects, calling him "the preying cock" and one of those angling for the top job. Not otherwise present in this chunk.

## Kazryn Nyantani
At the shouting council over Janussi's body, Kazryn — slender and gray, with wrinkles around the eyes — cut through the argument insisting "first you must find the murderer." Not otherwise present in this chunk.

## Alkrist
A bronze dragonborn Great Reader, described as formidable and furious. At the council over Janussi's body he demanded that the gate warden (Kalan Strongbranch) be dismissed, arguing the murder happened on his watch. Not otherwise present in this chunk.

## Daral Yashenti
Interviewed by Daz and Thorin at the Hearth, where Daral refused to come to them — a venue choice Daz noted as a suspect picking his own ground. Found boisterous, flushed, and drunk, greeting them as "Faerûn's most famous detectives! Bookwyrm's brownnosers" and buying them a round of spiced brandies. Daz read his good cheer as the relief of a man who had punched his superior that morning and learned he'd merely be fired rather than annihilated; Thorin's alternate theory was that Daral was happy because "he finally killed the person that was holding him back." Daral admitted an eleven o'clock meeting where he clashed with Janussi — whom he called a "philistine," an "uneducated bookseller… not fit to be keeper of the tomes" — over Janussi's plan to sell priceless poetry to fund a tome about demonic planes "just to please his horned tart," Sylvira. He blessed "whoever bumped him off." He admitted an evening visit: he found Janussi reading the very book Daral had gifted him, took it back, and when Janussi "sat upon him like a common thug," Daral "may have caught him lightly on the nose." Throughout the interview he wet his finger and flicked through the book's pages, laughing at passages — which Daz watched closely, believing he was witnessing the poison-delivery mechanism. Daral named suspects freely: Teles ("the deluded old git"), A'lai ("the preying cock"), Fheminor ("steely nature"), and Bookwyrm ("no telling what's going on in her lizard brain… cold-blooded, they eat their young"), earning Thorin's retort that "some of my good friends are lizards." When told Janussi was poisoned via a licked, page-turned book, Daral threw the book down and blurted "you've got to do something!" The party took the book into evidence and resolved to keep Daral close until midnight, with a cleric and a poison-drawing sword at hand — reasoning that if Daral died at midnight it would prove him innocent, since "a man does not lace the pages he's been licking all night."

## Janussi
The murder victim; deceased and examined. Keeper of the tomes, a former bookseller whom rivals sneered at as uneducated. He died at roughly midnight, poisoned by midnight tears delivered on a doped book he read by licking his fingers to turn pages — his fingers and lips had gone black from the poison reacting with saliva. His heart had been hacked out after death to prevent resurrection, and an attempt to question his corpse reportedly failed because "Y" (the impersonator) had locked his mouth first. He wore one of the two High Tower keys in a locket around his neck; the locket was gone. His apartment showed signs of two searchers — one who knew exactly where the locket-key hid, and one who tore through the safe instead. On the day of his death he held an eight o'clock meeting where he refused Kalan Strongbranch's request for more watchers, and an eleven o'clock meeting with Daral, whom he clashed with over selling poetry to fund a demonic-planes tome; that evening Daral found him reading the gifted book and struck him on the nose after Janussi "sat upon him." At the council, Fheminor asked whether Janussi had named a successor. Thorin's guiding conviction: "the person that killed him is the one that took the heart."

## Kalan Strongbranch
The gate warden and holder of one of the two High Tower keys (Janussi held the other). Officially barred from the case by Bookwyrm, Alkrist having demanded his dismissal because the murder happened on his watch. Kalan stepped out of an unnoticed doorway, checked the corridor both ways, and pressed a small brass key specifically into Grygum's palm — choosing the orc cleric deliberately — warning "whatever Bookwyrm is telling you, listen for what she isn't" and that if he wasn't seen tomorrow, not to look for him at his post, since "it is best that if I am killed, the key not be found." He explained the High Tower is the entrance to all the super-magical artifacts every evil entity would covet, and that it needs both keys turned at once (Thorin's "war-game swapper" / two-officer missile-launch metaphor). He argued the tower could not have been secretly compromised because it is always watched regardless of the key, which Thorin took as clearing him. Zalthir followed him for a private conversation: Kalan probed the party's motives, decided fate had brought disinterested outsiders here for a reason, and shared his own findings — he'd noticed the poison in the left hand, the saliva, the heart cut out after death to spoil resurrection, and deduced the murderer was a magic user who could open any lock, arcane or mundane. Zalthir corrected his hasty assumption that the safe-rifler and the murderer were the same person (given the locket-key and multiple visitors), and Kalan conceded "perhaps it was two different people." Kalan suspected one of the Great Readers was the murderer and that Bookwyrm was either protecting them or manipulated into firing him; he believed the party was hired to bungle the case or be silent. Zalthir persuaded him to collaborate "unofficially," proposing a five-way meeting to merge findings. Kalan confirmed he could question the dead and detect lies ("of course I could"). He gave his timeline: the eight o'clock meeting where Janussi refused more watchers, a quarter-past-ten visit to Sylvira on a cosmological question, research in the Chamber of Lost Lore, then the morning inspection just after eight, the cordon, the posted watcher, and the doubled guard at the High Tower Library. He went pale when Zalthir revealed someone had impersonated Sylvira the night of the murder — "No. That is new information… You are very good at this" — and warned that whoever named him to the wrong people would be disappointed. Zalthir warned him to watch his back and beware shapeshifters. Kalan departed to his research; the party flagged him and his second key as a key thread. Zalthir noted the locket Kalan produced to demonstrate carry-method was empty and proved nothing — the key he held could be Janussi's, handed off by a man building a clean story.

## Teles Ahvoste

## Y
Referenced only; the impersonator behind the crime. ## Glabbagool
Summoned to the Whispering Dome for his "little quest" / trial. The party was told Glabbagool is now permanently attached to Zalthir, who logged no objection. In the Dome — where supplicants learn something about themselves — a monk posed the question "What is the art of movement." Glabbagool went utterly still for the better part of a minute, then answered "The body that mimics nothing is already shaped by what it watches," which the monk accepted as wisdom. Glabbagool then declared "I've been waiting to do this for a very long time" and flowed up onto Zalthir's forearm, settling as though he'd measured it in advance — "Glabbagool and Zalthir, together now… We ride." His new abilities: teleport in darkness, a flurry of strikes, forcing an acidic shadow-key into a wound, and a costly corrosive embrace that dissolves a target Zalthir has already bound (leaving nothing to hold). He also manifested four eyes on Zalthir's scales, which Zalthir suggested he blend away to look like normal scales; Glabbagool considered it. Before settling in, he reminded Zalthir that the drow in the bag of holding still needed watching — housekeeping at the moment of his own transformation. A former shadow monk observed that Glabbagool had mastered in under a minute the silent-communication lesson Zalthir had slept through. Glabbagool is now traveling with the party permanently, riding on Zalthir's arm.

## Archibald Roots Junior
Referenced as the author of *Fey-mous Flowers & Fungi*, the book Grygum found in the Flora Theca — "one Archibald Roots, Jr., son of the legendary gnome botanist Archibald Roots Sr." The book's early chapters are the son writing about his father's explorations and collections, but it contains the crucial entry on midnight tears: a highly toxic pale ochre dust from Feywild pollen, lethal in food or liquid, causing no effect until the stroke of midnight, and neutralizable into a pain-relief solution when mixed with the sap of a certain Feywild tree. This entry let Grygum identify the poison, its rarity (narrowing the suspect list), and the black-finger/black-lip saliva-reaction mechanism that made the licked book the murder weapon.

## Archibald Roots Senior
Referenced only. The "legendary gnome botanist," father of Archibald Roots Jr. and subject of much of *Fey-mous Flowers & Fungi* — his life story, explorations, and long gnomish life "cataloging things that grow where no one wants to go." Described by his son in the careful tone of a man whose father saved lives.

## Brother Heslin
Referenced only, in Zalthir's memory. Zalthir recalled a class on silent communication — "communication without sound" — that he thought might have been taught by "Brother Heslin, the one who always smelled of incense, or possibly the quiet one with the ink-stained hands." He remembered the light through the shutters but not the lesson itself, which is precisely the lesson Glabbagool mastered in the Whispering Dome while Zalthir "was sleeping."

## Stroud

As Grygum found the right book almost effortlessly in the Flora Theca, he thought: "Stroud couldn't play chess either, and he still rolled the winning move" — invoking his mentor-figure's philosophy of arriving at the right outcome without conventional cleverness.
