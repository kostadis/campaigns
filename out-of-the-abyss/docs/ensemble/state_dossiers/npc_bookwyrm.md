### Bookwyrm

**Current status:** Alive  
**Current location:** Candlekeep, likely in her chambers on the top floor  
**Allegiance/faction:** Acting Head of Candlekeep; leader of the Avowed  
**Current possessions / notable items:** None explicitly stated, but her chambers are described as comfortable, neatly arranged, and devoid of extravagance or incriminating physical evidence.  
**Current assignment / role:** Acting Head of Candlekeep, overseeing institutional stability during the aftermath of Janussi’s murder; actively maneuvering to retain power through political theater and manipulation.  
**Key relationships:**  
- Nephew: Alkrist (confessed to killing Janussi; now finished)  
- Rival: Kalan Strongbranch (opposed her on investigative controls; now under suspicion or sidelined)  
- Instrumental target: Sylvira Savikas (steered investigation toward her without naming her)  
- Puppeteered: The adventurers (used as unwitting enforcers to remove threats and divert scrutiny)  
- Former subordinate: Janussi (whose research she destroyed; who opposed her authority)  

**Defining recent actions:**  
- Concealed her nephew Alkrist’s confession and murder of Janussi by hiding the heart in the chalice of a statue.  
- Orchestrated a campaign of psychological misdirection: feigned grief, pretended concern for investigators’ safety, and secretly delighted in their removal from power.  
- Opposed Kalan’s investigative controls, calling them “absurd,” and may have delayed detection of the murder.  
- Directed investigators toward Sylvira Savikas as a diversionary suspect.  
- Provided Deneir’s Sanctum as the investigation’s office while maintaining control over access and narrative.  
- Ordered the adventurers confined to Deneir’s Sanctum and exited the scene, declaring, “The spectacle had gone on long enough.”  
- Survived a full search of her chambers — no physical evidence found, but the absence itself was significant.  

**Revealed motivations or secrets:**  
- Motivated by institutional power and control; sought to remove Kalan and install a new Keeper aligned with her agenda.  
- Willing to sacrifice others (Janussi, Alkrist, the adventurers) to preserve her position.  
- Secretly relieved by Janussi’s death and the adventurers’ removal — feigned concern to mask delight.  
- Capable of high-level deception: turned suspicion into compliance, made investigators act as her agents.  
- Not guilty of murder, but guilty of conspiracy after the fact — concealing a crime to protect her bloodline and her authority.  

## Uncertainty
- Contradiction: One fact calls Bookwyrm a “dragonborn sage,” another a “half-orc.” Later facts consistently refer to her as dragonborn and never mention half-orc — the half-orc reference (ch56) is likely an error or misattribution and is overridden by consistent later use of “dragonborn.”  
- Ambiguity: Was Bookwyrm the one who removed Janussi’s heart? The text says she “may have” used a polymorph spell to alter the body and was alone with it afterward — but never confirms she performed the act. Alkrist confessed to the murder; Bookwyrm is implicated only in concealment.  
- Unclear attribution: The quote “She drank some, watching us over the rim” is used twice to describe her delight — but is this Zalthir’s observation or an independent narration? The source is attributed to Zalthir, but the interpretation (“she was delighted”) is the narrator’s inference — is this the narrator’s conclusion or Zalthir’s direct observation?  
- Chronological ambiguity: The phrase “She walks” (ch61) is cryptic — does it mean she is walking away, or that she will be allowed to retain her position? The meaning is contextually implied but not explicitly defined.  
- Unclear status: Is she still the Acting Head at the moment of the latest chapter? The text says she is “expected to be removed” during the naming ceremony — but the ceremony’s outcome is not stated. She is not confirmed to have been deposed.  
- Contradictory role: Is she “the spiritual and administrative leader” (ch58) or merely the “acting head” (ch61)? The former suggests institutional authority, the latter suggests interim status — but later facts treat her as the de facto leader, so “acting head” likely reflects political vulnerability, not loss of function.
