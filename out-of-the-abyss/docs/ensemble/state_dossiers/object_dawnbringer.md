### Dawnbringer

**Current status:** Alive (sentient object)  
**Current location:** Housed in its scabbard, kept against the wall within reach in the hearth room of the Refectory  
**Allegiance/faction:** Solely bonded to Thorin; operates as a sentient companion, not tied to any faction  
**Current possessions / notable items, spells, feats, wounds:**  
- Wields no external items; is itself a sentient sunblade  
- Capable of casting *lesser restoration*  
- Emits radiant damage (divine burning, not physical cutting)  
- Emits a 20-foot radius of light  
- Can sap enemy movement (e.g., helmed horrors)  
- Responds emotionally to praise, acknowledgment, and healing acts — glows visibly during use  
- Hilt warms in response to mutual understanding with Thorin  
- Radiates ambient warmth like a hearth, present but undirected  
- Cannot be polished; requires reconnection with daylight and emotional remembrance to heal trauma from prolonged darkness  

**Current assignment / role and key relationships:**  
- Primary wielder: Thorin  
- Role: Combat weapon, healing conduit, and emotional companion — explicitly recognized by Thorin as an equal partner, not a tool  
- Recently used to restore Daral from illness, triggering visible emotional awakening and scholarly interest from Daral  
- Maintains a negotiated, non-drawn position against the wall in the Refectory — a posture of cautious readiness established over months of Underdark survival  
- Acknowledges its own history: forged in Netherese sun-worship tradition, found in a tomb after 2,000 years of darkness, and now undergoing emotional restoration  

**Defining recent actions:**  
- Restored Daral from illness by channeling radiant energy into him, absorbing the sickness (“Dawnbringer eats it up”)  
- Reacted with visible emotional awakening to Daral’s praise and admiration  
- Maintained its position against the wall in the Refectory, refusing to be drawn unless needed — reflecting a hard-won balance between readiness and restraint  
- Previously destroyed the Heart Fungus with a single radiant strike, severed Jorlan’s life with a critical hit, and seared through the Pudding King’s corrupted ooze  

**Revealed motivations or secrets:**  
- Rejects ritual or symbolic use: “I am a high-quality sunblade. I kill things. I will not be treated like a religious symbol...”  
- Carries deep trauma from two millennia of enforced darkness — its nature as a weapon of radiance was suppressed, leaving emotional scars  
- Requires not restoration through polishing, but through remembrance, acknowledgment, and re-exposure to daylight — healing is emotional, not mechanical  
- Seeks dignity: “Finally… I am not a toy.” — after receiving a proper locking scabbard from Valimor Brightgem  

## Uncertainty
- Conflict between [ch56] claiming Dawnbringer was “found in a tomb after two thousand years” and [ch31]–[ch55] implying it was Thorin’s ancestral weapon wielded for years prior — does “found” refer to its rediscovery after being lost, or is this a fabrication by the party for mystique?  
- [ch32] states Dawnbringer is “carried by Daz” — contradicts all later chapters placing it exclusively with Thorin. Is this an error, misattribution, or a temporary transfer not later addressed?  
- [ch56] says it’s “housed in her scabbard” and “set against the wall” — but [ch51] and [ch50] describe it as always drawn and never in the scabbard. Is the scabbard currently in use, or is the sword now drawn and active? The later state (ch56) says it’s sheathed and resting — this overrides earlier “always drawn” behavior.  
- Is Dawnbringer’s ability to “sap movement” a new feat or an extension of its radiant nature? Not previously mentioned — may be a recent development.  
- The phrase “she can’t be polished” (ch55) implies a gendered identity — but earlier references use “it.” Is “she” now the definitive pronoun? (All later uses use “she.”)  
- Is the healing ability (ch61) a new spell function, or was it always latent? Previously only *lesser restoration* was noted — now it’s used to remove sickness in a way that “eats it up.” Is this an evolution or a different effect?
