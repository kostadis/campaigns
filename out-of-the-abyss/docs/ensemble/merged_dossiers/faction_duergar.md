### Duergar

**Current status:** Active, fractured, and under existential threat.  
**Current location:** Primarily centered in Gracklstugh and surrounding Underdark networks, with control over the Darklake Brewery, Overlake Hold, and key trade routes.  
**Allegiance/faction:** Ruled by the Deepking, though his madness has destabilized their hierarchy. Noble houses compete for power amid internal decay.  
**Current possessions / notable items, spells, feats, wounds:**  
- Innate ability to become invisible at will.  
- Control over forges and smelters, including the exploitation of Themberchaud (a red dragon) as a living forge-lighter.  
- Maintain stone guard forces and surveillance infrastructure, including identity checkpoints demanding bribes and proof of innocence.  
- Operate the Blade Bazaar as a central hub for trade and slave commerce.  
- Use acid magic and ambush tactics in enforcement and defense.  

**Current assignment / role and key relationships:**  
- Enforce strict taxation and control over Underdark trade routes; demand bribes at gates.  
- Dominate Gracklstugh through militarized order, segregation (clean duergar dwellings vs. Derro slums), and fear.  
- Manipulate Themberchaud through psychological control — pampering and false authority — to suppress his autonomy and use him as a tool.  
- Fear and are actively targeted by the Ember Vanguard, whom they mistake for the party upon sighting Daz.  
- Historically enslaved the Derro; now facing a genocidal campaign by Derro cultists in service to Demogorgon.  
- Suspected of complicity in Gartokkar’s genocidal scheme against the Derro.  
- Maintain remnants of guardhouses in the West Cleft, though these were abandoned after Deepking Tarngardt I liberated the Derro.  

**Defining recent actions:**  
- Panic and mobilize warnings upon sighting Daz, believing the party to be the Ember Vanguard.  
- Continue exploiting Themberchaud as a forge-lighter despite his immense power, revealing a preference for psychological domination over efficiency.  
- Enforce rigid identity checks and tolls, demonstrating economic control even amid internal collapse.  
- Exhibit heightened paranoia and militarization, with civil discourse impossible due to innate invisibility.  

**Revealed motivations or secrets:**  
- Not inherently evil, but driven by terror — fear of power, loss of control, and internal decay has made them cruel.  
- Believe dragon scale color determines morality, a misconception shaping their judgment and policy.  
- Their rigid clan system and invisibility have turned their greatest strength into a prison, preventing trust, debate, or reform.  
- Their economy and social order are crumbling under authoritarianism, economic disparity, and the Deepking’s madness — yet they outwardly maintain oppressive rigidity.  

## Uncertainty
- Contradiction between [ch18] (Duergar and Derro were once peaceful companions) and [ch17]–[ch24] (Duergar are depicted as long-term oppressors and slavers of Derro) — timeline of the “Faerzress blinked” event and its causal role is unclear.  
- Unclear whether the Duergar are actively still using the abandoned guardhouses in the West Cleft or if they have any operational presence there now.  
- Ambiguous whether “the Deepking” referenced in [ch28] and [ch29] is the same individual as Tarngardt I’s grandson (from [ch17]) or a later, current ruler whose madness is newly manifesting.  
- Unclear if the Duergar’s innate invisibility is a racial trait or a magical ability — sources treat it as innate, but no mechanism or limit is specified.  
- Uncertain whether the Duergar’s suspicion of Themberchaud’s self-importance implies they are aware he is a dragon of great power, or if they genuinely believe him to be a mere tool.  
- Unclear if the Duergar’s complicity in Gartokkar’s scheme is official policy or the action of rogue agents.  
- No confirmation of current status of the Council of Savants’ network as a counter to Duergar taxes — Buppido’s use of it is historical.
