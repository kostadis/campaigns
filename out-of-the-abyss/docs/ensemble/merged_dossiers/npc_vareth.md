### Vareth

**Current Status:** Alive  
**Current Location:** Candlekeep, maintaining his side office  
**Allegiance/Faction:** Bahamutian scholar (self-identified), with formalized theological schools (Bahamutian, Stroudite, Grygum)  
**Current Possessions / Notable Items:**  
- A curated collection of Stroudite papers (held with ritualistic intensity)  
- Active research proposals centered on “Stroud’s Gambit” and the “strong-weak hypothesis”  
- Institutional authority as a senior librarian at Candlekeep  

**Current Assignment / Role:**  
- Senior librarian at Candlekeep, having maintained his side office for forty-one years  
- Official handler of Grygum (assigned without ceremony)  
- Leading researcher in theological frameworks on mortal agency, divine providence, and cosmic fixed points  
- Developer of the “Grygum school” of thought (a compromise within the Bahamutian tradition)  

**Defining Recent Actions:**  
- Formulated the “strong-weak hypothesis,” categorizing theological positions:  
  - Strong Stroudite: denies fixed points  
  - Strong Bahamutian: affirms fixed points  
  - Weak Bahamutian: operates outside fixed points  
- Assigned himself the label of “weak Stroudite” to the observer, signaling intellectual flexibility  
- Rapidly developed a new theological school — the Grygum school — inspired by an offhand remark, positioning it as a compromise between Stroudite and Bahamutian views  
- Actively collects, files, and systematizes Stroudite papers with ritualistic precision  
- Maintains institutional restraint and decorum despite personal fervor for his research  

**Revealed Motivations / Secrets:**  
- His decades-long niche study of half-orc Bahamut converts became suddenly significant after “Stroud’s Gambit” — a chess move that triggered a cascade of theological and academic interest  
- He is deeply motivated by the validation of his once-ignored field; he recognizes “what a fashion looks like” and is now riding the wave of academic interest  
- He believes in three competing frameworks:  
  - Bahamutian: divine providence prevails; mortals are instruments  
  - Stroudite: mortal choice is sovereign; gods and dragons exist, but Providence may not  
  - Grygum (his own): fixed points of cosmic constraint exist amid free agency — a compromise  
- He sees himself as both scholar and archivist — not merely theorizing, but *cataloging* the evolution of thought  

## Uncertainty  
- Contradiction in race: Multiple facts label Vareth as a half-elf scholar, but two state he is a half-orc cleric of Bahamut. Later facts (ch56) describe him as a scholar who studies half-orc converts and references his own position as a “weak Stroudite” — implying he is not himself a half-orc cleric. The later, more detailed ch56 descriptions (institutional role, theological framing) override earlier conflicting race claims — so he is likely a half-elf scholar who studies half-orc Bahamut converts, not a half-orc cleric himself.  
- Unclear if Vareth is a cleric of Bahamut: Early facts call him a “half-orc cleric of Bahamut,” but later facts describe him as a scholar who analyzes Bahamutian theology, not a practitioner. No evidence he casts spells, leads worship, or holds clerical office. This appears to be a misattribution in early chapters.  
- Ambiguous attribution of “Stroud’s Gambit”: Was it Stroud’s actual move, or a label Vareth coined? The text implies Vareth named it, but the origin of the event is not confirmed.  
- Unclear if Grygum is still under Vareth’s care: Grygum was assigned to him in ch55, but ch56 does not confirm ongoing custody — only that Vareth is his handler. No evidence of departure, but no confirmation of continued proximity either.  
- “Grygum school” is named by Vareth, but it is unclear if others recognize or use this term — it may be entirely his personal construct.
