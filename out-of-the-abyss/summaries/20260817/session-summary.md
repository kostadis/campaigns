# Session 2026-08-17

Date: 2026-08-17

## Summary
With the battle in the High Tower finally over, the party secured the captured A'lai Aivenmore and stripped him of his key to the inner sanctum, quietly passing it to Zalthir before tucking it safely away inside Glabbagool for safekeeping — the gelatinous cube "standing just a little bit taller with pride at the responsibility." Before they could act on their advantage, Manshoon's simulacrum arrived — alone and visibly drained from the enormous magical effort it had taken to reach Candlekeep. He conjured a wall of shimmering force and shattered the magically protected door with overwhelming power, breaching the inner sanctum as though its ancient wards were nothing. A'lai watched in trembling horror as the truth settled over him like a shroud: Manshoon had never come for him. After eleven years of service — a courier in a glove whose face he never saw — he had not even been worth the effort of a single spell. "It was not discretion, it was that I wasn't worth a face!" The GM framed what followed as a race: Manshoon on square one, the party on square zero, and if he reaches square six, he wins.

Broken and desperate, A'lai offered the party everything he had left. He revealed the existence of a secret room beneath Candlekeep holding artifacts of extraordinary power, guarded by a cryptogram he had spent years quietly researching. He knew the answers to two of its riddles — "Fustilugs," the base insult magically enchanted centuries ago under one of the oversized black marble chess pieces on the Philosopher's Court, which successive Keepers of Tomes had quietly ensured was never replaced; and "Bow," the reply of the legendary halfling bard Batbayar's statue when A'lai, late one night, had applauded it and watched it bow deeply from the waist. In exchange for this information, A'lai begged not for freedom but for a cell in Candlekeep's null magic prison, the one place he believed no wizard — not even Manshoon — could reach him (Grygum tactfully declined to mention that Candlekeep's wards had already failed once today). When asked about the forces surrounding Daz, A'lai admitted he could not trace the payments, only the patience behind them, and concluded with quiet certainty that only one house in Menzoberranzan could afford to wait eleven years for nothing: House Baenre. Grygum appealed to the Watcher Tadric, invoking the party's record of service and their deputization by Kalan Strongbranch, and Tadric — after securing a promise of a co-authored monograph — agreed to take A'lai into custody and march him off toward the prison.

The party regrouped in the investigator's office, where A'lai Aivenmore — whose photographic memory allowed him to reproduce the cryptogram in full — spread the riddles across the floor for every sharp mind in the room to examine. Sylvira Savikas arrived shortly after, grateful for the party's efforts and eager to help Candlekeep in its moment of crisis, though too weak from her illness to act herself — and still unaware that her son Moziqodo lay dead at the party's hands. She laughed at the riddles as "almost too easy," provided the true name of Miirym, the Sentinel Wyrm and dragon guardian of Candlekeep — **Vydykyq** — as the answer to the first riddle, and identified the Eastern Light of Mystra's Mantle — **Limniz** — as an obscure star recorded in the charts of the Orrery of the Astronomicon — though she noted with quiet unease that three unfamiliar figures had been inside the Orrery when she passed it. Working together, the party decoded the letter substitution cipher of the cryptogram, with Thorin leading the effort (with an assist from a cryptogram website and a lively argument over whether certain glyphs were an I or a J) to the stunned admiration of every scholar in the room; Grygum, solving in parallel, supplied the one line Thorin had missed. The full text revealed a sequence of instructions: feed the quill of Alaundo the Seer, tread as many steps as he lived in years, utter the original prophecy to unseen ears, sprinkle dust of Mechanus on dormant gears, one last guardian of knowledge remains to verify the chosen Reader's claims, and that those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore may fearlessly pass the obsidian door. Kalan Strongbranch arrived huffing and puffing moments later, eager to contribute a clue he had solved — only to find the party had already cracked the entire cipher, leaving him to recover his composure and insist on being useful anyway by identifying the statue of Alaundo in Founders Court.

The party made their way to Founders Court and the House of Alaundo, where a great brass statue of the ancient seer stood upon a dais, bearing a quill and an empty ink pot worn smooth by centuries of superstitious scholars touching it for luck — the superstition being that touching the quill guarantees a well-critiqued piece of academia. After a brief debate over whether ink, blood, or the poisoned ink sample was the appropriate offering — "I'm not actually evil, so I'm gonna start with ink" — the party borrowed fresh ink from the assembled scholars and poured it into the statue's ink pot. The dais ground forward with a deep mechanical groan, revealing a long, unlit staircase descending into darkness below. Thorin's observation that in all these centuries nobody had thought to put ink in the ink pot drew looks of horror from the scholars: why would anyone deface the thing? To know how many steps to count, the party needed to know how long Alaundo had lived — a fact nobody present knew, and one that turned out not to be on the plaque — and so rather than hunting down the biography *Alaundo, the Wonder Years* in the Hall of Momentous Deeds, they traveled to the grove where Candlekeep's keepers of tomes were buried, finding his grave and learning he had died at the age of ninety-seven.

With the prophecy provided by the scholars who knew it by heart, the party still needed one final ingredient: dust of Mechanus, required to sprinkle on the dormant gears described in the riddle. They made their way to the House of Mechanus, a rectangular building with a blue roof and a clock tower, where the gnome librarian Spanner oversaw thirteen modrons — strange, box-like mechanical creatures from the plane of Mechanus — in their precise and orderly work ("sort of like an OCD person's wet dream"). Spanner explained that the dust was the most perfect lubricant in existence, and that the only ways to obtain it were to kill one of his modrons — absolutely unacceptable, and the party agreed: "we don't kill librarians. They're our friends" — or to use specialized tools his modrons had crafted. Spanner wanted collateral; Thorin pointed out they only needed the dust, not the tools. The party persuaded Spanner to lend them the tools, and he agreed on the condition that Zalthir and Glabbagool remain behind for a study of their mechanical symmetry — an interview Zalthir accepted on the condition of "constructive feedbacks," deferred until after the descent. The modrons then performed a strange, synchronized dance as they deliberated among themselves in a language no one else in the room could begin to understand.

Armed at last with everything they needed, the party descended the hidden staircase beneath Alaundo's statue, counting each step carefully toward the ninety-seventh. Daz positioned himself higher on the stairs for a clear line of sight, while the others spread out below, Grygum pouring 44 points of healing into Zalthir, who was down 27 hit points with no short rest taken. Thorin, suspicious of the GM's repeated questions about exact positions, was proved right: without warning, a ten-foot section of the staircase hinged open beneath their feet, catching everyone standing between the 93rd and 102nd steps. Daz, safely above, cast Telekinesis to arrest the fall. Everyone but Thorin caught hold of the stairs and scrambled clear, but Thorin lost his footing entirely and plummeted downward into the vast, dark circular well below — his fate uncertain as the darkness swallowed him whole.

An area within Candlekeep containing oversized black marble chess pieces which "see plenty of use." ### School of the Drama Library
A library within Candlekeep whose entry hall is dominated by a statue of the legendary halfling bard Batbayar, which comes to life and bows deeply from the waist when applauded.

### Orrery of the Astronomicon
A chamber within Candlekeep containing star charts on which the Eastern Light of Mystra's Mantle — the easternmost star of an obscure constellation — can be looked up. Sylvira noticed three suspicious individuals inside who did not appear to work there.

### House of Alaundo
A building in Founders Court whose ground floor stretches to the top with a gallery mezzanine. It features a large brass statue of Alaundo the Seer on a dais, quill tip worn smooth by scholars seeking well-critiqued papers, which conceals a hidden staircase triggered by filling the statue's ink pot.

### Founders Court
A central area in Candlekeep where the House of Alaundo is located, also near the House of Mechanus and the Immortal Chambers.

### The Grove
A burial ground within Candlekeep where the keepers of tomes are laid to rest. The party visits to find Alaundo's grave and learn he died at the age of 97 — a fact not recorded on his statue's plaque.

### Hall of Momentous Deeds
Mentioned as the home of Alaundo's biography, *Alaundo, the Wonder Years* — the alternative source for his age that the party skipped in favor of the grove.

### House of Mechanus
A large rectangular building with a blue roof and a clock tower, situated between the Immortal Chambers and Founders Court. Thirteen box-like mechanical creatures from the plane of Mechanus who work under Spanner in the House of Mechanus. ## Items
### High Tower Key
One of two keys required to access the inner sanctum of Candlekeep. Stripped from A'lai Aivenmore after his capture, it was quietly passed to Zalthir and then stored inside Glabbagool for safekeeping. The party takes care not to bring the two keys together.

### Sapphire Gem
*(Chapter 63 context, recapped by the GM at the top of this session — not a Chapter 64 event.)*
A valuable gem that had been sought by A'lai's assassin, who searched Daz's unconscious body for it only to find nothing — it had long since passed to Grygum's possession.

### Vault Cryptogram
A letter substitution cipher — "like the ones that you used to find in newspapers" — containing the instructions needed to access the Vault of Secrets beneath Candlekeep. Solving the riddle questions supplies letters that feed into the ciphered paragraph. The party successfully decoded it in the investigator's office with help from Sylvira Savikas and Kalan Strongbranch; Thorin led with a cryptogram-solver site and Grygum solved in parallel, recovering the one line Thorin missed ("Utter the original prophecy to unseen ears").

### Black Marble Knight
An oversized black marble chess piece in the Philosopher's Court. The word 'Fustilugs' — the base insult of a Black Knight — is magically etched into its base, serving as one of the answers to the vault's riddles. Successive Keepers of Tomes have quietly ensured the knight is never replaced.

### Statue of Alaundo
A large brass statue of the famous seer Alaundo, bearing a quill and an empty ink pot, mounted on a large dais in the House of Alaundo. Scholars touch the worn quill tip for luck with their papers. When ink is poured into the ink pot, the statue slides forward to reveal a hidden staircase leading to the Vault of Secrets — a trigger nobody in centuries had tripped, because defacing the statue was unthinkable.

### Dust of Mechanus
A legendary and perfect lubricant sourced from the plane of Mechanus, required by the vault's riddle to be sprinkled on dormant gears. Not related to the Dust of Suleiman, and not, as Thorin proposed, Coke. Could be dust from the plane itself or the remains of a slain modron, which turn to dust when they die on the Prime Material. The party negotiates with Spanner at the House of Mechanus to obtain tools to produce it without harming the modrons.

### Modron Tools
Specialized mechanical instruments created by the modrons of the House of Mechanus, lent to the party by Spanner as an alternative means of obtaining Dust of Mechanus without killing any modrons. Spanner initially wanted collateral until Thorin pointed out the party only needed the dust, not the tools.

### Poisoned Ink Sample
A sample the party took earlier from the poisoned ink that killed a scholar. Zalthir proposed using it to feed Alaundo's quill; the party chose fresh borrowed ink instead.

### Potion of Flying
A magical draught belonging to **Zalthir**, concealed inside Glabbagool. Raised as one of Zalthir's options during the positioning discussion before the descent into the hidden staircase.
