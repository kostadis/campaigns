# Chapter 60

Date: 2026-07-27

## Summary
The party stood over a healed Tadric when something else changed — the endless chant of prophecies that had filled Candlekeep without interruption fell suddenly and completely silent, a phenomenon Grygum was the first to notice. It was, apparently, the first time in history that the chanting had ever stopped, stopping Tadric cold in his tracks. Looking around, Tadric realized that Moziqodo was dead. He urgently insisted that they must tell Sylvira about her son's death, but the party deflected, suggesting that telling her they had killed her son was a problem for another moment, and pressed on toward the High Tower with Tadric insisting on accompanying them. Grygum cast a protective blessing over his companions as they moved, and along the way they discovered a dead Watcher on an indoor walkway, killed by a crossbow bolt whose fletching Daz immediately recognized as foreign to Candlekeep — proof that outside attackers had already passed through, and recently.

They arrived at the High Tower lobby to find two Zhentarim raiders looting books from a written manifest, their weapons bloodied, one of them carrying the bow that had killed the guard on the walkway. On a second-floor landing above them, waiting near a sealed door that required the High Tower keys to open, stood A'lai Aivenmore himself. Grygum launched a bolt of radiant energy at the wizard in an attempt to catch him off guard, but A'lai Aivenmore's magical defenses deflected it entirely, and combat erupted in earnest. Zalthir and Glabbagool surged into the fray, delivering punishing blows to the wizard, while Daz conjured a massive hand of compacted soil that erupted from the ground and seized A'lai Aivenmore, crushing him and holding him in place. The wizard burned through his magical reserves trying to resist and escape, ultimately teleporting across the room to free himself, but not before taking significant punishment.

Enraged and cornered, A'lai Aivenmore unleashed a relentless barrage of raw arcane energy, severely wounding Daz and hammering Zalthir with a blow that nearly took the monk off his feet. Then, unexpectedly, the wizard paused. He looked around at the carnage — Bookwyrm dead, Daral nearly killed, Janussi gone, guards slain — and his voice, when it came, was not the voice of a megalomaniac. "I have been at Candlekeep for 31 years," he said. "I do not have a house or a family or a country. I have a chair. He proposed to take the chair." He had not set out to be a murderer, he insisted, and the party could almost believe him. He revealed that he had been secretly removing specific books from the library for eleven years on behalf of a mysterious figure whose courier always arrived wearing a glove over a metal hand. "His name is Manshoon," he said quietly. "You have not heard of it. That is not an accident. It is a career." The party recognized the name immediately — a legendary and feared wizard tied to the Zhentarim, a man who had died more than once and kept returning.

As if to punctuate his confession, A'lai Aivenmore produced the stolen sapphire and made clear he intended to smash it. But Daz was faster, reaching out with an invisible force of will and wrenching the gem directly from the wizard's grasp before he could act. Moving quickly and with practiced sleight of hand, Daz feigned a retreat into the hallway while secretly pressing the sapphire into Grygum's keeping — so that Grygum now held both the real High Tower key — key #2, the one recovered from Tadric — and the artifact, while A'lai Aivenmore seethed with fury at having been outmaneuvered. Robbed of the gem, the wizard turned his fury on Zalthir and battered the monk unconscious, Glabbagool settling protectively on top of his fallen body. Thorin engaged the Zhentarim thugs directly, striking one hard and following up with a menacing display that the hardened thug shrugged off, while Grygum surged forward and unleashed a torrent of caustic acid that washed over both raiders. One died instantly as the corrosive brew melted through him, and the other stood soaked in acid and barely scratched — two points of damage — with the acid still eating at him and the confrontation against A'lai Aivenmore far from over.

## Locations
### Candlekeep
The great library fortress where the party is investigating a conspiracy involving stolen keys, murdered scholars, and ancient protective wards. The endless chant of prophecies that normally fills the keep falls silent during this session — an event that has never happened before.

### North Gallery Walkway
An indoor passage leading toward the High Tower where the party discovered a Watcher killed by a crossbow bolt with foreign fletching, indicating outside attackers had already passed through.

### High Tower Lobby
The entrance to the High Tower of Candlekeep, featuring bookshelves and a second-floor landing. It served as the site of the session's climactic confrontation, with Zhentarim raiders looting books from a manifest while A'lai Aivenmore waited above near a sealed door requiring the High Tower keys.

## NPCs
### Tadric
Kalan's lieutenant, freshly healed as the session opens. Shaken but emboldened by his survival — "I faced Moziqodo!" — he insists on accompanying the party to the High Tower, and it is he who says someone must tell Sylvira that her son is dead.

### Moziqodo
Sylvira's demonspawn son, killed before this session opens. He is referenced twice: as the death Sylvira has not yet been told about, and as the arrival A'lai Aivenmore is still waiting for at the sealed door.

### A'lai Aivenmore
A powerful drow wizard and long-time scholar of Candlekeep who orchestrated the conspiracy to protect his academic position. He revealed he has been secretly funneling specific books out of the library for 11 years on behalf of Manshoon and the Zhentarim. He is self-preserving, desperate, and willing to use lethal magic, but also capable of a surprisingly sympathetic monologue about losing his only home.

### Manshoon
A legendary and feared wizard associated with the Zhentarim and Zhentil Keep, identified by A'lai Aivenmore as the true architect behind the book thefts. His courier always wears a glove over a metal hand. The party recognized the name and noted he has died more than once.

### Glabbagool
An intelligent gelatinous ooze and loyal companion to the party. During the battle at the High Tower, Glabbagool assisted in combat alongside Zalthir and stood over the fallen monk to protect him after he was knocked unconscious.

### Zhentarim Raiders
Mercenaries working for the Zhentarim, found looting the High Tower's lobby and bagging specific books from a written manifest. Their weapons were bloodied, and one carried the bow used to kill the Watcher on the walkway. One was killed instantly by Grygum's caustic brew.

## Items
### Dawnbringer
Thorin's sentient radiant sword. When the party looked for a way to destroy the sapphire, the GM ruled Dawnbringer could not be used to smash it — prompting Thorin to offer the hilt instead ("she wouldn't mind").

### Real High Tower Key
The genuine artifact required to manage the wards of Candlekeep. This is High Tower key **#2**; key #1 is still in A'lai Aivenmore's possession, and the sealed door on the second-floor landing needs both. Key #2 is now held by Grygum.

### Foreign Crossbow Bolt
A crossbow bolt found in the body of a dead Watcher on an indoor walkway. Daz identified its fletching as foreign to Candlekeep and inconsistent with the bolts used by the Watchers, confirming the attackers came from outside the keep.

### Written Manifest
A list used by the Zhentarim raiders to identify specific books to be looted from the High Tower's collection, suggesting the thefts were carefully directed by an outside party.

### Sapphire Artifact
A magical gem held by A'lai Aivenmore, which he produced and threatened to smash to trigger a specific effect. Before he could destroy it, Daz used telekinesis to wrench it from his grasp. It was then secretly passed to Grygum via sleight of hand, so that Grygum now holds both the real key (#2) and the sapphire.

## Spells
### Bless
Cast by Grygum on his companions as they approached the High Tower, granting each of them a 1d4 to add to attack rolls and saving throws. It held for most of the battle but was dropped at the very end, when Grygum needed his concentration for Tasha's Caustic Brew.

### Guiding Bolt
Launched by Grygum at A'lai Aivenmore in an attempt to surprise the wizard, but the bolt failed to penetrate his mage armor and had no effect.

### Mage Armor
A protective magical force surrounding A'lai Aivenmore that shielded him from Grygum's initial guiding bolt attack.

### Maximilian's Earthen Grasp
Cast twice by Daz during the confrontation. The first casting was negated by A'lai Aivenmore's counterspell. The second casting succeeded — the wizard failed his saving throw, took significant bludgeoning damage, and was restrained by a hand of compacted soil rising from the ground, forcing him to burn Misty Step to escape.

### Counterspell
Used by A'lai Aivenmore as a reaction to negate Daz's first casting of Maximilian's Earthen Grasp, consuming one of his spell slots and preventing him from casting other powerful spells that turn.

### Misty Step
Used by A'lai Aivenmore to teleport across the room and escape the restraining earthen hand after being caught by Daz's second casting of Maximilian's Earthen Grasp.

### Arcane Burst
A cantrip used repeatedly by A'lai Aivenmore to strike multiple party members with bursts of raw arcane energy. He unleashed several of these in quick succession, severely wounding Daz and ultimately knocking Zalthir unconscious.

### Telekinesis
Cast by Daz to exert mental force on the sapphire artifact held by A'lai Aivenmore. The wizard failed his strength saving throw, and the gem was wrenched from his grasp and pulled into Daz's hand, preventing A'lai Aivenmore from smashing it.

### Tasha's Caustic Brew
Unleashed by Grygum in a stream of acid that coated both Zhentarim thugs. Both failed the DC 17 dexterity saving throw, and at the start of their turns the acid burned through them for 2d4 — 8 on the first, killing him outright, and only 2 on the second, who ended the session still standing, coated, and taking 2d4 at the start of each of its turns. Concentrating on this spell cost Grygum his Bless.
