Tadric was on his feet and breathing evenly. The healing had taken. It usually does when I'm the one applying it, and I don't advertise why.

He pressed the second key into our hands before anyone asked for it.

"I held it all night, and I didn't know what it was. Now that I know what it is, I'm not giving it to you and going to bed."

Fair. Wrong, but fair.

"You could be helpful," I said. "It's also dangerous." Both things were true. I said them in the order that was kindest.

"I faced Moziqodo! I faced the full… I… no, I, I, I will—"

The desperate desire to be heroic was all over him. I've seen it on acolytes. I've seen it on prisoners. You don't argue with it. You manage it.

"Well, come along. I do have resurrection magic if worst comes to worst."

He stared at me. "If Daral Yashenti was here—" and then, "But what about me?"

I answered the part I understood. "Well, I didn't have it then."

Theologically airtight. Judging by his face, not the comfort he was after.

And then the keep did something I had never heard it do.

The acoustics of Candlekeep carry sound a long way: stone corridors, high vaults, everything echoes into everything. What they carried to us, from far off, was an absence. The Endless Chant had stopped.

"Was this ever mentioned? I don't remember this."

"I mentioned it the first day we were there," Daz said. "Maybe."

He may have. Here is the honest accounting: I hadn't noticed the chant for the last three days. That is how a constant works on the mind. It becomes the room. Once it stopped, I noticed that it had stopped, the way you notice a heartbeat by its skipping.

The Avowed read the prophecies of Alaundo continuously. When a prophecy comes true, they delete it, and they keep reading. Which meant a silence had exactly two explanations. Either every prophecy left on the list had come true tonight, or nobody was left to read them.

Neither explanation improved with reflection. I put it in my notes anyway. Bahamut may or may not have been listening. The chant certainly wasn't.

Tadric had gone still. "This never happens," he said, and he said it like a man watching water run uphill.

Then his eyes found Moziqodo's body, and he arrived at the other problem.

"Sylvira Savikas… someone needs to tell Sylvira Savikas that her son is dead."

He looked at the four of us.

"Oh. Well. Ugh."

There was an awkward silence, and then, one at a time, the four of us looked at Dawnbringer. The news had several complicating features. We were most of them.

"It might best be someone who knows Sylvira Savikas," I said.

Daz picked it up smoothly — whoever told her would want to be empathetic, would have the best understanding of how to be empathetic to her as they explained this unfortunate news. It was passing the buck. We all knew it was passing the buck. Daz made it sound like pastoral care, which is a skill I respect professionally.

"I'm happy to, you know, back you up, but… I'll go with you," I told Tadric. An offer of support for a task no one was performing tonight costs very little. I made it sincerely. Both things can be true.

"We'll take care of it later," Daz said.

"Yeah," I said. "It's a later… it's a later—"

It was a later kind of problem. A'lai Aivenmore, up in the High Tower with the first key, waiting on a second key that was now in our possession — that was a now kind of problem. A mother's grief keeps. A man with one key and an appointment does not.

My old teacher used to interrupt at the worst possible moment to ask what, exactly, I thought I was measuring. Standing in a keep that had gone silent for the first time in centuries, holding a dead man's key, I could have told him: the tower. We were measuring the tower.

So we went. Tadric, fortunately, knew where the High Tower was, and he took the lead, walking a little too fast, a little too upright, a man determined to be in the story.

"I'll cast Bless," I said, "on the three of us who are here."

A small request for Bahamut's attention, made politely, spread as far as it goes. He may introduce himself properly one of these days. In the meantime, the warmth settled over us, and we followed Tadric into a keep that had, for the first time since Alaundo, nothing at all to say.
