The watcher had died with an arrow in him, so we went up quietly.

The Avowed called this room the Skyscraping Lobby. For once a name was honest: shelves climbing into lamplight, a staircase along the wall, a second-floor landing, more open air than any room needs. Months of tunnel ceilings teach you that vertical rooms are honest about nothing except how far you can fall.

"Skyscraping?" Grygum whispered.

Then, working it out: "Oh, okay, it's the… the… yes. The entrance. Basically."

He was not wrong. A grand mouth with valuables in it and no honest cover.

Two raiders were working the shelves. One held a written manifest; the other pulled books and bagged them. Their swords had blood on them. They weren't looting. They were shopping. Someone had written that list, and the list was the interesting part.

"I suppose they haven't noticed us yet," Grygum said. They hadn't.

Up the stairs, on the landing, a wizard stood in front of a sealed door. We knew him. A'lai Aivenmore.

"Oh," Grygum breathed. "This is the guy who has the key."

"He is through the second door?"

He was not. The door behind him was still sealed, and he stood in front of it the way you stand at a gate waiting for a runner. He needed the second key. The second key was coming with Moziqodo. The other key was in Daz's pocket.

"Oh, okay," Grygum said. "So we have the other key."

I watched the wizard wait. Unhurried. Comfortable. The most dangerous person in a room isn't the one with the blade drawn. It's the one who has already decided they've won. A'lai Aivenmore had decided.

Daz, low: "So, it's safe to assume that one of these people that's in front of us is one of the people that shot the guard."

I looked again. A bow, slung across the second raider's back, half under the bag strap. It had been there the whole time and I had walked my eyes past it. There was a monastery class on inventorying a room before your foot crosses its threshold. Brother Aldas taught it — or possibly Brother Harren. I remember the light through the shutters. Not the content.

"Okay, well that clears up a mystery," Grygum said. "Good."

Then: "I'm more worried about the wizard than about these raiders. We're probably more than a match for them."

Right on both counts. Everything we had heard made A'lai Aivenmore a powerful wizard. And powerful wizards are more obsessed with staying alive than with fighting to the death. It is the most reliable thing about them.

"Just like dragons," Grygum said.

Thorin squinted up at the landing. "So is he the one with the third eye?"

The wizard had two. Nobody asked where Thorin had met one with three.

"One thing we could do, guys," Grygum said, "is try to eliminate the raiders so that we can focus on A'lai Aivenmore — so I could cast some kind of spell to try to get them in one spell casting. Because I suspect that we just need to fight them and kill them."

Daz said, "How tall is the wizard?"

"He wasn't expecting that question," Grygum murmured. Nobody had been.

Somewhere between five and six feet, by my eye. "Human," Daz said. Then: "I had a plan for maybe boxing — literally boxing the wizard in — while we took care of the other guys, just to make sure we were clear. But I think I'll wait on that. Maybe use it for something else. I like the plan of knocking down the DPS towards us by getting the two weaker guys out of the way."

"Excellent," Grygum said.

My concern was neither the raiders nor the wizard. From the doorway I could not see under the landing. I could not see behind the far shelves or the blind side of the stair. Rooms like this are never as empty as their visible portion, and the empty portion of this one was large. Grygum followed my eyes into the corners and said, "Oh."

The wizard stood ten, fifteen feet above the floor. He would see us the moment we crossed the threshold. He was watching the main door for Moziqodo, expecting him to run in at any point with the key.

"Well, he's going to be pretty surprised by us, then," Grygum said.

Glabbagool stirred against my left forearm. "Is this where we use the flying spell?"

I didn't answer. The staircase reached the landing, and there was shadow enough on the wall below it. I had cheaper ways up. The enthusiasm was new, though. I marked it.

Grygum was working his geometry. "So, with the spells that I've got, I could get both the thugs if I could get at the end of a line of them. Or I could target any one of the people in the room. They're not lined up for a good aim from the door, though. So maybe I should aim for A'lai Aivenmore instead, and then the rest of us can work on the thugs? What do you think, guys?"

"So what was the plan again?" Thorin said.

"Target A'lai Aivenmore instead of the thugs."

"Who? Me? You?"

"Me with my spell. I'm assuming that I can get a spell off before everybody realizes that we're there."

"Yeah, and I'm happy to sort of distract and try and kite the thugs a little bit."

Sound economy. Remove the cheap pieces first; isolate the expensive one. I said nothing, which was approval.

"So, should I try to cast a spell now?" Grygum said. "I was thinking Guiding Bolt, toward A'lai Aivenmore."

He held his breath and leaned into the doorway. The wizard hadn't noticed us. He'd heard feet on the stairs and thought nothing of it, because he was expecting feet on the stairs. The bolt of radiance crossed the lobby and broke a hand's width from the wizard's chest, scattering against a shimmer of warding. Mage armor. He had come dressed.

"I sort of jumped the gun on that," Grygum muttered. "Oops."

Beside me, Daz was already weighing the next thing — some working that would set the wizard's mind against his own craft. He studied the man on the landing, the composure, the warding he had worn into the room before anyone gave him a reason to, and let it go. A mind like that does not falter often enough to bet on.

"Alright," Daz said. "I'll probably not cast that unless we get desperate."

Desperate keeps its own schedule. I found a patch of shadow deep enough to step through, and waited for the room to answer.
