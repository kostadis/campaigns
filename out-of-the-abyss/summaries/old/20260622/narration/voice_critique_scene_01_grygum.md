## Flags

### [1] em-dash

> The talk had gone well. Extraordinarily popular, as I was informed by several people on my way out — each one delivering the assessment in tones normally reserved for resurrection announcements.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; a period break keeps the paratactic rhythm the spec calls for.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The talk had gone well. Extraordinarily popular, as I was informed by several people on my way out. Each one delivered the assessment in tones normally reserved for resurrection announcements."

### [2] em-dash

> I found the others in a state that suggested they, too, had had a productive afternoon — though *productive* was doing some work in that sentence.

**Why:** Clause is a light qualifier, not a break in thought; a comma reads more like Grygum's own examples.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...had had a productive afternoon, though *productive* was doing some work in that sentence."

### [3] em-dash

> Thorin explained. The book had delivered the poison to Janussi, and Daral had taken it back — as men who give gifts sometimes do when the recipient fails to appear sufficiently grateful.

**Why:** The clause after the dash is explanatory, not a hard pivot; comma suffices.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...and Daral had taken it back, as men who give gifts sometimes do when the recipient fails to appear sufficiently grateful."

### [4] register-wrong vocabulary

> There was a kind of moral geometry to it: Daral had been so precise about how the poison reached someone else that he'd administered it to himself with equal precision.

**Why:** "Geometry" is exactly the analytical/mathematical default the mechanical scan targets. Grygum's spec vocabulary for irony is plain and moral, not spatial ("He sees the true shape of a thing and accepts it" is as abstract as the spec gets, and "shape" ≠ "geometry" — the spec never reaches for math terms).
**Suggested rewrite:** "There was a kind of justice in it: Daral had been so careful about how the poison reached someone else that he'd delivered it to himself with the same care."

### [5] register-wrong vocabulary

> The flattery adjusted its angle of approach.

**Why:** "Angle of approach" is a targeting/ballistics metaphor — abstract and tactical-clinical in a way the spec's examples never are. Grygum's read-people instinct (WIS 20, Insight +8) is rendered in the spec through plain, direct sensory observation, not geometry.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The flattery tried a different opening line."

### [6] em-dash

> He was operating on an instinct he couldn't quite articulate — that the one with the Lesser Restoration wasn't ready to spend it, and that arguing wouldn't accelerate the timeline.

**Why:** The dash introduces an explanation of the instinct; a colon reads more precisely and drops the parenthetical feel.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He was operating on an instinct he couldn't quite articulate: that the one with the Lesser Restoration wasn't ready to spend it, and that arguing wouldn't speed anything up."

### [7] register-wrong vocabulary

> ...and that arguing wouldn't accelerate the timeline.

**Why:** "Accelerate the timeline" is project-management vocabulary — nobody in this party, least of all a Low-INT priest who reasons from observation rather than abstraction, would reach for it.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...and that arguing wouldn't make the healing come any sooner."

### [8] voice spec conflict — sentence rhythm

> He came in the way people do when they've been waiting in the corridor working themselves up — that quality of entrance that's trying not to be a dramatic entrance while being entirely a dramatic entrance.

**Why:** The spec is explicit: sentences average about eight words and are paratactic, coordinate clauses chained with "and," not nested subordinate constructions like this one. This sentence is a single 40-word essayistic unit built on self-referential abstraction ("that quality of entrance that's trying not to be..."), which reads like a different, more literary narrator.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He came in like a man who'd been rehearsing in the corridor. Trying not to make an entrance. Making one anyway."

### [9] generic fantasy prose / house-style convergence

> There is a moment — I have seen it before, in different forms, with different people — when someone realizes that the thing they came to present is about to be handed to a gelatinous cube.

**Why:** "There is a moment..." is a stock literary-essay opener that could sit in any narrator's section; nothing here is Grygum-specific until the last four words. Also carries a parenthetical em-dash pair (mechanical scan).
**Suggested rewrite:** "I've seen this before, in different forms, with different people: someone realizes the thing they came to present is about to be handed to a gelatinous cube."

### [10] em-dash

> Glabbagool examined them with professional detachment — the focused, slightly acidic attention he gives to anything he's evaluating for nutritional content.

**Why:** Comma reads cleaner and matches the plainer rhythm of the surrounding Glabbagool material, which is otherwise some of the strongest voice work in the scene.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Glabbagool examined them with professional detachment, the focused, slightly acidic attention he gives to anything he's evaluating for nutritional content."

### [11] em-dash + register-wrong vocabulary + generic simile

> Fembris was visibly, structurally crushed — the way a person is when they've come to a producer's office with a script and been told to leave it with the secretary, and the secretary is a gelatinous entity who has just declared their work inedible.

**Why:** "Structurally" is a direct mechanical-scan hit (register B: engineering vocabulary applied to a person's feelings). The "producer's office" comparison is also a modern show-business idiom that appears nowhere in the spec or examples — it's a stock workshop simile grafted onto a medieval fantasy setting, not something grounded in Grygum's own frame of reference (Bahamut commentary, priestly training, note-taking, cataloging).
**Suggested rewrite:** "Fembris looked like a man who'd brought his life's work to an audience and had it handed off to the help — and the help was a gelatinous cube that had just declared the work inedible." *(Even here, prefer breaking the dash: "...and had it handed off to the help. The help was a gelatinous cube that had just declared the work inedible.")*

### [12] em-dash

> He suggested a duograph. I allowed that this was possible — a dual log, perhaps, something in that register.

**Why:** The dash introduces a definition/elaboration; a colon is more precise than a dash here.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I allowed that this was possible: a dual log, perhaps, something in that register."

### [13] em-dash

> A'lai had shown up at the Oak Tree Apothecary at half past two on the day of the murder, asking Leuwin for fresh fennel. Indigestion. While Leuwin was helping A'lai in the garden, a dragonborn had arrived — a huge, lizard-skinned figure, per the testimony of Nibbles the squirrel, who had witnessed things Leuwin had missed.

**Why:** Appositive; comma is sufficient and matches the plain descriptive rhythm elsewhere in the paragraph.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...a dragonborn had arrived, a huge, lizard-skinned figure, per the testimony of Nibbles the squirrel, who had witnessed things Leuwin had missed."

### [14] em-dash

> Zalthir had caught that this referred to the apothecary intrusion, not whoever had later removed the heart and looted the safe — two separate acts, two separate threads.

**Why:** Summary clause; colon reads as a cleaner "here's the tally" beat, closer to Grygum's cataloging habit.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...not whoever had later removed the heart and looted the safe: two separate acts, two separate threads."

### [15] em-dash

> The locket can't be taken from someone alive and in possession of it — that's just how the enchantment works.

**Why:** The clause restates/explains the rule; a period reads as more declarative and matches Grygum's flat, closed-file cadence ("Your divine mission is now over.").
**Suggested rewrite:** "The locket can't be taken from someone alive and in possession of it. That's just how the enchantment works."

### [16] em-dash

> Thorin proposed that one person could have done both — stolen the locket, then taken the sapphire to close off pursuit.

**Why:** Colon introduces the elaboration more precisely than a dash.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Thorin proposed that one person could have done both: stolen the locket, then taken the sapphire to close off pursuit."

### [17] voice spec conflict

> The locket can't be taken from someone alive and in possession of it — that's just how the enchantment works. The sapphire is what you'd use to recover the locket if it had ended up somewhere else. So if you already had the locket, you didn't need the sapphire. And if you needed the sapphire to recover the locket, you didn't have it yet.

**Why:** The spec is direct about this: "Low INT (−1)... He reasons from what he observed, what Bahamut implied, and what the notes say — not from abstract principle. He's right about people and wrong about puzzles." This passage has Grygum narrating a clean, self-contained logical proof (mutual exclusion of two conditions) with total confidence — precisely the kind of puzzle-solving the spec says isn't his strength. If this deduction belongs to anyone, the spec's own framing suggests it should be Thorin's confusion that Grygum merely observes and notes, not a chain of logic Grygum resolves himself.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Thorin walked it through twice and still couldn't square it. I wrote down what he had: you can't steal a locket off someone who's alive and holding it. You need the sapphire to find a locket that's already lost. So whoever had one didn't need the other — I didn't try to work out which. That's Thorin's kind of puzzle, not mine."

### [18] register-wrong vocabulary

> I added the safe to the active list. We had not brought a gelatinous cube this far through the Underdark to discover his strategic capabilities in a retrospective discussion about a safe we still hadn't opened.

**Why:** This sentence is a direct echo of the spec's own quoted line — "We had not brought a gelatinous cube this far through the Underdark to lose him to biting insects" (spec: "wry, possessive, tactical, and quietly tender, all in one breath") — but "strategic capabilities" and "retrospective discussion" dilute the concrete, tender directness of the template into bureaucratic filler. Also "active list" trends the same direction.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I added the safe to the list. We had not brought a gelatinous cube this far through the Underdark to find out he could eat through walls in the middle of a conversation about a safe we still hadn't opened."

## Verdict

The sapphire/locket deduction (flag 17) is the sharpest problem: it hands Grygum a flawless logical proof the voice spec explicitly says isn't his to give ("he's right about people and wrong about puzzles"), and it's compounded by a run of analytical-bureaucratic vocabulary — geometry, angle of approach, structurally, accelerate the timeline, strategic capabilities, retrospective discussion — that keeps surfacing exactly where the spec's plain, concrete idiom should be. The Glabbagool and Fembris comic material is otherwise close to the mark. This scene is worth a targeted register pass on the connective narration prose (13 em-dashes, the vocabulary list above, and flag 17's logic passage) rather than a full re-narration.
