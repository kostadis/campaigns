## Flags

### [1] em-dash

> The chambers were quiet. Not the comfortable quiet of a well-loved library — Candlekeep has plenty of those — but the particular stillness of a room that had stopped being someone's.

**Why:** Parenthetical aside; a comma pair is plainer and keeps the paratactic rhythm.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Not the comfortable quiet of a well-loved library, Candlekeep has plenty of those, but the particular stillness of a room that had stopped being someone's."

### [2] generic fantasy prose

> The chambers were quiet. Not the comfortable quiet of a well-loved library — Candlekeep has plenty of those — but the particular stillness of a room that had stopped being someone's.

**Why:** A well-crafted atmospheric opener, but a generic literary one — the "quiet that isn't the comfortable kind" scene-setting move could belong to any narrator in the document. Nothing in it is anchored to Grygum's note-taking frame or plain diction until the scene moves on to the wrapping paper.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The chambers were quiet. Not library-quiet. The quiet of a room that had stopped being someone's."

### [3] register-wrong vocabulary

> But Bahamut's domain is, among other things, the preservation of truth — and a cantrip that restores things to their original state is, when you think about it, a very precise instrument for finding out what a thing originally was.

**Why:** "A very precise instrument" is clinical-mechanical framing (the same register family as "calculated," "configured," "structural" on the scan list) — abstract engineering language for what is, in the spec's own terms, a matter of faith and habit, not precision tooling.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...and a cantrip that restores things to their original state is, when you think about it, a pretty good way to find out what a thing used to be."

### [4] em-dash

> But Bahamut's domain is, among other things, the preservation of truth — and a cantrip that restores things to their original state is, when you think about it, a very precise instrument for finding out what a thing originally was.

**Why:** The clause is a continuation of the thought rather than a break; a comma matches the flow better.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...the preservation of truth, and a cantrip that restores things to their original state is..."

### [5] em-dash

> There it was. A clean cut, straight as a rule, separate from all the ragged tears. Someone had taken something sharp to this paper — and then wrapped it back around the contents, and tied the bow, and handed it to Janussi, or left it on a table, or had it carried by someone who had no idea what they were carrying.

**Why:** The dash splits an "and"-chained list that the spec explicitly favors as Grygum's connective tissue; the list flows better without an interruption.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Someone had taken something sharp to this paper, and then wrapped it back around the contents, and tied the bow, and handed it to Janussi..."

### [6] em-dash + register-wrong vocabulary / house-style convergence

> Someone had planned this with enough patience to acquire the poison, to identify the target, to arrange the presentation, to re-wrap the package cleanly enough that a Keeper of Candlekeep — a scholar who had spent a lifetime reading things carefully — had not noticed.

**Why:** The parallel infinitive list ("to acquire... to identify... to arrange... to re-wrap") is procedural, checklist-style construction — closer to a tactical debrief or police report than Grygum's "and"-chained paratactic sentences. "Identify the target" and "arrange the presentation" are the same bureaucratic-tactical register the scan targets elsewhere in this session's narration.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Someone had planned this: got the poison, picked the target, wrapped it up clean enough that a Keeper of Candlekeep, a scholar who'd spent a lifetime reading things carefully, never noticed."

### [7] em-dash

> I took notes. The straight cut. The two sets of tears, the deliberate and the incidental. The fact that someone had walked out of Janussi's rooms — or never entered them at all — carrying discarded wrapping paper they felt was safe to throw away in the dining hall, because who looks at trash?

**Why:** Parenthetical alternative; commas keep the fragment-heavy cataloging rhythm that otherwise works well in this paragraph.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The fact that someone had walked out of Janussi's rooms, or never entered them at all, carrying discarded wrapping paper..."

## Verdict

This scene is the strongest of the three for voice fidelity — the closing lines ("Bahamut may or may not have been watching. I took notes either way") pull a catchphrase verbatim from the spec, "I said it flatly, which is how I say things when the thing I'm saying is worse than the voice I'm using to say it" is genuinely on-model, and "crunchy-granola" is exactly the kind of flat anachronistic-slang joke the spec documents at the table. The remaining issues are mechanical (5 em-dash instances) and a small cluster of clinical/procedural vocabulary drift (flags 3 and 6) rather than deep characterization problems.
