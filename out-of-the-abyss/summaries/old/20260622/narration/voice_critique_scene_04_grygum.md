## Flags

### [1] em-dash

> Janussi's chambers. I'd been in the lower floors — we all had — but the third story had sat there like an unopened file, and there was still the matter of the wrapping paper.

**Why:** Parenthetical aside; commas keep it inside the sentence without the essayistic dash-interruption.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I'd been in the lower floors, we all had, but the third story had sat there like an unopened file..."

### [2] em-dash

> It wasn't shock exactly. More like the mild distaste of walking into a room where someone has arranged their private life to be very comfortable, and that private life is not yours to judge, and you are still faintly offended on behalf of the general concept of institutional propriety. I poked around — maybe a little cautiously, because you never know if you're going to find something you'd rather not — but I looked.

**Why:** Parenthetical pair around a qualifier; a comma pair is plainer.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I poked around, maybe a little cautiously, because you never know if you're going to find something you'd rather not, but I looked."

### [3] voice spec conflict — sentence rhythm + register-wrong vocabulary

> It wasn't shock exactly. More like the mild distaste of walking into a room where someone has arranged their private life to be very comfortable, and that private life is not yours to judge, and you are still faintly offended on behalf of the general concept of institutional propriety.

**Why:** One 40-plus-word sentence stacking three "and"-clauses of increasing abstraction, capped by "the general concept of institutional propriety" — bureaucratic-academic phrasing far outside Grygum's plain vocabulary. The spec's sentence average is about eight words, paratactic, not this kind of accumulating subordinate clause.
**Suggested rewrite:** "It wasn't shock exactly. More like walking in on someone's private arrangements and feeling vaguely rude about noticing them."

### [4] cliché / on-the-nose simile

> That's the thing about investigations. Once you stop being surprised that people leave evidence of their own intentions lying around, the work gets considerably easier. Documents are just thoughts that got impatient. Janussi clearly had thoughts.

**Why:** "Documents are just thoughts that got impatient" is a workshopped, too-clever aphorism — the kind of clever wordplay-metaphor the examples don't show Grygum reaching for. His actual epigrams are flat and plain ("I took notes. It's what I do." / "That's not a lock. It's a law.").
**Suggested rewrite:** "Once you stop being surprised that people leave evidence of their own intentions lying around, the work gets considerably easier. Janussi wrote down what he thought. People do that."

### [5] em-dash

> I've read enough Bahamut commentary — the kind written by scholars who've spent forty years sharpening the same argument against a rival they've never met — to recognize the genre.

**Why:** Parenthetical aside; commas would do the same work without the essayistic interruption.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I've read enough Bahamut commentary, the kind written by scholars who've spent forty years sharpening the same argument against a rival they've never met, to recognize the genre."

### [6] voice spec conflict — MAJOR

> Academic insults have a specific grammar. They don't call you wrong. They call you *not worth engaging with*. They situate your work as beneath serious response. They imply that entertaining your ideas would constitute an intellectual charity the author cannot afford to extend.

**Why:** This is sustained literary-critical rhetoric analysis ("specific grammar," "situate your work," "intellectual charity") — the register of a literature professor deconstructing prose style. The spec is explicit that Grygum's Arcana/Religion +7 comes from rote priestly training (Thaumaturge), not intellectual breadth, and that his Investigation is −1: "He's right about people and wrong about puzzles." Parsing an insult's rhetorical architecture this precisely is a different character's competence — it reads like Kalan or an omniscient narrator, not the man whose narration voice is "paratactic... a constant low hum of cataloging," not literary theory.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Academic insults don't call you wrong. They just don't call you anything — they write around you like you're not worth the argument. I've read enough of this kind of letter to know Janussi was good at it."

### [7] em-dash

> What came through — with precise and brutal efficiency — was that Janussi had reviewed Kalan Strongbranch's proposal for new criminal investigation methodologies, found it to be solipsism, and had blocked it.

**Why:** Parenthetical adverbial; a comma pair is simpler and closer to the plain reporting tone elsewhere in the scene.
**Suggested rewrite:** "What came through, precise and brutal, was that Janussi had reviewed Kalan Strongbranch's proposal for new criminal investigation methodologies, found it to be solipsism, and had blocked it."

### [8] generic fantasy prose

> Not because it was ambiguous. Because it was so specifically targeted that it took a moment to appreciate the craft of it.

**Why:** "Appreciate the craft of it" continues the literary-critic voice bleed from flag 6 — evaluating prose as *craft* is a writer's-workshop framing, not something grounded in the spec or examples.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Not because it was ambiguous. Because it took a second to see how precisely it was aimed."

### [9] em-dash

> He'd characterized Kalan's entire framework as a vanity project — as though Kalan had constructed an elaborate methodology for no purpose other than to make himself feel like a detective.

**Why:** The clause after the dash explains/restates the accusation; a colon is more exact.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He'd characterized Kalan's entire framework as a vanity project: as though Kalan had built an elaborate rulebook for no purpose other than to make himself feel like a detective."

### [10] house-style convergence

> That's a particular kind of cruelty. Not the cruelty that shouts. The cruelty that dismisses. The kind that doesn't bother with anger because it doesn't consider you worth being angry at.

**Why:** A generic literary meditation on "kinds of cruelty" — well-constructed, but it could sit in any narrator's section of the document. Nothing anchors it to Grygum specifically (no note-taking frame, no Bahamut reference, no tactical observation).
**Suggested rewrite:** "Not the cruelty that shouts. The cruelty that doesn't bother being angry at you. I filed it under the second kind."

### [11] em-dash

> And it had worked. Kalan's entire professional project — the work he'd apparently staked his reputation and career on — buried under one man's contemptuous signature.

**Why:** Parenthetical aside; a comma pair reads plainer.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Kalan's entire professional project, the work he'd apparently staked his reputation and career on, buried under one man's contemptuous signature."

### [12] em-dash

> Kalan's entire proposal for dealing with crime — the Threefold Proof, the methodologies, the framework we'd been handed as a legitimate investigation tool — had been blocked by the man who was now dead.

**Why:** Parenthetical list; comma pair is sufficient.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Kalan's entire proposal for dealing with crime, the Threefold Proof, the methodologies, the framework we'd been handed as a legitimate investigation tool, had been blocked by the man who was now dead."

### [13] em-dash + register-wrong vocabulary

> It was a counterargument to Janussi's contempt, composed with the slow patience of someone who had all the time in the world — because the person who'd told them they were wrong was no longer alive to say so.

**Why:** Comma is cleaner than a dash for the causal clause. Separately, "But it makes him a man who had been destroyed and rebuilt himself around the destruction" (the sentence before this one) is pop-psychology/therapy framing — abstract psychoanalysis of Kalan's inner life rather than the concrete, observation-based read the spec attributes to Grygum's WIS 20 ("he notices lying correctly... those notes are precise").
**Suggested rewrite (dash):** "...composed with the slow patience of someone who had all the time in the world, because the person who'd told them they were wrong was no longer alive to say so."
**Suggested rewrite (the "destroyed and rebuilt" line):** "That doesn't make him a murderer. But it means he built his whole methodology in the wreckage of the man who told him he was wrong. I noted that."

## Verdict

Flag 6 — "Academic insults have a specific grammar... They imply that entertaining your ideas would constitute an intellectual charity the author cannot afford to extend" — is a full paragraph of literary-critical rhetoric analysis that reads like a different, more erudite narrator entirely, and it directly contradicts the spec's Low-INT / "wrong about puzzles" characterization along with the mandated short paratactic sentence rhythm. This is the single most voice-broken passage across all three scenes reviewed. Combined with 8 em-dash instances and a recurring drift into bureaucratic-academic vocabulary ("institutional propriety," "appreciate the craft," "destroyed and rebuilt himself"), this short scene is worth re-narrating rather than spot-editing — roughly a third of it is written in a register Grygum's spec doesn't support.
