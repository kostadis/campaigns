## Flags

### [1] Mechanical — em-dash (also see [13] for the simile itself)

> The confession had settled into the room like dust after a collapse — slowly, thoroughly, covering everything.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; see flag [13] for the deeper problem with this sentence's imagery — the punctuation fix alone doesn't resolve it.
**Suggested rewrite (punctuation only):** "The confession had settled into the room like dust after a collapse, slowly, thoroughly, covering everything." *(See [13] for a full rewrite that also fixes the imagery.)*

### [10] Register-wrong vocabulary (Mechanical Scan B)

> Alkrist had given us the shape of it: A'lai as the architect, himself as the instrument, and at least two others swept up in the machinery.

**Why:** "Shape" is reserved elsewhere in the spec for Daz's Sculpt Spells signature — "the shaped fireball," "the capacity for precision in destruction." Using it generically here, stacked with "architect / instrument / machinery," pulls the sentence into a construction/engineering register that isn't Daz's default frame — his is audit and currency, not mechanism.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Alkrist had given us the accounting of it: A'lai as the one who set it in motion, himself as the hand that carried it out, and at least two others swept into his orbit."

### [11] Register-wrong vocabulary (Mechanical Scan B)

> But the shape was this: A'lai does not pay in gold, and does not pay in books.

**Why:** Same dilution of the Sculpt Spells-reserved "shape" vocabulary into a generic "gist of it" use.
**Suggested rewrite:** "But the terms were these: A'lai does not pay in gold, and does not pay in books."

### [12] Register-wrong vocabulary (Mechanical Scan B)

> The alignment of incentives was almost too clean.

**Why:** "Alignment" is generic systems/business-analysis vocabulary that doesn't appear anywhere in Daz's established lexicon (audit, tally, account, column, balance, debt, leverage, market value); it reads as an LLM default rather than a deliberate word choice.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The incentives ran the same direction, and that was almost too convenient to trust."

### [13] Cliché/on-the-nose simile — the scene's opening line

> The confession had settled into the room like dust after a collapse — slowly, thoroughly, covering everything.

**Why:** This is a generic disaster-image simile with no anchor in Daz's established imagery. The examples show his visual vocabulary running to concrete, sensory, often Underdark-specific detail — rocks as "stone teeth," Gracklstugh's "hammers warping metals" and "acrid smoke [that] burns through his sense of smell, which he will remember most" — or to his audit/currency/tactical frame. "Dust after a collapse" is the kind of workshopped, portable simile that could open any narrator's scene in any setting; it isn't observed, it's supplied. It also seeds the paragraph's subsequent construction-metaphor cluster ("architect... instrument... machinery," flagged at [10]), compounding the drift into a register that belongs to a different narrator's voice.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The confession had gone into the account, and there was nothing left to dispute. Alkrist had given us the accounting of it..." *(ties forward to the paragraph's own closing line, "held a rather complete account," instead of introducing an unrelated image system)*

### [14] Repetition/convergence — cross-scene

> ...which had worked *cleanly*... (line 11)
> The alignment of incentives was almost too *clean*. (line 55)
> The intersection was *clean*. (line 79)

**Why:** Combined with scene 05's four narration-level uses of "clean/cleanly" (see that scene's report, flag [22]), this word appears seven times across the two scenes' narration as an unexamined default adjective for "correct/successful/true" — crowding out the spec's own, more specific audit vocabulary (tally, account, balance, debt).
**Suggested rewrite:** Vary per instance — "worked without waste," "almost too convenient to trust," "The intersection didn't need forcing." Reserve "clean" for at most one use per scene.

## Verdict

The scene opens on its weakest line: "settled into the room like dust after a collapse" is a stock literary simile that has nothing to do with how Daz actually sees things, and it drags a cluster of construction-metaphor vocabulary ("architect," "instrument," "machinery," "shape") into the same paragraph — a register conflict, not just a word choice. Everything after that opening is markedly stronger and closely spec-grounded: "the unknown variable in your own accounts," "I noted the placement," and the closing "I had an alternative proposal forming" are direct, well-earned callbacks to Daz's own vocabulary. This is a spot-edit job — replace the opening simile and the two "shape" instances, fix the nine em-dashes, and the scene is close to done.
