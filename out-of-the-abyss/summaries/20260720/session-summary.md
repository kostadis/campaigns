# Chapter 59 The Key is secured.

Date: 2026-07-20

## Summary
The peace of Deneir's Sanctum shattered without warning when three towering helmed horrors — animated suits of plate armor with cold, glowing eyes — smashed through the windows and burst through the door. The constructs moved with terrifying, mechanical purpose, ignoring Thorin, Zalthir, and Grygum almost entirely and converging with singular focus on Daz. It was only mid-battle, as the horrors relentlessly bypassed every other target, that Daz realized with a chill what they were truly after: the mysterious key he carried. He passed it to Grygum and threw himself clear as his companions surged into the fight, and one by one the constructs collapsed into heaps of rust and broken metal — leaving the party standing in the wreckage, breathing hard and deeply unsettled.

What disturbed them most was not the violence itself, but the implication behind it. The helmed horrors were mindless, incapable of thought or speech, and yet they had known exactly who held a key that no detection spell in Candlekeep should have been able to find. The only person who had witnessed the key change hands from Grygum to Daz was Fembris, the acolyte who had been quietly present throughout their stay. Suspicion settled over the group like a cold fog, and Daral — the grateful scholar Dawnbringer had recently cured of his affliction — pressed himself close to Thorin's side, unwilling to be far from the radiant sword's protection.

Daral produced a scroll of Detect Magic and read it aloud to examine the key. Every piece of magical equipment in the room blazed with light — Dawnbringer most brilliantly of all, and Daral, still carrying a residual shimmer from Dawnbringer's cure, "even brighter than usual" — but the key itself remained stubbornly, completely dark. Daral's own conclusion was blunt: "This is not the key. This is not the key." The party finally pieced together the true timeline of events: Janussi had been killed by Alkrist, and Bookwyrm had subsequently gone into Janussi's chambers to protect her nephew and secure the Hightower key. The party realized that Bookwyrm had possessed one of the keys, and that someone else had stolen the sapphire. Whoever stole the sapphire was now after the keys. Before the party could fully process this revelation, an institutional alarm bell began tolling through Candlekeep in a pattern that was not the hour — and a breathless Avowed messenger rushed in with devastating news: Bookwyrm was dead.

The party raced to her office and found a gruesome scene. Her throat had been torn apart with savage, animalistic force — the work of something with claws and teeth, not the surgical precision that had claimed Janussi's life. Her heart, notably, was still in place, though no longer beating. On her desk lay a half-finished note in her own hand, reading simply: *He is using the beast to—* Daz, reaching out with his arcane senses (after a disastrous natural 1 that Grygum helped rescue into a 26), felt something deeply wrong with the magical wards that protected Candlekeep — as though a firewall had been breached, a hidden port quietly forced open. The connection clicked into place: Sylvira's abyssal spawn son, the creature he had struggled to keep contained, was the beast the note referred to.

Daral confirmed what the party already suspected about the wards. The great shield that prevented teleportation and suppressed fire throughout Candlekeep was maintained within the room locked by the two high tower keys. To test whether it still held, Grygum attempted to conjure a small flame with Thaumaturgy onto a piece of paper — and watched it erupt briefly before evaporating into nothing, the ward consuming it instantly in a "Burning Bush kind of situation." The protection was still active, but the situation was deteriorating fast. Bookwyrm had almost certainly possessed one of the real keys, and whoever had torn out her throat had taken it.

The party realized they needed to find the missing key, and that Kalan was the one who knew its location. Unsure of where he was, they first thought to head to the Gatehouse. However, Daz remembered Kalan mentioning he would not be where he normally would be ("if I don't see you tomorrow, do not look for me at my post"), and Daral realized he must be in the Sea Warden's Tower, rallying the troops. The party rushed there, finding Kalan Strongbranch frantically buckling on his armor. When they told him Bookwyrm was dead and the key was gone, he went pale — and then, under the pressure of their questioning, admitted the full truth. The key he had given them had always been a fake, a decoy meant to make them a visible but survivable target while the real key remained hidden. He had entrusted the genuine artifact to his loyal lieutenant Tadric, posted quietly away from the barracks in the North Galleries. Kalan then grandly deputized the party as Watchers of Candlekeep. When they asked about a stipend, Kalan was dismissive, but when they pressed for an honorarium, he assured them it would be paid, though it was merely symbolic in value. It was then that Fembris, cornered and frightened, broke down and admitted that when he had reported the key's location to Bookwyrm, A'lai Aivenmore had been present in the room. This crucial revelation explained exactly why Tadric and the party had been targeted while Kalan himself remained untouched. Suspicion now turned heavily toward A'lai Aivenmore as the architect behind it all.

Kalan pressed a Watcher's stair key into their hands — a shortcut through a barred door leading directly to the North Gallery — and urged them to run. He tried to keep pace but, being overweight and clearly out of shape, fell behind quickly, huffing and puffing as he waved them on with a breathless gesture before they disappeared ahead of him — still calling out that the fake key had been given only to keep them safe. The party arrived at the top of a domed rotunda to find Tadric already in the grip of a massive, terrifying creature: Moziqodo, Sylvira's abyssal spawn son, who had grappled the young man and was tearing him apart with savage claws. There was no time for hesitation.

Thorin charged in first, striking hard with Dawnbringer and planting himself squarely between the demon and the rest of the room — a Menacing Attack fixing the pit fiend with such a terrifying display of focused violence that the demon recoiled in fear, unable to advance, forcing Moziqodo's attention onto him. Zalthir followed with a ferocious assault, driving his magically enhanced fists into the creature and unleashing torrents of corrosive acid through Glabbagool, then stunning it outright with a burst of focused will. Before Moziqodo could recover, Zalthir pressed the advantage further, hammering the stunned and reeling creature with blow after devastating blow until it could barely stand. Grygum hurled a javelin of crackling lightning at the fiend, though it narrowly missed its mark.

Daz struck the helpless demon with a volley of unerring magical force, and then Thorin raised Dawnbringer and delivered a final, critical blow that brought the pit fiend crashing to the floor. Moziqodo lay dead in the wreckage of the rotunda, and the party stood over him, breathing hard. Grygum knelt beside Tadric and channeled divine healing into the young man's wounds, closing the lacerations and stopping the bleeding before they could claim his life. Tadric, pale and shaking, looked up at the creature that had nearly killed him — Sylvira's own son — and could only sit in stunned silence.

With one real key now secured and Moziqodo slain, the party took stock of what they knew. A'lai Aivenmore held the key stolen from Bookwyrm, and he was almost certainly already moving toward the high tower, where he and Moziqodo planned to meet. The party still possessed Kalan's fake decoy key alongside the real one — a potential advantage, since anyone who didn't know the truth might be fooled into thinking they held both. Yet, as they prepared to move, they realized they still did not know who was truly pulling the strings behind this conspiracy, and their suspicions turned heavily toward A'lai. The party leveled up to level nine. The high tower loomed ahead of them, and with it, the final confrontation that would determine the fate of Candlekeep itself.

## Locations
### Candlekeep
A legendary fortified library-fortress where a series of murders and the theft of magical high tower keys have thrown the academic community into chaos. Its primary defense is a magical ward that prevents teleportation and suppresses fire within its walls — fire spells erupt briefly then evaporate instantly. Despite claims of the "finest defensive mechanisms in the realms," the party notes it has suffered two murders and a helmed-horror ambush.

### Bookwyrm's Office
The site of two significant events: first, a tense confrontation where Kalan was reinstated after evidence was presented (and where Bookwyrm offered tea that Zalthir quietly had Glabbagool absorb); later, the scene of Bookwyrm's gruesome murder, where her throat was torn out — though her heart remained, no longer beating — and a half-finished note reading 'He is using the beast to—' was found on her desk.

### Deneir's Sanctum
A supposedly secure room within Candlekeep where the party was escorted for protection, only to be ambushed by three helmed horrors that smashed through the windows and door targeting Daz for the key he carried. The party had been locked inside, discovered when Daral tried and failed to leave.

### Alkrist's Room
A room containing a safe with gold, potions, and a gold coin engraved with a red herring — dubbed "Jimmy Hoffa's chest" by Thorin, a fitting symbol for an investigation full of misdirection.

### Great Library
Marked as location 10 on Candlekeep's map (near Mithral Hall, the Bahamutian School, and Selûne), a central hub of knowledge from which the party began their frantic run to find Kalan Strongbranch after Bookwyrm's murder was announced.

### Sea Warden's Tower
A defensive structure within Candlekeep, home to its watchers and swordsmen, where Kalan Strongbranch was found frantically arming himself after hearing the alarm bell. It was here that the party learned the truth about the fake key and were deputized as Watchers.

### North Galleries
A section of Candlekeep away from the main barracks — reached through the Echoing Hall — where Kalan had hidden Tadric to protect the real key. Accessed via a shortcut through a barred door, it features a domed rotunda at the top (with acoustic reflections) that became the site of a bloody confrontation with Moziqodo.

### High Tower
The ultimate destination of the two keys, currently suspected to be where A'lai Aivenmore and Moziqodo planned to meet. The room within it contains the very wards that protect all of Candlekeep from teleportation and fire.

## NPCs
### Kalan Strongbranch
The reinstated Head of the Avowed and Gatewarden of Candlekeep, whose 'Threefold Proof' methodology was recently vindicated. He admitted to giving the party a fake key as a decoy to protect the real one, which he entrusted to his lieutenant Tadric. When the party captured two suspects, he congratulated them on securing "two of the pillars of the thrice-fold proof," and when Thorin reframed it as "three strikes, you're out," Kalan offered "Three slashes, perhaps?" He swore "On my honor" that the shortcut key was genuine, deputized the party as Watchers of Candlekeep, and directed them to the North Galleries, though he was overweight, out of shape, and too out of breath to keep up with them, huffing and puffing as he fell behind.

### Bookwyrm
A high-ranking, 18th-level magic user of the Avowed and Janussi's beloved partner, suspected of covering up his murder to protect her nephew Alkrist. She was found dead with her throat torn apart in her office — her heart intact but no longer beating — likely murdered for the high tower key she possessed. A half-finished note on her desk read 'He is using the beast to—'

### Daral
An Avowed scholar who was cured of a lingering affliction by Dawnbringer and launched into an enthusiastic monologue about making sentient magical swords an area of serious academic study, expounding on "the semiotics of the subject." Terrified after the attacks, he pressed close to Thorin's side to stay near Dawnbringer's protection. He assisted the party by using a scroll of Detect Magic to confirm the key they held was not magical (declaring "This is not the key"), and explained the nature of Candlekeep's protective wards, deducing that the ward is maintained within the room protected by the two keys.

### Fembris Lancer
An acolyte adjutant who served as a food tester for the suspicious party in the sanctum. He witnessed the key being handed from Grygum to Daz and was suspected of leaking information. Paralyzed in horror after Bookwyrm's death, under pressure he broke down and admitted that A'lai Aivenmore was present in the room when he reported the key transfer to Bookwyrm — explaining why Tadric and the party were targeted instead of Kalan.

### Alkrist
The individual whose confession and physical evidence linked him to the murder of Janussi — and revealed to be Bookwyrm's nephew, whom she moved to protect. His room contained a safe with gold, potions, and a gold coin engraved with a red herring.

### Janussi
The deceased member of the Avowed whose murder is the central mystery of the investigation. Universally beloved — everyone mourned him even though he was about to fire them all. His key was stolen from his locket after his death, and his room was visited by Bookwyrm shortly after the murder.

### Helmed Horrors
Three towering animated suits of plate armor with cold, glowing eyes that smashed through the windows and door of Deneir's Sanctum. They ignored most of the party and converged almost entirely on Daz, seeking the key he carried, before being destroyed. Their mindlessness, contrasted with their uncanny knowledge of who held the key, deeply unsettled the party.

### Tadric
Kalan's loyal lieutenant, a short young human recognizable by his bowl-cut hairstyle, who was entrusted with the genuine key to the high tower and hidden in the North Galleries. He did not know what he carried or what was coming for it. He was grappled and nearly torn apart by Moziqodo before the party arrived and saved him, then healed by Grygum. In shock, he asked whether Kalan was alive and confirmed the creature was Sylvira's son.

### Moziqodo
A massive and powerful pit fiend revealed to be the abyssal spawn son of Sylvira Savikas — the product of a pregnancy in the Abyss — kept locked in a magical sanctuary. Driven by the madness of the abyss, he attacked Tadric in the domed rotunda to seize the real key. He possessed abyssal demon resistances (immune to fire) and the ability to Disengage and fly, but the party stunned and slew him before he could act or flee.

### A'lai Aivenmore
A powerful male drow antagonist revealed to be the associate of the abyssal spawn Moziqodo and the suspected mastermind. He was present when Fembris reported the key's location to Bookwyrm, and is now believed to hold the key stolen from Bookwyrm and to be waiting in the high tower for Moziqodo to arrive with the second. His likely aim is opening the high tower to obtain a magical artifact of ineffable power.

## Items
### Potion of Flying
A magical elixir that Zalthir surreptitiously stole from Alkrist's safe using Glabbagool while the Avowed were distracted and no one was watching.

### Gold Coin with a Red Herring
A gold coin engraved with a red herring, found in Alkrist's safe. A fitting symbol for an investigation full of misdirection, dubbed "Jimmy Hoffa's chest" by Thorin.

### Dawnbringer
A sentient magical sword wielded by Thorin that radiates radiant — not fire — damage. It cured Daral of his lingering affliction, glowed brightly under Daral's Detect Magic scroll, and delivered the critical killing blow to Moziqodo. It expressed pointed offense at being mistaken for a mere flaming sword ("I'm a radiant sword. Thank you.") and, per running gag, looks forward to therapy sessions.

### Scroll of Detect Magic
A standard tool carried by Candlekeep sages ("if your Candlekeep's age, you have one of those in your pocket at all time"), used by Daral to examine the key. It caused the party's magical equipment to glow brightly — Daral, still shimmering with residual magic from Dawnbringer's cure, brightest of all — while revealing the key held by the party to have no magical aura whatsoever.

### Bookwyrm's Note
A half-finished, handwritten note found on Bookwyrm's desk at the scene of her murder, reading 'He is using the beast to—'

### Fake High Tower Key
A non-magical decoy key given to the party by Kalan Strongbranch to draw the attention of potential thieves away from the real key, and to make the party a visible but survivable target. Confirmed to have no magical properties when examined with Detect Magic. Retained by the party as a potential means to bluff enemies into thinking they hold both keys.

### Real High Tower Key
One of two essential magical artifacts required to access the high tower and the room containing Candlekeep's protective wards. It was held by Tadric in the North Galleries and secured by the party after defeating Moziqodo.

### Watcher's Stair Key
A key provided by Kalan Strongbranch that grants access to a barred shortcut leading directly to the North Gallery, allowing the party to reach Tadric quickly. Kalan swore its authenticity "on my honor."

### Javelin of Lightning
A magical javelin Grygum was excited to use, hurled at the pit fiend Moziqodo, intended to strike with a bolt of electricity, though it narrowly missed its mark.

## Spells
### Cure Wounds / Cure Affliction
A restorative power channeled through Dawnbringer to heal Daral of his lingering affliction, filling the man with such gratitude that he launched into an enthusiastic monologue about sentient magical swords. Later, Grygum cast Cure Wounds to close Tadric's life-threatening injuries and stop his bleeding after the battle with Moziqodo.

### Detect Magic
Cast by Daral using a scroll to examine the key held by the party. The spell caused all magical equipment in the room — including Dawnbringer, the party's armor, and Bookwyrm's tower — to glow brightly, while the key remained stubbornly inert, confirming it was a non-magical decoy. Daral's conclusion: "This is not the key."

### Thaumaturgy / Firebolt
Used by Grygum to test the status of Candlekeep's protective wards. The resulting fire erupted briefly on a piece of paper but then evaporated instantaneously — a "Burning Bush kind of situation" — confirming the ward suppressing combustion within the keep was still active, and by extension that the room locked by the two keys still held.

### Magic Missile
Cast by Daz at first level against the stunned pit fiend Moziqodo, striking him with unerring bolts of magical force that contributed to the creature's defeat.
