Kalan Strongbranch had a key in his hand and a plan in his mouth, and I trusted neither.

"Tadric, he's loyal, and he's alone, and he does not know what he carries or what's coming for it," Kalan said, already moving, already breathing harder than a man should who's only just started to run. "The Echoing Hall, then the North Galleries. I posted him away from the barracks on purpose. Run!"

He pressed the thing into my hand. A Watcher's Stair key, he called it. Barred North Gallery door, he called it. A shortcut. Take it or don't.

Here's the thing about a shortcut in Candlekeep. It's a tunnel that puts you where you weren't a minute ago, and I've spent enough time underground to know that a tunnel is only ever as honest as the man who hands you the map. And this man had already handed us a fake key once. So I looked at him and I said what I was thinking, which is what I always do.

"And we say, sure, and I'm like — is this key for real this time, or are you just fucking with us again?"

He stopped. Not for long. Just long enough to put a hand somewhere over his chest and get the words out.

"On my honor," he said.

And there it was. The whole thing, laid out in three words, and every one of them a wall that didn't hold.

"And the other one wasn't?" I said.

Grygum lost it. Somewhere off my shoulder the orc just cracked open. "Ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha!" I'll give myself this much: it was a good line. I meant it and it was true and it was funny, which is the best kind. But the terrain doesn't wait for a laugh. Tadric was up ahead getting torn apart by something we couldn't see yet, and standing in a hall arguing about a man's honor wasn't going to close the distance.

"Okay, wait, never mind, never mind," I said. "Let's go. We run. Okay, we can argue about this later."

So we ran.

Kalan tried to keep up. He really did try. But he was carrying more of himself than a running man ought to, and he was older on top of it, and the gap between us opened the way a gap opens when a rock starts down a slope: slow at first, then not.

"Little Rotund," I said, mostly to myself.

He kept talking, which is a thing men do when they should be saving their breath. Something about the fake key. He'd given it to us on purpose. "Just for the record — I figured that if they saw you with the key, it would be fake, the fake key would be safer. I couldn't trust you with the key."

I didn't slow down for it. "All right. Yeah. It's like — have you seen our weapons? Like, Dawnbringer here can take anybody on."

He was still going. "That's why he gave you the fake key, because he wanted —"

"Alright, alright," I said. "We can argue about this later."

That's the second time I said it and I meant it both times. Later is a real place. Right now was the North Gallery.

He made it a little further, and then he did the thing a body does when it's out of margin. He just — stopped. Planted himself in the hall behind us, hands on his knees.

"Carry on!" I told him, and kept my feet moving.

"A little out of breath," Daz said, dry, the way he says everything.

He was. Kalan was done. We weren't.

The stair opened up onto a dome. A big one: a rotunda, curved stone overhead, the kind of ceiling that isn't low for once, which is its own strange thing after months of walls that make you stoop. And a curved dome like that got me thinking about a thing I've heard about but never got to use.

"Oh, are we going to do the type of thing where we overhear somebody because there's reflecting sounds in the rotunda?" I asked. "Because have you ever been in one of the rotundas where you stand in the right place and then there's another place and you hear exactly the sound from the other place because it gets reflected off?"

The question hung there a second, and then came back: would I like that.

"Okay," I said, "if you could write that in, that'd be cool."

And it was in. Officially in. A whispering gallery, mine to use. Grygum liked it too. "I like the idea of having something special in a rotunda. That sounds like fun." A rock in the right place is a wall. That's the terrain now.

Then the situation came clear, and there wasn't anything special about it at all.

Up under the curve of that dome, while we'd been running and arguing and naming the fat man, the creature had gotten to Tadric. It had him. Grappled, on the floor, and it was pulling him apart.

"Alright," I said. "So we gotta save Tadric. Tadric may not survive."
