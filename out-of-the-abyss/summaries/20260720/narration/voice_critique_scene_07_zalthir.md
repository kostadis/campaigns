## Flags

### [1] Mechanical scan A — em-dash overuse (densest in the batch: 12 instances in 7 lines)

One match, line 17, sits entirely inside Zalthir's italicized internal aphorism (`*The most dangerous thing in a room isn't the one with its blade drawn — it's the one that has already decided it has won.*`) and is correctly excluded as a protected italic span.

| Line | Text | Suggested |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | "It had jumped him while we ran — grappled, and now it was tearing him apart." | Colon: "...while we ran: grappled, and now it was tearing him apart." |
| 21 | Three dashes: "Thorin moved first — I felt him go..."; "...And then — I would not have believed it if I hadn't watched it land — the thing flinched." | Convert at least two of the three. E.g. "Thorin moved first. I felt him go before I saw it..." and "I would not have believed it if I hadn't watched it land, but the thing flinched." |
| 27 | Two dashes: "...before the demon registered me — the whole point of me is that I'm already there — and I hit it." | Commas: "...before the demon registered me, the whole point of me is that I'm already there, and I hit it." |
| 33 | "...a demon that can't do anything at all — a demon that has become geometry..." | See flag 2 below — this line has a bigger problem than the dash. |
| 37 | Two dashes: "...a thing immune to fire — no fireball, no flame — and put a plain old cold burst..." | Commas: "...a thing immune to fire, no fireball, no flame, and put a plain old cold burst..." |
| 39 | "...the next one came down as a killing thing — I saw it land and I saw the demon stop being a demon." | Period: "...came down as a killing thing. I saw it land and I saw the demon stop being a demon." |
| 45 | Two dashes: "The demon had had one attack in it — one — and one was all it needed..." | This is doing real rhetorical work (the repeated "one" for emphasis); weakest candidate for change in the scene. |

**Why:** Same systemic pattern as the rest of the batch, at its highest density here. Line 45 is arguably earned emphasis rather than a tic — worth a second look before flattening.

### [2] Register-wrong vocabulary — "geometry," used twice in one section

> "Stunned is a demon that can't do anything at all — a demon that has become **geometry**, a stationary thing waiting to be dismantled."

> "I didn't feel triumphant. I felt the way you feel when the **geometry** was right and you were the one standing in the correct spot. Filed it. Moved on."

**Why:** "Geometry" is directly on the scan-B register-wrong list, and it shows up twice in the same section, not once. It's also the wrong narrator's vocabulary specifically: Thorin's spec is the one built on terrain, position, and reading a room as leverage ("giants negotiate through position... the ground under your feet says everything"). Zalthir's spec is sensory and observational, not analytical — he "notices the shape of a tunnel before he notices who's in it," registers sensation, and reasons in stillness and breath-counts, never in geometric abstraction. Two uses of an analytical noun that belongs to a different character's mind is a clear voice-spec mismatch, not a one-off word choice.
**Suggested rewrite:** Line 33: "...a demon that can't do anything at all — a stationary thing waiting to be dismantled." (cut the "become geometry" clause entirely; the rest of the sentence already carries the image). Line 45's closer: "I felt the way you feel when you were the one standing in the correct spot, and it held. Filed it. Moved on." (the "Filed it. Moved on." closer is itself a canonical, spec-sanctioned Zalthir line — keep that part as-is.)

## Verdict

The combat choreography and Zalthir's stillness-under-pressure ("That was my moment. I don't waste those," "Filed it. Moved on.") are strongly on-voice, and this is the scene where his monastery-training thought-source device does real tactical work rather than just decorating a pause. The recurring issue is "geometry" leaking in from what reads like Thorin's register — worth a targeted fix before this feeds assembly, since it's the kind of cross-narrator convergence the genre spec explicitly warns against.
