# The Key is Secured


---

## Zalthir — The Aftermath of the Sanctum Attack

The room had gone quiet the way a room goes quiet after the last thing standing stops moving. Three helmed horrors lay where we'd put them down: empty steel, no bodies inside, which is its own kind of wrong. I don't like enemies that were never alive to begin with. There's nothing to interrogate.

Daral Yashenti was at the door, hammering, calling to be let out. He hadn't yet noticed the door was already open, smashed off its frame by the thing that came through it. When he did notice, he went still. I watched him do the arithmetic. Four of us in the room. Everyone else in Candlekeep, as far as he could tell, would prefer him dead. He turned around.

"What do we do next, gentlemen?"

I said nothing and watched him work it out. Grygum did the talking. Grygum usually does.

"So we now suspect Fembris Lancer?"

That was the name that mattered. Fembris Lancer saw the key pass from Grygum's hand to Daz's. He's the only witness. And a witness who watches the wrong thing at the wrong moment is either an accident or an asset, and I have stopped believing in accidents this deep underground.

But it was the key itself that scratched at me. We'd checked it once. We'd looked for the magic on it and found nothing. Grygum was working the same thread out loud.

"You mean we tried to detect magic, and it didn't come out as magical? Makes me wonder if it's actually the right key."

Then he remembered the phrasing. Kalan Strongbranch, when he handed it over — *I have given it now to someone who is not me.*

"Someone who is not me. Yeah, that's a weird phrasing." Grygum turned it over. "And I guess it could be sort of a hot potato kind of thing. You know, bad things are coming to whoever is holding the key when something happens."

A hot potato. He said it lightly. I did not find it light. A man who phrases a handoff like a riddle is a man building himself an alibi.

Grygum held the key out to Daral. "Does this look like the right key? Does anything look odd about it? The key to the tower?"

Daral studied it, careful. "Well, I'm not a locksmith, so I couldn't tell you for sure."

"I was given to understand that these keys were supposed to be magical."

"Oh, yes, absolutely. They're the two keys that are necessary to open Candlekeep's High Tower. They're not some lowly, cheap-ass keys that you acquire at a locksmith. Why?"

"But this key is not magical. Go ahead and examine it. Try to detect magic on it. We did not succeed."

Daral had a scroll. Of course he had a scroll. A Candlekeep sage keeps one in a pocket the way I keep a spare cord coiled at my hip. He read it. The room lit up in the way that magic lights up when you finally look at it correctly: Dawnbringer flared, Grygum's armor blinked, and Daral himself glowed brighter than any of us, still soaked in the light Dawnbringer had poured into him.

Everything in the room answered. Everything but the key.

The key stayed dark. Inert, dead metal in a room full of things singing.

"No, no," Daral said, and there was no drama in it, which is how I knew it was true. "This is not the key. This is not the key."

*There was a class for this.* Reading the false thing set beside the real things. Brother Harren taught it, or the tall one who smelled of incense; I was never certain which. I remember the light through the shutters. I remember wanting to be somewhere I could hit something. I don't remember the lesson. But I didn't need the lesson to read this. The key we'd been guarding, bleeding for, locked in a room over: it was a decoy. Someone had switched it before it ever reached us.

And then the bell.

Not the hour. A watcher's bell, tolling wrong: an alarm pattern, cold and institutional, as if the keep itself had noticed something and started to scream about it.

"What does that mean?" Thorin asked.

I said nothing. The Garden Shadow had a saying — or possibly Brother Quellin did — that the most dangerous person in a room isn't the one with the blade drawn. It's the one who has already decided they've won. Somebody in this keep had already decided. The dead key in Grygum's hand and the bell going off in the same breath were not two events. Two things arriving together this deep down are never two things.

Someone rushed in through the ruined doorway.

Bookwyrm was dead. Throat torn out.

"Who?" Daz said. "Bookwyrm?"

"Bookwyrm is dead."

The decoy in one hand, the bell overhead, the First Reader with her throat open somewhere in the dark. I filed it and watched the door.

## Daz — The Death of Bookwyrm

The bell hadn't finished ringing before an Avowed was shouting it down the corridor: Bookwyrm is dead. Not *someone*. The First Reader. The one who was guilty and unaccused, whose ceremony we'd cancelled ourselves. I had her sorted into the column marked *loose ends I intended to collect from later*. Someone had collected first.

"How did it happen?" I asked.

"Who killed her?" said Grygum, correcting the Avowed's pronoun and his own in the same breath.

Her throat had been torn apart. That was the answer that came back: torn, not cut. And when Grygum asked whether she still had her heart, the answer was yes, present, intact. Not beating, but there. I noted the discrepancy immediately. It was the whole story in one line. Janussi's heart had been carved out with intent. Bookwyrm's had been left where it was. Whatever did this hadn't come for organs.

We went up. Fembris Lancer and Daral Yashenti brought us to her office and it was a mess, and the mess itself was information. She was an eighteenth-level magic user relaxed in the safety of her own tower: no Time Stop cast, no ward raised, an academic who spent her days validating citations and identifying false ones and never once expected to need Wish at her own desk. Whoever did this knew exactly how confident she'd be. That is not luck. That is planning.

On the desk was a half-finished note in her own hand. *He is using the beast to—* and then nothing. Grygum read it aloud and asked Daral Yashenti what the beast was. Daral Yashenti had no idea what the beast was.

I reached for it, the way I always reach, ritually, off the top of my mind. And the reach came back empty. Worse than empty. My own head was the problem; the pressure behind my eyes that's been getting worse fogged the whole thing over, and for a bad moment I had nothing. *Are you fucking kidding me.*

Grygum offered to help. I took it. On the second pass, with his hand steadying the work, it came clean. Cleaner than clean, everything snapping into place at once. And what came was this: the wards over Candlekeep were wrong. The pressure of them, the same pressure that's been sitting in my skull, didn't sit right. Something was broken.

"Oh," I said. "Like somebody hacked the firewall and opened another port."

Yes.

Thorin caught it before I could. "And they snuck in some, like, animated armor to attack us through that thing."

"Thank you," I said. Someone had opened a hole in the perimeter and pushed the helmed horrors through it, at *me*.

"We were hired to solve one murder," Thorin said. "This is really not ours to solve." He wanted to renegotiate the fee. It was unclear with whom, now that the person who'd hired us had her throat opened on the floor.

I let it sit. Then I said the thing that mattered. The two killings were not the same hand. Janussi was surgical: magical, deliberate, a heart removed for a reason. Bookwyrm was claws and teeth. A beast. And I was still looking at that word on the desk when the account balanced somewhere in the back of my head, off a thread I hadn't known I was holding.

Sylvira Savikas has a son. Abyss-born. Abyssal spawn. She's a tiefling, she was in the Abyss, bad things happen — and she keeps him locked in a magical sanctuary to protect him. To protect *him*, or to protect everyone from him; the note didn't specify and neither did my memory. But there it was. The beast. The madness of the Abyss, the same madness we've been swimming in since Velkynvelve, wearing someone's son.

"Interesting," I said, which is what I say when something has just cost me more than I want to show.

Daral Yashenti explained the defenses then: the shield that stops anyone teleporting in or out. I said what I already knew. "Are we sure the teleport protection is working? Something feels off to me." Grygum, who has done as much information-security work as I have, put it more plainly: inconceivable things happen several times a day, so could he please *check*.

The check was a candle. You can't light a fire in Candlekeep. Grygum threw Thaumaturgy at a scrap of paper and the flame leapt and then evaporated the instant it touched: a burning bush that refused to burn. Same ward. Same effect. Held in the one room locked by the two high tower keys.

The room protected by the keys held the ward that protected everything else. Bookwyrm had one of those keys: she'd gone into Janussi's chamber, taken the heart, secured the key. Someone had torn her throat out to get it. Consistent with the attempt on me. Consistent with the port left open in the firewall.

The other key was Kalan Strongbranch's, or had been. *If I don't see you tomorrow, do not look for me at my post.* Not the gatehouse, then.

"Sea Warden's Tower," Daral Yashenti said. "Lost among the other watchers."

Go interview him. Or protect him. Or find out he's already dead.

## Grygum — Racing to the Sea Warden's Tower

We needed the key, and the key was somewhere Kalan Strongbranch knew, and Kalan Strongbranch was not where Kalan Strongbranch was supposed to be.

Daz worked it out. He does that. I watched him go quiet for half a second, and then he had the line Kalan had left us like a breadcrumb — *if I don't see you tomorrow, do not look for me at my post.* Not the gatehouse, then. Daral Yashenti said the likeliest place was the Sea Warden's Tower, that Kalan was probably down among the other watchers, rallying them with beers and alcohol, celebrating whatever there was to celebrate.

I stopped on the word.

"There's troops here? I thought we were in some sort of like academic tower or something."

"Well, there's some."

"Okay."

"We have some," Daral said. "We are not completely defenseless."

I turned that over. A tower full of scholars keeping swordsmen. It shouldn't have surprised me, and yet.

"Well, you have plenty of magic, but I guess swords still have their place, huh?"

"Yes, we have some swordsmen. It's always important to have one."

I noted that for the column marked *things nobody tells you about the wise.* The people who read the oldest books in the world still keep a man with a blade near the door. It's always important to have one. I have met McGruff-shaped truths dressed up worse than that.

"I still have the fake key, right?" I said, though I said *fake* out of habit by then, and the word tasted wrong in my mouth even as I said it. Bookwyrm had had a key. It was stolen by some sociopathic creature. That much I knew. Everything else was a knot.

Then we ran.

The library stretched out in front of us like something the map itself was struggling to hold: the walls smearing, the floor pulling long. We ran through it anyway, at a speed no honest set of legs earns, all the way to the Sea Warden's Tower.

Kalan was buckling on his armor when we found him. He was rushing toward us as we rushed toward him, belting himself in, hearing the same bell we'd all heard. "Grygum, Daz, I heard the bell. Is everything okay?"

"Uh, no, Bookwyrm is dead."

He paused. Then, first thing, before grief, before anything: "Does she have the key?"

Daz said we hadn't seen the key. Thorin wanted to be sure we'd actually looked, and I told him yes, we'd been to the site, we'd seen the body, and no: "We did not find a key."

Kalan took a breath he thought was relief. "Well, that's fortunate. They only have one of the two keys. Right? It was an accident. It had nothing to do with the keys, right? It was just a maddening attack by Sylvira Savikas's son. I told her — I told Bookwyrm that that creature would be the death of us."

We told him we'd been attacked too. By things that came for a key.

The color went out of the comfort. "Gadzooks. Someone is definitely after the keys."

That's the moment I want to write down honest. He looked at the four of us, adventurers, he called us, and deputized us as Watchers of Candlekeep, an official thing said in a hurry, and I answered "Aye, aye," because what else do you say. Daz asked whether it came with a stipend.

"So far we've done badly," I said, because it was true. We were the ones who were supposed to serve and protect the thing, and the thing was gone, and a woman was dead, and here was a frightened man handing us a badge.

Then Kalan told us Tadric had the key. The real one. The boy who'd guarded Janussi's murder scene. And the whole knot came loose at once: the key he'd handed me, that I'd handed Daz, that we'd worked out was false. He'd given us a decoy. He'd made us the target.

"Okay, I'm confused now about who thinks where all the keys are," I said, "because don't people think that one of them was given to us?" Then, when it all lined up: "I mean, does Kalan know that we know that it was fake?"

He didn't care. He was past it. "Okay, he's basically admitting he gave us a fake."

Thorin read the notes aloud like a man reading a duty roster: short human, young, Tadric, his role is Tadric.

They put the screws to Fembris Lancer, and Fembris admitted A'lai Aivenmore had been in the room when he reported the key. Thorin built the case fast: one hand behind both attacks, both keys, one man who knew what no one else knew.

"I mean, there's other theories you could come up with," I said, "but that seems like a good start for sure."

Kalan was impressed with us anyway. "Well done. You have captured two of the pillars of the thrice-fold proof."

Two pillars. A woman dead, a decoy in our hands, and a boy somewhere in the North Galleries holding the only real thing, not knowing yet that everyone was coming for him.

## Thorin — The Shortcut to the North Gallery

Kalan Strongbranch had a key in his hand and a plan in his mouth, and I trusted neither.

"Tadric, he's loyal, and he's alone, and he does not know what he carries or what's coming for it," Kalan said, already moving, already breathing harder than a man should who's only just started to run. "The Echoing Hall, then the North Galleries. I posted him away from the barracks on purpose. Run!"

He pressed the thing into my hand. A Watcher's Stair key, he called it. Barred North Gallery door, he called it. A shortcut. Take it or don't.

Here's the thing about a shortcut in Candlekeep. It's a tunnel that puts you where you weren't a minute ago, and I've spent enough time underground to know that a tunnel is only ever as honest as the man who hands you the map. And this man had already handed us a fake key once. So I looked at him and I said what I was thinking, which is what I always do.

"s this key for real this time, or are you just fucking with us again?"

He stopped. Not for long. Just long enough to put a hand somewhere over his chest and get the words out.

"On my honor," he said.

And there it was. The whole thing, laid out in three words, and every one of them a wall that didn't hold.

"And the other one wasn't?" I said.

Grygum lost it. Somewhere off my shoulder the orc just cracked open. "Ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha!" I'll give myself this much: it was a good line. I meant it and it was true and it was funny, which is the best kind. But the terrain doesn't wait for a laugh. Tadric was up ahead getting torn apart by something we couldn't see yet, and standing in a hall arguing about a man's honor wasn't going to close the distance.

"Okay, wait, never mind, never mind," I said. "Let's go. We run. Okay, we can argue about this later."

So we ran.

Kalan tried to keep up. He really did try. But he was carrying more of himself than a running man ought to, and he was older on top of it, and the gap between us opened the way a gap opens when a rock starts down a slope: slow at first, then not.

"Little Rotund," I said, mostly to myself.

He kept talking, which is a thing men do when they should be saving their breath. Something about the fake key. He'd given it to us on purpose. "Just for the record — I figured that if they saw you with the key, it would be fake, the fake key would be safer. I couldn't trust you with the key."

I didn't slow down for it. "All right. Yeah. It's like — have you seen our weapons? Like, Dawnbringer here can take anybody on."

He was still going. "That's why I gave you the fake key, because I wanted —"

"Alright, alright," I said. "We can argue about this later."

That's the second time I said it and I meant it both times. Later is a real place. Right now was the North Gallery.

He made it a little further, and then he did the thing a body does when it's out of margin. He just — stopped. Planted himself in the hall behind us, hands on his knees.

"Carry on!" I told him, and kept my feet moving.

"A little out of breath," Daz said, dry, the way he says everything.

He was. Kalan was done. We weren't.

The stair opened up onto a dome. A big one: a rotunda, curved stone overhead, the kind of ceiling that isn't low for once, which is its own strange thing after months of walls that make you stoop. And a curved dome like that got me thinking about a thing I've heard about but never got to use.

Then the situation came clear, and there wasn't anything special about it at all.

Up under the curve of that dome, while we'd been running and arguing and naming the fat man, the creature had gotten to Tadric. It had him. Grappled, on the floor, and it was pulling him apart.

"Alright," I said. "So we gotta save Tadric."

## Zalthir — Battle in the Domed Rotunda

The rotunda opened above us into a dome, and the thing was already on Tadric. It had jumped him while we ran: grappled, and now it was tearing him apart. Massive. Abyssal. Not built for negotiation. Its claws worked at Tadric the way you'd work at a knot you'd decided not to untie.

"Alright, so we gotta save Tadric," Thorin said. 

Grygum said it plainly, the way he says the hard things. "So if we have to prioritize the man or Tadric, we're going to have to make a choice."

"Yeah," Thorin said.

I didn't say anything. I was reading the demon. It hadn't looked up at us yet. It was still busy. That was the tell. *The most dangerous thing in a room isn't the one with its blade drawn — it's the one that has already decided it has won.* The Garden Shadow said that, or possibly Brother Heslin. I don't remember the face. I remember the demon had its head down over its kill, certain, and I remember deciding it was wrong.

Everything after that happened fast, and it happened in our favor.

Thorin moved first. I felt him go before I saw it, that dwarf who never gets the jump on anything getting it now. He closed the distance and planted himself as the wall, and Dawnbringer came down radiant, not fire, twice, and the demon's attention snapped off Tadric and onto him. Then the second thing: Thorin sank whatever reserve he had left into another strike, and something in the demon's balance went wrong. It sagged. I would not have believed it if I hadn't watched it land, but the thing flinched. Backed a half-step. Thorin had frightened a pit fiend. It couldn't come at him now.

Which meant it was locked in place, staggered, and looking the wrong way.

That was my moment. I don't waste those.

I crossed the floor before the demon registered me, the whole point of me is that I'm already there, and I hit it. The first strike bit deep, into the meat of it, and I felt the second one land in the same instant my breath went out. Then I spent a measure of that breath, the controlled kind the monastery drills into you until it isn't a decision anymore, and I put everything into a single precise strike meant to seize the nervous system. The demon locked rigid. Stunned. Whatever it was going to do next, it was not going to do it.

"Nice," Daz said, somewhere behind me.

"Even better than frightened," Thorin said.

It was. Frightened is a demon that won't come at you. Stunned is a demon that can't do anything at all: a stationary thing waiting to be dismantled. I spent another measured breath and hit it twice more while it stood there helpless, unable to guard, unable to answer, and those two landed clean and heavy and the thing was carved down to almost nothing. There is no clean word for what that felt like. The monastery combat drills finally paid for themselves. I stepped back and let it stand there, ruined and upright, and told the others it was theirs.

Grygum moved wide so his lightning wouldn't catch any of us, then threw a javelin that arced and hummed and missed. "So, uh, that, uh, missed." He paused, "Fuck."

Daz declined to waste anything clever on a thing immune to fire, no fireball, no flame, and put a plain old cold burst of force into it instead, three small bright darts, because it wasn't doing great and he wanted to hold the rest in reserve.

Thorin finished it. He was owed that. A solid strike, and then the next one came down as a killing thing. I saw it land and I saw the demon stop being a demon.

"You killed him," the world said. "You killed him."

"Really?" Grygum said. "I expected this guy to be a lot tougher."

He hadn't seen what I'd seen. The demon had had one attack in it — one — and one was all it needed to open Tadric's throat and disengage into the air and vanish with the key clutched to its chest. That was the whole plan it had come with. Kill the man, take the artifact, be gone before we crossed the floor. It never got the attack. We took its whole turn away from it before it could spend a single beat of it.

I didn't feel triumphant. I felt the way you feel when you were the one standing in the correct spot, and it held.

Grygum knelt over Tadric and closed the wounds, and the blood stopped. Tadric came back into himself all at once, shaking, grabbing for the key even before his eyes focused, staring at the ruin of the thing that had been on him. Sylvira Savikas's son. He looked at the corpse and then at us, and the only thing he could get out was a question, quiet and cracked.

"Is Kalan Strongbranch alive?"

## Thorin — Aftermath and Strategy

Dawnbringer was still warm in my hand when the thing on the floor stopped being a threat and started being a corpse. Nobody in the room said the fight was over, which is how I knew it wasn't.

"Well done," came the word, and I clocked the *but* coming before it landed. "You, like, all sort of ganged up on him and knocked him out. But you still have a problem. You have A'lai Aivenmore, who's got one of the two keys."

Right. Two keys. We'd been so busy with the one on the ground we'd forgotten the one that walked off.

"He's probably in Candlekeep's High Tower."

A'lai was up in the tower, waiting for this dead thing to come tell him the job was done. He'd be waiting a while.

I looked down at what we'd stopped. "Yeah, and so saving Tadric doesn't really get us anything except we're good people."

"Right, but — oh, if he had killed him, he would have actually disappeared with the key."

That caught me. "Oh, does he have some special ability to, like, just blink out of there once he sort of… It's called…"

"Disengage and fly."

Of course it was. Cut the last thread and lift off, key and all, gone before any of us could reach him. That was the whole game, and we'd broken it without even meaning to. "No — if he killed him, he would kill him and then flee. My theory was he would flee, but you guys did a massive—"

"I mean, like, not only did we keep them from fleeing," I said, "we saved Tadric." Two things at once. Held the position, kept the prize on the board. Giants would call that a good day.

"Yes, you saved Tadric." Then, to Grygum: "Are you going to heal him or are you going to let him bleed out to death?"

Grygum knelt and put his hand on Tadric and said the words, and the blood stopped and the gashes pulled themselves shut. Tadric came back into himself, grabbed for the key, then looked at the dead thing on the floor, and something in his face fell in.

"Moziqodo," he said. "Sylvira Savikas' son. What has happened here?"

They asked if I wanted to fill him in. I did. "I mean, he seems like he's a bit player in this drama, so I figure we might as well tell him what's going on." No reason to run angles on a man who nearly bled out on the stone. Radical transparency's cheaper anyway.

He sat down hard when he heard it. "Is Kalan Strongbranch alive?"

"I mean, when we left him," I said. "But he was breathing really hard because he was running, so who knows how things ended there." That was the honest answer and I gave it. I didn't dress it up.

And then it came. That quiet knock inside the chest that means you've crossed some threshold you didn't notice crossing. Not a hit. Given the last while, worth specifying. Just the other kind of thing, the one that settles into your arms and legs and says *you're more than you were an hour ago.* I kept it to myself.

"Evil geniuses have more than one plan," someone said, and Grygum lit up.

"What fun. So we have one real key and one fake key, and anybody who doesn't know that we have a fake key, we can fake them out with it somehow. And eventually, the person that has the real key will be like, how are these guys saying they have two keys?"

"Yeah, that's a good idea," Daz said. "Interesting."

"I'm going to ferret him out," Grygum said. "Him or her. Or they. Or it."

So there it was, all of it on the ground where I could see it. A'lai up in the tower with the other key, waiting for a dead man's report. Us with a real key and Kalan's decoy.

Daz walked it forward. "So we show up in Candlekeep's High Tower, challenge, we get both keys, and then we own Candlekeep, right? That's how this works?"

"Exactly."

"Yeah," I said. "And then we can extract whatever boon we'd like since we deferred negotiation."

"Yeah!"

We hadn't closed the deal earlier. That wasn't a mistake. That was leverage held in reserve, waiting for the right-sized moment. And the tower was that moment. You stand where you stand, and the ground under your feet says the rest.
