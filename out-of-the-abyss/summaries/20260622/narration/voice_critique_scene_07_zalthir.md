# Voice Critique — Zalthir, scene 07: The Interrogation of Alkrist

**Narration:** summaries/20260622/narration/session_doc_scene_07_the_interrogation_of_alkrist.scrubbed.md
**Voice spec:** voice/zalthir_voice.md
**Per-char examples:** examples/zalthir.md

## Flags

### [1] mechanical-em-dash

> He entered Janussi's chambers with the careful energy of a man who had rehearsed his arrival, and then deployed grief the same way — deliberately, as an announcement he wanted witnessed.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before an explanatory clause; a colon reads cleaner.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...and then deployed grief the same way: deliberately, as an announcement he wanted witnessed."

### [2] mechanical-em-dash

> Thorin responded with something about being happy to help for the right remuneration — citizens of the world, not concerned with petty matters — and I watched Alkrist miss the edge of it entirely.

**Why:** Two narration-level em-dashes bracketing a paraphrase. The spec's "short and stacked" cadence handles this better as separate sentences than as a dash-interrupted one.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Thorin responded with something about being happy to help for the right remuneration. Citizens of the world, not concerned with petty matters. I watched Alkrist miss the edge of it entirely."

### [3] register-wrong-vocabulary

> He took it as sincere. He was pleased. I filed this as data: self-absorption sufficient to blunt irony.

**Why:** "Filed" alone is exactly on-voice (spec: "Filed it." is a listed example of his declarative full-stops). But "as data" is a modern computational/analytics term with no footing in his monastic-and-sailor background — it reads like an LLM default reach for clinical vocabulary rather than Zalthir's plain register.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He took it as sincere. He was pleased. I filed it: self-absorption sufficient to blunt irony."

### [4] mechanical-em-dash

> He didn't know that yet. Or rather — he was about to find out, the way you find out a floor is wet by walking onto it.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; the interrupting "Or rather" reads more naturally with a comma.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Or rather, he was about to find out, the way you find out a floor is wet by walking onto it."

### [5] mechanical-em-dash

> There was a class at the monastery — Brother Essivhan, possibly, or the quiet one with the ink-stained hands — about what you find in someone's room telling you more than what they'd ever say with their face.

**Why:** Two narration-level em-dashes bracketing the monk-attribution aside. Breaking into short stacked sentences suits the spec's cadence better than the dash-parenthetical.
**Suggested rewrite:** "There was a class at the monastery about what you find in someone's room telling you more than what they'd ever say with their face. Brother Essivhan, possibly. Or the quiet one with the ink-stained hands."

### [6] voice-spec-conflict

> I had not been paying close attention during that class. I did not need to have been.

**Why:** The spec is explicit about when the monastery-class device fires: "when Zalthir fails a skill check, botches something non-combat... or finds himself in a situation that clearly required preparation he skipped" — and the payoff is rueful ("he does not remember the content," "it watches him pay for the gap"). Here Zalthir just succeeded at reading Alkrist's room. Closing with "I did not need to have been" flips the device into a boast about not needing the class, which undercuts the documented rueful-gap texture rather than playing it straight.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I had not been paying close attention during that class. I don't remember what they said. I found the book anyway."

### [7] mechanical-em-dash

> Alkrist came apart under the zone of truth the way a tight knot comes apart in cold water — not fast, but continuously.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; a comma carries the modifying clause without the dash.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...the way a tight knot comes apart in cold water, not fast, but continuously."

### [8] generic-fantasy-prose

> He defended the research with the practiced urgency of someone who had been explaining its importance for years to people who still didn't understand. I filed it. Not the thread I was pulling yet.

**Why:** "Filed it." is exactly on-voice. But "not the thread I was pulling yet" is a stock detective-narration idiom (pulling threads, loose ends) that could belong to any mystery narrator — nothing in the spec or examples shows Zalthir reaching for thread/investigation metaphors; his filing device is usually just stated plainly and left there.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He defended the research with the practiced urgency of someone who had been explaining its importance for years to people who still didn't understand. I filed it. Not what I was after yet."

### [9] cliché-simile

> A method, he said, from a book. His hand moved toward the shelf before his mouth had finished the sentence. Not pointing. Just drifting. The way a compass needle drifts.

**Why:** "The way a compass needle drifts" is a common, general-purpose writing simile with no particular grounding in Zalthir's specific imagery (spider silk, coiled rope, shards of ice). It also lands as the fourth instance of the identical "the way [X] [verb]s" simile template in this one scene (see the convergence flag below), which compounds a mild issue into a visible tic.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Not pointing. Just drifting, toward the shelf, the way a hand drifts when the mind has already decided."

### [10] mechanical-em-dash

> Midnight tears. Tidy, annotated, marked — the margins of a man who had been studying rather than reading.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before an explanatory clause; a colon fits.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Tidy, annotated, marked: the margins of a man who had been studying rather than reading."

### [11] mechanical-em-dash

> He was burning the label when Irony and Orrin Glass arrived with a basin of water for supper, and he had washed his hands in it — just in case there was any midnight tears on them.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; a comma carries the trailing clause fine.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...and he had washed his hands in it, just in case there was any midnight tears on them."

### [12] mechanical-em-dash

> She went somewhere. He went home. Someone had known about the chalice — what it was for, what could be hidden inside it, why no one would think to look there without specific reason.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before a list of explanatory clauses; a colon is the standard fit.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Someone had known about the chalice: what it was for, what could be hidden inside it, why no one would think to look there without specific reason."

### [13] mechanical-em-dash

> The answer wasn't about Bookwyrm specifically — it was that the question was the wrong size.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash joining two independent clauses.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The answer wasn't about Bookwyrm specifically. It was that the question was the wrong size."

### [14] mechanical-em-dash

> By the end of his last day alive, Janussi had managed to hand Bookwyrm, Alkrist, and A'lai — separately, apparently without coordination — reasons to want him gone.

**Why:** Two narration-level em-dashes bracketing an aside.
**Suggested rewrite:** "By the end of his last day alive, Janussi had managed to hand Bookwyrm, Alkrist, and A'lai, separately and apparently without coordination, reasons to want him gone."

### [15] mechanical-em-dash

> "Alkrist?" I said — meaning: is it technically him, or A'lai?

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash outside the quoted dialogue; a comma before the existing colon is enough.
**Suggested rewrite:** "\"Alkrist?\" I said, meaning: is it technically him, or A'lai?"

### [16] mechanical-em-dash

> The most dangerous person in a room, Brother Quellin had said — or possibly Brother Harren, I was never certain of the attribution — isn't the one with the blade drawn.

**Why:** Two narration-level em-dashes bracketing the attribution aside; the spec's own phrasing of this exact aphorism variant ("The Garden Shadow had a saying — or possibly Brother Quellin did") uses one dash, not a full parenthetical pair, and reads cleaner restructured.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The most dangerous person in a room isn't the one with the blade drawn, Brother Quellin had said. Or possibly Brother Harren. I was never certain of the attribution."

### [17] convergence-with-house-style

> (line 21) the way you find out a floor is wet by walking onto it
> (line 31) the way a tight knot comes apart in cold water
> (line 45) The way a compass needle drifts.
> (line 57) the way a book goes to the place it has spent the most time

**Why:** The identical "the way [X] [verb]s" simile construction fires four times in one ~110-line scene. Individually most of these are fine (the knot-in-cold-water image even earns its keep given Zalthir's sailor background), but stacked this close together the template reads as a narration-pass tic rather than a character choice — the kind of repeated scaffolding an LLM reaches for when smoothing multiple beats into felt prose, not something the examples show Zalthir doing.
**Suggested rewrite:** Keep one, at most two, of these four; render the others as plain declarative statements instead (e.g., swap #9's rewrite above, and drop the "compass needle" line entirely in favor of the flat "Not pointing. Just drifting.").

## Verdict

The strongest issue is volume, not any single sentence: sixteen narration-level em-dashes in a scene that runs about a hundred lines, most doing the job of a period or colon and working directly against the spec's "short and stacked" declarative cadence. Layered on top is a self-repeating "the way [X] [verb]s" simile template used four times, which reads as house-style residue from a narration-smoothing pass rather than Zalthir's voice. This is a punctuation-and-repetition spot-edit, not a re-narration — the interrogation beats themselves (the mind-control collapse, the book reveal, the Julius Caesar aphorism) land solidly on-spec.
