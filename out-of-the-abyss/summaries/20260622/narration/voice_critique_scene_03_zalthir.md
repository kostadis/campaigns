# Voice Critique — Zalthir, scene 03: Splitting the Leads

**Narration:** summaries/20260622/narration/session_doc_scene_03_splitting_the_leads.scrubbed.md
**Voice spec:** voice/zalthir_voice.md
**Per-char examples:** examples/zalthir.md

## Flags

### [1] mechanical-em-dash

> He had been filling the air with noise since we arrived — useful noise, I'll grant him that, but still noise — and the current iteration was:

**Why:** Narration-level em-dashes (the italicized Fembris quote that follows is exempt; these two are not). Spec calls for "short and stacked" declarative sentences, not dash-linked run-ons.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He had been filling the air with noise since we arrived. Useful noise, I'll grant him that. Still noise. The current iteration was:"

### [2] mechanical-em-dash

> The third-story apartment in Janussi's chambers was still unvisited — we knew the wrapping paper from the poisoned book had been taken there, and we hadn't gone back.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash joining two independent clauses.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The third-story apartment in Janussi's chambers was still unvisited. We knew the wrapping paper from the poisoned book had been taken there, and we hadn't gone back."

### [3] mechanical-em-dash

> The apartment was in Janussi's chambers — the wrapping paper trail led there, and there was still evidence about Kalan we hadn't turned over.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; a colon reads cleaner given the explanatory relationship.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The apartment was in Janussi's chambers. The wrapping paper trail led there, and there was still evidence about Kalan we hadn't turned over."

### [4] mechanical-em-dash

> I noticed this the way I notice the shape of a room before I notice the people in it — not as sentiment, not as the blood asserting itself, just as a tactical seam worth naming.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before an explanatory clause; a colon fits the relationship better than a dash.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I noticed this the way I notice the shape of a room before I notice the people in it: not as sentiment, not as the blood asserting itself, just as a tactical seam worth naming."

### [5] voice-spec-conflict

> I noticed this the way I notice the shape of a room before I notice the people in it — not as sentiment, not as the blood asserting itself, just as a tactical seam worth naming.

**Why:** The spec is explicit that "he doesn't explain these observations to himself; they're just true, and he moves on," and that heritage "isn't a lever for him... he doesn't reach for it to claim or excuse." This sentence spends three clauses disclaiming sentiment and blood before landing on the tactical read — it argues the point rather than just noting it and moving, which is the exact move the spec rules out.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I noticed this the way I notice the shape of a room before I notice the people in it. A tactical seam. I filed it and said nothing."

### [6] mechanical-em-dash

> The room produced a brief negotiation about the name. Daral. No — different person. The correct answer surfaced: Kalan Strongbranch.

**Why:** Not inside quotes or italics, so this is narration-level. A short correction like this reads cleaner with a comma.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Daral. No, different person."

### [7] mechanical-em-dash

> There is something almost monastic about how Fembris operates — the total commitment to the immediate task, no friction between decision and movement.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before an explanatory clause.
**Suggested rewrite:** "There is something almost monastic about how Fembris operates: the total commitment to the immediate task, no friction between decision and movement."

### [8] mechanical-em-dash

> Daz was the natural choice for it — he had the social architecture for delicate conversations, and he was drow, which was not irrelevant given what we knew about the Menzoberranzan thread running under all of this.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash joining two clauses.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Daz was the natural choice for it. He had the ear for delicate conversations, and he was drow, which was not irrelevant given what we knew about the Menzoberranzan thread running under all of this."

### [9] register-wrong-vocabulary

> Daz was the natural choice for it — he had the social architecture for delicate conversations, and he was drow, which was not irrelevant given what we knew about the Menzoberranzan thread running under all of this.

**Why:** "Social architecture" is abstract, analytical vocabulary the spec doesn't license for Zalthir. The spec's one "geometry" reference (Evasion: "he protects himself by ensuring the geometry is wrong for whoever's swinging") is about physical combat positioning, not social/epistemic structuring. This register — assessing a person's social skill in architectural terms — reads like Daz's wizard-analyst voice (party.md: Daz is the Evoker who works "social" angles), not Zalthir's plain physical noticing.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Daz was the natural choice for it. He knew how to make someone talk, and he was drow, which was not irrelevant given the Menzoberranzan thread running under all of this."

### [10] mechanical-em-dash

> Grygum was already moving for the stairs. He stopped, turned back — the deliberate pause of someone who has just remembered the one thing that shouldn't be forgotten.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash before an explanatory clause.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He stopped, turned back. The pause of someone who just remembered the one thing that shouldn't be forgotten."

### [11] generic-fantasy-prose

> He stopped, turned back — the deliberate pause of someone who has just remembered the one thing that shouldn't be forgotten.

**Why:** "The deliberate pause of someone who has just remembered the one thing that shouldn't be forgotten" is workshopped scene-description — it could sit in any narrator's section describing any character. Zalthir's own prose in the examples stays plainer and more physical ("He looks a little bit more carefully and notices a spider web").
**Suggested rewrite:** "He stopped, turned back. He'd remembered something."

### [12] register-wrong-vocabulary

> I had been working through the zone-of-truth architecture, the dragonborn angle, the sequencing of Kalan's arrival.

**Why:** "Architecture" (second occurrence in this scene) and "angle" stack three abstract-noun phrases in a list that reads like generic tactician/detective prose rather than Zalthir's terse notice-and-move-on register. The spec's only sanctioned use of this cluster of words is physical ("battlefield geometry" is explicitly named as a monastery class he skipped, not a tool he wields analytically).
**Suggested rewrite:** "I had been working through the zone-of-truth timing, the dragonborn opening, when Kalan would arrive."

### [13] cliché-simile

> The feast detail had been sitting in the back of my thinking like an unread note.

**Why:** "Like an unread note" is a mild, slightly modern-office simile (inbox/desk imagery) that doesn't match the specific, physical, often nautical or monastic imagery the examples use (spider silk, coiled rope, shards of ice). It's serviceable but generic.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The feast detail had been sitting there, unflagged."

### [14] register-wrong-vocabulary

> "That's what it says." A good call-out. Filed.

**Why:** "Call-out" is modern workplace/gaming slang (as in "good catch," "nice call-out") that breaks the plain, declarative economy the spec asks for. "Filed." on its own is exactly on-voice (spec: "Filed it." is a listed example) — the problem is the sentence in front of it.
**Suggested rewrite:** "A good catch. Filed."

### [15] mechanical-em-dash

> Three lines of inquiry. Three investigators. The work divided cleanly — cleaner than most things we attempted.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; the clause is a simple comparison, better served by a comma.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The work divided cleanly, cleaner than most things we attempted."

### [16] generic-fantasy-prose

> I stood in the space between all of it. Waiting. Watching. Ready to be wherever I was needed first.

**Why:** "Waiting. Watching." lands exactly on-spec (short, stacked, matches his stillness). But "Ready to be wherever I was needed first" tips into a generic eager-helper closing line — Zalthir doesn't frame himself as available-on-demand; he calculates and acts precisely, he doesn't volunteer readiness. Nothing in the spec or examples shows him narrating his own usefulness this way.
**Suggested rewrite:** "I stood in the space between all of it. Waiting. Watching."

## Verdict

The strongest recurring issue is em-dash overuse — ten narration-level dashes in fifty-four lines, most doing work a period or colon would do better and flattening the "short and stacked" cadence the spec calls for. The second issue is a pair of abstract-analyst word choices ("social architecture," "zone-of-truth architecture," "the dragonborn angle") that read like Daz's register bleeding into Zalthir's section rather than his own plain, physical noticing. Neither problem is structural — this is a spot-edit pass (punctuation conversions plus a handful of word-choice swaps), not a re-narration.
