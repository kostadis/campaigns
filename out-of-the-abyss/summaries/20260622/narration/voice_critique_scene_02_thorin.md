# Voice Critique — Thorin, scene 02: The Methodology of Murder

**Narration:** summaries/20260622/narration/session_doc_scene_02_the_methodology_of_murder.scrubbed.md

## Flags

### [1] Voice spec conflict — anachronistic pop-culture reference (special item, required evaluation)

> "Do you remember the holodeck episodes from Star Trek: The Next Generation, where they'd do murder mysteries?" I said. "Alright, I appreciate this — this is fun."

**Why:** The spec draws an explicit, load-bearing line between "Joe at the Table" and "Thorin in Narration." The table-speech section says real-world/meta references (VMware, Internet Explorer, executive-briefing analogies, Austin Powers) are "off-topic chatter" that "only shows up in pre/post-game banter... When the scene starts, he stays in lane." The narration-register section separately requires "plain-spoken, terrain-aware" prose with concrete vocabulary and rare abstractions "framed as physical facts" — never a 24th-century sci-fi franchise a dwarf raised by giants has no in-fiction way to know about. This line is not bracketed as an aside or a player joke; it's rendered as Thorin's actual in-scene spoken line to Elian and Grygum, which crosses the boundary the spec itself draws. **Verdict: this is voice drift, not a sanctioned device.** The spec has a slot for real-world analogy (mundane frames for supernatural problems — chicken pox/shingles, punk-band names) but that slot is for *grounding the fantastic in the mundane*, not for the reverse move of a fantasy character referencing a real 20th/21st-century media franchise by name. If this was genuine table banter Joe said in the moment, it belongs bracketed as an aside or cut, not delivered as Thorin's diegetic dialogue.
**Suggested rewrite:** Drop the named reference and keep the *shape* of the observation (someone treating a real murder like a parlor game) without the anachronism: "This is like one of those parlor mysteries where somebody stages a locked room just to watch people argue about it," I said. "Alright, I appreciate this — this is fun." [grounded in spec's "mundane frames" principle, without the franchise name]

### [2] Mechanical scan A — em-dash

> oned book, frames a colleague, and disappears the evidence — too elaborate. Which is exactly why it worked.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash (not dialogue/italics); the clause after the dash is a terse fragment reaction, which reads cleaner as its own sentence in Thorin's short, stacked style.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...disappears the evidence. Too elaborate. Which is exactly why it worked."

### [3] Voice spec conflict / register-wrong vocabulary

> There was a pattern here that said something about the incentive structures of a library full of scholars.

**Why:** "Structures" is on the register-wrong list, and the whole clause is an ungrounded abstraction — the spec requires Thorin's abstractions to be "framed as physical facts" (rage as a vector, a threshold you cross), not institutional-economics jargon. This reads like a narrator's essayistic aside, not a dwarf noticing a pattern.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Two men after a monograph in one afternoon. That told me something about how this library keeps score."

### [4] Register-wrong vocabulary (missed by mechanical scan B)

> Elian recalibrated.

**Why:** "Recalibrated" is a systems/engineering verb applied to a person changing their pitch mid-conversation — not on the literal banned list, but the same class of analytical default the scan is meant to catch, and out of step with Thorin's plain, physical verbs (land, strike, hold, break).
**Suggested rewrite:** "Elian backed up and tried again."

### [5] Mechanical scan A — em-dash (two instances, one sentence)

> Elian accepted the distinction, then offered, generously, that even if we failed to solve the murder — even if all I could write was that these techniques *would have* helped — the academic community would still approve.

**Why:** Both dashes are narration (not inside the italic span, which only covers "would have"); the parenthetical clause reads fine as commas.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...even if we failed to solve the murder, even if all I could write was that these techniques *would have* helped, the academic community would still approve."

### [6] Voice spec conflict — passive, ungrounded abstraction

> The case was made that counterfactual endorsements carried weight on their own merits.

**Why:** Passive construction with no actor, and "counterfactual endorsements carried weight on their own merits" is exactly the kind of free-floating abstraction the spec says Thorin doesn't produce — he thinks in "direct observations and immediate decisions," not institutional argot. Compare the spec's own throughline: he "picks a direction and shouts *East*."
**Suggested rewrite:** "Elian's pitch: even a maybe still counts. An endorsement doesn't need the murder solved to be worth something."

### [7] Voice spec conflict — passive, distanced narration

> The logic was presented. I considered it.

**Why:** Two passive/flat sentences that describe an argument happening near Thorin rather than Thorin engaging with it directly — the spec's Thorin narrates through action and physical framing ("buries feelings in observations"), not procedural summary.
**Suggested rewrite:** "He walked me through it. I turned it over."

### [8] Mechanical scan A — em-dash

> Stature. I thought briefly about Velkynvelve, which is where this all started — a dwarf in the wrong place, in chains, at the bottom of the world.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash. Note: the content of this sentence is strong, on-voice work (concrete, physical, restrained) — this is a mechanical-only flag, not a content flag.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...which is where this all started: a dwarf in the wrong place, in chains, at the bottom of the world."

### [9] Mechanical scan A — em-dash

> Collect facts from different angles — physical, testimonial, procedural.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; the list after it reads cleanly off a colon.
**Suggested rewrite:** "Collect facts from different angles: physical, testimonial, procedural."

### [10] Mechanical scan A — em-dash (two instances, one sentence)

> But the ground under your feet — the angle of a bridge, the width of a corridor, the placement of a glyph — that was fixed.

**Why:** Both dashes are narration. This whole paragraph is otherwise the strongest passage in the scene — it lands the spec's core giant-wisdom image ("a rock in the wrong place is just a rock...") almost verbatim and used exactly once, as the spec instructs ("use this sparingly"). This is a mechanical-only flag.
**Suggested rewrite:** "But the ground under your feet was fixed: the angle of a bridge, the width of a corridor, the placement of a glyph."

### [11] Convergence with house style — indirect literary construction

> "Oh wait, don't — please, I don't need another —" I started, because Elian was developing the expression of someone about to produce a second document.

**Why:** The dialogue and its internal dashes are protected (verbatim speech). The dialogue tag, though, is not — "developing the expression of someone about to produce a second document" is a roundabout, literary way of describing a facial expression. It reads like a distanced third-person-omniscient narrator's aside, not Thorin's blunt directness.
**Suggested rewrite:** "...I started, because Elian had the look of a man reaching for a second document."

### [12] Continuity / sense error — misapplied established term

> The document sitting in the Ember Grapple said the same thing in academic terms: magic doesn't close contradictions.

**Why:** Per the voice spec, "the Ember Grapple" is the party's named combat formation (Zalthir grapples and binds, Daz incinerates, Thorin holds position and intercepts) — not a location, container, or anything a document can be "sitting in." This isn't a tone problem, it's a misuse of a spec-established proper noun in a way that doesn't parse; it reads like the model pattern-matched on party vocabulary without tracking what the term actually names.
**Suggested rewrite:** "The document Elian gave us said the same thing in academic terms: magic doesn't close contradictions."

### [13] Mechanical scan A — em-dash

> Someone had used something more than a simple polymorph, or used it repeatedly, or used something else entirely — something we didn't have a name for yet.

**Why:** Narration-level em-dash; the trailing clause reads as a period-break beat, matching Thorin's short stacked-sentence rhythm elsewhere in this same paragraph ("It creates them.").
**Suggested rewrite:** "...or used something else entirely. Something we didn't have a name for yet."

## Verdict

The strongest issue is the Star Trek line at [1] — it's the one place Thorin breaks the fiction itself rather than just drifting register, and the spec's own "stays in lane" clause makes this an unambiguous call, not a close one. The recurring theme underneath everything else is that the scene's first half (the monograph/thesis banter with Elian, roughly lines 9–39) keeps sliding into passive, institution-flavored abstraction — "the case was made," "the logic was presented," "incentive structures" — exactly where the spec says Thorin stays concrete and direct; the second half (Velkynvelve, the terrain passage, Zalthir's deduction) is markedly stronger and needs only the em-dash mechanical fixes. This scene doesn't need a full re-narration — the back half is close to exemplary — but the front half (through the monograph negotiation) is worth a targeted rewrite pass, not just spot-edits, since three separate sentences in that stretch independently drift into the same ungrounded-abstraction register.
