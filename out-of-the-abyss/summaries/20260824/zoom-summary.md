
D&D Gaming Session - Candlekeep Vault - Meeting Summary

Pre-Game Conversation

Before the session began, participants discussed a home design story: a lamp was purchased and designers were asked to design a room around it. The original architect objected, arguing it would ruin the aesthetic of the house. The group debated whether clients have the right to override an architect's vision when they are paying the bills.

This led to an extended story about Renzo Piano, a famous architect hired by the Nierhuis Foundation (which donates approximately $500 million a year) to build a state-of-the-art research hospital in Thessaloniki, Greece. Renzo Piano declared the standard hospital layout — a central core surrounded by patient rooms for efficient patient transport — was wrong and redesigned it. This led to approximately two years of arguments with the speaker's father, a prominent figure in intensive care medicine, over functional vs. aesthetic decisions (e.g., door placement, window placement, hallway layout). The redesign required patients to navigate 3 stairs and 4 doorways to reach surgery from the ICU. The president of the Nierhuis Foundation noted he had managed to shut down a hospital in Greece but could not build one because the two parties could not agree on window placement. The speaker noted that during COVID, his father would relay these debates daily, as his mother has Alzheimer's and was no longer available as the usual audience.

The group also briefly discussed laptop efficiency (M5 vs. M1), the Owlbear virtual tabletop tool, Discord's per-user volume controls, and dermatology emergencies.




GM Session Recap (Previous Session Summary)

The GM read the following recap aloud:

With the battle in the Hightower finally over, the party secured ally Evanmore and stripped him of his key to the Inner Sanctum, giving it to Glabagul. Broken and desperate, Ally offered the party everything he had left. He revealed the existence of a secret room beneath Candlekeep holding artifacts of extraordinary power, guarded by a cryptogram he had spent years quietly researching. He knew the answers to two of its riddles: Fustiligus (a base insult magically enchanted centuries ago, hidden under one of the oversized black marble chess pieces) and a bow (the reply of the legendary halfling bard Bayar's statue).

The party regrouped in the investigator's office, where Ally Avonmore — whose photographic memory allowed him to reproduce the cryptogram in full — spread the riddle across the floor. Silvara Savickas arrived shortly after, grateful for the party's efforts and eager to help Candlekeep, though too weak from her illness to act herself, and still unaware that her son Mozicoto lay dead by the party's hand. She identified:
The true name of Miriam, the sentinel worm and dragon guardian of Candlekeep, as the answer to the first riddle.
The eastern light of Mystara's mantle, Limniz, as an obscure star recorded in the charts of the orrery of the Astronomicon — though she noted with unease that three unfamiliar figures had been seen inside the orrery when she passed it.

Working together, the party decoded the substitution cipher of the cryptogram, with Thorin leading the effort (assisted by a cryptogram sight) and a lively argument over whether certain glyphs were an I or a J. Grygum, solving in Palaiar, supplied the one line Thorin had missed. The full text revealed:

Feed the quill of Aluanda the seer, tread as many steps as he lived in years, utter the original prophecy to unseen ears, sprinkle dust of Mechanus on dormant gears, one last guardian of knowledge remains to verify the chosen reader's claims, and those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore may fearlessly pass the obsidian door.

Kaelin Strong Branch arrived moments later, eager to contribute a clue he had solved, only to find the party had already cracked the entire cipher. He recovered his composure and identified the statue of Oluwando in the Founder's Court.

The party made their way to the Founder's Court and the House of Aloando, where a great brass statue of the ancient seer stood upon a dais bearing a quill in an empty ink pot, worn smooth by centuries of superstitious scholars touching it for luck (the superstition being that touching the quill guarantees a well-critiqued piece of academia). After a brief debate over whether to offer ink, blood, or a poisoned offering, the party borrowed ink from the assembled scholars and poured it into the ink pot. The dais ground forward with a deep mechanical groan, revealing a long, unlit staircase descending into the darkness below. Thorin's observation — that in all those centuries nobody had thought to put ink in the pot — drew looks of horror from the scholars.

To know how many steps to count, the party needed to know how long Aluando lived. Rather than hunting down his biography in the Hall of Momentous Deeds, they traveled to the grove where Candlekeep's keepers of tomes were buried, finding his grave and learning he had died at the age of 97.

For the final ingredient — dust of Mechanus — the party made their way to the House of Mechanus, a rectangular building with a blue roof and a clock tower. The gnome librarian Spanner oversaw 13 Modrons (strange box-like mechanical creatures from the planes of Mechanus). Spanner explained the dust was the most perfect lubricant in existence, obtainable only by killing one of his Modrons (unacceptable) or by using specialized tools his Modrons had crafted. Spanner wanted collateral; Thorin pointed out they only needed the dust, not the tools. The party persuaded Spanner to lend them the tools, on the condition that Zaltir and Glabagul remain behind for a study of their mechanical symmetry — an interview Zaltir accepted on the condition of constructive feedback deferred until after the descent. The Modrons performed a strange synchronized dance as they deliberated.

Armed at last with everything they needed, the party descended the hidden staircase beneath Aloando's statue, counting each step toward the 97th. Daz positioned himself higher on the stairs for a clear line of sight while the others spread out below. Dragon poured 4 points of healing into Zaltir (who was 27 points down with no short rest taken). Thorin, suspicious of the GM's repeated questions about exact positions, was proved right: without warning, a 10-foot section of the staircase hinged open beneath their feet, catching everyone standing between the 93rd and 102nd steps. Daz, safely above, cast Telekinesis to arrest the fall. Everyone but Thorin caught hold of the stairs and scrambled clear. Thorin lost his footing entirely and plummeted downward into the vast circular well below, his fate uncertain as darkness swallowed him whole.

The party also brought one Avowed as a researcher — an expert on the history of Candlekeep who was not among the top scholars but was willing to talk at length.




Session Events

Descent and the Cylindrical Cavern

Doran fell through the trap along with Thorin. Zaltir flew after them (confirmed via wings and/or a potion of flying). Glabagul exclaimed "Wee!" while falling. After falling approximately 500 feet, the party hit a feather fall zone, making the descent safe. The remaining party members jumped to follow.

The single passage at the bottom led to a huge cylindrical cavern approximately 200 feet high and 100 feet across. The party entered the cavern halfway up the chamber via a small ledge. Below, a steaming lake of molten lava lay approximately 100 feet down. In the center of the chamber stood a three-floor stone tower on a thin pedestal of glassy black obsidian soaring from the lava pool. A double obsidian door stood 30 feet away from the party's ledge with no obvious way across. The base of the tower was surrounded by a narrow walkway; on the opposite side, a flat stone bridge led from the back of the tower to a second tunnel.

The Avowed scholar identified the tower as a classic example of the architectural style invented by Candlekeep's founding architect, noting that many such structures had been destroyed through needless rehabilitation.

The ledge featured several cogs and mechanical components embedded in the stone. Investigation (passive investigation 24, roll of 18) revealed the mechanical system was a way to extend the bridge. Using the Mechanus tool, the party extended the bridge with a grinding sound. A warning sign noted that the entire airspace was surrounded by an anti-magic field — later clarified to extend only 10 feet from the bridge (not the full 30 feet as initially stated).

The Owlbear Guardian and Three Questions

The party entered the tower's ground floor. The only noteworthy feature was a tiny iron statue of a winged owlbear atop an 8-foot-high stone pedestal, with a staircase behind it leading to the next floor. Upon entry, the statue's eyelids opened to reveal white glowing eyes; its wings unfolded and it flew up to hover 12 feet above the ground.

The guardian declared: "Halt, seekers! Before you consult the mysteries stored herein, you must prove yourselves a worthy keeper of secrets. Three questions you must answer before I let you pass."

Question 1: "Where is the only refuge for flames in Candlekeep?"
Answer: The hearth. (Daz recalled this from personal experience — it was the only warm place.)

Question 2: "Which holy triumvirate patron the great library?"
Answer: Oghma, Denir, and Milil. (Grygum answered with a roll of 26.)

Question 3: "How many feet does the High Tower of Exaltation reach above the sea?"
The Avowed scholar (Evaldo) noted this was a question every member of the Avowed was expected to know, but the practice of memorizing such details had long fallen out of fashion. He knew it was over 500 feet. Because the party brought the Avowed, the DC was reduced from 23 to 16 (nature check). Both Thorin and Daz rolled 20s and answered correctly.

The guardian declared: "You have answered all three questions correctly. You may pass."

The party noted that Manchun had apparently destroyed the previous owlbear guardian, and a new owlbear had taken its place.

The Spectral Silver Dragon

As the party prepared to go upstairs, they heard a cough from outside — from the direction of the lava. A spectral silver dragon appeared, the lava passing through her. She was visibly displeased.

She stated: "Someone entered this chamber without being a reader." She identified the intruder as Manchun, who had entered and was still inside. She confirmed the protections fell when he entered.

The party recognized her as the "unseen ears" from the cryptogram verse, and as the final guardian referenced in the line: "One last guardian of knowledge remains to verify the chosen reader's claims, and those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore may fearlessly pass the obsidian door."

She asked the party to identify who among them was the Reader and who were witnesses. Zaltir identified himself as the Reader (he had read the prophecy aloud). She asked him to tell her something about Candlekeep; he described the books the party had researched and tracked down. She accepted this and offered to grant one request.

Options the dragon could fulfill:
Hold the curtain: Widen the anti-magic band from 10 feet to 30 feet for 10 minutes.
Prevent Manchun from teleporting out.
Reveal what Manchun took (if he had already left with something).
Confirm whether all party members are who they say they are (relevant to Daz's mystery).
Fight beside the party — declined: "I was bound to be witness, not weapon. If I could have stopped him, I would have stopped him at dawn."

She engaged Grygum (cleric of Bahamut) in a brief philosophical exchange about the age, and Grygum's response — describing a new school holding that there are certain fixed points among which one may make one's own paths (the Gargami School) — earned her approval: "Wisdom truly flows from Bahamut into you, cleric of Bahamut."

The party chose: Hold the curtain — extend the anti-magic zone to 30 feet for 10 minutes, to be deployed when engaging Manchun (a powerful wizard). The dragon responded: "Well chosen."

Confronting Manchun

The party entered the vault on the second floor. The room contained a single bookcase against the eastern wall and a desk and chair in the middle. A note on the desk, written in archaic Common, read:

"This vault was built to contain Candlekeep's most dangerous secrets. Consult here what can benefit civilization. But leave the tomes on these shelves. Whilst the most holy, unholy knowledge can be used for good, should circumstances lead you, keeper of secrets, to believe that such dogma might be grasped by foul hands, you will find the means to destroy the contents of this vault in the chamber above."

The room contained approximately 100 of the most powerful magic books imaginable. Manchun was found sitting at the desk, reading. He looked up and said: "By all means, browse. The trick is leaving with what you came for. Did you come for the book, or did you come for me?"

The party declared they came for him, not the books. Manchun sighed and initiative was about to be rolled.

Daz's opening move: Before rolling initiative, Daz proposed moving the fight downstairs to avoid damaging the books. Manchun — noting that destroying books with magical spells and glyphs of warding would be a disaster — agreed. Daz rolled Persuasion with advantage (result: 22, meeting the required DC). Manchun cast a buff spell (Mirror Image) on himself before descending.

The party repositioned near the bridge (anti-magic zone starts 10 feet from the bridge entrance).

Initiative order: Daz (21), Thorin (19), Zaltir/Altair (17), Manchun (12), Grygum (3).

First round of combat:
Daz cast Phantasmal Killer (4th level) on Manchun, requiring a Wisdom DC 17 save. Manchun rolled an 8 on the die but has +9 Wisdom, totaling exactly 17 — he made the save. He took 13 points of psychic damage (half of 26) but did not gain disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls.
Daz's plan going forward: hit Manchun and retreat toward the bridge to draw him into the anti-magic zone, where the party can engage with crossbows.

Session ended here. The fight with Manchun is to continue next session.
