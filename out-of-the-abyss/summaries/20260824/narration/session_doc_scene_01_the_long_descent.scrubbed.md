Thorin was falling.

The stairs had done exactly what Thorin suspected they would. A section the length of a cart hinged open under everyone standing between the ninety-third and the hundred-and-second step. Daz, safely above, threw Telekinesis at the problem and arrested the fall; the rest of us got a hand on stone and scrambled clear. Thorin didn't. He lost his footing entirely and went down into the well, and the dark took him whole.

Grygum went through his prayers, looking for one that reached that far down.

"Nothing."

I was already at the edge. The plan, such as it was, was to grab him. I have wings. I almost never use them: I position, then I walk the last distance. This was a position problem, and the position was down.

I stepped off.

Grygum's voice came after me: "Are… are you trying to say you can fall faster than him?"

A fair question.

Glabbagool, fused to my forearm, screamed "WEE!" Somewhere far below, thin and receding, Dawnbringer: "NOT THIS AGAIN!"

Thorin had gone in with nothing left. Every out he had, spent on the way through the gap. Everything anyone tried had failed, all of it, at once. It was almost impressive.

There was a class about this at the monastery: aerial pursuit, or falling with intent, something in that family. Brother Ostrin taught it, or possibly the short one with the burn scar. I wasn't listening. What I knew without the class was simple enough: wings do not catch a body that is already falling. You catch a faller by falling with him. So I folded the wings and dropped.

The dark was total. Good. Total dark is my ground. Five hundred feet down the shaft stopped being a distance and became a door, and I remembered I could teleport. Shadow Step. I stepped through the dark and came out beside Thorin, and he grabbed me. Held on, both arms, an actual hug. We spread out into a controlled fall together.

Glabbagool offered that one should always be Zen.

"Yes, we're very… we're very zen at the moment," I said.

Glabbagool wondered whether this was too much hugging.

"There are worse things about Google," I said.

Thorin patted his chest and established, to his own satisfaction, that he was not dead. Then, in the ooze's defense: "I mean, Glabbagool's all about the hugging, let's be clear." And, warming to it: "What is this oozing without being ooze?"

Below us the well kept going. Another five hundred feet, at least.

"I mean, I think the point was to go down, wasn't it?" I said. "How about we go gracefully down and just see what's there?"

From far above, Grygum: "Hey, you guys okay down there?"

"So far."

Thorin shouted up that he was still kicking. The scholar we'd dragged along called "Come back!" down the shaft, and Grygum shouted something the well ate half of.

"We could at least go scout a bit," I said.

"Sounds good," Thorin said.

We dropped the last five hundred feet and the air caught us. Thick, sudden, gentle. A featherfall woven into the bottom of the shaft, sitting there the whole time like a net under a rope walk.

"Oh," I said. "So there was no danger here."

The well did not apologize.

We shouted the news up, and then came the jumping.

"Oh, God, that's gonna be hard for me," Grygum said.

"Literally? Like, should someone push you, or…"

"I'll just say a quick prayer, and I don't know… Take it from me." A pause, the length of a prayer. "And… and hope that the featherfall works for me, too."

He jumped on faith. It has not failed him yet.

Daz went next, reasoning it out loud on the way over the edge. "Yeah, I mean, I don't want to get split from the party. My whole, like, trying to hold the thing up and save us all didn't work, so…"

"If all your friends jumped off the stairs, would you?" Grygum asked.

"Yeah," Daz said.

"Oh, shit," I said.

Then Edvaldo, his voice wobbling down the shaft: "I've — you know, this is… this is an area that has been… I didn't even know it existed."

"Come on in, the water's fine," Thorin called up.

"I intend to write a very long monograph."

He jumped too. The featherfall took them one by one and set them down soft, a thousand feet below anything Candlekeep had a map for.

A single tunnel led straight ahead. The point, as I'd said, was to go down. Here we were.
