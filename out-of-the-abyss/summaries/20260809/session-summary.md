# Session 2026-08-03

Date: 2026-08-03 (played) — exported 2026-08-09

## Summary


A'lai told the surviving Thug to ignore the cleric and go after Daz, shouting that the wizard had the key — "Don't go after the cleric! Go after the wizard! The wizard's the one that we want, he's got the key!" — though in truth Grygum quietly held both the key and the gem. The thug swung twice at Grygum with his mace and missed both times while the caustic brew still clinging to him burned away another 8 points of his life, then broke and ran, giving Grygum and Thorin free swings as he fled. Daz misty-stepped into position and conjured a massive explosion of fire that engulfed the surviving thug — a 25-point roll, halved to 12 by his successful save (he rolled a 17), leaving him alive on exactly one hit point — and the successful detonation sent shockwaves of a different kind through all of Candlekeep — bells rang, screams echoed, and scholars scrambled for their notebooks, because the fireball's very existence proved that the library's legendary magical wards had been broken. As Daz put it at the table, "everybody is gonna realize that… Candlekeep is now in its underwear." A'lai killed the wounded thug himself and then, hands trembling, proposed a surrender: "I'd like to stop, but not surrender, I'm aware how that sounds." He admitted his arsenal was spent — "I have nothing left. You will have probably counted how many spells I've casted… I'd be insulted if you hadn't" — and revealed he knew the location of a secret room beneath the keep, the two lines of a cryptogram that mattered, and claimed to know the name of a figure in Menzoberranzan who had been paying to keep a certain drow breathing — canon records this as an intuition about a *house*, not a name: "I would very much like to tell all of it to somebody with a locked door." He begged not for freedom, but for a cell inside Candlekeep's magic-nullifying prison — the one place he believed he might survive what was coming — warning that "in about 4 minutes, people will be coming up these stairs, will be very disappointed in me, and you strike me as the more reasonable disappointment." Grygum used restorative magic to bring Zalthir back to consciousness from zero of his 57 hit points, and the party began to consider the wizard's offer.

Before they could act, a Zhentarim assassin appeared. A'lai, hands bound, looked at the newcomer and said coolly, "Took your time, but they have had me since the landing… It's not a loss, it's a position, I've been in the room, I know exactly who is carrying what." He nodded toward Daz. The assassin launched a brutal assault, striking Daz again and again — 20 points, then 23 — until the wizard crumpled unconscious to the floor from 30 hit points to zero. The assassin then searched Daz's body for the sapphire — and found nothing, his horror plain on his face, for the gem had long since been passed to Grygum. Thorin's fury was immediate and absolute. He struck the assassin with a menacing blow that filled the man with dread — "Now he's frightened… Because I'm so badass" — then unleashed a relentless flurry of attacks — including a devastating critical radiant strike from Dawnbringer for 18 points — that left the assassin crumpled and broken on the High Tower floor. "Fuckin' A!" Grygum rushed to Daz and poured healing energy into him — 40 hit points' worth — pulling him back from the edge of death, while A'lai Aivenmore looked on in tearful panic, his last hope of rescue lying dead at his feet, sobbing "you don't understand."

The party secured A'lai — Zalthir binding him with his **Street Justice** feat — and stripped his High Tower key from him, taking care not to bring the two keys together. Thorin quietly slipped the key to Zalthir, and it was ultimately tucked away inside Glabbagool for safekeeping — the grey ooze standing just a little taller with pride at the responsibility. Sensing that remaining in the room would be catastrophic — the GM's "Do you want to stay in the room while he explains it to you, or do you want to leave the room?" struck Thorin as "a very loaded question… like, you know, roll for initiative type of question" — the party moved A'lai out of the High Tower lobby, and the GM confirmed it on the spot: "you made the right choice." The wisdom of it was proven moments later, when Manshoon's simulacrum arrived. It conjured a wall of force and shattered the magical door that the two keys were meant to protect, breaching Candlekeep's inner sanctum through sheer, overwhelming power. He had arrived alone and visibly weakened, having expended enormous magical resources to get there — the attrition around the party had neutralized Alkrist, left Bookwyrm and Janussi dead, A'lai captured, and Kalan fled, so "5 of these wizards are out" of the Avowed who were meant to be the final line of defense, and none of Manshoon's goons could come with him. But he had arrived. And A'lai, trembling in the party's custody, revealed the one card he still held: he had never told Manshoon the truth about what lay inside the depository. That secret, and whatever A'lai intended to trade it for, would have to wait for another day.
