# The Right Heroes — the retreat, and the Undying Temple

Co-GM working doc. **Arc prep, not a single session** — this covers **DDEX3-16 Part 5's scripted withdrawal (§255) and the whole of Part 6 (§261–§382)**. Written ahead of schedule, on request, in case the table moves faster than expected. Move to `session_prep/` when it has a date.

Party: **12th level** — Akritas, Bramgrim, Daein, Felkur, the Owlbear. **Players: Daein and Felkur.**
Register: **the war was always the diversion.** Part 6 is the only part that changes the ending (§382). Everything before it buys the time to reach it.

Companion docs: `notes/ddex316_structure.md` (module skeleton, house rules), `notes/maerimydra_the_audit.md` (the ledger heist, which **must** resolve before the retreat), `notes/session_prep/20260830_nobody_came_looking.md` (Part 2).

## The one thing that un-confuses it

**The army was never going to take the city, and the module knows it.** §255 fires regardless of performance. §162 guarantees the assault is marred even if the party plays perfectly — if the first three offscreen objective checks succeed, **the fourth automatically fails.**

So Part 5's retreat is not a fail state. It is the module **switching games**: the mass battle ends, and the actual adventure — a dungeon into the Undying Temple to break Graz'zt's anchor — begins.

**Do not let it read as defeat at this table.** This party spent two modules personally recruiting five factions, and the coalition-as-renown house rule means the offscreen failures have their friends' names on them. A rout lands as *your recruitment didn't matter* unless the register is right, and the register was set at the muster: **kill Hledh, provoke chaos, make the enemy look the wrong way.** Taking the city was never the brief.

### Ruling 1 — the Harper gate, in detail

**Bramgrim is a Harper.** Alder commissioned him in Ch24, presumably with the same quill she signed his sword with, and the gate the module gates on (§213) is open. **Part 4's Rescue branch fires normally**: Elanil comes in person to tell them Solom was taken at the Eastern Gate by a gaunt drow woman in the piwafwi of House T'sarran.

**The price, which the muster doc never wrote because it recommended against Bramgrim taking it:**

> He asked a legendary hero to sign his sword. She gave him a job instead, and the job — **Handout One, verbatim — is *"protect Solom Ned'razak at all costs."***
>
> **Solom Ned'razak is a clone, and Bramgrim knows it.** He now holds a formal, recorded obligation to an organisation that keeps records, to protect a fraud he is actively helping the party conceal from six allied contingents. **That is a much better price than either of the ones offered to the players**, and it costs him something no faction leash costs Felkur or Daein: he is the party's moral anchor, and he has just been made complicit on paper.

**Two consequences worth playing:**

1. **Elanil is "a public agent of the Harpers."** Bramgrim now has a nominal peer relationship with the coalition's commander — and she already knows Alder is behind everything and resents it bitterly. **She will treat him differently after Ch24 and he will not enjoy it.**
2. **The Harpers debrief people.** Bramgrim is now on a list, and he is the party member holding the single most damaging secret in the campaign. **If anyone ever formally asks him what happened to the original Solom Ned'razak, he is the worst possible person to be asked.**

> **The Part 4 fork does not collapse — see `maerimydra_the_audit.md`, updated.** Bramgrim was the Rally the Lines advocate and now carries the brief arguing the opposite. **Do not give him both.** The module supplies two NPC advocates already — **Coralynn Winsail** for the North Gate (§209) — **she met this party in the Hillsfar arc and watched them free the city**, see `notes/npcs/coralynn_winsail.md` — and **Elanil** for Solom (§213). **Bramgrim's job is to be visibly torn, say so once, and defer to the players.**

### Ruling 2 — the Symbols, and what the final battle actually is

**Module canon (§377).** At the top of the U29 fight a portal tears open — **Vizeran's ritual, firing.** Demons are dragged screaming through it. Then the seven symbols glow and **the demon standing in front of each is paralysed in dark red flame.** Graz'zt laughs: *"Vizeran you fool… I'll be staying here, lord and master of Toril."* The symbols are his anchors; the seven demons are what they run on; **Elisande destroys one per round** by pulling a paralysed demon's heart out.

**Your Crystal Cascade** (`20260622_szith_morcane_the_keystone_is_dead.md`) says each symbol binds one of the *other* lords, that the OOTA table is banishing them offscreen, that Graz'zt therefore walks in pre-shattered — and *"in U29, replace Elisande with the live cascade."*

> **The two designs were competing for the same seven objects. They no longer are.**

## ⭐ The design: **Hold**

**The final battle is not a damage race. It is a discipline test, and the correct play is to not fight.**

When the anchors begin to fail, **Graz'zt's demons turn on each other.** Not scripted chaos — *causal* chaos, and the party was told the rule years ago, by you, at the table:

> *"If you put a demon in charge, they're gonna try to break the deal, because that's what demons do."*

**That is the whole mechanism.** Graz'zt's hold on that room is a deal, the deal is anchored to the symbols, and every symbol that goes dark is a clause failing. **The demons do the rest themselves.**

So the party's job in the climax is **exactly what Yestral's army is doing three hundred feet below them: hold the line long enough.** They do upstairs what the coalition does downstairs, and *"we bought you the time"* stops being a consolation and becomes the literal victory condition.

### The cue — make it Felkur's, not an NPC's

**Do not have anyone tell them to wait.** Have the thing that has been telling them all campaign tell them again.

**Felkur's Shard of Crystallized Faerzress** has been the Cascade's barometer since Szith Morcane — the object that reads *who just fell*. In U29 it does what it has always done, and it does it **continuously**:

> The shard in Felkur's pocket has been warm since the bridge. Now it is *hot*, and it is not stopping — it is the feeling of standing next to a wall that something enormous is hitting from the other side, over and over, and getting closer.

**A player reads that correctly or does not.** If nobody does by the end of round two, **Ausuriel says it** — one line, in pain, before Graz'zt strips her shape:

> "Do not spend yourselves. **They are already coming.**"

### The clock — **two hands on it**

**Symbols fall from two directions at once, and they feel completely different.**

| Source | Texture |
|---|---|
| **Elisande** | Intimate and appalling. A thirteen-year-old walks up to a paralysed demon, puts a dagger in its chest, **pulls its heart out**, and giggles. Close, bloody, hers. |
| **The Cascade** | Cosmic and distant. **A gem just explodes.** Nobody in the room touched it. Somewhere unimaginably far away, another party finished another demon lord, and the wall came apart. |

**Alternate them.** Round by round, one symbol goes — sometimes she takes it, sometimes it detonates on its own, mid-sentence, while everyone is busy.

### The gems exploding — run it as a hazard, not a cutscene

They are carved into the eastern and western walls of a crowded room.

> The symbol on the east wall goes off like a struck bell made of glass.
>
> **Stone and burning crystal across a thirty-foot spread.** The demon paralysed in front of it does not get out of the way. Neither does anything else standing there.

- **Treat each detonation as a real hazard** — a burst everyone in range saves against, party and demons alike. **It does not care whose side anyone is on.**
- **The paralysed demon in front of it dies with it.** Elisande does not need to visit that one.
- **The room thins itself twice over:** once from the blast, once from the demons who turn on each other as the deal fails.

### ⭐ And they can name who just died

**This is the payoff of the entire Crystal Cascade conceit, cashed in as a combat mechanic.**

Each detonation carries **the per-lord sensory tell the party has been learning to read for months** — and **Felkur's Shard**, or **Bramgrim's Religion**, reads it instantly:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Demogorgon** | Two snarling voices out of sync, then silence |
| **Orcus** | A wave of grave-cold; **and every madness on the party clears** |
| **Yeenoghu** | A hyena's laugh swelling to a roar |
| **Baphomet** | A far-off bellow, the smell of a blood-soaked maze |
| **Fraz-Urb'luu** | Reality stutters; a false memory, then gone |

*(Zuggtmoy and Juiblex are already spent — Agaricus reported their defeat at the muster.)*

> **They have been hearing these for two months and not knowing what they were.** In U29 they are standing inside it, they can name each one as it lands, and **every single one makes Graz'zt smaller.**
>
> **Say the tell. Do not say the name.** Let Felkur's player say the name. That is the moment the campaign's longest-running mystery pays, and it should come out of a player's mouth, in combat, out loud.

### What it does to Graz'zt

Each symbol — hers or the Cascade's — lands its per-lord effect. **Call every one out loud as it happens.**

> He is a creature of enormous, unhurried composure, and **the first symbol is the first time all night he is not in control of his voice.** By the fourth he has stopped talking to the party as prospects. By the last he is standing in a room where nothing is on his side, short 230 hit points, two Legendary Actions, two Legendary Resistances, and every 1/day and 3/day spell he has.
>
> **Then they kill him.**

> **And this dissolves the rate problem completely.** It no longer matters how many crystals you detonated in prior sessions — **whatever is left blows out here, live.** Fire them beforehand whenever they land best; the remainder arrives on schedule in U29.

### Why it isn't passive — and this is the part to get right

**"Wait" must never mean "do nothing."** Four things keep the players' hands full:

1. **Graz'zt does not wait.** He is on them from round one. They are surviving a CR 24 with a clock running, which is not passive in any sense.
2. **Everything in the room wants Elisande.** She is killing them and they know it. **The party's actual job is bodyguarding a thirteen-year-old** while a demon lord tries to reach her — and if she dies the symbols stop falling and they lose.
3. **The demon civil war is terrain, not scenery.** They have to move through it, and it is trying to kill everyone including them.
4. **They feel him weakening in real time.** Call each effect out loud as it lands. A boss visibly coming apart while you refuse to hit him is a *better* tension than a damage race.

### ⭐ Graz'zt's counter-play: he needs them to attack him

**This is the fight's real antagonist logic and it makes his §377 recruitment offer part of the machine.**

He has CHA 26, Deception +15, Persuasion +15, truesight 120 ft and telepathy. **He can see that the correct play is for them to hold. So his entire game is to break that discipline** — because a party that commits to killing him dies, and a party that holds wins.

So he **offers** (the job, the name of Daein's home, Felkur's record erased, Akritas's ledger). He **taunts**. He goes after Elisande specifically to force them to close. He hurts someone slowly to make it unbearable to stand still.

> **A player who works out that Graz'zt is baiting them into fighting him has understood the whole encounter**, and that realisation is worth more than any roll in the campaign.
>
> And it is the exact inverse of his own strategy: **he built the Temple so he would never be forced to fight on someone else's terms, and he loses because he cannot make anyone fight on his.**

### What each ending means now

- **They hold, and he falls.** Killed by an offscreen war, a fey's smuggled child, and four people who worked out that the answer was to not swing. *"The smartest demon lord outsmarted himself"* — **and the June 22 payoff survives intact, because the children who did it to him are still the ones who did it to him.**
- **They break and fight.** He is CR 24 with 346 HP and they are four 12th-level PCs. **Do not fudge this.** §380 already tells you what happens: if they flee, he does not chase — he is safe on the Material Plane with his plan complete, and teleports to places unknown.

# Part 5 — The Retreat

## The Win Condition

**The party walks out of the city believing they bought something, and the doc is right that they did.**

Not "they feel okay about losing." They should be able to name the purchase: Hledh's forces are disorganised, the derro are spent, the city is looking the wrong way, and **something is stirring in the stone above the castle** that nobody would have found if the army had not come and made this much noise.

## The read-aloud, and the one word to change

Module text (§255), Elanil:

> "I don't know what that thing was, but the swarm of derro has caused as much chaos in the city as it has amongst our people. Even so, it seems unlikely we will be able to push any further into the city. The giants and demons have rallied and are pushing back. We're going to have to retreat. **I think we have failed.**"

> **Keep the line. Do not soften it.** Elanil believing they failed is correct and in character, and it is the setup for §261, where Agaricus and Yestral talk her out of it four minutes later. **The party watches a commander lose faith and get it back** — that is a better scene than a commander who was fine all along.
>
> **What you change is not her line. It is who is in the room when she says it.** Put the coalition's survivors on stage: Rilira's farmers, Grundar's duergar, whatever is left of Semdran's rangers, the myconids. Let the party see the bill. Then let Yestral say the thing about holding the line.

## The long rest, and the save that fires there

A long rest is permitted before Part 6 — **and it triggers the standing regional effect**: any humanoid spending 1 hour within 1 mile of Graz'zt makes a **DC 23 Wisdom save or gains a madness level.** Success grants 24-hour immunity.

**DC 23 at 12th level is brutal.** This is what the braincaps were issued for, and it is the moment the party finds out. Roll it in the open.

- **Braincap = *greater restoration*.** Clears madness, and grants advantage on the next madness save within the hour.
- **A second inside 24 hours = 1d4 madness levels, no save.** If they burned theirs in Part 2, this is where that decision arrives.
- Sovereign Agaricus's people can resupply **if the myconid contingent survived** and if the party is on good terms. **Make that contingent's survival matter here**, retroactively, and let them notice.

## The deadline nobody told them about

**Anything still inside the Spire when the retreat is called stays there.** The ledger, the journal, Vraxen, all of it. If the heist is unfinished, this is the last moment it exists — and the party should feel the door closing without being warned about it.

> If Akritas's Enclave brief is still unfulfilled at this point, **she says nothing.** She has been overruled twice and she does not revisit. Let a player be the one to remember.

# Part 6 — The Undying Temple

## What Graz'zt is actually doing (§038)

The Undying Temple is a former temple to **Kiaransalee**, built inside a stalactite for research into **undeath and planar travel**, half of it magically shifted into the Ethereal Plane. Graz'zt revived it and **regrew its heart — not with necrotic energy, but with the raw chaos of the Abyss.**

He knows Vizeran DeVir intends to summon all the demon lords together so they destroy each other and banish themselves. **The Temple makes him immune to being forcibly moved between planes** — and gives him permanent access to both the Material Plane and the Abyss through the Ethereal overlap, so he can convert the Material into another layer of his realm.

> **He is not here to conquer Maerimydra. Maerimydra is the scaffolding.** Hledh's war, the fire giants, the whole occupation is a construction site he needed to be left alone on. That reframes everything the party has done for two modules, and a player should get to say it out loud.

## The handoff (§261)

The leaders gather. Elanil: *"I don't know that we can take the city anymore."* Yestral: *"I agree. It appears no longer militarily possible."*

Then **Agaricus, in their heads**:

> "If we withdraw, we have lost forever. **Something stirs in the stone above the castle.** Whatever the Dark Prince plans, if he succeeds, we should not expect to survive."

Yestral looks up. *"We could hold however. Hold for a while at least. Time enough for heroes to stop whatever it is."*

Elanil smiles. ***"Now we just need the right heroes."***

> **Stage direction.** Elanil does not look at the party when she says it. **Everyone else does.** Hold the silence and let a player volunteer — this is Alder's Plan B activating in public, and it should feel like a debt coming due rather than a compliment.

## The bridge (§262)

> "Stand near me and be ready."
>
> For a moment you feel disoriented, and then you are on a bridge connecting the highest tower of the castle with a massive hanging stalactite. Sitting on the railing, **covered head to toe with blood**, is a small, smiling human girl and a blind goat. **Bloody footprints lead from the castle tower door to the rail.**
>
> "Willkommenm Outsides! Eyes wishin' to be on greet avontuur wit youse! Wese hab greet fun."

Then their teleporting ally leaves. *"I see you have ample company. I'll be on my way to see what I can do down below."*

> **Do not explain her.** She has been unexplained for the entire campaign and she stays unexplained until §379. **The broken pidgin is the module's, keep it.** The blood is not hers and the footprints lead *out* of the tower they are about to enter.

## The temple — general features (§264)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ceilings** | 9 ft unless noted |
| **Dimensional Lock** | No teleporting *through* the walls; **teleporting within works.** Plane-shifting only to Material / Ethereal / Abyss, and **only within the tower.** ***Banishment does not function at all.*** |
| **Light** | Braziers, dim light, Material side only. The Ethereal provides its own. **Ethereal fog limits sight to 60 ft.** |
| **Sound** | Material: screams, moans, thumps, insane singing throughout. **Ethereal: muted.** |
| **Doors** | Same smooth stone as the walls. **Every door is concealed — DC 15 Perception to find.** AC 17, 27 HP. Locked doors: DC 20 Thieves' Tools or DC 25 Strength. |
| **Traps** | DC 20 Perception; DC 20 to disable non-magical, **DC 25 magical** |
| **Walls** | Magically reinforced marble |

**Ethereal Solids (§276) — the temple's real gimmick.** Half the structure was built in stone and shifted to the Ethereal. That half is **invisible and insubstantial from the Material side.**

- Characters in the ethereal portion **move in any direction as if flying** — which is why there are **no staircases, only holes in the floors and ceilings.**
- Ethereal characters pass through Material-only walls (which are opaque to them) but **cannot pass ethereal walls** — those are as solid to them as stone.
- **Material-side characters with *see invisibility* or *true seeing* can see ethereal creatures and objects, and can walk straight through ethereal walls.**

> **Bramgrim's *True Seeing* is the map.** He has been the party's illusion-and-lore specialist all campaign and this is the dungeon built for him. **Do not gate the temple behind a puzzle he solves in one casting — let him solve it, and make the cost the spell slot.**

## Running it (§263, the module's own advice)

> *"It is okay to gloss over or summarize those rooms that have nothing of value... or play up the simple fights such as the two drunken drow warriors as foregone conclusions."*

**Take that advice.** Two mapped levels, a lot of living quarters. **Prep the four things that matter and narrate the rest:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Matliedun** (§424) | Male cambion, blood of Graz'zt, **master of the temple's security.** The Fortify Gains prisoner named him in Part 2 — that intel pays off here. |
| **Bexron** | The glabrezu holding **Maforie Chûmavh** (§285). |
| **Maforie Chûmavh** (§285) | Drow noblewoman, lifelong captive, malnourished. **The Lords' Alliance secret mission is to rescue her and keep her alive.** She gives the party the temple's shape — the west door and downstairs, the portal on the floor above to "a strange place filled with fog where you can fly" *(DC 15 Arcana: the Ethereal Plane)*, and that the Dark Prince's closest followers have not been seen for a day. **They are all in the other half.** |
| **U29, the Greater Temple** (§375) | The end. |

**The half-demon babies.** Felkur took the Zhentarim's contract at the muster: retrieve newborn children of Graz'zt from Castle Maerimydra to raise as Black Network operatives. §382 confirms they exist and that **the factions take custody of any the party rescues.** They are in here. Felkur's private plan was to hand the Zhentarim decoy goods and keep the ledger — **that plan does not survive contact with actual infants**, and that is the scene.

## The orb (U21, §340)

Worth staging on the way up, because it tells them what the temple is:

> Opposite the holes that lead up and down, the entire right half of the wall is open, revealing the side of **a huge fiery globe hanging outside the tower. Drow faces, contorted in agony, scream soundlessly in the dancing flames.** The room is filled with extreme heat.

> **That is the heart Graz'zt regrew, and it is made of Kiaransalee's dead.** Say nothing. Bramgrim's Religion, or Velasta if she is present, can name whose faces those are.

# U29 — The Greater Temple (§375)

## The room

> A massive **six-fingered hand** is inlaid in black tile upon the red marble floor. **The symbol shimmers like it is wet.** A huge throne, made of the corpses of hundreds of drow, is against the northern wall.
>
> A handsome six-fingered, bare-chested man with black skin and green eyes stands before it, **a wavy greatsword dripping with ichor** in hand, and a fire giant woman with **leathery wings and hair of flames** beside him.
>
> **Seven symbols** are carved into the eastern and western walls, glowing with a dark fiery light like the orb outside.
>
> The room is **crowded with demons** — barely enough room to enter. Most look ecstatic. **The seven standing directly in front of each symbol look upset.**

*(Per Ruling 2, if you take the reconciliation: **two of the seven symbols are already black**, and there is nobody standing in front of them.)*

## The opening — Vizeran's ritual fires (§377)

> Suddenly a swirling light flares into being in the centre of the room. **Like a hole in space**, the air is sucked into it and a far-off roar can be heard. **Demons around you begin to scream as many are dragged through the portal.** Then each of the symbols begins to glow and just as suddenly the swirling light dissipates. The demon in front of each symbol stands stock still, **bathed in dark red flame and paralysed.**
>
> Graz'zt gives a full-throated laugh. **"Vizeran you fool. Let the others dance to your strings and kill each other. I'll be staying here, lord and master of Toril. Soon all will revel with me!"**

## **Graz'zt's offer — play this properly, it is the scene**

He turns to them **for the first time**, and offers a pleasant smile.

> **"There is no need for us to conflict. I reward my servants well and as you can see"** *(he gestures to the largely empty room)* **"I have some new openings in my organization."**

> **This is a real offer and it must be played as one.** CHA 26, Deception +15, Persuasion +15, truesight 120 ft, telepathy. He can see every one of them completely and he is the best negotiator any of them have ever met.
>
> **What he has to sell, per PC, and he knows all of it:**
>
> - **Felkur** — a false identity, a criminal legend, the Brotherhood of the Cloak hunting the Phantom Thief, a devil owed his location, and a voided Maram contract. **Graz'zt can make every one of those go away with a sentence, and he is the one entity in the setting who would find the whole résumé charming rather than damning.**
> - **Daein** — origins unclear even to him, from "Far Far Away," and chasing FU money. **Graz'zt knows where Daein is from.** He does not say so. He *implies* that he could.
> - **Akritas** — an Enclave order she concealed for an arc, and a ledger she never got. He can produce it.
> - **Bramgrim** — nothing. Offer him nothing. **Let that be conspicuous**, and let Bramgrim notice he was not worth bidding on.
>
> **Do not rush past this into initiative.** Let both players actually answer. The correct amount of table time is however long they want, and if one of them hesitates, **do not have an NPC break the tension for them.**

## The cambion rumour, detonating

The party told Maerimydra's demons that Graz'zt had withdrawn favour from Hledh's giants. **The room is full of demons and he is standing in it.**

> Somewhere in the ecstatic crowd, a cambion the party has never met is working out that everything he has been told for two years is wrong — right up until the portal drags him through the ceiling. **If Iaveth survived Part 2 and the party bought his hedge, he is in this room.** Put him where they can see him, once, and do not comment.

## The fight

**Graz'zt** — MTF, **CR 24, AC 20, 346 HP.** Truesight 120 ft, telepathy 120 ft, all languages. Immune poison; resists cold, fire, lightning. Magic Resistance. **Legendary Resistance 3/day.** Shapechanger.

- **Multiattack:** two attacks with **Wave of Sorrow** (+13, reach 10 ft, **20 (4d6+6) slashing + 10 (3d6) acid**).
- **Teleport:** 120 ft, as an action *or* a legendary action.
- **Legendary Actions:** *Attack* · *Dance, My Puppet* (a charmed creature uses its reaction to move as he directs) · *Sow Discord* (*crown of madness* or *dissonant whispers*) · *Teleport*.

> **Verify his innate spellcasting against MTF p. 149 before play** — the 5etools export did not render that block cleanly, and the symbol effects above key off his **1/day and 3/day** spells specifically.

**Also present:** **Hledh Hellspawn**, **Squallocks the Vrock** (Graz'zt's advisor), and a crowd of demons.

**§378 adjustments — for a party of four 12th-level PCs, use *strong* as the floor:**

| Tier | Change |
|---|---|
| Strong | Replace 1 vrock with a **hezrou** (wears the robe). **Hledh has *dispel evil and good* active, DC 18.** |
| Very strong | Replace 1 vrock with a **glabrezu** (robe). **Graz'zt loses 5 fewer HP per symbol.** Hledh's *dispel evil and good* at **DC 23**. **Squallocks has a *scroll of maze* (DC 18), used round two** — not lootable. |

> **A CR 24 solo against four 12th-level PCs is not survivable without the symbols coming down, and that is the design.** The fight is a clock, not a slugfest: **Elisande is the damage.** Your job is to keep the party alive long enough for her to work, and to make them realise that protecting a blood-soaked child is the win condition.

## Elisande's mechanism (§379)

**The first time Elisande acts**, she skips across the room toward a frozen demon, the goat walking calmly behind. Graz'zt notices them for the first time:

> **"Do not think I do not see you, Ausuriel. Show us your pretty smile."**
>
> Making a cruel gesture in the air, **the goat falls to the ground in agony and her body melts away** until you see a screaming gnome-like woman with extremely long hair and goat legs.
>
> **"You should have stayed in your woods. You have no power here, Herald of Shiallia."**
>
> Through pain-gritted teeth, the goat-legged woman whispers: **"That is why I brought these heroes and the daughter of the Dark Sun."**
>
> A smile across her blood-splattered face, Elisande **plunges her dagger into the chest of the unmoving demon and pulls out its heart.** With a swirl of scintillating light, the demon disappears and the symbol goes dark. Graz'zt screams in pain. **"What have you done!?!?!?"**
>
> Elisande giggles.

**She destroys one symbol per round** thereafter, as long as manned symbols remain.

**Ausuriel's mercy (§380).** Once per fight, if the party is being overwhelmed, the DM may have Ausuriel **sing a clear note to one target: heal 40 HP and free them from any curse, charm, domination, disease, poison, blindness, deafness, or madness.** The module says to save it for a character who would otherwise be **out of the fight**, because sitting out is no fun.

**When she runs out of symbols**, Elisande moves to the nearest foe and stabs for **30 magical piercing damage per round.**

> **Two campaign-sized things land in this read-aloud, and you have been sitting on the first for a year.**
>
> **The goat was never a goat.** Every appearance of Elisande in this campaign — on the mushroom at Sporedome, on the rock beside the marching column, blocking a passage with milky-white eyes — was a fey Herald of Shiallia in a shape, escorting a child. **The blindness was the disguise.**
>
> **"The daughter of the Dark Sun" is not explained by the module and is not explained here.** You have played Elisande as unexplained all campaign, both of §382's endings close on *"you wonder what became of young Elisande and her goat?"*, and the correct move is to let the phrase land and never gloss it. **If you want to own it, that is a decision for after this module, not during it.**

# Branches

### They kill Graz'zt (§382)

His form fades. Without their demonic allies the giants break, the allied forces stream in, **Maerimydra is liberated.** The drow of Szith Morcane join the faithful of Eilistraee to occupy the city. Ryxyg, Sporedome and every faction send people to rebuild and set up trading enclaves. On the surface, **New Hillsfar and Elventree come together** and commit to supplying Mulmaster and marching on Phlan.

> **For this campaign that ending has a specific sting: it is Quil'bryn's shield doctrine vindicated and Rilira's promise kept, simultaneously.** The party spent their friends and it worked. Do not editorialise. Let Rilira be alive, and let her thank them.

### They flee (§380)

**Graz'zt does not give chase.** He is safe on the Material Plane with his plan complete, and teleports to places unknown. **This is the worse ending and it is a survivable one** — the loss text in §382 is explicit that they *bought time*, which is the same language the retreat used.

### They take the offer

Not in the module. **It is on the table because he put it there**, and this party has two members with real reasons to listen. If a player says yes, do not punish it reflexively — **ask what they want and let him pay it**, and let the rest of the party be in the room while he does.

### Elisande dies

The module does not contemplate it. **If she goes down, the symbols stop coming down**, and a CR 24 at full strength kills this party. **Protect her by making the demons want her** — they should be trying to reach her all fight, and the party should work out why before the GM says it.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Did we lose?"** → No. **"We bought you the time"** — and the module's own loss text says the same thing. Elanil believing otherwise for four minutes is a scene, not a verdict.
- **"Can we just not retreat?"** → The army cannot. The party is not the army. **What they can do is go up.**
- **"Can we teleport out / into the temple?"** → **No through the walls** — and ***banishment* does not function at all**, which will disappoint somebody. Within the tower, teleporting works.
- **"Can we banish Graz'zt?"** → **No.** That is the entire point of the building.
- **"Why is there no staircase?"** → Because half the temple is ethereal and **ethereal characters fly.** There are holes in the floors and ceilings instead.
- **"What's the orb?"** → The temple's heart, regrown with raw Abyssal chaos. **Not answered in narration:** whose faces those are. Let Bramgrim or Velasta name them.
- **"Is the girl on the bridge dangerous?"** → **She is covered in someone else's blood and the footprints lead out of the tower you are entering.** Answer nothing else.
- **"Is the goat okay?"** → *(after §379)* No.
- **"What is 'the daughter of the Dark Sun'?"** → **Unexplained, deliberately, in the module and here.** Do not invent an answer at the table.
- **"Can we take his offer?"** → Yes. He means it. **Not answered:** what he does with them afterwards.
- **"Why are seven demons standing still?"** → They are the anchors. **Let them work out that killing them is the win, and let Elisande demonstrate it first.**

## Stage directions

- **Elanil says "I think we have failed" with the survivors in the room.** Not in a command tent. Where Rilira can hear it.
- **When Yestral offers to hold the line, he looks up first.** At the stalactite. Before anyone else does.
- **"Now we just need the right heroes" — Elanil does not look at them. Everyone else does.** Then silence until a player speaks.
- **On the bridge, describe the footprints before the girl.** Out of the tower, to the rail.
- **Graz'zt's offer gets as long as the players want.** Do not cut to initiative. Do not let an NPC answer for them.
- **When the first symbol goes dark, Graz'zt screams — and it is the first time all night he is not in control of his voice.** Everything before that is charm.
- **Do not narrate Elisande's reveal quickly.** The goat's transformation is a full paragraph and the table has been waiting a year for it.
- **Both endings close on the same line about Elisande and her goat.** Read it either way. Do not change it.

## Open decisions — yours

1. **The Symbols reconciliation** (Ruling 2). Decide before you detonate another crystal, because the Cascade's meaning depends on it.
2. **Is Velasta in the temple?** She has better reason than anyone. **If yes, is she an ally in U29 or a third party?**
3. **Does the blood-purge ever fire?** Hledh is standing in U29 and the ritual works on fiendborne blood. **The module gives you nothing here; it is entirely invented if you want it.**
4. **Vraxen's survival**, and whether the ledger stays stolen after the city changes hands.
5. **What Graz'zt actually knows about Daein's origins.** He has truesight, telepathy, INT 23 and every language. **If he knows, that is the single most dangerous sentence available to any NPC in this campaign, and you should decide now whether it is true before a player asks.**
6. **What happens to the half-demon infants**, given Felkur made a deal with the Zhentarim for them and §382 hands them to the factions.
7. **Elisande, after.** Both endings ask what became of her. **You have run her for a campaign; the module never answers. Decide whether you want to.**
