# The Audit — the Soulcoin Ledger heist

Co-GM working doc. Arc prep, not a single session — the heist runs **inside DDEX3-16's assault**, so it interleaves with that module's Parts 2–5. Move to `session_prep/` when it has a date.

Register: **heist inside a siege.** The city is a live three-way political board and the party's own past cleverness is most of what's holding it in tension. The Spire is a hardened repeat target: **every defence in it is a portrait of the last robbery.**

Party: 12th level. Players: **Daein and Felkur.** GM-run: **Bramgrim, Akritas, the Owlbear.**

## What's actually in the vault (settled)

**Both prizes are still on the shelf.** In Ch11 the party read the **Soulcoin Ledger** and **Vraxen's betrayal journal**, called the ledger "practically radiating blackmail potential," and walked out with neither. They took only the **Faerzress Shard** and the **blank infernal contracts**. *(Grounding docs corrected.)*

**Two consequences worth building on:**

- **The heist has a second prize.** The journal is the single best piece of leverage over Vraxen in existence, and it is sitting eight feet from the objective. A party that grabs both walks out able to *negotiate* with him rather than flee from him — which matters enormously in **The Bait**, below.
- **Vraxen's treason is still undetected.** Because the journal never left the building, nothing has been pressuring him from above. **There is no real audit.** That makes **Route B a pure con** — the party isn't exploiting an investigation, they're inventing one — and it means the thing Vraxen is actually afraid of is not his superiors. It is the person who edited his ledger and left everything valuable behind, which is the behaviour of someone who wanted something other than money.

---

## The Win Condition

**The party gets the Soulcoin Ledger out of a building that was rebuilt specifically to stop them — and pays a price for it somewhere else in the battle.**

The heist landing is not the win. The win is that **taking it costs them one of the other two things they wanted**, and Daein and Felkur have to say out loud which.

The heist window is **DDEX3-16 Part 3**, when the party is already inside the city on a breach objective. **Part 4 interrupts it on the module's own schedule** — Mother's derro hit the rear lines whether or not the vault is open.

# Part One — The Spire, hardened

## Why this is a different heist

Vraxen was robbed by people who left almost no trace, and then found his own ledger edited. **He responded like an accountant: he audited the building.** Every single method that worked in Ch11 has been closed, and closed *legibly* — the party should recognise their own fingerprints in the new security and understand, slowly, that this is a portrait of them.

| Ch11 method | What it is now |
|---|---|
| **The runic key**, lifted off the obsidian table | **Physical keys abolished.** Access to the lift is now by *contract* — the Spire opens for signatories. To ride it you must be party to an agreement with the Spire. **Very yugoloth: security as contract law.** |
| **The lift's "Abyssal 23" OS**, bypassed cleanly | Patched. New build, new quirks, and Felkur gets to be contemptuous about the upgrade too — but the bypass that worked is gone |
| **Prime-numbered glyph tiles**, solved by *True Seeing* | **Re-sequenced, and warded against divination.** New safe sequence: **Fibonacci** (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34…). It overlaps with primes at 2, 3, 5 and 13 — so **the old answer is half-right, which is worse than wrong.** A party walking the Ch11 solution avoids tile 2 correctly and then dies on tile 4 |
| **The stone golem, keyed to Vraxen's scent**, beaten with a flavour-extractor | Scent-lock retained but **the scent must be fresh** — minutes old, not hours. It cannot be lifted off a passing owlbear any more. **This is the load-bearing change: to rob Vraxen, the party must first get into Vraxen's presence.** |
| **The vault door**, picked clean with thieves' tools | Not a lock problem any more. Contract-sealed, and keyed to the **anomaly** — see below |
| **The secret door out of the vault** | Found during his own audit. Sealed, or watched, or both |
| **The recalibrated teleportation circle at the peak** | **He found it. He left it working.** See The Bait. |

## The vault key, and the tell

Vraxen made the wound into the lock. **The vault is keyed to the transaction number of the tampered entry — 44781.** Anyone who knows the anomaly can open it, and the set of people who know the anomaly is Vraxen, his auditors, and the thief.

> **The moment this lands is the scene.** Felkur works out the key and realises it is *his own crime, weaponised as a password.* Vraxen has turned the theft into a filter designed to catch exactly one kind of person: someone who knows which record was altered.
>
> Opening that door is a confession.

## Why he knows it was touched but not who

The forged entry names **Veris of Mulmaster**, High Inquisitor. Vraxen audited it. **Veris is dead**, and the dates do not work. A record naming a man who could not have done it is not evidence of Veris — it is proof of a forger. He has the wound, the timestamp, and nothing else.

For an accountant, that is worse than being robbed. **Play his paranoia as tidiness, not rage.**

## The Bait

He understood what the recalibrated circle at the peak was for. **He left it recalibrated.**

A door you know about is worth more than a door you close. The exit the party engineered in Ch11 — their proudest piece of work — is now the one place in Maerimydra where Vraxen is certain the thief will eventually stand.

> Do not foreshadow this. Let them reach the peak with the ledger and feel relief.

# Part Two — The city, alive

Three factions, all destabilised, and **the party caused two of the three destabilisations themselves.**

## 1. The cambions — "Graz'zt has withdrawn from the giants"

**Origin: the party.** In Ch9 Daein mimicked Graz'zt's voice at the Six-Fingered Courtyard to scatter the cambion guard. What the cambions took away was not "we were tricked" — it was **that the Dark Prince has withdrawn his favour from Hledh's fire giants.** They have been living inside that belief ever since, and they have spread it.

- **What they want:** to be on the right side of a succession they think is coming. They are hedging — slow to enforce, quick to document, looking for a new patron.
- **What they know:** the household. Who visits the Spire, when Vraxen dines, which contracts are being quietly not-enforced.
- **Their price:** confirmation. They want someone to tell them the rumour is true, from a source that isn't them.
- **The clock:** **DDEX3-16 canon puts Graz'zt physically in the city**, in the castle courtyard with Hledh, performing a ritual. The rumour is false and the cambions are about to be catastrophically, publicly wrong. When that lands, whoever fed the rumour is exposed.

> **GM-only.** The party's cleverest trick is a lie with a detonation date on it. They can spend it before it goes off, or be standing next to it when it does.

## 2. Vraxen vs. the fire giants — the cold war

Graz'zt's contract-enforcer versus the debtors he enforces against. **Veperyd** was caught skimming the Coliseum betting pool — the lever the party used to ignite the Ch9 dinner brawl — and vowed "a debt in blood." Vraxen secretly plans to **betray his own yugoloth superiors** (journal, Ch11).

The withdrawal rumour makes this worse for everyone: **if Graz'zt has withdrawn, the giants' contracts are void** — which either frees them or ruins Vraxen, depending on who reads the paperwork first.

- **The Soulcoin Ledger is the evidence in this fight.** It records who owes whom, in souls. Whoever holds it can prove the giants have been defaulting, or that Vraxen has been skimming, or both.
- **That is why the Emerald Enclave wants it**, and why the party will find they are not the only bidder.
- **Veperyd is the wedge.** He wants Vraxen humiliated and he does not care who does it.

## 3. The drow insurgents

Maerimydra's surviving drow. **DDEX3-16 canon (§171): every non-warrior drow slave in the city was rounded up and taken somewhere two days ago. Nobody has seen them since.**

- **What they want:** the disappeared. That is the whole agenda and it overrides everything else.
- **What they know:** the city's bones — service passages, the plateau climbs, where the patrols actually walk. They are the only faction who can put the party at the Spire's base unseen.
- **Their price:** help finding the taken, or weapons, or a guarantee about what happens to Maerimydra's drow when Szith Morcane's drow arrive as conquerors.
- **The friction:** Quil'bryn's people are marching on this city. To Maerimydra's drow, Szith Morcane's drow are not liberators — and the Cave of Black Whispers massacre was *Szith Morcane's work*.

## Playing them against each other

The sandbox works because **each faction holds one third of the heist:**

| Faction | What they can give |
|---|---|
| **Cambions** | **Access to Vraxen's presence** — the fresh-scent requirement. They know his schedule and can get the party into a room with him |
| **Drow insurgents** | **Approach and exfiltration** — the Spire's base, unseen, and a way out that isn't the peak |
| **Veperyd / the giants** | **A reason for Vraxen to be distracted** — a formal dispute, an audit demand, a brawl. Anything that makes the arcanaloth look away from his own building |

**Nobody gives anything for free, and each price hurts a different ally.** That's the design: three factions, three ways in, and every purchase is a betrayal of somebody the party already promised something to.

# Part Three — Ways in

Three routes. All of them require **Vraxen's fresh scent**, which means all of them route through getting near him.

### Route A — The Second Dinner
Get invited back. The party posed as merchants once and it worked. Vraxen is a creature of contracts and appetite; a sufficiently interesting proposition gets a seat at the table.

- **Cost:** they must offer something real. Vraxen wants leverage on the giants, or on his own superiors.
- **The risk:** he knows someone edited his ledger, and he is looking at everyone. Being at that table is being under audit.
- **Best beat available:** Felkur sitting across from the man whose books he forged, being polite.

### Route B — The Audit That Isn't
**There is no real audit** — his treason is undetected because his journal never left the building. So the party **manufactures one.** Arrive as the auditors. Forged credentials are literally in their inventory: **blank infernal contracts**, taken from this vault in Ch11.

- **Cost:** requires the cambions (who would be expected, and when) and a working forgery.
- **The delicious part:** they'd be using Vraxen's own stolen stationery to walk back into his vault.
- **The risk that makes it sing:** an accountant with a genuine secret responds to a surprise audit by *panicking about the wrong thing.* He will assume they are here about the journal. Watch what he does to protect it — and note that whatever he does tells the party exactly where it is.

### Route C — The Blood Debt
Let **Veperyd** make the noise. A giant/yugoloth dispute pulled into the open drags Vraxen out of the Spire and into a formal proceeding.

- **Cost:** Veperyd wants a debt repaid in blood and he remembers the party ignited the last brawl. He will want them to do something ugly first.
- **The risk:** it does not get them *near* Vraxen, so the scent problem stays unsolved. **This route pairs with A or B, it doesn't replace them.**

> **Steer toward combining two.** The Spire needs distraction *and* proximity, and no single faction sells both.

# Branches

### They get the ledger and leave clean
Not free. Whatever they abandoned in Part 4 — the North Gate or Solom — happened without them, and the module's own failure text handles it. Akritas was right and everyone knows it.

### They get the ledger and take the bait
They reach the peak, the circle works, and Vraxen is waiting. **He does not fight.** He is an accountant with a thief in a room and no proof — so he opens negotiations. He wants to know who edited his book, and he will trade the ledger for the name.

> The party can hand him a name. **Veris is already in the record, and Veris is dead.** Selling a dead man twice is very much this party.

**If they also took the journal, the negotiation inverts.** He came to the peak holding the only exit; they came holding his treason. Two people who each have exactly one thing the other cannot survive losing, on a bridge, above a burning city. **Neither of them can afford to be the one who fights.**

> Play him working it out in real time — the pause where an accountant recalculates. He is not frightened. He is *revising*. And what he offers second is much better than what he offered first.

### They abandon the heist
Akritas does not argue. She says "understood" and goes. **The Enclave order remains unfulfilled and she has now been overruled twice** *(see the Velasta scene)*. Decide before the next session whether she accepts it or files it.

### They're caught inside when Part 4 fires
Best outcome. They are in a hardened vault with the city's alarm structure collapsing around them for reasons unrelated to them, and they have to choose whether to run *toward* the emergency.

### Vraxen works out who
He gets the name — from Pipyap, from a cambion, or from the party themselves. He does not retaliate directly. **He files an entry.** The Lexicon updates automatically with every infernal contract in Maerimydra, and now it names Felkur Oldenwood again, correctly, and permanently.

## Stage directions

- **First sight of the corridor:** describe it as unchanged. Let them walk in confident. The tell arrives when someone counts.
- **The vault key:** don't hint. Let Felkur solve it, then let the table sit with what the number *is.*
- **Vraxen at the table** (Route A): he is delighted to see them. He is delighted to see everyone. The threat is entirely in how carefully he listens.
- **The peak:** play the relief straight. They earned it. Then he's there.
- **The Part 4 three-way fork:** Bramgrim, the Harper brief, and Akritas each get one sentence. Then silence, and look at the two players.
- **The cambions' rumour detonating** — when Graz'zt is confirmed in the city, don't explain it. Show a cambion realising, in public, and let the party work out what they did.
