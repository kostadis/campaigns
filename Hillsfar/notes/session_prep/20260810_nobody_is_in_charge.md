## The Table — who actually decides

**Players: Daein and Felkur. You run Bramgrim, Akritas and the Owlbear.**

That means tonight is **not** "wait and see if a player finds the clever line." It's:

> **Bramgrim and Akritas state opposite positions, once each, in character. Then you stop talking and look at Daein and Felkur.**

Every major decision tonight has a Bramgrim answer and an Akritas answer, both defensible, and **the two players are the tiebreak.** That is the session's engine. Use it deliberately.

| The question | **Bramgrim argues** | **Akritas argues** |
|---|---|---|
| Velasta, standing in the room | Offer her rest. She's been trapped a century. | Destroy it. It should not exist. |
| Do we tell her Solom is alive? | Only if we mean to protect him after. | **Yes — and let her have him.** |
| The five giants who ran | Let them run; we have a city to hold. | Hunt them. |
| Who holds Szith Morcane | Whoever we can live with having lied to. | Whoever can be removed later. |
| The Nerevenan alliance generally | We agreed to it; we honour it until Graz'zt. | We agreed to spend them. This is *after*. |

### The craft rule for running two PCs

**A GM-run PC advocates and then defers. Always.** Bramgrim and Akritas each get one clear statement of position and one follow-up if pressed — then they turn to the players. Neither of them resolves anything, neither of them acts unilaterally without the players' say, and neither gets the last word before a decision.

If you catch yourself having Bramgrim *and* Akritas talk to each other for more than two exchanges, stop and ask Daein or Felkur a direct question.

### Akritas's kill list

**Three names, one doctrine: Velasta, Solom, every fire giant.** She is not being bloodthirsty; she is being consistent, and she has been consistent since Ch19.

> **GM-only — her motive, and it's better than "ranger hates undead." It is one doctrine, not three grudges.**
>
> Akritas is an **Emerald Enclave** operative, and the Enclave's remit is the natural order. Her list is three entries in a single category: **things that refuse to be what they are.**
>
> - **Velasta** — a woman who should have ended a century ago, held in the world past her term by a goddess's hand. Undeath sustained by divine intervention is a wound, not a tragedy.
> - **The fiendborne fire giants** — a demon-blooded bloodline propagating in the Realms. Textbook.
> - **Solom** — **he built himself a second body.** A man who arranged in advance not to die, and who is now walking around in manufactured flesh with a dead man's memories in it.
>
> **That last one is the point, and it is the trap.** From where Akritas stands, **Solom and Velasta are the same crime** — a man who would not die and a woman who could not. She is being perfectly consistent, and she is the only one in the room who has noticed.
>
> Which means: **Bramgrim's argument for resting Velasta is Akritas's argument for destroying Solom, with the mercy removed.** Do not let anyone point that out. If Daein or Felkur spot it on their own, that is the best moment available in the session — and it makes both of your NPCs suddenly much harder to dismiss.
>
> She agreed to the Nerevenan alliance in Ch22 by prioritising the Realms over purging the Underdark — **she meant "not yet," not "never."**
>
> She has form for private doctrine: she sat on the Enclave's ledger order for an entire arc and only surrendered it to an insight check. **If the players overrule her tonight, the open question is whether she accepts it or files it.** See Open Decisions.

> **Play her flat, not angry.** She says the appalling thing in the same register she uses for range increments. The line from Ch19 — *"One false step, and I'm killing them. Just so you know, Bram, Daein."* — is the voice. Statement of intent, delivered as a courtesy.

## The Win Condition

**The party has to hold together a coalition they built out of lies while the building burns down around it.** By end of session: Velasta is either satisfied, redirected, or has taken something and gone; the salamander problem is resolved one way or another; and — the actual win — **the party has decided who holds Szith Morcane, out loud, in front of everyone they promised it to.**

Dengor's corpse is not the win. It's the invoice.

The second thing that must land: **they own an arsenal and no longer own a factory.** Rilira's question — *how are you going to arm my people?* — now has a very specific, very finite answer sitting in crates.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Tell Velasta the truth about Solom.** Nobody dies, she leaves immediately, and she stops being tonight's problem. Cost: they hand a century-old vengeance the location of their own patron, and Solom is currently *weak*.
2. **Upgrade the lie instead of defending it.** Dengor was a forgepriest taking orders from Maerimydra. **This is substantively true** — which means a player who spots it is making a *Persuasion* check, not a Deception one. See Scene 1.
3. **Pay her.** Solom's signet ring and the password "Kural" is the single most valuable object they own from her point of view — and handing it over is telling her about Solom *without saying it.* Delightful and terrible.
4. **Point her at the salamanders.** She is the best answer to a fire elemental problem in the room and she is standing right there being difficult.
5. **Save the crates, not the forge.** The forge is unsalvageable the moment the channels go. The Hellfire projectors and the warhammers are moveable. Someone has to say this out loud within about four rounds.
6. **Get the stone giants out first.** Three wounded, freshly unshackled, in a burning room. They are Decha's price and they are also the strongest backs available for hauling crates.
7. **Settle the succession tonight, in the cavern, in front of everyone.** The party is the only party all four claimants trust. That window closes the moment somebody dies.

# Scene 1 — "Who Killed Dengor"

### Setup
Dengor's chamber, beneath the Forge of Surtr. Bodies: fire giants, helmed horrors, hobgoblin-grade wreckage. Three stone giants standing loose in broken shackles, bleeding. The Owlbear badly hurt. Felkur's *passwall* still open between the chamber and the main floor. Somewhere above, the hammering has stopped — and nobody has noticed yet, because the room is full of a woman who does not breathe.

**Velasta arrived into the tail of the fight and banished a fire giant that was still standing.** She is holding that spell right now.

### Approach — play her as unhurried, not enraged

She is not screaming any more. She has been doing this for a hundred years and she has never once rushed.

> "I heard him die." She looks at the body without much interest. "I have listened for a fire giant to die since before your grandmothers, and when it finally happened I was in a corridor."
>
> A pause that goes on slightly too long.
>
> "Which of you."

> **GM-only — her interiority, and it is the whole scene.**
>
> **She is careful because Solom can *Wish* her out of existence.** Eighteenth-level wizard, and she is one well-worded sentence from not having ever been. A century of not overcommitting is a century of that arithmetic. She does not fight things she cannot leave.
>
> **And underneath that: she does not actually want to finish.** A revenant whose vengeance completes stops existing. Kiaransalee stretched her past the year she was owed — which is a gift with a hook in it. Velasta has been *managing* her own vengeance to keep herself in the world.
>
> Which means: **she wants the Maerimydra story to be true.** A bigger enemy is more time. If the party offers her a larger target she will take it faster than she should, and part of her will know she took it too fast.
>
> Play the caution as manners. Play the reluctance as patience. Never let her look eager.

### The thing in her pocket

She is concentrating on *Banishment*. She holds one of Dengor's fire giants somewhere unpleasant, and she is entirely willing to mention it.

> "I am holding one of them. Not for you. I have not had one to myself in a long time." Beat. "I can stop holding him."

**Use this as the pressure valve, not the salamanders.** It is a hostage, a threat, and a gift, depending on the sentence before it. If the conversation goes badly she simply stops concentrating and a fire giant lands in the room mid-argument — which is both a real problem and, in this register, funny.

### The four exits

**A. Tell her Solom is alive.** *(Ruling 5 — the off-switch.)*
She stops. Everything about her stops. She asks one clarifying question, gets the answer, and **leaves** — no kill, no bargain, no thanks. The banished giant drops back into the room as her concentration goes with her, which is the party's problem now.

> **GM-only.** She does not go to the tower tonight. She goes to *plan.* A hundred years of caution does not sprint up a stairwell. But every session from here has a revenant somewhere in it, working, and the party's patron is on her list because they put him there.
>
> **And Akritas will argue for this exit, hard, for her own reasons.** She wants Solom destroyed. Handing his location to an unkillable century-old assassin is, from where she stands, the single most efficient thing the party could do tonight. **She will not say that part.** She will say it saves a party member, which is also true.
>
> That means **the safe answer and the assassination plan are the same sentence**, and Daein and Felkur have to decide whether they're taking the exit or pulling a trigger. Don't clarify it for them. If one of them asks Akritas directly why she's so keen, let her answer honestly — she doesn't lie to the party, she just doesn't volunteer.

**B. Redirect to Maerimydra.** *(Ruling 6.)*
The pitch: Dengor was a forgepriest. A middle manager with a hammer. The order came from **Hledh** and the fiendborne line in Maerimydra — the bloodline source — and Szith Morcane was an outpost of theirs.

> **GM-only — this is the important mechanical note.** **That is true.** Dengor genuinely served the Maerimydra occupation. A player who argues the *substance* is making a **Persuasion** check (say DC 16), not a Deception one. A player who just tries to lie harder is rolling **Deception vs her Insight**, and she has already been lied to once about this exact subject by this exact man.
>
> Reward the realisation. This is the cleanest, cheapest exit available and it requires a player to notice that the truth is more useful than the cover story.

She takes it. She takes it a little too readily, and if anyone is watching her closely — passive Insight, or a player who asks — **let them catch that she wanted to be convinced.**

**C. Pay her — a rare magic item.** *(Ruling 7. **You pick which one at the table.**)*

A stolen kill is a debt, and Velasta is a hundred-year creditor. She does not want coin and she is insulted by bulk. **She names a magic item, and she names it by looking at whoever is wearing it.**

> **GM-only — how she chooses.** She is not appraising. A revenant's instinct is to take the thing that will be *missed*, because that is what a debt feels like from the inside. She watches the party for four rounds of conversation and asks for whatever someone has touched twice.

**The menu, by what it costs the party narratively** — pick live:

| Item | Holder | What taking it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **Oathbow** | Akritas | Disarms the party's artillery going into Maerimydra. The cruellest and the most interesting. She'd also be taking it from the one person who has openly said she'll kill drow at the first excuse. |
| **Belt of Frost Giant Strength** | Daein | The liar pays. Poetic, and it costs him the thing that makes him a frontliner. |
| **All-Purpose Tool +2** | Felkur | Guts the infiltrator right before a city assault. He will offer something else first; let him try. |
| **Ring of Evasion** | Bramgrim | Takes the survivability off the one person who could permanently end her. **She would know that.** |
| **Canaith Mandolin** | Daein | Cheaper, and funnier — a revenant confiscating a lute. Good if you want the register light. |
| **Wand of the War Mage +2** | Felkur | Glor's reward, one arc old. Real loss, no plot cost. |
| **Rod of the Pact Keeper** | party bag | **The dark-comedy option.** Nobody's used it since Ch16 because there's no warlock. Watch them try to pay a century-old grudge with the thing nobody wanted. She may take it. She may recognise the insult. Both are good. |
| **Shard of Crystallized Faerzress** | Felkur | Unique, load-bearing for locks. Expensive in ways that show up later. |
| **Solom's signet ring + "Kural"** | party | *This is telling her Solom is alive without saying the words.* She doesn't know what it opens; she knows it's an archmage's key and it smells like him. **If they offer this, take it** — but understand you've just run Exit A on a delay, and the party won't realise for a session. |

**Non-item offers she'll entertain as partial payment, not settlement:**
- **The crypts, formally** — the Nerevenan claim on the drow governors' burial ground, backed by the party. Cheap tonight, expensive in Scene 4.
- **Dengor's head.** Proof, not the kill. She'll say so.
- **The banished giant, formally hers to keep.** She's already holding him. Costs the party nothing and is very funny.
- **An ally's body** — not a PC. If someone offers a drow, note who offered, and note Akritas has been waiting for an excuse.

**D. Nothing, or they insult her.** *(Rulings 3 & 4.)*
She takes **whoever is most isolated at that moment** — geography, not grudge. One strike, and then she is gone through the crypt wall. She does not stay to win.

> **PC death, staged three ways — call it live.**
> - **Lethal:** she opens on the isolated one with everything and it can genuinely land. Give her the exit in the same turn — she leaves whether or not it worked.
> - **Near-miss:** she drops them to 0 and takes a trophy off the body — a hand, the Oathbow, a holy symbol — and leaves them dying. Bramgrim can save them. The trophy is gone.
> - **Theft instead:** she takes something they'll chase. **Best candidates:** the Oathbow, Solom's signet ring, Bramgrim's Alder-signed sword *(cruel and perfect)*, or one of the freed stone giants.
>
> All three run off the same setup. Watch the table and pick on her initiative.

### Failure costs (material, not damage)
- **She learns Daein lied** (a bad Deception roll, or a drow veteran contradicting the story) → she doesn't attack. She *files it.* From here on she is an ally who is measuring them, and every future negotiation runs at a permanent disadvantage. Say nothing; just have her look at Daein a half-second too long from now on.
- **Bramgrim reveals what he is** — a priest who could lay her body *and* soul to rest is the one existential threat in the room. If he threatens it, she stops being careful about him specifically. Good scene, bad idea.
- **They threaten her with Solom without confirming he lives** → she assumes bluff, and the exit closes. Ruling 5 requires an actual confirmation.

### NPC notes
- **Velasta T'sarran** — wants her vengeance *and* wants it to last. Tic: leaves pauses a living person would fill; states appalling things in the register of an administrator.
- **The three freed stone giants** — no names yet *(pick one — see Open Decisions)*. Wounded, disoriented, eighteen feet of gratitude with nowhere to put it. They will do whatever the party says and they are about to be standing in a fire.
- **Bramgrim & Akritas (both yours)** — one statement of position each, then defer to Daein and Felkur. See "The Table" above. Bramgrim wants her rested; Akritas wants her gone; **the two players decide which of their friends was right.**
- **The Owlbear (yours)** — severely wounded, and the reason three stone giants are free at all. If Velasta moves on anyone, the Owlbear moves first. It will not survive that. **Ask Daein and Felkur before you let it happen** — it's their sidekick as much as anyone's, and it's a death worth them choosing.

# Scene 1½ — The Mercy

**You run this scene from both sides. Bramgrim makes the offer. Akritas objects. Daein and Felkur decide.**

*Bramgrim knows he can do this — a revenant destroyed simply gets back up, but a priest who lays **body and soul** to rest ends it, and he is a Life cleric with the training and the standing to try. **He should raise it himself, unprompted**, the moment the standoff gives him room. Then get out of the way.*

> **The shape, and don't deviate from it:**
> 1. **Bramgrim offers.** Plainly, to Velasta, in front of everyone. He does not ask the party first — that's the point of him.
> 2. **Akritas reframes it as extermination**, immediately, in the same breath. *"Do it. Not because she's suffering."*
> 3. **You stop.** Look at Daein and Felkur. The two of them own what happens next, and they have to pick a **reason**, not just an outcome.
>
> **The trap worth setting:** Bramgrim's mercy and Akritas's doctrine produce **the identical result** — Velasta ends. They disagree only about whether it's kind. If the players just say "sure, do it," they have accidentally voted with Akritas, and Bramgrim will notice.

### The thing that makes this scene work

> **GM-only — read this before you play a single line of it.**
>
> **Velasta has been outrunning rest for a century.** A revenant whose vengeance completes stops existing; Kiaransalee stretched her past the year she was owed, and she has been *pacing her own vendetta* ever since to stay in the world. That is why she is careful. That is why she never overcommits. It reads as patience. It is fear.
>
> So when Bramgrim offers to lay her to rest, he is not threatening her with destruction. **He is offering her the one thing she has spent a hundred years running from, and he is offering it kindly.** She has fought armies. Nobody has ever offered.
>
> And Bramgrim is a **Life** cleric. His domain is not *destroy undead*; it is restoration. **The rite is not an attack. It is the completion of a healing that was interrupted a century ago** — and that framing is exactly what lets a 12th-level dwarf contest a goddess.
>
> **Since you're playing him: let him say that out loud, and then make the advantage contingent on the players.** If Daein and Felkur back the mercy framing — argue it to Velasta, argue it to Akritas, mean it — the rite runs with every advantage you can justify. If they green-light it as a tactical cleanup, **Bramgrim is performing an execution he believes is a kindness, and the magic knows the difference.** Make it harder. Don't announce that you're making it harder.

### What the rite actually needs

Four things. The party has three of them and doesn't know it.

1. **Her true name.** *Velasta T'sarran.* They have it.
2. **Her body**, at rest or destroyed. Either works — see the two forms below.
3. **Her unfinished business, named out loud and released.** Her mother, her sister, herself. Someone has to say what was done to her and to whom, in her hearing. **Bramgrim has to know her story, which means someone has to ask her for it.** That conversation is the real scene.
4. **Her family's remains.** House T'sarran lies in the **crypts of the drow governors** — the bone-littered passage the party already walked through to meet her. It has to happen there. *(They stood on her mother.)*

And then the fifth thing, which is not a component: **Kiaransalee's hold has to break.** Velasta wears the goddess's symbol openly.

### Two forms

**Forced.** Destroy the body first, then perform the rite over it before she reconstitutes. Doable, ugly, and it is unambiguously killing an ally. **Kiaransalee resists hard** — treat it as a sustained contest across the rite's duration, and let something in the crypt push back audibly. If they fail partway, she rises with the rite half-done and she knows exactly what they tried.

**Consented.** She stands there and lets him. Far harder to *reach*, far easier to *perform* — a goddess's grip on a soul that is done holding on is not much of a grip. **This is the version worth building the arc toward.**

> Set the DCs where your table lives. The number is not the scene. What matters is that **consent is worth more than any modifier**, and that whatever Bramgrim rolls, Kiaransalee gets a say.

### Approach — the conversation, not the ritual

Don't ritual first. **Bramgrim asks her what happened**, because the rite needs her story and because he'd ask anyway.

> "Solom Ned'razak came to my mother's house with a proposal and stayed for dinner." No inflection at all. "He killed my mother in the receiving room. My sister on the stair. Me in the doorway, going for the stair." A pause where a living person would breathe. "I got up. They did not."

And, if he offers:

> She is quiet long enough that Akritas checks whether the spell ended.
>
> "You are offering to put me down."
>
> Let a player correct her. Let Bramgrim say the word *rest*, and watch what a hundred-year-old drow does with it.
>
> "I have been holding on. That is all I have done. I do not know what I would be if I let go, and I have never had the luxury of finding out." Beat. "Ask me again when he is dead. Ask me then."

> **Stage direction.** That last line is a promise and a door. She is not refusing — she is naming her price, and her price is the one thing the party has been lying about since the crypts.

### The two arguments, for lifting verbatim

**Bramgrim, offering.** Give it to her, not to the party. He isn't campaigning.

> "Lass. I've been at a lot of gravesides." He is not looking at anyone else in the room. "You've been standing at your own for a hundred years and nobody's ever said the words over you. I know them. I'd say them, if you'd let me."

**Akritas, immediately after.** Flat, correct, and appalling — and she is not wrong about any of the facts.

> "Do it." Beat. "Not because she's suffering. Because she's a hole in the world with a goddess's hand in it, and we're about to hand her a city." She doesn't raise her voice. "And when that's done, we do the same for the archmage. He built himself a spare body so he'd never have to be dead either. I don't see the difference and neither should you."

**Then you shut up and look at Daein and Felkur.** Do not have Bramgrim rebut her. Do not have Akritas escalate. **The next voice in the room should be a player's**, and if the silence gets uncomfortable, let it.

> **GM-only.** Akritas naming Solom out loud, here, in front of a revenant who has spent a century hunting him — **that is a catastrophic operational error and she has just made it.** Velasta hears *"the archmage"* and *"a spare body."* That is not a hint; that is the answer to the only question she has ever asked.
>
> Whether she puts it together tonight is your call. Whether Daein or Felkur notice and shut Akritas up is *theirs*, and it's the best thirty seconds available in this scene. **If they miss it, you have not lost anything** — you have just chosen when Exit A fires, and it fires without them.

### Branch — the players green-light it tonight, forced

Bramgrim will do it if Daein and Felkur tell him to; he'll do it sadly, and he'll do it properly. It works if the dice hold. And then:

- **The Nerevenans lose their priestess three hours after allying.** Asala inherits a cult that just watched its immortal leader be unmade by an ally. Twenty warriors and the raising of fire giant dead become unreliable at best.
- **The lie dies with her.** Nobody is left to be angry about Solom. **The cold tactical read is that this is the cleanest possible cleanup, and someone at the table will notice.** Let them. Then let Bramgrim sit with having been the instrument of it.
- **Kiaransalee learns Bramgrim's name.** A goddess of vengeance, specifically, now has a specific dwarf. That is a standing thread, not a punishment — spend it slowly.
- **Quil'bryn is permanently safe**, and will not understand why nobody looks pleased.
- **Hledh's counter-preparation is now aimed at a threat that no longer exists.** Maerimydra is bracing for a revenant who has been laid to rest. The party's advantage got bigger and they can never tell anyone why.

### Branch — the players tell Bramgrim to stand down

Entirely legitimate, and the coalition survives intact. **Cost:** Akritas has now been overruled on the first name on her list, in public, by two people who were not there when the Enclave gave her the order. Don't have her argue. Have her say "understood" and go check on the Owlbear. *(Whether she accepts it or files it is Open Decision 6 — decide it before you play the next session, not this one.)*

### Branch — the players side with Akritas explicitly

They vote to destroy Velasta *as a threat*, not to rest her. Bramgrim performs the rite anyway, because he's Bramgrim — **and it's harder, because he's lying to himself while he does it.** If it fails partway, that's why. He will not say so.

### Branch — Bramgrim does it consenting, later

The version to want. She asks. Probably after Solom, probably after Maerimydra, probably in the crypts with her mother's bones under the party's boots. **No roll should decide this one** — if she is willing, it is done, and the scene is what everyone says while it happens.

> If you get here: **give Felkur's player the last line.** He shattered a god's face with a beer bottle to free a stone giant from a perfect thing. This is the same act, done gently, on purpose, by someone else — and Felkur is the only person in the room who has done it before.

### Branch — he offers and she refuses

Most likely tonight, and the strongest outcome for the arc. She now knows that the cleric in the party can end her, **and that he offered rather than tried.** That reframes every future interaction: she is careful around Bramgrim in a way she is not careful around anyone else, and she trusts him more than she trusts the ones who have never threatened her. Both of those are true at once and she is aware of it.

### Branch — he threatens instead of offers

Different scene entirely, and worse. She stops being careful about him specifically. See Scene 1 failure costs. **The rite requires her story; a threat means nobody ever asks for it.**

### Failure costs (material, not damage)
- **Rite fails partway (forced):** she rises knowing. The alliance is over, she leaves, and she takes the Scene 1 exit that hurts most — one isolated party member, on her way out.
- **Someone interrupts to argue tactics mid-rite:** she hears the tactical version of the offer. Whatever chance the consented route had is gone permanently.
- **They do it and tell the Nerevenans it was mercy:** Asala believes them or doesn't, and you decide which cult they inherit.

### NPC notes
- **Velasta, in this scene only** — drop the administrator register. She is not managing anyone here. Tic: she answers questions in the order they were asked, exactly, including the ones nobody wanted answered.
- **Bramgrim (yours)** — he agreed last session to use this cult as a meat shield and "deal with them after." **It is three hours later and dealing with them turns out to be a kindness.** Do not let anyone point that out. Let it sit on the table. He offers once, means it, and then he waits for Daein and Felkur like a man who has already decided he'll abide by whatever they say.
- **Akritas (yours)** — first name on a list of three. She wants this done and she wants it done for the wrong reason, and she will say the wrong reason out loud in front of the wrong person. **She defers when overruled.** She does not forget.
- **Asala** — if present, she does not intervene. Watch what she does with her hands.

# Scene 2 — The Workforce Files a Grievance

**Runs concurrently with Scene 1. Start the clock the moment Velasta asks her question.**

### The cascade, in order — use these as sound cues, one per round or two

1. **The hammering stops.** It has been the cavern's heartbeat since Ch19. Nobody notices until it's been quiet for a while. *(Let a player notice.)*
2. **Roughly a hundred azer, across patrols, barracks, forge and palace, come apart into inanimate metal where they stand.** The binding rituals were keyed to Dengor. A hundred bodies hitting stone, all at once, everywhere in the outpost.
3. **In the Forge above them, the two salamanders' circle fails.**
4. **The screaming starts.** Not a roar — salamanders sound like metal under stress.
5. **They come looking for whatever bound them.** It's dead. So they burn the room it was in, which is the room the party is standing under.

> **GM-only — the joke, and don't let anyone say it before the players do.** The azer *were* the answer to Rilira's question. A hundred bound smiths in an industrial forge is how you arm two hundred slaves. The party killed the man holding the leash, and the entire workforce became scrap in the same instant. **They solved the boss fight by destroying the solution to the next problem.**
>
> Do not let an NPC point this out for at least a scene. Let it dawn.

### The salamanders
Two, freed and **uncontrolled** — the pendant-control option in the Aug-8 doc is off the table, because the azer wearing the pendants are scrap.

- **AC 15, HP 90, Fire Aura 2d6/turn, Flame Spear +7 ×2 (2d8+4 + 2d6 fire), Constrict.**
- **Vulnerable to cold.** Felkur's cold-infused cannon and ice bullets double up. **This has been set up since Ch19 — let it pay.**
- CR 5 each. Against a 12th-level party this is not a lethal fight. **It is not supposed to be a fight.** It's a fire.

### What's actually at stake up there
The **molten channels** are untended for the first time in years. Two salamanders wrecking the floor plate is all it takes.

- **~4 rounds** before the first channel breaches.
- **~8 rounds** before the forge floor is impassable and the crates are gone.
- In those crates: **three finished Hellfire projectors, four half-assembled, and roughly 1,000 gp of ceremonial ware, rare metals and finely-made warhammers** — *which is a slave army's worth of weapons.*

**The party can save the arsenal or kill the salamanders. Doing both is tight and they will feel it.** That's the scene.

### Branch — they fight the salamanders properly
They win. It costs them rounds, and the rounds cost them crates. Have Felkur's cold cannon shine — then have someone realise, mid-victory, what's melting behind them.

### Branch — they evacuate and let the forge go
Correct call, painful. **The three stone giants are the strongest backs in the room** and hauling crates is the exact thing Decha's kin can do. Nice symmetry: the giants Denagor enslaved to build carry out the weapons that free everyone else.

### Branch — they point Velasta at the salamanders
**Yes. Let this work.** She is a fire-immune revenant priestess standing in a room complaining about a stolen kill; two salamanders is a gift. If the party thinks of it, she'll do it — with visible irritation at being useful.

> Play her the way a senior colleague reacts to being asked to cover a shift. She *does* it. She wants everyone to know it was beneath her.

### Branch — nobody handles it
The forge goes. See Default Trajectory. Rilira's people stay unarmed, and the party has to go tell her.

### The banished giant
He returns the instant Velasta drops concentration — she leaves, she gets distracted by salamanders, she gets bored. **Drop him into whichever round is already worst.** He is one fire giant against a 12th-level party; he is not a threat, he is a *scheduling problem*, and that is the joke.

# Scene 4 — Four People Were Promised This City

# What Five Survivors Tell Hledh

*(Offscreen. Not a scene — the horizon. Resolve it tonight in your head; the party learns it in DDEX3-16 or never.)*

**Ten fire giants ran for Maerimydra when Dengor's body hit the floor. Five arrive.** Velasta's Nerevenans took the road and took the rest — which the party did not ask for, did not pay for, and will not be credited with.

### What the survivors actually report
Not "adventurers took Szith Morcane." They were asleep, or at the palace works, or running. What they *saw* was the dead coming out of the crypts for them, and a woman with grey skin who did not hurry.

**So Maerimydra braces for a revenant.** In Maerimydra, **Velasta T'sarran's vengeance is known** — a name and a century of it. Hledh is warned about a Kiaransalee death cult moving on fire giants, and she orients accordingly: wards, priests, undead-turning, the Undying Temple thread pulled tight.

> **GM-only — this is the best thing the party's lie ever did for them, and they will never know.**
>
> Daein told a revenant that fire giants killed Solom. That lie has now propagated into enemy intelligence as *"the dead are hunting us."* **The party is invisible in Maerimydra's threat picture.** Hledh is not preparing for a 12th-level adventuring party, a drow resistance, stone giants, two hundred armed slaves and a Vhaeraunite cell. She is preparing for one undead priestess.
>
> **And the flip side, which is the actual cost:** the one asset in the coalition Maerimydra has specifically prepared for is Velasta. If the party marches with her — or worse, marches *behind* her — they are walking their army in under the only banner Hledh has counter-planned.
>
> Both halves land in DDEX3-16. Neither is knowable tonight.

### Threads this opens for the march
- **Hledh's defences are mis-aimed.** Anti-undead preparation is close to useless against everything the party actually brings. A first strike that does not include Velasta is a first strike into the wrong wall.
- **Velasta is now a named enemy of Maerimydra.** She'll like that. It extends her — see her interiority in Scene 1.
- **Five giants who ran are five giants who saw Dengor die.** If any of them is captured or interrogated later, the version of events in their heads is the party's lie, told back to them as fact.
- **Nobody in Maerimydra knows Szith Morcane fell to a coalition.** How long that holds is a clock you control.

## Stage directions

- **Open on Velasta's question and nothing else.** No initiative, no boxed text about the room. She asks "which of you," and you stop talking.
- **The hammering stopping** should be noticed by a player, not narrated. Mention it once, quietly, mid-Velasta. Then don't mention it again until someone asks.
- **A hundred azer falling apart:** one sound, everywhere, at once. Do not itemise it. "Everything metal in Szith Morcane that was standing up is now lying down."
- **The salamanders** scream before they arrive. Metal under stress, from above, getting closer. Two rounds of sound before anything is visible.
- **If she takes someone:** one strike, then gone. Do not let her monologue and do not let her stay to be avenged on. The horror is that she leaves.
- **If they tell her about Solom:** total stillness, one clarifying question, and she walks out. **No thank you, no bargain, no goodbye.** Let the table sit in the anticlimax — that's the joke and it's also the dread.
- **When she drops concentration** and the fire giant lands back in the room, do not build to it. Just say it happened, in the middle of another sentence.
- **Scene 4:** let all four claimants talk at once for a beat before anyone looks at the party.
- **Scene 1½, if it fires:** when Bramgrim says the word *rest*, stop. Don't have her react in the same breath. A hundred years is a long time to go without anyone offering.
- **Any time Bramgrim and Akritas disagree:** one line each, then **silence, and look at a player.** Never let your two NPCs resolve a decision between themselves — the moment they do, the session belongs to you instead of to Daein and Felkur.
- **When Akritas says the wrong thing in front of Velasta:** don't flag it, don't pause, don't give a tell. Just say the line and move on. If the players catch it, that's theirs. If they don't, it's yours to spend later.
- **If the players overrule Akritas:** "Understood." Nothing else. No sulk, no argument, no meaningful look. She is a professional and that's what makes her frightening.

## Open decisions — yours, don't let me pick

1. **Does the Owlbear interpose** if she moves on a PC? It's severely wounded and it would not survive. That's a player call at the table, but you should know whether you'd allow it to work.
2. **Name the three freed stone giants** — or at least one. They're about to be load-bearing (Decha's price, and the strongest backs in a burning room) and "stone giant #2" will not carry the scene.
3. **Can Felkur finish the half-assembled Hellfire projectors?** Yes = the party owns working siege weapons going into Maerimydra, which is a lot of rope. No = the crates are all they get.
4. **Does Kiaransalee's sustaining of Velasta come with a hook you want to use?** A goddess keeping a revenant past her term has an interest in Szith Morcane, and the party is about to decide who holds it. **This is also the resistance Bramgrim contests in Scene 1½** — how hard she pushes back is the same decision.
5. **Set the bar for the forced rite.** Consented costs nothing but the arc's patience; forced should cost something real. Pick whether failure means "she rises knowing" or "she rises knowing *and* Kiaransalee marks him."
6. **If the players overrule Akritas, does she accept it or file it?** She has form — she sat on the Enclave's ledger order for a whole arc. If she files it, she will act on Solom eventually without asking, and that is a session in itself. Decide before the *next* game, not this one.
7. **Does Akritas's Enclave order cover any of this?** She's carrying a High Council brief about the Arcanaloth's ledger. If the Council's position on Solom, or on Kiaransalee's cult, is anything other than silence, she'd have it — and that's a card she could play on Daein and Felkur. Decide whether she's freelancing or authorised.
8. **When does the truth about Solom's death reach Velasta?** Quil'bryn's silence order is twenty soldiers deep. Pick the session you want that to land in — it's the best-shaped detonation left in the arc.
9. **Does the party ever learn that Velasta thinned the retreat for them?** Five giants instead of ten, unasked and unpaid. Telling them makes her an ally they owe; withholding it keeps her alien.
10. **How long before Maerimydra learns Szith Morcane fell to a coalition** and not to a death cult? That clock is the party's entire margin in DDEX3-16.
