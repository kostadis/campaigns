## The Win Condition (state it plainly to yourself)

The party takes both objectives, arrives at the gate, and **understands that Maerimydra is not being held — it is being neglected.** Not "the enemy is weak." *Nobody is checking.* The occupation has a demon corps that files rather than fights and an accountant who stopped counting, and both of those are the party's own fingerprints, two arcs old, coming back as tactical advantage.

That is the mechanical and thematic floor. The session **lands** if three things are true when the lights come up:

1. **They chose at the Cave** — and whichever way they chose, somebody who came to help them paid for it.
2. **They are holding at least one piece of physical evidence that the Spire has stopped functioning.** Not a rumour. An object.
3. **They breach the city already spent**, with no long rest available and Part 4 sitting on the module's own clock.

If they clear both objectives cleanly and walk to the gate feeling competent, you got the encounters and missed the session.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

Not a script. This is what an optimal table does, so you can recognise a good move and reward it.

1. **Roll Insight on Vragni before answering him.** DC 20 says his anger is the engine and the rest of the dwarves are not evil. That single check converts the whole scene from a CR 15 boss into a hostage situation with one hostage-taker.
2. **Kill Vragni to free the dwarves, not to satisfy the Order.** Same act, and it satisfies Zern Xerkstil, Glenna Moonsmith, and the dead simultaneously. The Order's contract and the humane answer are the same swing. Do not point this out.
3. **Get to the Cave ahead of the Nerevenans.** They are assigned here. If the party resolves it first, Kiaransalee's cult arrives to find their objective already completed by their own allies — which is either a service or an insult, and the party gets to decide which by how they say it.
4. **Frame Fuse 3 at the Cave.** The Nerevenan runner is standing right there. This is the party's last cheap chance to decide what Velasta hears about Asala, and it costs one conversation.
5. **Search the bone pile properly, not for coins.** The ornate boxes are Spire courier crates. Two weeks of undelivered freight with the seals intact and no recovery notice filed. **The Spire has stopped auditing its own losses.** That is the tower thread, and it is lying on the floor.
6. **Fight the umber hulks with their eyes shut.** The reflections make averting useless; closing works. A party that works this out in round one takes a wildly easier fight, and Bramgrim, Daein and Felkur all have ways to fight blind that Akritas does not.
7. **Prepare the ground during Fortify Gains instead of resting.** The module hands them an hour and a supply train. Glyphs, embankments, an eldritch cannon on overwatch, Felkur's cold infusion on the approach. **The alarm is already sounded and they don't know it** — every minute spent on defences is a minute that pays.
8. **Take the cambion alive and offer him a patron, not a threat.** DC 15 Intimidation gets facts. A Persuasion that offers him a *side* gets the hedge itself — that the demons of Maerimydra have decided Graz'zt has withdrawn favour, and are waiting to see who wins.
9. **Work out what the city is actually braced for, and leave the dwarves at the Cave.** The giants are prepared for undead. The freed dwarves are undead. Bringing them switches on every counter-preparation Maerimydra made and takes the coalition out of its own invisibility — and the spectral dwarves maim the party's drow allies on top of it. **A party that frees the dead, thanks them, and deliberately does not bring them has made the best call of the campaign.** See *Braced For The Wrong War*.
10. **Notice that the western tunnel was never patrolled.** Two weeks of disappearances on an approach to a besieged city and nobody investigated. Somebody's job is not being done, and Felkur knows exactly whose.

## The Rot (GM-only — read this before play)

Three things are wrong with Maerimydra's western sector, and the party caused two of them.

**1. The demons are hedging.** In Ch9 Daein mimicked Graz'zt's voice at the Six-Fingered Courtyard. What the cambion guard took away was not *we were tricked* — it was *the Dark Prince has withdrawn his favour from Hledh's giants*, and they have been living inside that belief ever since. It is now sector policy. Requisitions get filed, not filled. Patrols get scheduled, not walked. Nobody refuses an order; nobody executes one either. **The cambion at Fortify Gains is the last demon in the west still doing the job, and he is doing it badly because he doesn't believe in it.**

**2. Vraxen stopped counting.** Somebody edited the Lexicon of Oaths and left everything valuable on the shelf. He knows the wound and the timestamp and nothing else, and he has spent the intervening period running a forensic audit on a forger instead of running the occupation's books. The consequences are ambient and physical: a courier route nobody investigated, giants nobody paid, contracts nobody enforced.

> **This is the session's gift, and it is precisely dosed.** The party gets an easier western approach *because Felkur robbed a tower two arcs ago and did it so cleanly it broke the man's concentration.* Nobody at the table will connect this unprompted. Let the evidence accumulate — the tunnel, the crates, the demon's three unanswered reports — and let a player say it out loud in Scene 4. Do not say it for them.

**3. The dwarves were never relieved.** Ten years ago Szith Morcane's drow ambushed Vragni Moonsmith's company, wrapped his body in his own clan's banner, and desecrated it. Nobody came back. Not the clan, not the Order, not anyone — until Glenna Moonsmith asked, and she asked a *faction*, not her own people. **Every undead in that cave is standing a post nobody ended.**

That is the session's spine, and it is the same sentence three times: *nobody came looking.* Say it about the dwarves in Scene 1, let them find it about the freight in Scene 3, and let the cambion say it about himself in Scene 4.

## Braced For The Wrong War (GM-only — the biggest thing on the board)

The Rot explains why the west is *neglected*. This explains why it is **empty**, and it is worth more than both other causes combined.

**Maerimydra's entire counter-preparation is pointed at a revenant.** Five fire giant survivors got out of Dengor's collapsing forge and reached Hledh with a report: the dead coming out of the crypts, and a grey-skinned woman who did not hurry. Hledh believed them, because it was true. So the city has spent the intervening weeks doing what you do about an undead priestess of Kiaransalee — consecrating ground, distributing holy water, moving priests forward, pulling the Undying Temple thread tight, drilling turning.

**None of that works on a stone giant.** Or two hundred armed slaves, or a duergar shield line, or four twelfth-level adventurers with a hellfire projector.

> **The coalition is invisible in Hledh's threat picture, and it is invisible because Daein lied to a revenant about who killed his patron.** That lie is currently the single most valuable asset this army has, and nobody at the table knows it.

**And Velasta is out ahead of them, alone, absorbing all of it.** Which is, tactically, the best possible use of her. Akritas will notice this and should say something cold and unpleasant about it being a good use of the woman, but only if a player gets there first.

### The tells to plant tonight

Show the preparations and let the party work out that they're aimed somewhere else. **Never say it in narration.**

- Salt lines and consecrated ground across approaches that are otherwise unwatched.
- Corpse-burning pits, recent, thorough — the giants have stopped leaving bodies.
- A priest's sigil chalked over a doorway on a tunnel with no door.
- Holy water caches at junctions, stocked, undisturbed, guarded by nobody.
- **The prisoner in Scene 4 says it outright** if asked what they're braced for: *the grey woman.*

### **The trap, and it is the best thing in this session**

Per §148, freeing Vragni's dwarves means **they aid the coalition in the attack**. Per §162, spectral dwarves on the field hit **every enemy and every drow character** for 4d6+3 necrotic.

So if the party frees the dead and brings them:

1. They march an **undead contingent** at the one city in the Underdark that has spent a month building anti-undead defences. **Every ward, every priest, every consecration suddenly has something to do.**
2. **The coalition stops being invisible.** The moment Maerimydra sees undead in the line, the threat picture Hledh built is *correct*, and every preparation she made comes online against an army that was walking in under it untouched.
3. And the dwarves hurt **Quil'bryn's drow, Rilira's drow, and every drow ally the party recruited** — indiscriminately.

> **Do not warn them.** This is a real, discoverable, entirely fair trap made of three facts they already have access to: the giants are braced for undead, the dwarves are undead, the dwarves hate drow. A party that reasons it out and **leaves the dwarves behind, at the Cave, deliberately** has made the best strategic decision of the campaign.
>
> If they bring them anyway, that is a decision too, and Part 3 gets much louder. **Let Akritas make the case for leaving them and let the players overrule her if they want to.**

## Scene 1 — The Cave of Black Whispers

### Setup

Half a day out from Sporedome on the Order of the Gauntlet's errand, before the coalition columns split for their bases. A strong, warm wind blowing *out* of a cave mouth, which is wrong for the Underdark and should be the first thing anyone notices.

- Natural columns staggered around **30-foot crevasses** (DC 15 Athletics to climb).
- **Bone piles** alight with faerzress — dwarves, drow, animals, worse. Difficult terrain, disadvantage on Stealth.
- Dim light. Even with darkvision, they cannot see across the cavern.
- Centre: a mound of rock and skulls topped by the **T-shaped crossed poles of a war banner** — with a skull where the colours should be.

**Present:** the party, the Owlbear, and **Oxania's clerics of Eilistraee** as the allied detachment (§148). **Offstage but inbound:** the Nerevenan contingent, assigned here.

### Whose job this actually is — say the chain once

**Glenna Moonsmith** — dwarf, proprietor of the Plodding Plow at The Stop, and **Charles's girlfriend** — begged the Order of the Gauntlet to lay her ancestor to rest. Zern Xerkstil carried it to the party at the muster.

**Charles is Felkur's business partner** — "Dumb Karl," the grain farmer who became the leader of La Résistance. He is in Hillsfar right now building Stargate through Isthana Rao, with Felkur's money, while Felkur is a long way under the Underdark. **He farms the Tradeway just north of The Stop; Glenna runs the inn at The Stop. They are neighbours.** See `notes/npcs/charles.md`.

**The party already accepted, on the spot.** They recognised Glenna's name the moment Zern Xerkstil said it and took the job without deliberating — **not as a contract, but because of Karl.** Daein and Felkur both have a real relationship with him.

> **So this is not a faction errand and it is not a decision. It is family business one degree removed, already agreed to, sitting on a player.**
>
> **Which means the question tonight is not whether — it is whether they can do it right.**
>
> Zern Xerkstil does not know that and does not need to. **Your job is to make sure Felkur's player has the connection in mind before they decide anything** — one sentence in the recap, no emphasis: *"Glenna Moonsmith. Charles's Glenna."*
>
> **And what she asked for, precisely: that her ancestor be laid to rest permanently.** Not avenged. Not freed. Not recruited. **At rest.**
>
> **The thing nobody has said, and nobody has to:** the party's coalition is the drow of Szith Morcane, and **the drow of Szith Morcane are who massacred Vragni's company and desecrated his body.** Glenna's ancestor is undead because of the people Felkur now commands. **If a player gets there, that is the scene.**

### Scene 0½ — the cart

**Before the cave. Before anything.** They come around the last bend and there is a cart.

> There is a cart in the Underdark.
>
> Not a wagon — a farm cart, two wheels, the kind you'd walk a load of grain to market behind, and somebody has got it four hundred miles from the nearest road and a very long way underground. There is a lantern hung off the shaft. There is a folded tarpaulin. There is a **long pine box**, planed and pegged and clearly made by hand, roped down flat in the bed.
>
> And sitting on the tailgate, eating something out of a cloth, in a constable's coat that does not fit him and **a beret**, is **Charles.**

**He sees them. He is on his feet before he has finished swallowing.**

> **"AH — !"** *(both arms up)* **"Mes amis!"**

**Then, before anyone can ask the obvious question, he answers it** — and this is the line the scene is built on:

> **"Non — non, do not look at me like that. This time it is personal. It is not La Résistance!"**
>
> *(and then, drawn up to his full and considerable lack of height, with total sincerity)*
>
> **"It is for L'AMOUR."**

> **He is not diminishing it. He is promoting it.**
>
> Charles has spent his whole invented life believing that some things are worth everything. **He has not stopped believing that. He has found another one.** La Résistance was a cause; **L'amour is a cause**, and he says the word with exactly the conviction he used to spend on freedom, because to him it is the same category of thing and possibly the higher one.
>
> **Nobody gets to hear this as small.** He did not come down here for a woman. **He came down here for L'amour**, and the fact that it is also Glenna is, to Charles, a detail of implementation.

### What he is doing here

**He came for Vragni Moonsmith's body.**

Glenna asked the Order of the Gauntlet to lay her ancestor **to rest**. Charles heard that and understood it the way a man who farms and buries people understands it: **at rest means brought home and put in the ground properly, with his family, by someone who loved him.** And somebody has to carry him.

**So he built a box, and he came to collect.**

- **He walked.** Stargate is not operational — it is a half-built idea in Hillsfar with Felkur's money in it. **There was no shortcut.** He put on the beret, attached himself to the coalition's baggage train somewhere above Sporedome, and came down into the Underdark on foot with an army, pushing a cart.
- **Nobody stopped him**, because he is a constable with papers, a dwarf with a cart, and an absolutely immovable quantity of confidence — and because seven contingents are marching and nobody is auditing the baggage train. **He is one more thing nobody came looking for.**
- **He is not here to fight and he knows it.** He is a grain farmer and a village constable and there is a mummy lord in that cave.
- **He is bright, so he has thought it through** — the cart, the lantern, the tarpaulin, the rope, the box built to a dwarf's measure. **He has thought through everything except the part where he might die**, which he has simply declined to consider.

> **"Non, non, écoutez — I do not go in. I am not a fool, hein? I wait. You do the — "** *(a vague, generous gesture at the entire concept of heroism)* **" — and I take him home. Glenna cannot come. Glenna has the inn. So I come."**
>
> *(and then, because he cannot help himself)*
>
> **"Funny thing you mentioned that. A dwarf of that clan, of that age — they would bury 'im feet to the east, on account of the old Moonsmith holdings bein' west of the river, so 'e faces the door of the hall, see, an' I made the box a hand longer than standard because the old lines ran tall before the — "** *(he stops)* **"Ah. Pardon. L'amour makes a man talk."**

### Why this is worth doing

**It converts the errand into an object.** The party has been asked to lay a mummy lord to rest. **There is now a box, and a man, and a cart**, and "at rest" has stopped being an abstraction the Order phrased for them.

**And it is a hostage, and you should be honest with yourself about that.** A beloved NPC has walked into the Underdark behind an army and is now standing forty feet from a CR 15 encounter, entirely under his own power, having asked nobody's permission.

> **Rules for using him, and hold to them:**
>
> - **He does not enter the cave unless the party brings him in.** He said he would wait. He means it. **He is not a fool; he is in love, which is different.**
> - **Do not kill him for a stinger.** He dies only if the party's own choices put him where the dead can reach him. **If that happens, do not soften it either.**
> - **Do not have him rescued by a die roll.** If he is in danger, a player has to do something about it.
> - **Do not let him be useful in the fight.** He has a shield, a truncheon and forty years of grain. **His job is to be worth protecting.**

### Branch — they tell him to go home

He will not argue. **He will agree, warmly, at length, and not go.**

They will find him exactly where they left him when they come out. If pressed a second time, this is the one place his accent slips and **Karl** answers:

> "She asked the paladins because she didn't want to ask me." *(beat)* "So I ain't waitin' on bein' asked."

**Spend this or don't** — it is the reserve noted in his dossier, and this is a legitimate place for it.

### Branch — they bring him in

Then he is in a war grave full of dwarven dead, and **he is the only person present who knows how any of them should have been buried.** He will say so. Out loud. To Vragni.

> **That is a real card and it is not a joke.** DC 20 Insight says Vragni's fury is the engine; **a living dwarf who came four hundred miles with a hand-built coffin and correct opinions about grave orientation is an argument nobody else in the party can make.** If they use Charles as their opener, give them advantage on it, and let it be the reason.

### Branch — Vragni is destroyed and the cairn burns

Charles loads what is left into the box himself, alone, and takes a long time about it, and **nobody should help him unless they ask first.**

> Then he ropes it down, checks the knots twice, and says: **"Bon. We go home."**
>
> **And the shield goes in the box, or it doesn't.** He will not ask for it. He will look at it once.

### The opening image — lead with this

> The wind coming out of the cave is warm. Not hot — warm, like breath. It smells of dry stone and old iron and, underneath, something clean, which is worse.
>
> Inside, the bones glow. Not the faerzress on the walls — the bones themselves, lit from inside, blue-white and patient, in drifts and mounds the way snow piles against a wall. And in the middle of it there is a banner pole, crossed at the top, planted properly, dressed properly, with the guy ropes still taut.
>
> There are no colours on it. There is a skull.

**Bramgrim, free, no roll:** that is a regimental standard, planted the way you plant one when you intend to hold ground, and somebody has been maintaining the ropes.

### Vragni rises

He comes up out of the cairn **on a nightmare**, with a **ghost** at his side in the posture of a squire. Then the rest: specters and ghosts, more and more, forming a shield wall of thousands on three sides of the party.

> **They are scenery. Say so to yourself now.** The module is explicit — the host is not meant to be fought. Its job is to make the room feel like a verdict. If the party attacks the wall, it does not fight back; it *closes*. Describe the ranks stepping in one pace and stopping. Let the threat be that it could take one more step.

**Vragni's demand** — he speaks in Dwarvish first, then Common, and the switch is a courtesy he clearly resents:

> "You came in with drow."
>
> *(a beat; he looks at Oxania's people, then back)*
>
> "State your house, state your business, and understand that I have heard every answer there is."

### The read — DC 20 Wisdom (Insight)

Success: **Vragni's fury is the engine and it is his alone.** The other dwarves do not radiate evil. They are not vengeful; they are *waiting*. They are standing a line because their commander never told them to stand down, and he never told them because he cannot — the desecration took that from him.

DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) on the cairn: the **skull** is the desecration. While it sits on the poles, undead within 60 feet have advantage on saves against Turn Undead. **15 damage destroys it, or an action knocks it down.**

> Bramgrim gets both of these free if a player asks him to look. He is a Life Cleric with a religion bonus standing in a dwarven war grave. Do not make him roll to notice that his own people were buried wrong.

### The three prices

Vragni will treat, and every route costs somebody who came here to help.

| Route | What it costs | What it gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Give him the Eilistraeans** | **Oxania's clerics, to be destroyed.** They are a coalition contingent and they came as the party's support. | The dead serve the party for one day. |
| **Point him at Szith Morcane's drow** — DC 20 Persuasion | They will fight *through the battle* to reach them and kill everything in the way. Quil'bryn's people. Rilira's people. Anyone between. | An undead army on the field that the party cannot steer. |
| **Destroy Vragni** | A CR 15 fight, at the top of the night, with no long rest afterwards. | The dwarves are freed, they aid the coalition willingly, Zern Xerkstil is satisfied, and Glenna gets what she actually asked for. |

> **The distinction nobody will state for them:** **freeing the host without ending Vragni satisfies neither client.** The Order asked for him slain. **Glenna asked for him at rest.** A party that knocks the skull off the poles, frees the dwarves and leaves Vragni animate has helped the coalition and **failed the errand** — and Felkur is the one who goes home and tells Charles.
>
> **And per Appendix B1, if the heart survives he is not at rest either.** Ending him permanently means the cairn burns. **That is Glenna's request, expressed in mechanics.**

**Oxania, if the first route is raised.** Do not have her plead. Play her flat and let it be unbearable:

> "We will not resist you."
>
> *(she is already unbuckling her sword belt)*
>
> "If you decide it, we will not resist you. I would only ask that you do it with your hands, and not by walking away."

> **Stage direction.** After Oxania says that, **say nothing at all.** Not a prompt, not a "so what do you do." Let the table sit in it. This is the single best silence available tonight and it is worth twenty seconds of real time.

### Branch — the party frees him instead

If somebody makes the Insight and says it out loud — *he is not evil, he is on duty* — let it work, but not for free.

Vragni cannot be persuaded to stand down, **because being unable to stand down is what he is.** The desecration is the wound; the skull on the pole is the wound made visible. **Knocking the skull off the poles does not end him — it lets the other dwarves hear someone other than him.** Play the moment as the ranks turning their heads, all at once, toward the party.

He attacks anyway. He has to. But when he goes down, the host does not swarm.

> "Ten years." *(the ghost squire, the only other one who speaks)* "We sent runners. Nobody came looking."

### The offer

**Locked: they offer to march** (Ruling 18). Play it as the squire discovering, in real time, that he is now in command of thousands of men and has no idea what to do with them.

> The wall does not come apart. It does something worse — it **stands to attention.**
>
> Thousands of them, all at once, across three sides of the cavern, and the sound of it is not armour, because none of them have armour any more. It is just the idea of the sound, arriving late.
>
> The squire is looking at the empty place where his commander was. Then at the banner pole. Then, eventually, at you.
>
> "They're waiting."
>
> *(a long pause, and when he speaks again it is very carefully, like a man reading instructions in a language he only half has)*
>
> "There's a city up the road that belongs to fire giants now. He'd have gone. He'd have gone tonight, and he'd have taken every one of them, and it would have been the wrong order and they'd have followed it anyway."
>
> *(he straightens)*
>
> "So I'm asking instead. **We can march with you.** Tell me where you want us."

**Then stop, and do not say anything else.** No caveat, no hint, no "are you sure." The offer is clean and generous and made by a man who has just been handed his first command.

> **What the party is not being told, and must work out themselves:**
>
> 1. **Maerimydra has spent a month preparing for undead.** Salt lines, burn pits, consecrated ground, priests moved forward, turning drilled — all of it aimed at Velasta, all of it useless against this coalition **right up until the coalition fields undead.**
> 2. **§162: spectral dwarves on the field damage every enemy and every drow character** — Quil'bryn's, Rilira's, the Eilistraeans, indiscriminately, 4d6+3 necrotic.
> 3. **These dwarves were massacred by Szith Morcane's drow**, and the party's army is Szith Morcane's drow.
>
> Every one of those facts is available to them. **Do not connect any of them.** See *Braced For The Wrong War*.

**Akritas argues to leave them** — once, flatly, on grounds 2 and 3, and she will not mention ground 1 because she has not thought of it. **Bramgrim argues to take them**, because refusing a soldier's offer of service is a thing he finds physically difficult. Then defer to the players.

### Branch — if they say no

The squire takes it well, which is worse.

> "Right. Yes." *(he nods at nothing, twice)* "Then we'll hold here. Somebody should."

**They hold the Cave.** No spectral dwarves in Part 3, the coalition stays invisible in Hledh's threat picture, and the party has made the campaign's best strategic decision without anyone telling them so. **Do not confirm it.** Let it pay off silently in Part 3 when the city's defences are pointed the wrong way.

### Branch — if they say yes

Take it graciously and let them enjoy it — an army of the dead is a genuinely enormous prize and they earned it in a fight. **Then run §162 honestly in Part 3:** the dwarves hit every enemy *and every drow character on the field*, and the first time Quil'bryn's people take 4d6+3 necrotic from the party's new allies, do not soften it.

And the larger bill arrives at the gate: **Maerimydra sees undead in the line, and every ward Hledh built comes online against an army that was walking in underneath them.**

**Then destroy the heart, or don't.** See Open Decisions — if you rule the heart is intact in the cairn and the party doesn't find it, **Vragni reforms in 24 hours**, which is exactly when Part 4 fires.

### Branch — the Nerevenans arrive mid-scene

They are assigned here (Ruling 3). Arrive them at the *worst* moment: after the price is on the table and before the party has answered.

Kiaransalee's cult looks at a mummy lord commanding a host of the wrongly-buried and sees, correctly, **a colleague**. Their delegate — Asala's second, who has said nothing since Szith Morcane — starts talking to Vragni without asking the party's permission, and is *better at it than the party is*.

- **If the party has already resolved it:** the Nerevenans arrive to find their objective completed by their allies. Read as a service or an insult entirely by how the party says it. The delegate says "thank you" in a voice that means the opposite.
- **If the party has not:** the cult offers Vragni what the party would not, and the party has about one round of table time to outbid them or let it happen.

### Fuse 3 — fire it here

Whatever else happens, **a Nerevenan runner leaves this cave for Maerimydra.** Routine traffic; nobody hides it. What that runner carries:

> *A drow archmage named Solom Ned'razak disintegrated Asala in front of the assembled factions at Szith Morcane.*

Not "Solom is alive." A name attached to a killing. **The party can intercept the framing — and only the framing — right now, in this scene, by talking to the delegate.** They cannot stop the runner and they cannot dictate the timing.

> **Bramgrim's brief makes this his problem now.** Handout One says *preserve the drow alliance and protect Solom Ned'razak at all costs*, and a Nerevenan runner is about to carry, to Velasta, a sentence with Solom's name and a killing attached to it. **He is the only person present under a formal obligation to care**, and per the craft rule he argues it once and defers.
>
> Let him say it plainly and awkwardly — he is not good at this — and then let the players decide whether to act on it.

> **Do not flag this.** Mention the runner once, in a sentence about the Nerevenans' logistics, and move on. If a player catches it, they have just found the highest-leverage action available tonight for the price of one conversation. If nobody catches it, Fuse 3 fires unframed and Part 3 gets much worse.

### The fight, if it happens

**Vragni Moonsmith — mummy lord, CR 15.** On a **nightmare**, with a **ghost** squire. Strong-party adjustment (§148): **add 2 specters and 1 ghost**; very strong adds *dispel magic* to his lair actions.

- **Vulnerable to fire.** Felkur's flamethrower cannon infusion. The Hellfire Projectors, if they brought one and can crew it. This is not subtle and they will find it — let them have it, they earned the Projectors in a collapsing forge.
- **Cairn:** advantage vs Turn Undead within 60 ft while the skull stands. Bramgrim's whole kit gets better the moment somebody knocks it down. **Make that a real tactical objective, not a lore detail.**
- **Crevasses:** 30 feet deep, DC 15 Athletics. Daein at Str 23 can throw things into them. The nightmare flies and will use the geography.
- **Faerzress:** any creature targeted by *blasphemous word* makes a Wis save (same DC) or gains a madness level. **Any character taking psychic damage** makes a Wis save at the effect's DC, or DC 10.
- **Eilistraeen support:** end of round 1, one cleric turns the noncorporeal undead (DC 13). End of round 2 and every round after, *healing word* on whoever needs it most (1d4+3). **They will not touch the mummy.** They are afraid, and they should be visibly afraid.

> **Stat block locked: 2024 XMM.** Full tactical build-out — including the two things that make it a fight instead of a damage race — is in **Appendix B**. Read it before Scene 1; the nightmare is load-bearing and it is not obvious why.

### Treasure

In the bone pile: **a shield set with gems and chased with silver, 1,000 gp.** Dwarven clan work — **Moonsmith work**, off her ancestor's own bone pile.

> **Do not price it for them.** Let them find it, let somebody read the clan-mark, and then let it sit there being worth a thousand gold pieces. **Selling it is a real option and a real choice** — Felkur is a businessman capitalising a teleportation network, and it is a great deal of money for a shield nobody in the party can use.
>
> **Handing it back to Glenna is worth nothing in gold and everything else.** Never say so.

### NPC notes

- **Vragni Moonsmith** — a company commander who was murdered mid-posting and has been holding the line for ten years because nobody told him the war was over. Voice: clipped, formal, exhausted underneath. *He never raises his voice.* What he wants from this scene: an order, from anyone with the standing to give one. What he'll take instead: drow.
- **The ghost squire** — speaks twice, total. Once to correct Vragni on a fact, once at the end. Play as a young soldier who has been managing his commander's decline with enormous tact.
- **Oxania** — priestess of Eilistraee, come to make peace with the dead on behalf of a people who did this to them. She knows what she is. She will not use it as leverage and that is what makes it leverage.
- **Asala's second** — has not spoken since watching their envoy turn to dust. Speaks here, competently, for the first time, and every word is a small audit of whether this alliance is worth anything.

## Scene 2 — The march west, and what the rest costs

### Setup

The coalition columns split. The Stone Giants north, singing. Rilira's people and Grundar's duergar east. The Hidden already gone. Quil'bryn's drow standing in place, watching everyone else leave, in full view.

**This is a short scene. Twenty minutes at most.** Its job is bookkeeping made emotional.

### The rest question — make it explicit, once

Module rule, and they should hear it plainly from Elanil's staff or from Semdran:

- **Short rest between objectives is permitted — but it diverts the allied detachment**, and the second objective runs without support.
- **A long rest replaces the second objective entirely.** They do not get both.
- **No long rest after the second objective.** Part 4 does not wait.

> They just fought a mummy lord. They will want the long rest. **Let them see the exact price and choose.** If they take it, Fortify Gains does not happen, the coalition doesn't get its defences, and Part 3 runs against un-softened enemies — and you say nothing about it until Part 3.

### What arrives on the road

Three pieces of news, one per messenger, spaced out. Deliver flatly.

1. **Quil'bryn's people were noticed.** Rilira sent a runner. The message is two words long and it is not a question. *(He was held in reserve and every faction saw it. Ruling 4.)*
2. **The Stone Giants are ahead of schedule** on the Northern approach and are very pleased with themselves. This is real and good and should be enjoyed.
3. **Nothing from the Hidden.** They went to the Underdoor and there has been no word. That is normal for them. It is also what it would look like if it were not normal.

### Branch — if a player asks about Velasta

She is somewhere in Maerimydra, hunting fire giants, on the party's own lie. Nobody has heard anything. **Confirm the absence, refuse the detail.** If they ask whether the runner from the Cave will reach her: yes, eventually, days.

### NPC note

- **Semdran** — druid of Mielikki out of Elventree, leading the ranger detachment attached to the party for the Western Base. Surface elf, has never been this deep, is handling it by being relentlessly practical about equipment. He is not a fighter and he knows it. What he wants: to bring his rangers home. **Give him a name-tag moment now so that his people mattering later costs something.**

## Scene 3 — The Mirror Cavern (Western Forward Base)

### Setup

The rarely-used western approach. §110. And it is the most beautiful room in the campaign, which is the joke.

> Every surface is a mirror. The walls are polished to a sheen no tool made — Graz'zt is within a mile and stone doesn't get a say. Stalagmites and stalactites burn purple and green, floor and ceiling both, and each one is standing in its own reflection, so the room is twice as tall as it is and half of it is upside down.
>
> It is bright. After a tenday of the Underdark, it is *bright*.
>
> In the middle of the floor there is a low grey drift that is not stone.

**Ceiling 15–20 ft. Bright light throughout.** Bones in the centre: difficult terrain, disadvantage on Stealth.

### The reflections rule — say it out loud before initiative

Because it changes everything and it is not fair to spring it:

- **Gaze attacks do not need line of effect.** Averting your eyes is useless. **Closing them still works.**
- **Any creature can see any other creature within 30 feet**, regardless of cover or intervening objects.

### The fight

**3 umber hulks**, hidden in the walls, waiting on tremorsense. Two weeks without a meal. Driven to frenzy by the demon lord's proximity — **they fight to the death.**

- **The burst-out:** anyone adjacent to the wall an umber hulk erupts from takes **5d6 slashing** from flying mirror-shards; **DC 15 Dex** halves.
- **Strong-party adjustment (use it):** +20 HP each, claw damage +4, **confusing gaze DC +1**, and every creature targeted by confusing gaze is *also* targeted by **dispel magic (+7)**.
- **Faerzress:** the first time in 24 hours a creature is targeted by confusing gaze here, it also makes a **Wis save at the gaze DC or gains a madness level.**
- **Semdran's rangers:** at the start of each round, every foe takes one longbow attack (+4, 1d8+2). The druid has two 1st-level slots at +3: *faerie fire* (DC 13) and *healing word*. **They are no match for umber hulks. If the party leaves them exposed, one of them dies.**

> **The tactical read you want them to find.** Akritas needs her eyes; Bramgrim, Daein and Felkur do not, to varying degrees. A party that closes eyes and fights by *Spirit Guardians*, cannon, and reach takes a much better fight than one that tries to out-stare a mirror. **Do not hint.** Describe the reflections a third time instead.

> **The dispel magic rider is the real threat.** It targets each gaze victim's highest-level active spell. Bramgrim's concentration, Felkur's infusions, Daein's mandolin *invisibility*. Play it as the room stripping them, not as damage.

### Developments — the captured hulk

DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation) on a captured hulk, which is crazed and wants only to fight or mate:

- Their eggs get taken by **giant spiders that live nearby in the great arena** *(the Coliseum — Part 3 geography)*.
- **"Sometimes prey comes by with boxes of junk. Pretty to look at. Not important."**
- It would like to mate and kill, in that order or the other one.

### **The bone pile — this is the scene**

The module's treasure line is **1,000 gp in coin and small gems, "some of which is contained in the broken remains of ornate wooden boxes."** Rule those boxes hard:

> They are **Spire courier crates.** Black lacquer, brass corners, and on each lid a pressed wax seal in the shape of a contract sigil — a fox's head over a column of figures. Two weeks of them. Perhaps nine, perhaps a dozen, in a heap, chewed open by something with no interest in paperwork.
>
> The seals on the intact ones are **unbroken.** Not by a thief — by anyone. Nobody opened them to check. Nobody cut them to reclaim the contents. And tucked in the wreck of one, a waybill: consignor, consignee, a transaction number, and a countersign line at the bottom for the receiving auditor.
>
> **Every countersign line is blank.**

**What that means, and let them assemble it:**

- The Spire ships freight along this tunnel on a schedule.
- The freight stopped arriving **two weeks ago**.
- **Nobody opened a file.** No recovery notice, no investigation, no patrol, no replacement route. A yugoloth contract-enforcement house lost fourteen days of consignments on a fixed route and *did not notice*.
- Which is why the western approach — the approach the coalition chose — was defended by nothing but hungry animals.

> **GM-only.** This is the tower thread, delivered by freight instead of by a drow. Vraxen has been running a forensic audit on a forger for months and the occupation's books have gone unminded behind him. **The party's Ch11 robbery is the reason their chosen approach was undefended.** Nobody will say this tonight and they should not have to. Let Felkur hold a waybill with a blank countersign line and think about it.
>
> **Akritas recognises the seal.** She has an Enclave brief naming this building. She says nothing. See The Table.

**If they ask what's actually in the crates:** coin, small gems, and — in one — a sheaf of blank forms. Not the infernal contracts they already hold; ordinary internal requisition slips. Worthless, and therefore the point.

### Branch — they burn the crates or leave them

Fine. The waybill is the payload and it is portable. If they take nothing, have the Owlbear pick one up, because the Owlbear likes shiny boxes and this is a legitimate use of a companion.

### Branch — they try to backtrack the courier route

Good instinct, wrong night. The route runs east toward the city, through ground they have not taken. **Note it and hold it for Part 3** — it is a clean second approach to the Spire's base if they want one later, and it does not require the drow.

## Scene 4 — Fortify Gains (the alarm is already sounded)

### Setup

The base is taken. §122. **The party has one hour, and you do not tell them how long they have.**

> Out of the dark, unhurried, the mushroom men of Sporedome. There are more of them than anyone expected. They come in waving — actually waving, with the whole arm, the way you wave at someone across a field — and the spores come off them in a slow bright drift that settles on everything and tastes, faintly, of thanks.
>
> Behind them: tents. Rope. Water. Bandages. Somebody's soup.

The party may **take a short rest** or **help build defences**. Both, partly, if they split the hour. Let them be creative: glyphs in the passageways, embankments of mushroom and rock, cannon emplacement, tripwires, Felkur's cold infusion iced across the approach. **Adjust the map for whatever they build and honour it.**

> **The trap, and it is not a gotcha.** §110 is explicit: doing Fortify Gains from the *western* position means the incoming patrol is investigating why so many travellers vanish in this tunnel, and **the alarm counts as sounded automatically.** The buffed version comes. The party has no way to know this and every way to prepare for it in general — which is exactly the right shape. **Reward preparation, punish resting.**

### The patrol

**1 cambion + 2 drow elite warriors**, alarm-sounded loadout:

- Cambion: *aid* (+5 hp), *see invisibility*, *longstrider* (+10 ft), **drinks a potion of invisibility just before approaching**, and holds a **scroll of *cloudkill*, used round one.**
- Drow: *longstrider*, *aid*, potions of invisibility.
- They approach under stealth, **strike from surprise, and go for crippling one or two party members immediately.**
- **Scale up.** Against four 12th-level PCs and an owlbear, RAW is thin. Strong: add 2 orogs, drow poison on all weapons (DC 13 Con, poisoned 1 hr, fail by 5+ unconscious 1 hr). Very strong: add 2 more drow elite warriors. **The lethality here is the invisible *cloudkill* opener on a camp full of myconids, not the stat blocks** — if they built defences, let the defences matter; if they rested in tents, let the tents burn.
- **Myconid support:** three times, once per round, they rush one grounded foe with **pacification spores** — DC 11 Con or stunned 1 minute. **If they stun one, they run away.** They are civilians and they are being brave and they should look like it.
- The drow break if the cambion falls. **The cambion flies away the instant it is clearly lost.** He has no intention of dying here.

### The interrogation — the second half of the scene

If they take the cambion or a drow alive: **DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation)** gets the module's facts.

- **Matliedun**, a cambion, is master of the Undying Temple. Blood of the Dark Prince. Works directly under **Squallocks the Vrock**, advisor to Graz'zt.
- **The drow slaves.** Every non-warrior drow slave in Maerimydra was taken by demons into **Castle Maerimydra about a day ago.** They have not been seen since. *(The drow prisoner is genuinely frightened about this and it is the only thing he volunteers.)*
- If the party believes the alarm was sounded on them: he says all of Maerimydra knows they are coming and is rising against them. **DC 15 Insight: half-truth at best.**
- **If asked what the city is braced for** — and somebody should ask — he answers without needing a check, because it is not a secret and he thinks it is stupid:

> "The grey woman."
>
> *(off their faces)*
>
> "You didn't know. **You didn't know.** Half the priests in Maerimydra are stood on consecrated ground waiting for a dead drow to walk up the eastern stair, the giants have burned every corpse in the city, and nobody — *nobody* — has said one word about an army." *(a long look at the stone giant standing behind them)* "Is that yours?"

### **The hedge — play for this instead of Intimidation**

The better scene is not a threat. It is an offer.

**Iaveth** *(name is yours — the module doesn't give him one)* is a mid-grade cambion doing sector patrol in a war he has decided is already over. He is not loyal, not brave, and not stupid. He is holding a fistful of *scrolls he had to sign for*, which tells you everything about how he thinks.

Play him with the specific exhaustion of middle management:

> "Three times. I filed for reinforcement on this approach three times." *(he is bleeding and he is still counting on his fingers)* "Do you know what came back? Not a refusal. I could work with a refusal. **Nothing.** No stamp, no return, no acknowledgement of receipt. I sent three requisitions into a building and the building did not answer."

And, if they offer him a side rather than a knife:

> "You want to know what everyone in the west knows and nobody writes down? The Prince has taken his hand off the giants. He came down here for the *ritual*, not for Hledh. Ask yourself why nothing gets enforced any more. Ask yourself why I'm out here with scrolls instead of a company."
>
> *(he watches their faces very carefully here)*
>
> "You've heard it before. Somewhere. Haven't you."

> **GM-only, and this is the payoff.** He is describing **Daein's Ch9 impression of Graz'zt**, laundered through two years of demonic gossip into settled fact. The party manufactured this belief. It is now doing more damage to the occupation than any battle they have fought. **And it is false — Graz'zt is physically in the city, in the castle courtyard, right now.** The rumour has a detonation date, and whoever fed it is standing next to it.
>
> Do not confirm anything to the players. If a table works out what he is quoting, let the silence do it.

### What he wants

Confirmation, from a source that isn't another cambion, that the rumour is true — so he can pick a winner in time. **That is his price and it is cheap and the party can pay it with a sentence.** Paying it makes the western sector even softer and puts the party's name on a lie with a fuse in it.

### Branch — nobody takes a prisoner

Then Part 3 arrives blind: no Matliedun, no Squallocks, no missing slaves, no hedge. Playable, much worse, and entirely their doing. **Do not fudge a survivor.**

### Treasure

### Setup

The hour is spent, the patrol is dead or fled, the base is fortified and manned by myconids and whatever's left of Semdran's rangers. **No long rest.** The coalition's other columns are moving. The gate is ahead.

# Appendix A — if they move fast: the Singed Veil

**Do not stage this.** Do not send anyone out to meet them. This is here in case they clear both objectives early, push, and start reaching for the city.

**What it is.** A magic shop buried beneath a drow manor near the ruins of the **Shattered Tower**. A claustrophobic pocket of air in a city of smoke; ceiling made of the underside of a crushed marble staircase; lightning bottled in cracked jars; the air smells of ozone, old blood, and burnt cinnamon. Sign outside reads *The Singed Veil*, and its proprietor is quietly furious that anyone read it.

**Who she is.** A female drow **mage** — which is itself remarkable, and Bramgrim's elven religion knowledge says so: drow women become clerics, not wizards, and drow mages hate Lolth's clerics with an abiding passion. Half her face is beautiful. The other half is a rune of stars that she will tell you was a gift and is actually **a fire giant's branding scar.** Tattered finery, ash in the fabric, one red eye doing all the work.

**Her standing position, in her own words:** she doesn't know about economies or supply and demand. She knows she is one of the few stores left, and she sells to people she knows will never betray her. The party qualified once, on the strength of being too conspicuous to survive informing on her.

**Prior business (Ch8–11).** Scroll of *seeming* at 600 gp — bought and used to walk a portable hole and a breathing apparatus into Vraxen's dinner. Potions of fire resistance at 300. Dust of disappearance at 500. And **the faerzress crystals Felkur used to recalibrate the Spire's teleportation circle came from her** (`docs/recent_events.md`). **She is already, unknowingly, an accessory to the robbery that broke Vraxen's concentration.**

**Why she is the drow resistance angle.** Maerimydra's surviving drow want one thing: **the non-warrior slaves taken into Castle Maerimydra two days ago.** That overrides everything. They know the city's bones — service passages, plateau climbs, where patrols actually walk — and they are the only faction that can put the party at the Spire's base unseen. Their friction with the coalition is exact and unfixable: **Szith Morcane's drow are marching on this city as conquerors, and the Cave of Black Whispers massacre was Szith Morcane's work.**

**What she can sell tonight, if they get to her:**

- That the Spire **abolished physical keys.** Access is by *contract* now — the lift opens for signatories. Very yugoloth: security as contract law.
- That **the scent-lock on the golem requires a fresh scent**, minutes old. To rob Vraxen they must first stand in a room with Vraxen.
- That the corridor sequence changed **and is warded against divination**. She does not know the new sequence. *(It's Fibonacci. Their old prime-number answer is half-right, which is worse than wrong.)*
- Gossip, free, because she is enjoying it: **nobody has audited anything in months.** She has not been invoiced. She finds this hilarious and slightly ominous.

**Her price:** the disappeared. Not gold.

> **If they reach her tonight, keep it to fifteen minutes and end the session there instead of at the gate.** Her scene is worth more with the city around it, and the "we need a dinner invitation" realisation is a better cliffhanger than the wall. But do not build toward it — if they don't push, she waits.

# Appendix B — Encounter Build-Outs

All three encounters, 2024 stat blocks, tuned for **four 12th-level PCs plus the Owlbear**.

**Before you read the tables, the honest assessment:** this party's round-one output is roughly **180–220 damage** — Akritas with the Oathbow and Bracers of Archery, Daein at Str 23 with three attacks, Felkur's cannon plus spells, Bramgrim's *Spirit Guardians*. **No single 2024 stat block survives two rounds of that.** So none of these three fights are built as HP races. Each one has a different structural spine:

| Encounter | What the fight is actually about |
|---|---|
| **The Cave** | A boss who **cannot be hurt properly until they kill his horse**, and **does not stay dead** until they burn the cairn |
| **The Mirror Cavern** | A **positioning puzzle** where the room is the monster and closing your eyes is the answer |
| **Fortify Gains** | **An alarm they don't know is sounded**, an opening burst that punishes resting, and a fight whose job is to produce a live prisoner |

## B1 — The Cave of Black Whispers

### The roster

| Creature | Source | AC | HP | CR | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vragni Moonsmith** | XMM *Mummy Lord* | 17 | **187** | 15 | The boss. Mounted. |
| **His nightmare** | XMM *Nightmare* | 13 | **68** | 3 | **The keystone. See below.** |
| **The ghost squire** | XMM *Ghost* | 11 | 45 | 4 | Bodyguard, and the only other voice |
| **2 specters** | XMM *Specter* | 12 | 22 | 1 | Chaff. Strong-party addition (§148). |
| **1 extra ghost** | XMM *Ghost* | 11 | 45 | 4 | Strong-party addition (§148). |
| **2 Chosen Champions** | **Ultimate Bestiary: The Dreaded Accursed**, p.88 | 16 | **136 ea** | 10 | **Recommended addition — see below.** |

Raw XP ≈ 16,300. Ignore the budget maths — the specters are speed bumps and the fight's difficulty lives entirely in Vragni's action economy.

### **The keystone: kill the horse first**

This is the whole design and it is pure RAW.

- **Vragni is Vulnerable to fire.**
- **The nightmare has *Confer Fire Resistance*** — it grants Fire resistance to its rider.
- **Resistance and vulnerability cancel.** While Vragni is mounted, **fire does normal damage to him.** Dismount him and it does double.

So the party's best weapon — Felkur's flamethrower infusion, a Hellfire Projector, *any* fire — is switched off until somebody works out that the 68-HP horse is the reason. **The nightmare is the softest target on the field and the most important one**, and nothing about it announces that.

> **How they find it.** Don't gate it behind a check. Let them *feel* it: describe a fire hit landing and the flames sheeting off him and pooling around the nightmare's hooves without marking him. Say it twice if you have to. **Arcana DC 15 confirms** the mount is doing it. The moment somebody says "shoot the horse," give it to them and let them be delighted.
>
> The nightmare also has **Ethereal Stride** — it can take Vragni and up to three others to the Ethereal Plane. **Use it once, at half HP, as an escape that turns into a re-entry behind the party's line.** Never use it to end the fight.

### ⭐ The fix for the solo-boss problem: **Chosen Champions**

**Source: *Ultimate Bestiary: The Dreaded Accursed*, p.88** (`/mnt/g/My Drive/Kickstarter/NordGames/…`). This solves the thing I flagged at the top of this appendix — 187 HP does not survive this party's 200-a-round — **and the book's own flavour text is Vragni's company, almost word for word:**

> *"Some underlings are of such fanatical loyalty that, at the point of their master's death, they will gladly give their lives in order to continue their service and become a **chosen champion**. The death of a monarch or nobleman can be followed by a wave of death, as their household guard and soldiers give their lives to fill their tomb with protectors."*

> **They did not merely die with him. They volunteered.** Which sharpens *nobody came looking* into something worse: **nobody came, and they had already chosen to stay.**

**Chosen Champion — CR 10, AC 16 (half-plate, shield), 136 HP, Speed 20 ft.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Vulnerable** | **Fire** *(same as Vragni — the answer stays the answer)* |
| **Resistant** | Nonmagical B/P/S |
| **Immune** | Necrotic, poison; charmed, exhaustion, frightened, **paralysed**, poisoned |
| ***Harrying Shield*** | **While it has its shield, the area within 5 ft of it is difficult terrain.** |
| ***Indomitable* (2/day)** | Reroll a failed save. |
| **Multiattack** | Dreadful Glare **plus two** Cursed Khopesh |
| **Cursed Khopesh** | +8, **7 (1d6+4) slashing + 14 (4d6) necrotic**, and **DC 16 Con or mummy rot** |
| **Dreadful Glare** | DC 13 Wis, 60 ft — **Frightened**; fail by 5+ and also **Paralysed** |
| **Legendary (3)** | *Dreadful Glare* · ***Shield Formation*** (move up to speed, **must end within 5 ft of its master or nearest ally**) · *Attack* (2 actions) |

### Why they transform the encounter

1. **The shield wall becomes real.** *Harrying Shield* means each champion drags a 5-foot bubble of difficult terrain around with it. **Two of them standing together is a wall the party has to go through or around.**
2. ***Shield Formation* is the whole design.** It is a **legendary action** — every round, at the end of someone else's turn, a champion repositions to stay beside Vragni or beside each other. **The wall re-forms itself, using its own action economy, in front of whatever the party just tried to reach.**
3. **The module's scenery gets a foreground.** §148 says the host of thousands is *"there for effect."* Keep that — **and have two of them step out of it.** The wall stays theatre; two named soldiers stop being theatre.
4. **Mummy rot is a second curse track.** Vragni's Rotting Fist already blocks healing on one target. **Every khopesh hit threatens the same thing at DC 16** — *can't regain hit points, −2d6 maximum per 24 hours, dead and dust at 0, lifted only by* remove curse. **Bramgrim's night gets considerably harder in an interesting way.**
5. **Cursed Weapons (p.88).** The khopesh keeps its curse if dropped, **and anyone can pick it up and use it** — taking 10 (3d6) necrotic at the start of each turn they hold it, plus a rot save. **Offer it. Somebody will take it.**

> **Run two, not three.** Two champions plus Vragni is **459 HP of real targets** and roughly 200 incoming a round, which is a genuine three-to-four-round fight for this party. **Three is the "strong party" dial and it tips toward lethal** — mostly because paralysis density climbs, and paralysed plus a melee attacker within 5 feet is an automatic critical.
>
> **If you add the third, drop the two specters** — they contribute nothing but initiative slots at this tier.

### Name them

They are Vragni's household guard, and **they should have names before the party kills them.** The ghost squire knows all of them. When the first one falls, **have the squire say its name**, once, and nothing else.

### **The second spine: Undying Restoration**

> ***Undead Restoration.*** If destroyed, the mummy gains a new body in 24 hours if its heart is intact. The heart is a Tiny object, **AC 17, HP 10, immune to all damage except Fire.**

**Recommended ruling — the heart is inside the cairn, under the skull.** The module never says where it is, and putting it there collapses three things into one object the party can see from round one:

- the **desecration** (advantage vs Turn Undead within 60 ft, DC 15 Religion to spot),
- the **rejuvenation anchor**,
- and the **insult that keeps him on post**.

And the exit is *fire* — the same answer as the nightmare. A party that burns the cairn ends him permanently, breaks the Turn ward, frees the dwarves, and satisfies Zern Xerkstil, in one action.

> **If you'd rather he comes back:** say nothing. He reforms in 24 hours — **which is when Part 4 fires.** A freed mummy lord loose behind the coalition's lines during Mother's attack is either your best complication or one thread too many. Still your call; see Open Decisions.

**Cairn as an object:** the skull takes **15 damage** to destroy (immune poison/psychic) or can be knocked off the poles with an action. The heart underneath needs the cairn opened first — one action to clear the rock and skulls, then the heart is exposed. **Total cost to end him permanently: two actions and a fire source.**

### Vragni's numbers

- **Multiattack:** one **Rotting Fist** *or* **Channel Negative Energy**, plus **Dreadful Glare**.
- **Rotting Fist** — +9, reach 5 ft, **15 (2d10+4) bludgeoning + 10 (3d6) necrotic**, and the target is **cursed**: cannot regain hit points, gains nothing from a long rest, and loses 3d6 maximum HP every 24 hours. **Reduced to 0 by this attack = dead and dust, no death saves.**
- **Channel Negative Energy** — ranged +9, 60 ft, **25 (6d6+4) necrotic**.
- **Dreadful Glare** — **DC 17 Wis**, one creature within 60 ft. Fail: **25 (6d6+4) psychic and Paralyzed** until the end of his next turn.
- **Whirlwind of Sand (reaction)** — on being hit: +2 AC against that attack, then **teleport up to 60 ft**, blinding chosen creatures within 5 ft of the destination.
- **Legendary Actions:** *Glare* (Dreadful Glare, once per round) and *Necrotic Strike* (one Fist or Channel Negative Energy).
- **Legendary Resistance 3/day — 4/day in his lair.** He is in his lair.
- **Magic Resistance. Truesight 60 ft.** Immune necrotic and poison.

> **Where the lethality actually is.** Not the HP total — the **curse plus paralysis stack.** Rotting Fist shuts off Bramgrim's healing on that target for the rest of the night, and Dreadful Glare paralysis means every melee attack against that PC is an automatic critical hit from within 5 feet. **One bad save on a front-liner and Vragni's next Legendary Action can kill them outright**, because Rotting Fist at 0 HP is dust, not death saves.
>
> Use that once, early, on Daein or the Owlbear. Then let the party solve it.

### Lair actions (initiative 20, one per round)

The 2014 block had these and the module assumes they exist (§148 offers adding *dispel magic* for a very strong party). The XMM block does not ship with any, so here are three built for a war grave:

1. **The Line Steps Up.** The spectral host advances **10 feet on all three sides.** The usable battlefield shrinks. After four uses the party is fighting in a 30-foot box with a wall of the dead at their backs. *(This is the fight's clock. It is also pure theatre — the host never attacks.)*
2. **Grave Cold.** Bone drifts grip. Each creature that started its turn in a bone pile makes **DC 15 Strength** or is **Restrained** until it uses an action to break free.
3. **The Roll Call.** Vragni speaks the name of a dwarf who died here. Each non-undead creature within 60 ft that can hear him makes **DC 15 Wisdom** or takes **10 (3d6) psychic** and has disadvantage on its next attack roll.
   - *Very strong party (§148):* add **dispel magic (+6)** as a fourth option.

### The faerzress rider, converted

The module hangs its madness trigger on *blasphemous word*, which the 2024 block doesn't have. **Move it to Dreadful Glare:**

> The first time in 24 hours a creature is targeted by **Dreadful Glare** in this cave, it must also make a **DC 17 Wisdom** save or gain **1 level of madness.**

And the general rule stands: **any character taking psychic damage here** makes a Wisdom save at the effect's DC, or **DC 10** if none is listed.

> That is a lot of madness pressure, and they each have exactly one braincap. **A second inside 24 hours is 1d4 madness levels with no save.** Let them make that mistake if they want to.

### Terrain

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Bone piles** | Difficult terrain; disadvantage on Stealth; the *Grave Cold* lair action keys off them |
| **Crevasses** | 30 ft deep, **DC 15 Athletics** to climb. Daein at Str 23 can throw things in. |
| **Columns** | Cover; **DC 15 Athletics** to climb |
| **Light** | Dim only. **Even with darkvision they cannot see across the cavern.** The nightmare sheds bright light in 10 ft / dim for 10 more — **it is the brightest thing in the room and it advertises where the boss is.** |
| **Ceiling** | 15–20 ft. The nightmare flies (90 ft, hover) and will use it. |

### Allied support (§148)

**Oxania's clerics of Eilistraee.** End of round 1: one cleric turns the noncorporeal undead (DC 13). End of round 2 and every round after: *healing word* on whoever needs it most, **1d4+3**. **They will not enter melee and they will not target the mummy.** Play their fear visibly — it makes the price Vragni named earlier land harder.

### Round-by-round, if you want a spine

- **R1** — Vragni opens with **Dreadful Glare on the most armoured PC** and a Legendary *Glare* on a second. He wants two people paralysed before anyone acts. Lair action: **The Line Steps Up.**
- **R2** — Rotting Fist on anything paralysed. If nobody is, Channel Negative Energy on Felkur or Bramgrim. Ghost squire uses **Horrific Visage** (DC 13 cone, 60 ft). Lair: **Grave Cold.**
- **R3** — First fire hit lands wrong; describe the flames pooling at the hooves. **Whirlwind of Sand** the moment a big hit connects — teleport him 60 ft, blind two people on arrival. Lair: **The Roll Call.**
- **R4+** — If the nightmare is dead, he stops repositioning and starts killing. If it lives, he keeps kiting. **The ghost squire interposes and dies rather than let anyone reach the cairn** — that is the tell that the cairn matters.

## B2 — The Mirror Cavern (Western Forward Base)

### The roster

**3 umber hulks** (XMM), with the module's **strong-party adjustment** applied:

| | Base | **As run** |
|---|---|---|
| AC | 18 | 18 |
| HP | 93 | **113** *(+20)* |
| Rend | +8, 2d6+5 (12), ×3 | **+8, 2d6+9 (16), ×3 — 48/round** |
| Confusing Gaze | DC 14 | **DC 15** |
| Extra | — | **dispel magic (+7)** on one target of each cone |

Also: **burrow 20 ft through solid rock**, tremorsense 60 ft, darkvision 120 ft. **They fight to the death** — frenzied by Graz'zt's proximity.

> **§148's "dispel magic on every gaze target" is too much in 2024.** Confusing Gaze is now a **30-foot cone**, not a single-target gaze — applying *dispel magic* to everyone in a cone would strip the whole party in one bonus action. **Rule it as one target of your choice per cone**, highest-level active spell. That still eats Bramgrim's concentration or Felkur's infusion when it matters.

### **The 2024 conversion — read this, the module rule doesn't survive translation**

§110's reflection rule assumes the 2014 *passive gaze*, where averting your eyes was the standard counter. **2024 Confusing Gaze is a bonus-action 30-foot cone**, and there is no averting. So convert it like this and **say it out loud before initiative:**

> **Mirror Cone.** An umber hulk in this cavern may originate its Confusing Gaze cone **from any reflective surface within 60 feet of itself** — which is every surface. Cover and intervening objects do not block it.
>
> **A creature that cannot see (eyes closed, Blinded, in darkness it can't see through) automatically succeeds on the save.**
>
> **Any creature can see any other creature within 30 feet**, regardless of cover, barring invisibility.

That preserves both halves of the module's intent: **the room makes the effect inescapable, and shutting your eyes is the answer.**

**Confusing Gaze on a failure:** no Reactions until the start of the hulk's next turn, and roll 1d8 — 1–4 do nothing; 5–6 no action or bonus action, move in a random direction; 7–8 melee attack a random creature in reach.

> **Recharge 5–6.** Three hulks means roughly **one cone per round** across the group. That is the pressure.

### The faerzress rider

> The first time in 24 hours a creature is targeted by **Confusing Gaze** here, it also makes a **DC 15 Wisdom** save or gains **1 level of madness.**

Note that this triggers on being *targeted*, not on failing — so a 30-foot cone can put a madness save on three PCs at once. **This is the most dangerous thing in the room** and it is invisible to the players as a threat.

### The ambush

Hidden in the walls on tremorsense. When a creature moves within range of one, **it bursts out and the others follow.**

- **Anyone adjacent to the wall it erupts from: 5d6 (18) slashing** from flying mirror-shards, **DC 15 Dex** halves.
- Three hulks, three burst-outs, potentially three different walls. **Stagger them across rounds 1 and 2** rather than firing all three at once — one erupts, the party turns, the second erupts behind them.

| Feature | Rule |

| **Bright light** | Faerzress bouncing off mirrors. **First bright light in a tenday.** Lead with beauty. |
| **Bone drift, centre** | Difficult terrain, disadvantage on Stealth |
| **Ceiling** | 15–20 ft |
| **Everything is a mirror** | Drives the Mirror Cone rule above. Also: **the party can see themselves from every angle at all times,** which is worth one sentence and no mechanics. |

### Allied support (§110)

**Semdran's rangers plus the druid of Mielikki.** Start of each round, **every foe** takes one longbow attack (**+4, 1d8+2**). Druid: two 1st-level slots at +3 — ***faerie fire* (DC 13)** and ***healing word***.

## B3 — Fortify Gains

### **The alarm is already sounded and they cannot know it**

§110 Developments, verbatim in effect: attempting Fortify Gains **from the western position** means the patrol is investigating why travellers vanish in this tunnel, and **the DM should automatically treat the alarm as having been sounded.** They are not looking for the party. Killing the party will do.

### The roster

| Creature | Source | AC | HP | CR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 cambion** *(Iaveth)* | XMM *Cambion* | 19 | **105** (+5 *aid* = 110) | 5 |
| **2 drow elite warriors** | FRAiF *Drow Elite Warrior of Lolth* | 18 | **71** (+5 *aid* = 76) | 5 |
| *Strong party:* **+2 orogs** | MM *Orog* | 18 | 42 | 2 |

**Alarm loadout (§122):** cambion has used *aid*, *see invisibility*, *longstrider* (+10 ft), **drinks a potion of invisibility just before approaching**, and holds a **scroll of *cloudkill*, used round one**. The drow have *longstrider* and *aid*, and potions of invisibility. Scrolls are consumed and **cannot be looted.**

> **The 2024 drow already carry the poison.** §122's strong-party note about coating weapons in drow poison is redundant — *Drow Elite Warrior of Lolth* has **3d6 poison on the shortsword** and a hand crossbow that inflicts **DC 15 Con or Poisoned *and Unconscious* for 1 hour.** That crossbow is the single nastiest thing in the encounter and they should lead with it.

### The numbers

- **Cambion:** two attacks, any mix of **Claw** (+7, 1d8+4 slashing + 2d6 fire) and **Fire Ray** (+7, **3d6+3 fire, range 120 ft**). Fly 60. Resists cold, fire, lightning, poison. **He fights at range and stays airborne.**
- **Drow:** two attacks, **Shortsword** (+7, 2d6+4 piercing + **3d6 poison**) or **Hand Crossbow** (+7, 1d6+4, **DC 15 Con or Poisoned + Unconscious 1 hour**). Stealth **+10**.
- **Orogs:** two greataxes (+6, 1d12+4). *Aggressive* bonus-action charge. **Crazed; fight to the death.** The cambion uses them as arrow-catchers without embarrassment.

### The opening

**They approach under stealth, invisible, and strike from surprise to cripple one or two PCs immediately.**

- **Round 1:** cambion drops ***cloudkill*** — **20-ft radius, DC 15 Con, 5d8 poison**, heavily obscured, moves 10 ft away each round. **Centre it on the myconid camp**, not on the party. It kills civilians, blocks line of sight, and forces the party to move toward the drow.
- **Simultaneously:** both drow open with **hand crossbows** on the two PCs furthest from cover. A failed save is a PC **unconscious for an hour**, which at this point in the night is the same as removed.
- **Round 2+:** cambion stays at 60–120 ft and throws Fire Rays. He does not close. **He flies away the instant it is clearly lost.**
- Drow break if the cambion falls and they cannot win. Orogs never break.

> **Honest tuning note.** As written this is **not** a hard fight for four 12th-level PCs — it is a hard *opening*, and then a mop-up. **That is correct and deliberate.** Scene 4's job is the interrogation, not the body count. If they prepared the ground during the hour, let the preparations shred the ambush and enjoy it; if they took the short rest in tents, let *cloudkill* and two crossbow bolts teach the lesson.
>
> **If you want it to actually threaten them:** give the cambion a second scroll (*greater invisibility*, or a second *cloudkill*) and add two more drow. Do not add HP — add action economy and crossbow bolts.

### Myconid support (§122)

Three times, **once per round**, the myconids rush one grounded foe with **pacification spores**: **DC 11 Con or Stunned 1 minute** (save ends at end of each turn). **The moment they successfully stun one, they run away.**

> Aim them at the orogs, or at a drow. **They cannot reach the flying cambion**, and somebody at the table should notice the mushroom people trying and failing to reach the thing that is actually killing them.

### The hour before

**Do not tell them how long they have.** One hour from the last encounter. They may take a **short rest**, help build defences, or split it.

Honour anything they build. Suggested handling:
- **Glyph of Warding / Alarm** in the approach passages — fires on the invisible patrol and **cancels their surprise.** Give them that outright.
- **Embankments** — half cover along one approach line.
- **Eldritch cannon on overwatch** — Felkur gets a free attack on the first thing that becomes visible.
- **Cold infusion iced across the floor** — difficult terrain, or DC 12 Dex to avoid prone.
- **Anyone who keeps watch instead of resting** gets a straight **Perception vs the drow's +10 Stealth** to spot the approach.

### Developments — the prisoner

**This is what the scene is for. Do not let both survivors die.**

**DC 15 Intimidation** on a captured cambion or drow yields the module's facts:

- **Matliedun**, a cambion, is master of the **Undying Temple**. Blood of the Dark Prince. Works directly under **Squallocks the Vrock**, advisor to Graz'zt.
- **Every non-warrior drow slave in Maerimydra was taken by demons into Castle Maerimydra about a day ago.** They have not been seen since. *(The drow volunteers this, frightened, unprompted.)*
- If asked whether the city knows they're coming: he says all of Maerimydra is rising against them. **DC 15 Insight: half-truth at best.**

**And the better route — Persuasion, not Intimidation.** Offer him a side and he gives up the hedge itself: the western sector believes Graz'zt has taken his hand off the giants, nothing is being enforced, and he has filed three requisitions into a building that did not answer. ## Stage directions

- **Open on the warm wind.** A cave that breathes out warm air in the Underdark is wrong, and it should be the first thing anyone reacts to, before any bones are described.
- **The shield wall of the dead steps forward one pace and stops.** Once, at the start. Never again. Its power is that it did not take a second step.
- **After Oxania offers her clerics, say nothing for twenty seconds.** No prompt. Let a player break it.
- **The ghost squire's "nobody came looking" is the session's title line.** Deliver it flat, at the end of the fight, and then move immediately to logistics. Do not let anyone respond to it.
- **Describe the mirror cavern as beautiful for a full beat before anything is wrong with it.** They have been underground for a very long time. Let them enjoy the light.
- **Say the reflections rule out loud before initiative.** It is a puzzle, not a trap, and it stops being interesting if it feels unfair.
- **When they open the crates, read the blank countersign line last.** Coins first, gems, the waybill, and *then* the empty line where an auditor should have signed. That is the beat.
- **Let Akritas be silent about the seal until someone rolls on her.** If nobody does, she stays silent all session and it pays off later.
- **The myconids waving.** Play it warmly and without irony. It is the only unguarded moment in the session and Scene 4 is about to burn their camp down.
- **The cambion counts on his fingers while bleeding.** That single gesture is the whole demonic hedge in one image.
- **End on "nobody is on the walls," then stop talking.** Whatever a player says next is the last line of the session. Let it be theirs.
