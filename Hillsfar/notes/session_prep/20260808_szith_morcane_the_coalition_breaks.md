# Szith Morcane Unbound — The Coalition Breaks

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging for the session of **Aug 8, 2026**, picking up the instant after Ch21 ends: Daein and Felkur sprinting out of the Cathedral of Faces, Bramgrim and Akritas walking back from Hamad with the Hidden in their pocket.

Source: **DDEX3-15 *Szith Morcane Unbound*, Parts 3–4.** Horizon: **DDEX3-16 *Assault on Maerimydra*.**
Party: 11th level — Akritas, Bramgrim, Daein, Felkur, the Owlbear.
Register: **political thriller that becomes a siege.** The coalition nearly eats itself, and then the outpost falls.

> **Time is relative in this campaign — no FR date stamps.** The clocks that matter are the ones already in play: the three-day patrol window from Ch19, the "eight days before you arrived" on Solom's murder, the ten-day ritual. Use those.

## The Win Condition

**Szith Morcane changes hands tonight, and the party gets the chance to decide who holds it.** Dengor dies, the azer collapse into scrap, the fire giants run for Maerimydra. But the session only *lands* if the coalition nearly comes apart first and the party is visibly the thing holding it together.

The second thing that must land: **Solom Ned'razak is alive, and the party finds out early** — and learns, in the same conversation, that the blood-purge ritual died while he was on that throne saying nothing.

---

## The Default Trajectory (if the party does nothing)

- **The three-day patrol clock expires.** The garrison notices the outpost patrol never returned. Gate guards double, Dengor stops sleeping in his cave.
- **Rilira rises anyway, unarmed, and is slaughtered.** ~50 drow and two hundred bodies with farming tools. She asked how they'd be armed and nobody answered.
- **Velasta hunts Quil'bryn.** She only *stops* because the party gives her Solom. If they never go up the tower, or never tell her, she takes him on her own schedule — possibly mid-assault.
- **Hamad takes the outpost.** Only faction with no internal fracture, a working route in, and a stated goal of drow rulership.
- **Dengor finishes the palace, the arsenal ships, and the giants turn toward Hillsfar** — the exact thing Charles briefed and Alder called their purpose.
- **If the party wins but doesn't claim the succession, Solom resumes the throne.** He is the legitimate deposed ruler and every other claim collapses on legitimacy. He will not ask permission.

---

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Take Grek's news.** The stone giants are cured — a fourth ally the party thinks they lost, and the only thing in the coalition that trades blows with fire giants.
2. **Go up the Inverted Tower early.** Murssvayas grants access for the asking. It yields Solom, Messanth, and the single most valuable piece of information in the session.
3. **Spend Solom on Velasta.** Her vengeance target is alive. That buys Quil'bryn's life and her twenty warriors in one move.
4. **Free Messanth** — he kills the behir that chased them out of the bazaar in Ch19 and wrecks the iron gate doing it.
5. **Get into the Forge of Surtr and free the salamanders.** Answers Rilira's weapons question *and* sends two salamanders hunting Dengor.
6. **Take Hamad's stream entrance.** Unguarded, and he's already pledged.
7. **Fight Dengor in the Forge, not the palace** — ten other fire giants work the palace site.
8. **Free the three enslaved stone giants** at the works (thieves' tools DC 17, or DC 17 Athletics at end of round once fighting starts).
9. **Kill Dengor and show the body.** The giants flee; the azer binding rituals shatter.

# Scene 1 — The Beer Bottle Worked

### Setup
Minutes after Ch21's last frame. Daein and Felkur are still moving fast, expecting pursuit. Bramgrim and Akritas inbound from the gnome ruins with the Hidden's pledge and a looted cache.

Nothing is chasing them.

### Approach
Let the party sit in the assumption that they ruined it. Let Felkur be defensive. Then **Grek** comes up out of the floor — not the weeping wreck from Ch17, moving fast.

> "You broke it." Grek's half-carved face works. "You *broke* it. Do you understand what you have done?"
>
> Beat.
>
> "None of us could. Not one. Seventy years of us standing in that gallery watching him starve in front of a perfect thing, and not one of us could raise a hand to the face of Skoraeus. It is doctrine. It is the *first* doctrine." He is trembling, which for a rock is unsettling. "And a gnome threw a beer at it."

> **GM-only.** Module canon, Part 3: the purple crystal *is* the source of the madness. "Immediately after the statue is destroyed the stone giants stop attacking and come out of their immediate madness, reeling in horror as they see the damage they have wrought." Decha is **thankful.** Felkur did the right thing by accident at the exact moment the party was certain they'd blown it.
>
> Play it horror-first, gratitude-second. That's honestly how it feels to Grek.

### The ask
Decha is lucid, humiliated, and looking at seventy years of defaced carvings he made himself. He'll send giants, but he wants:

1. **The three enslaved stone giants** at Denagor's palace works, back.
2. Someone to tell him what to do about the wall of ruined faces. He genuinely doesn't know. *(A gift to whichever player wants it. Bramgrim is the obvious taker; Daein giving a genuinely kind answer would be better.)*

### Branch — they go to the Lodge in person
Best case. Decha among his own wreckage, eighteen feet of it. Quiet and slow. He can't quite thank Felkur directly, so he puts a hand flat on the ground in front of him — which Grek later explains is how a stone giant apologises.

### Branch — they send Grek back with a message
Fine. They get the giants but never see the gallery. **Cost:** later, when the coalition fractures, the stone giants have no personal stake in the party and won't intervene on their behalf. Let that land silently.

### Branch — they double down on "leave before they recover"
Comedy. Grek has to physically chase them. Let him be undignified.

### NPC notes
- **Grek** — wants readmission to the Lodge, too proud to ask. Tic: states catastrophes as flat fact, then reacts a sentence late.
- **Decha** — wants his people back, doesn't know how to hold authority now the obsession is gone. Tic: long pauses mid-sentence; still carving in his head.

# Scene 2 — The Inverted Tower

**This is the session's hinge and it comes early. Do not let it slide to the back half.**

### Setup
Solom's old school: a short tunnel from the chasm into an octagonal shaft of glossy black rock, 20 feet across, **60 feet high**, four archways at 15-foot intervals up the walls. A subtle wind moves inside it.

Murssvayas grants access if asked — he leads the resistance now and the party has earned it. He hands over **Solom's arcane mark**: a signet ring, black jewel lit from within. Password: **"Kur'ral."** He asks their word they take only what they came for.

He believes he's sending them after a **bronze dragon.**

### The climb
Only the **fourth-level archway** is safe. Any other triggers all three wards (**DC 15**):
- **Confusion** adjacent to every doorway and archway.
- **Gust of Wind** down from the fourth level — Medium or smaller can't levitate or fly, and get pushed toward the chasm.
- **Fog Cloud** filling everything beyond the archways.

Sixty feet, by flight, levitation or climbing. Daein's mandolin has *Fly* once a day. Akritas climbs.

### The reveal
> A large throne dominates this audience chamber. Atop it is a weak-looking drow. Two doors exit to the right and left. Tapestries hang on each side of the throne.

It's **Solom Ned'razak.** Anyone who's met him knows him instantly.

> **GM-only.** Module, *Bronze Shadows*: *"This appears to be untrue. Instead, Solom's powerful magic has recreated him upon this throne. Solom's new form is weak from the ordeal and he is not yet back to his full power."* Not a ghost, not an illusion, not a trap. The man, remade and diminished.
>
> **Play the silence.** Don't have him explain immediately. Let him look at them. Let a player say his name first, and stay quiet longer than is comfortable.

### Full disclosure — what he tells them
He explains it, plainly, without apology. He does not acknowledge that anyone grieved.

- **The recreation worked and it cost him nearly everything.** He is a fraction of what he was and recovering slowly. He'll say how long. He'll say what he can and can't do now.
- **Quil'bryn did kill him.** The knife went in, the man died. What's on the throne is the contingency he built decades ago finally spending itself. He is not grateful to be here.
- **The blood-purge ritual died with him.** It was keyed to him; when he ended, it collapsed mid-cycle. He felt it break from the throne and could do nothing.
- **Brandyr is gone.** The assistants minding the shrunken giant scattered during the resistance's collapse.

> **GM-only — the region stays tainted.** The purge is permanently off the table. Graz'zt's madness in the Underdark is now a fact of the setting going into DDEX3-16, not a problem with a solution. This is the real price of Solom's four months of silence, and he should state it like a line item.
>
> **Sub-fork, yours:** did Brandyr **die** in the collapse, or **escape**? If he escaped, there is a fiendborne fire giant loose in the Underdark who was shrunk, bottled and drained by these people. That's a free antagonist whenever you want one. If he simply died, the loss stays clean and sad.

### What this detonates
- **Quil'bryn broke himself in public over a murder that *did* take** — and the man got back up anyway. That's stranger than absolution and worse for him. He isn't forgiven; he's been made irrelevant.
- **Murssvayas has been leading a resistance on a dead man's authority** — and the dead man was upstairs, letting him.
- **Bramgrim's copy of Solom's Pendant** is live again, not a relic.
- **Velasta's target is alive.** This is the card. See Scene 3.

### Messanth
If they're honest about what they want, he lets them take the dragon. He asks the ring back and tells them to look into the gem.

> Moments later, you find yourselves in a similar room, but several times larger. Everything is dark and fuzzy, as if seen through frosted glass. Curled in a corner lies a bronze dragon. Its eyes snap open. The edges of its wings and tail give off dark smoke, whips of shadow. It stands, and roars — and you see that its magnificent bronze scales have gone translucent and dark, like obsidian.

**Messanth** — young **bronze shadow dragon**, years in a Shadowfell pocket, changed by it. Not mad. Lashing out.

**Rage tracking (run it openly — it's good theatre):**
- Six tokens on the table.
- **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion)** removes one. Max two per round.
- **Every 20 damage Messanth takes ADDS a token.**
- Remind him who he was. Zero tokens and he's free.

> A talking-down encounter in a dragon costume. Opening with damage actively punishes them — that's the lesson. Daein leads; Bramgrim's sincerity is the dark horse.

**Payoff:** a freed Messanth **hunts and kills the behir** — the one that woke and chased them out of the old bazaar in Ch19. Their one botched infiltration becomes an open road, and the dragon and behir wreck the iron gate fighting. Let them realise it themselves.

### Rewards
*Potion of greater healing.* And the **spider-hide travelling spellbook** — *web, levitate, darkness, gaseous form, Evard's black tentacles, arcane eye, passwall.*

> Solom looks at the gnome with the cannon and the tools and files him as "wizard, minor." **Felkur gets the book and should be visibly insulted by the categorisation.** Let him take it anyway. *Gaseous form* and *passwall* are both directly load-bearing in the next two scenes — you have just armed your infiltrator, in exchange for a small dent in his professional pride.

### Branch — they attack Solom
He activates the wards and summons demons. Weak, not stupid. Survivable but expensive, and it costs them Messanth *and* the leverage over Velasta — which costs Quil'bryn his life in Scene 3.

### Branch — they bring Quil'bryn up the tower
Don't talk them out of it. Best scene available tonight.

### Branch — they tell nobody Solom is alive
Very much this party. Note who's in the room when they decide. **Akritas has form for sitting on information** — if she argues for silence, that's the arc rhyming. But withholding it means Velasta's price stands, and Quil'bryn's survival is no longer guaranteed.

# Scene 3 — The Coalition Council

### Setup
The resistance camp or a neutral cavern near the chasm. **The political heart of the session — give it real time.** In the room: Murssvayas, Quil'bryn and his twenty, Rilira, Grundar Ashmantle's duergar, Hamad, Rylzyn Veth, Sorn, Grek and whatever stone giants came.

And, arriving uninvited: **Asala**, scout of the Nerevenans, saying her mistress will speak with them.

### The room's actual problem
Every faction wants Szith Morcane **for themselves**:

- **Murssvayas** — for the resistance. It's their home and Solom's legacy.
- **Rilira** — she headed a noble house here and hid her identity through the slaughter. She wants **her house back**, and she has two hundred bodies.
- **Hamad** — told Bramgrim and Akritas plainly that the Hidden want drow rulership in Szith Morcane *and* Maerimydra. He wasn't lying. He also asked whether they'd committed to any other drow faction — **and they had already committed to Rilira.**
- **Velasta** — a temple to Kiaransalee, finishing a plan her family started a century ago.

> **GM-only — Hamad's question is the live grenade tonight.** He asked about prior commitments and was told, effectively, no. Rilira is sitting right there and is not a woman who waits. If she speaks first, Hamad learns he was managed. He doesn't shout. He goes very quiet and starts recalculating.
>
> **The shield doctrine does NOT come out tonight** (decision 6). Keep it loaded: let a drow veteran start a sentence Grundar could misread and have Quil'bryn cut him off. Near-misses only. It goes off on the Maerimydra march.

### Velasta — and the card
Asala leads them to the old commoners' crypts. Bones underfoot: drow, duergar, quaggoth, hellhound, crushed azer shells. Velasta comes with four drow — pallid, dead-skinned, eyes bright, Kiaransalee's symbol worn openly.

Her opening question, module-explicit: *do you know where Solom Ned'razak is?* She feigns wanting an alliance with him. **DC 20 Wisdom (Insight)** catches the lie — but the party already knows the answer she actually wants.

> "You have the look of people who have been busy. Good. Then answer me one thing and we can be friends." A pause that goes on slightly too long. "Where is the archmage?"

**They know. They've been up the tower. Play the moment they realise what they're holding.**

> **GM-only — how this saves Quil'bryn (decision 1).** In the module, if Solom is dead Velasta is robbed of vengeance and marks his killer instead: *"She demands to know who killed her betrayer and then works to destroy them. If Quil'bryn is still alive, Velasta then plans to kill him."*
>
> Alive changes everything. Her hundred-year vengeance is **back on the table** and Quil'bryn stops mattering to her the instant she hears it. She will want the tower, the ring, the password, and she will want them now.
>
> Let the party understand the trade before you spell it out. They are buying Quil'bryn's life with the location of a man who is nominally their patron.

**What she offers when persuaded:** twenty warriors and acolytes, plus "the blessed of Kiaransalee" — she raises the fire giants' dead to fight them. If pressed she confirms it.

### Branch — they give up Solom's location
Quil'bryn lives, they gain her twenty, and they have set a revenant priestess on their own archmage. Solom is weak and knows it. **This is the intended line and it should feel like a betrayal that was worth it.**

### Branch — they withhold it and protect Quil'bryn anyway
Harder. They need to give her something else — the outpost itself, a temple site, first claim on the crypts. Works, but she keeps hunting Quil'bryn in the background and the party has now lied to a creature that cannot be killed permanently.

### Branch — they never approach the Nerevenans
Legitimate; the module says one faction beyond the drow is enough. **Cost:** Velasta acts independently and hunts Quil'bryn on her own clock — possibly mid-assault. Not a punishment, a third party.

### Failure costs (not damage)
- Rilira learns about the shield doctrine → she pulls two hundred slaves and the assault has no numbers.
- Hamad learns he was managed → the Hidden don't withdraw, they stop *sharing.* The stream entrance quietly leaves the table.
- Velasta is insulted (her state of being, her goddess) → she attacks. **Velasta Revenant Priestess**, three drow veterans, one drow acolyte, four skeletons rising from the bones underfoot. **She rises again if destroyed** unless a priest lays body *and* soul to rest. Bramgrim can. That's a whole scene.

### NPC notes
- **Velasta T'sarran** — wants Solom dead and Szith Morcane as Kiaransalee's temple. Tic: unhurried to the point of rudeness; leaves pauses a living person would fill.
- **Rilira** — wants her house and city back, won't say so in front of Hamad until it profits her. Tic: contempt delivered as courtesy.
- **Hamad** — wants drow rulership; values honesty about *terms* specifically. Tic: materialises out of shadow rather than walking in.
- **Quil'bryn** — post-tower, a man whose defining crime got up and walked away. Tic: proposes the coldest available option in a reasonable tone. Wants Felkur to keep looking at him the way he did in Ch17.
- **Murssvayas** — wants someone else to be in charge. Not built for this and knows it.

# Scene 4 — The Forge of Surtr

### Setup
Formerly the **Fane of Lolth.** Entered from the **House Morcane ruins** or through Denagor's palace. This is the answer to Rilira's question — it's where the weapons *are.*

Murssvayas offers **gaseous form** from himself and his wizards to get them in through the collapsed House Morcane tunnels. *(Felkur now has it in the spellbook too, if you're letting him cast from it.)*

### Inside
Four **azer acolytes**, two **azer priests**, one **helmed horror**. Two **salamanders** confined by a magic circle. They're finishing a magical warhammer for the head priest.

**The lever:** each azer wears a pendant marked like the circle — *planar binding*, *protection from evil and good*, and *sanctuary* against the salamanders. Take a pendant, make a **DC 20 Intelligence (Arcana or Religion)** check, and you can free them.

- **Freed and uncontrolled:** they rampage into the main cavern hunting Dengor for binding them.
- **Controlled by pendant:** obey for **one hour**, then turn on the party, then go kill Dengor anyway.
- **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion):** convince them the party wants Dengor dead too. Cleanest.
- Pendants fade an hour outside the Forge (**DC 15 Arcana/Religion** to notice).

**Treasure:** the warhammer works as a *flametongue* for two hours before the incomplete enchantment fails. Plus 1,000gp of ceremonial items, rare metals and finely-made warhammers — **which is a slave army's worth of weapons.**

> Let a player say it out loud. Rilira asked how they'd arm her people and the answer has been sitting in a forge the whole time. If nobody gets there, have **Grundar Ashmantle** say it — he's a duergar, he knows what a forge is worth.

### Branch — they fight the azer straight
Loud. The Forge connects to Dengor's palace and, by a freshly dug tunnel, to Dengor's Cave. Noise brings the warden patrol off the palace doors.

### Branch — they free the salamanders and leave
Arguably the smartest play. The salamanders do the work — but the party doesn't control the timing and loses the kill. If they choose it, make sure they *hear* it happen across the cavern while they're elsewhere.

# Scene 5 — Denagor

### Setup
Roll **1d20** for where he is when they hit the main cavern:
- **1–5:** asleep in **Dengor's Cave** (3J) — two helmed horror behemoths, one helmed horror. 4,000gp, *ring of protection*, two *potions of firebreath* in an unlocked onyx chest.
- **6–15:** at the **palace works** (3F) — two fire giants, a hell hound, three enslaved stone giants in shackles nearby.
- **16–20:** at the **Forge of Surtr** — praying at the altar. On seeing intruders he roars and **slams his greatsword into the magic circle, freeing the salamanders himself.**

> The Forge result is the best and you're allowed to want it. If the party has primed the salamanders, Dengor freeing them personally is a magnificent own-goal.

**Denagor Bloodheart** — fire giant **forgepriest of Surtr**, megalomaniac, given visions of himself commanding an army from a magnificent palace. That palace is half-built around him and raised by slaves.

### The palace-works variant
The **three enslaved stone giants are chained twenty feet away and straining.** DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) sees it. Free with thieves' tools (**DC 17 Dex**) or they burst out on **DC 17 Athletics** at the end of any round.

> This is the payoff for Scene 1. The party promised Decha his giants back. Delivering that *inside* the boss fight — three stone giants tearing their chains apart and joining in — is the best-shaped moment available tonight. Steer toward it if the dice allow.

### When he dies
Play it as a cascade:
- The **ten other fire giants flee for Maerimydra.** They never wanted this posting. Show the body and they don't even fight.
- **The rituals binding the azer shatter** — roughly a hundred azer across patrols, barracks, forge and palace **fall apart into inanimate metal** where they stand.
- The hammering stops. That's the sound cue: the palace works have been the cavern's heartbeat since the party arrived.

# Scene 6 — Who Holds Szith Morcane

### Setup
The giants are gone. Every faction is standing in the same cavern, armed, and each came here to own the place.

Module, Conclusion: *"The coalition that allowed for defeat of the common enemy seems on the verge of collapse... Velasta wants control of Szith Morcane. Hamadh wants control of Szith Morcane as well as Murssvayas, and Rilira."*

**Give the party the chance to step in and settle it. This is the win condition — not Dengor's corpse.**

### The claims, by how loudly they'll be pressed
1. **Rilira** — noble house, two hundred bodies, here first. Loudest.
2. **Hamad** — did the quiet work, never lied to anyone. Coldest.
3. **Velasta** — century-old family claim and a goddess. Least negotiable. *(And if they gave her Solom's location, she may not even be here — she'll be climbing a tower.)*
4. **Murssvayas** — best right, least appetite. Defers to the party if asked directly.

### The default
> **If the party doesn't step in, Solom takes it** (decision 4). He is the legitimate deposed ruler; every other claim collapses on legitimacy the moment he walks into the cavern. He will not ask, and he will not thank them.
>
> That's a genuinely bleak ending to a won battle: they liberated a city for a man who calls them replaceable, whose silence killed the purge, and whom they may have just sold to a revenant. **Let them feel the room turn if they stay passive.** Don't warn them — the default is the point.

### The wildcard
If the party kept Solom secret, they're in a room full of people arguing over an outpost whose actual archmage is sixty feet up a tower listening. If they reveal it here, every claim collapses at once.

> Whoever holds Szith Morcane tonight is who the party marches with into **DDEX3-16 *Assault on Maerimydra***, which opens on **Gathering Forces.**

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Is Solom actually alive, or is this a trick?"** → Actually alive. Recreated by his own magic, weak, not at full power. Not a lich, not a simulacrum, not Graz'zt. He'll tell them the mechanism and the cost if asked — he has no reason to hide it.
- **"So Quil'bryn didn't kill him?"** → He did. The knife went in and the man died. What's on the throne is a contingency spending itself. Quil'bryn is not absolved.
- **"Can we still do the purge ritual?"** → No. It was keyed to Solom and collapsed when he died; Brandyr is gone. That thread is closed.
- **"Did we blow it with the stone giants?"** → No. The statue was the madness. *Not answered:* whether Decha forgives Felkur specifically.
- **"Can we skip the Nerevenans?"** → Yes, and one faction beyond the drow is enough. But Velasta hunts Quil'bryn on her own schedule unless they hand her Solom.
- **"How do we arm two hundred slaves?"** → The Forge of Surtr. Say it through an NPC if they don't find it.
- **"Can we take Denagor without fighting ten fire giants?"** → Yes — the Forge, or asleep in his cave. That's the intended clever line.
- **"Does killing Denagor end the threat to Hillsfar?"** → No. It takes the outpost. Maerimydra and the siege weapons are DDEX3-16.

## Stage directions

- **Scene 1:** let them finish being sure they failed before Grek arrives. Don't rescue them early — the reversal needs the despair.
- **Grek's speech:** the pause after "None of us could" is the whole beat. Sit in it.
- **The throne room:** say nothing after the read-aloud. Let a player name him. If the table goes quiet, stay quiet longer than is comfortable.
- **"The ritual ended when I did."** Deliver it as a line item, not a confession. The flatness is what makes it land.
- **Quil'bryn learning Solom lives:** don't narrate his reaction. Ask his scene partner what they see in his face — Felkur, ideally. The oath was to him.
- **Messanth's roar:** describe the scales before the roar. The wrongness lands harder than the volume.
- **Velasta's question about the archmage:** after she asks, go to the player least likely to lie and just look at them.
- **When Denagor drops:** hammering stops first, then a hundred bodies hit the floor as scrap. One sound cue, then silence. Don't rush it into loot.
- **Scene 6:** let the factions talk over each other for a beat before anyone looks at the party. They should have to *choose* to step in — and if they don't, let Solom walk in.

## Still yours to call at the table

These aren't pre-decidable — they depend on what the players do.

1. **Does the party give Velasta Solom's location?** The whole Quil'bryn resolution runs through it. If they refuse, improvise her price.
2. **Did Brandyr die or escape?** Escaped = a free antagonist with a personal grudge. Died = a cleaner, sadder loss.
3. **Do they tell Quil'bryn?** And do they bring him up the tower?
4. **Does Felkur cast from the spellbook,** or is it a trophy? *Passwall* in a stone outpost is a lot of rope.
5. **Who they hand the outpost to** — with Solom as the standing default if they stay out of it.
