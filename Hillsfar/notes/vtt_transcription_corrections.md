## Notes for future passes

Landmine list — wrong-forms that are risky under the applier's
case-insensitive `\bword\b` matching. Grep the transcript for lowercase
occurrences before applying, and prefer a targeted edit over a glossary row
for anything that is also a common English word.

Seeded from the Chapter 20 pass (2026-08-22). All were clean in that
transcript (zero lowercase hits) but each is a real word or real name that a
future session could use innocently:

- **`Azure → Azer`** — "azure" is an ordinary colour word.
- **`Acer → Azer`** — also a laptop brand; low count, easy to lose track of.
- **`Kraken → Korkan`** — a real D&D monster. If a kraken ever appears in
  this campaign, this row will silently rename it. Revisit then.
- **`Solon / Solan → Solom`** — Solon is a historical Athenian. This table
  demonstrably wanders into ancient-Greek tangents (Chapter 20 had a long
  Homer/Odyssey digression), so this one is likelier than it looks.
- **`Diane → Daein`**, **`Albert → Owlbear`**, **`Soren → Sorn`**,
  **`Roslyn → Rylzyn`** — all ordinary personal names. Safe only while no
  real person by those names is spoken about at the table.

Deliberately NOT rows (applied as targeted edits instead, per the
case-insensitivity gate):

- `Poker → Felkur` — "poker" is a common noun; fixed by hand in
  `summaries/20260726/…cleaned.vtt` only.

Canonical-spelling rulings that overrode a proposal this run:

- **`Daein`**, not `Daien` — the narrative spelling wins over the
  `docs/daien.md` heading, which carries a transposition typo. Same for
  `Akritas` (not `Akrita`). `Felkur Oldenwood` (not `Felkur Olwedond`) still
  holds for transcripts, but for a different reason as of 2026-08-22: Olwedond
  is not a typo, it is his real name. Spoken table dialogue means Oldenwood —
  the name everyone knows him by — so the correction stands; do NOT extend it
  to documents quoting an official record, where Olwedond is correct.
- **`Korkan`**, not `Corkan` — both independent transcriptions agree on the
  *sound*, which says nothing about spelling; `notes/` and the Chapter 20
  summary settle it.

### Added by the Chapter 24 pass (2026-08-23)

95 new wrong-forms across 44 canonicals, all confirmed by the GM in a batch
review artifact. Session: *The Council at Sporedome* (DDEX3-16 Part 1).

**Tooling trap — `add_to_glossary.py` does not know this file's section names.**
Its `--section` keys map to *its own* headings (`factions` → `Houses / factions`,
`table` → `Real-world / table`, `items` → `Items / artifacts`), which are **not**
the headings used here (`Factions and titles`, `Table and out-of-character`,
`Items and magic`). Passing those keys appended three brand-new sections at the
end of the file, after the prose notes, instead of merging into the existing
rows. They were merged back by hand this run. **After any bulk append, run
`grep -n '^## '` and check no new section appeared** — `lint_glossary.py` catches
it only as a `split_section` WARN, and only when the canonical already had a row.

**Canon ruling — the Olwedond passage (cues 985–1000) is deliberately NOT corrected.**
GM ruling, 2026-08-23: **Felkur never reveals his real name. He has told no one.**
There was genuine confusion at the table in this passage, and the garbled text is a
faithful record of it. The ASR renders *Olwedond* three ways here — `Old and done`
(×3, cue 987), `Mr. Owen died` (cue 989), `Cool and Don` (cue 995) — and cue 988
("you're giving your real name out, not your fake name") reads like a confirmation.
**It is not.** Rewriting any of those to `Olwedond` would encode a name-reveal into
the record that did not happen in fiction.

`Olden Wood → Oldenwood` (cues 985, 1000) is correct and stays — that is the name he
is *known by*, which is what cue 1000 says outright. Never extend it, or any Olwedond
form, to a reveal. All three wrong-forms are ordinary English and must never become
glossary rows in any case.

**Speaker content-slip corrected — `Hamadh` → `Hledh` in six cues.** The GM said
"Hamadh" repeatedly while meaning **Hledh Hellspawn**, and caught it on record at
cue 443: *"Sorry, not Hamadh, I keep saying the wrong name."* This is a *speaker*
error, not an ASR error, so the `Hamad → Hamadh` row is correct and stays. On the
GM's ruling the six referent-wrong cues (401, 402, 425×2, 426, 432) were rewritten
to `Hledh`; cue 443's two were **left alone** so the self-correction still parses,
and the four genuine Hamadh mentions (168, 331, 332, 382 — the Vhaeraunite drow
ally) are untouched. **Watch for this pattern**: a name that is correct as
transcription can still be the wrong character, and no lint or fuzzy-match will
ever catch it. Only reading the surrounding sense does.
