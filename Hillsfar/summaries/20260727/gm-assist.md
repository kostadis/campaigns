# Chapter 21

Date: 2026-08-08

## Summary
The party pressed deeper into the slave pens of Szith Morcane, where laborers of countless races toiled beneath the iron will of their overseers. Their drow ally Soren had been sent ahead to clear the path, and when he rejoined the group, a terse exchange with Rilzin in the tongue of the Underdark made it chillingly clear that not all the overseers had been killed — the survivors, Soren noted with casual coldness, were simply a different count. A duergar named Grundar then approached, his eyes fixed on Rilzin, whom he recognized immediately as the man who had once bought his freedom by killing several of Grundar's cousins. Beneath the old blood feud, however, Felkur sensed something more urgent — a desperate hunger for freedom that outweighed even the desire for revenge. The party offered Grundar a personal guarantee of escape in exchange for his cooperation, and with a slow nod he agreed, at which point a startling number of armed duergar who had been lurking unseen in the shadows quietly lowered their weapons.

Grundar led them toward the imprisoned drow noblewoman Rillira, but the path was blocked by a fire giant and a trio of azer sentinels. Before the party could devise a plan, a hobgoblin named Corkin stepped forward with his own followers, declaring himself the true leader of any uprising and warning that if the party threw their support behind Rillira, none of them would leave the pens alive. Felkur resolved the standoff in the most direct way he could imagine — he vanished in an instant, reappearing inside Rillira's guarded cell, leaving Corkin blinking at the empty space where the artificer had been standing. Rillira was haughty and imperious, every inch the drow noblewoman she claimed to be, and she made her terms clear: bring her Corkin's head, kill the fire giant, and the full strength of the drow slaves would be theirs to command. She punctuated her demands with a cold warning that there was no place in the Underdark or the surface world where the party would be safe if they betrayed her — Felkur, who had faced down creatures that made Rillira look like a minor inconvenience, was entirely unmoved.

Slipping back out to rejoin his companions, Felkur relayed the terms, and the group quickly settled on a plan that would solve the problem of Corkin and the fire giant at the same time. Daein approached Corkin with a convincing air of camaraderie, persuading the hobgoblin that the party would charge the fire giant alongside him. Corkin, eager and arrogant, rallied his followers and led the charge — only to find himself utterly alone as the party held back and watched from the shadows. The fire giant and his azer guards tore through the hobgoblins with brutal efficiency, and as Corkin reeled from the onslaught, the party and their allies struck. Akritas loosed a devastating volley of arrows from her oathbow, Daein closed in with his blade, the owlbear crashed into the giant with bone-shaking force, and Bramgrim called forth a swirling host of divine spirits that lashed out at both the fire giant and the treacherous Corkin — consuming the hobgoblin entirely. Enraged by the betrayal, Corkin had turned his blade on Bramgrim in a final, furious act of spite before the divine energies surrounding the cleric destroyed him, and the party would later remark with dark amusement that he had died of entirely natural causes. The fire giant, surrounded and bleeding, focused every ounce of his remaining fury on Bramgrim, driving his flaming sword into the dwarf twice in a savage, burning assault — but it was not enough, and the giant collapsed as the slave pens fell silent. From across the way, Rillira watched the bodies of her oppressors hit the ground, and for the first time, she smiled — a cold, satisfied expression that confirmed the alliance was sealed.

With Rillira's support secured, the party turned their attention to the broader campaign to liberate Szith Morcane. They needed allies, and they needed weapons — the forge in the ruins of House Morcane was the source of the fire giants' armaments, and the palace of the fire giant king Denagor loomed as the final obstacle. The party debated which factions to approach: the Hidden, a secretive group of Vhaeraunite rebels; the stone giants of the Lodge of Faces; and the Nerevans, worshippers of a goddess of death whose altar the party had previously destroyed. Deciding that Bramgrim and Akritas were poorly suited to negotiate with death cultists, the group resolved to split up — Bramgrim and Akritas would seek out the Hidden, while the others would approach the Lodge of Faces.

Bramgrim and Akritas made themselves conspicuous on the webs of Szith Morcane, waiting until a drow scout approached and directed them to a meeting at a set of gnome ruins. They arrived to find Hamad, the leader of the Hidden, who questioned their motives carefully and was satisfied to learn they had made no binding commitments to any other drow faction. Hamad declared that words were not enough — to earn the Hidden's support, the party would have to prove their worth through cunning rather than brute force. He led them to a giant-held weapon cache protected by a complex magical lock and guarded by roving patrols, and told them simply: bring back what is inside. Moving through the tunnels in near-perfect silence, Akritas worked the lock with practiced precision, successfully opening the gate — though not quietly enough to avoid triggering a silent alarm that sent guards rushing toward their position.

Rather than flee, Bramgrim drew his hammer and Akritas nocked an arrow, and the two stood their ground as a hellhound and two azer sentinels came charging down the tunnel. Akritas struck first, loosing a volley of magically infused arrows that erupted on impact, tearing through the hellhound and wounding the azers in a single devastating burst. The hellhound breathed a cone of searing fire across both of them, and the azers pressed in with their hammers, but Bramgrim's divine spirits continued to grind the attackers down with relentless spiritual force. Akritas finished the hellhound and one azer with precise follow-up shots, and the final azer, badly wounded and desperate, attempted to flee — only to be struck down by Bramgrim's divine energy before it could escape. The two secured the treasure and slipped out before further reinforcements could arrive, and when they returned to Hamad, he was visibly impressed, pledging the full support of the Hidden on the condition that the party dealt with the fire giant king Denagor.

Meanwhile, the rest of the party made their way to the Cathedral of Faces, a vast cavern where every surface — walls, pillars, and ceiling — was carved with hundreds of giant visages, each one deliberately and beautifully flawed: a missing ear here, a split lip there, an unfinished cheek left rough and raw. At the center sat the stone giant elder Datya, gray and still as the rock he had spent his life shaping, staring unblinking into a crystal the size of a war shield — a perfectly symmetrical, flawless carving of the stone giant god Skoraeus Stonebones. The surrounding stone giants stood at the gallery's edge in silent, motionless grief, unable to look away and unable to act. The Galeb Duhr Grek explained the terrible truth: to carve a perfect face was to carve a tomb, for the carver would see nothing left to improve and stop forever — and the stone giants, bound by doctrine, could not bring themselves to deface a sacred image of their own god. Felkur, surveying the situation with characteristic practicality, reached into his pack, produced a pocket bottle of beer, and hurled it at the flawless crystal statue. The bottle struck the statue in precisely the right place, and with a tremendous crack, the sacred artifact shattered into a thousand pieces. The stone giant king collapsed in shock as his all-consuming obsession exploded before him, the surrounding giants froze in horrified silence, and Felkur — surveying the wreckage with mild surprise — remarked simply that he thought he had broken him. Realizing they had almost certainly destroyed any chance of an alliance with the stone giants, the party wasted no time and fled the cavern before the giants could recover from their shock.

## NPCs
### Soren
A drow ally who scouts ahead and ruthlessly deals with duergar guards blocking the party's path, noting with chilling casualness that survivors were simply 'a different count.'

### Rilzin
A drow associate of the party who was once a prisoner in the slave pens and has a deep blood feud with the local duergar, having bought his freedom by killing several of Grundar's cousins.

### Grundar
A duergar prisoner with a blood feud against Rilzin who, beneath his grudge, harbors a desperate hunger for freedom. He agrees to cooperate with the party in exchange for a guarantee of escape, revealing a large group of armed duergar who had been lurking in the shadows.

### Rillira
A haughty and imperious drow noblewoman imprisoned in the slave pens. She demands the deaths of Corkin and the fire giant overseer before pledging her followers' support, and warns the party there is nowhere safe if they betray her.

### Corkin
An arrogant hobgoblin who attempts to seize leadership of the slave uprising. He is manipulated by Daein into leading a suicidal charge against the fire giant and is ultimately consumed by Bramgrim's divine spirits.

### Quilbrin
A drow leader who provided the party with information on how to contact the Hidden, and who has a plan for arming the freed slaves. Rillira sneers at the mention of him.

### Denagor
The fire giant king currently occupying the palace in Szith Morcane, identified as the primary obstacle the party must overcome to liberate the city.

### Hamad
The leader of the Hidden, a group of Vhaeraunite rebels. He is cautious and values stealth and cunning over brute force, requiring the party to prove their worth by stealing from a giant-held weapon cache before pledging his support.

### Grek
A Galeb Duhr with a partially carved face who acts as a guide and translator for the party among the stone giants. He explains the doctrine of imperfection central to the Lodge of Faces and was exiled by the stone giant elder Datya for being imperfect.

### Datya
The stone giant elder and king of the Lodge of Faces who became catatonic after carving a perfectly symmetrical, flawless crystal statue of his god, unable to look away from it.

## Locations
### Szith Morcane Slave Pens
A wretched sprawl of shacks south of the bridge where laborers of various races toil in miserable conditions under fire giant overseers.

### House Morcane
The ruins of a drow house within Szith Morcane where a forge has been established by the fire giants to manufacture weapons.

### Denagor's Palace
The seat of power for the fire giant king Denagor, located in a fortified area of Szith Morcane that the party must eventually assault.

### The Webs
The vast, spidery network of Szith Morcane where Bramgrim and Akritas waited to be contacted by the Vhaeraunite rebels known as the Hidden.

### Gnome Ruins
A small set of ruins within Szith Morcane where Hamad, leader of the Hidden, conducts secret meetings with potential allies.

### Giant-held Weapon Cache
A secure storage area within the tunnels of Szith Morcane, protected by a magical Faerzress-worded lock and guarded by patrols of azers and a hellhound.

### The Cathedral of Faces
A vast, dark gallery within the Lodge of Faces where every wall, pillar, and surface is carved with hundreds of intentionally flawed giant visages. The stone giant elder Datya was found here, entranced by a perfect crystal statue.

A powerful magical bow wielded by Akritas, used to loose devastating volleys of arrows against the fire giant overseer and later against the patrol guarding the giant weapon cache.

The massive, heat-radiating weapon wielded by the fire giant overseer, used to savagely wound Bramgrim twice in the giant's final moments of fury.

A set of specialized tools used by Akritas to bypass the complex Faerzress-worded lock on the giant weapon cache, successfully opening the gate though not silently enough to avoid triggering an alarm.

A rare magical mineral carried by Felkur that the party realized could have helped bypass the Faerzress-worded lock on the weapon cache, had Felkur been present during the trial.

A simple bottle of alcohol carried by Felkur, used as an improvised projectile hurled at the flawless crystal statue of Skoraeus Stonebones in the Cathedral of Faces. Thrown with extraordinary precision, it shattered the sacred artifact entirely.

A flawless, perfectly symmetrical crystal carving the size of a war shield, depicting the stone giant god Skoraeus Stonebones. It held the stone giant king Datya in an unbreakable trance until it was shattered by Felkur's thrown beer bottle.
