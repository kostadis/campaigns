# Chapter 22 The return of the  wizard, the cleric and the drow.

Date: 2026-08-08

## Summary
The dust had barely settled from the chaos at the Cathedral of Faces when the ground beneath the party's feet began to tremble. From the rock itself, the Galeb Duhr named Grek emerged — a living stone creature who had witnessed the madness of his people for seven long years. Rather than rage at Felkur for shattering the sacred crystal statue with a bottle of beer, Grek trembled with something far more unexpected: relief. He revealed that not one of the stone giants had been able to raise a hand against the artifact, bound as they were by the first doctrine of their faith, and that Felkur's reckless act had accomplished what seven years of helpless watching could not. The stone giant king was lucid once more, and he wished to speak with the party in person.

The party, however, was not yet ready to march into the stone giant's lodge. A more uncomfortable conversation loomed first — one about the nature of their growing coalition. Daein and Felkur pressed the case for allying with a clerical cult of evil, arguing that the threat of the demon lord Graz'zt demanded every available sword and spell. Bramgrim, ever the moral compass of the group, bristled at the suggestion, invoking the lines that even desperate times should not cross. It took a lengthy and surprisingly persuasive argument — drawing on a dwarven myth in which gods of good and evil shared a meal rather than starve — before Bramgrim relented, rationalizing that the cultists could serve as a meat shield and be dealt with after Graz'zt was defeated. Akritas, ever pragmatic, agreed without much ceremony, reasoning that her duty was to protect the Forgotten Realms, not to purge every shadow from the Underdark.

With the moral debate settled, the party made their way to the Stone Lodge, where the giant king sat humiliated among walls covered in carvings he had defaced with his own hands during the years of madness. He was grateful, deeply so, and pledged to send his giants to aid the party's cause. In exchange, he asked only one thing: that the party free his enslaved kin from the palace of the fire giant king, Dengor. When the king lamented the ruined carvings that surrounded him, Felkur offered a characteristically blunt solution — move them to a cave for things you no longer want. The king found this wisdom profound. He dropped a handful of uncut gems from his enormous hand onto the ground as a reward, and Grek quietly informed Daein that the king's earlier gesture of lowering his head had been a formal stone giant apology.

The party then turned their attention to the most dangerous alliance yet. Quil'Bryn, the drow leader, agreed to send a guard to lead them toward the followers of Kiaransalee — the drow goddess of vengeance — though he made clear his men would go no further than the edge of their territory. The drow guard Rizlyn led them through winding passages to the crypts of the drow governors of Szith Morcane, wished them luck with barely concealed skepticism, and retreated to wait a safe distance away. The crypts were littered with bones — drow, duergar, quaggoth, and the discarded exoskeletons of azer — a grim welcome mat for what lay ahead.

They did not wait long before a drow named Asala appeared and led them deeper into the crypts, where they were brought before a figure unlike any they had encountered. Velasta, a priestess of Kiaransalee, stood before them with pallid skin that did not look living and eyes that burned with fierce, unsettling intelligence. The party quickly realized she was a revenant — an undead creature bound to the world by an unfinished need for vengeance, unkillable until that vengeance was satisfied. She demanded to know the whereabouts of Solom Ned'razak, the drow archmage who had betrayed and murdered her mother, her sister, and herself. When she sensed that Solom was no longer among the living, her fury became a cold, focused thing, and she demanded to know who had killed him.

Daein, thinking quickly, told her it was the fire giants. It was a breathtaking deception, delivered with enough conviction to redirect the full force of a revenant's eternal wrath toward the party's enemies. Velasta's eyes blazed, and she declared that she would see every fire giant destroyed. The party had secured their most terrifying ally yet — one who could not be killed, could not be reasoned out of her purpose, and was now pointed squarely at the forces standing between them and victory.

Back at the drow camp, the party grappled with the fragility of their lie. The truth — that Quil'Bryn had killed Solom — could unravel everything if it reached Velasta's ears. Murssvayas Dhuurniv, Solom's former right-hand man, suggested that a memory-altering spell might exist somewhere in Solom Ned'razak's private quarters within the Inverted Tower, a structure built into a massive stalagmite deep in Szith Morcane. He provided the party with Solom's arcane signet ring — a black jewel that glowed with its own inner light — and the password needed to bypass the tower's deadly wards. Quil'Bryn, for his part, quietly ensured his troops would say nothing about the true circumstances of Solom's death.

The Inverted Tower was a place of glistening black rock and humming arcane power, its levels separated by archways and protected by wards that could destroy the unprepared. The party ascended to the fourth level and found something none of them had expected: a weak, pale drow seated upon a great throne. It was Solom Ned'razak — or rather, a magical clone of him, a contingency he had prepared long before Quil'Bryn's knife found its mark. The clone carried the same memories, the same personality, and the same sharp pragmatism as the original. He confirmed that memory magic was unlikely to work on a revenant, as their identity was bound too tightly to their purpose. When Daein casually promised Szith Morcane to every faction leader as a post-war reward, Solom laughed until he could barely breathe, declaring that Daein would have made a wonderful drow. He gifted the party a spellbook filled with arcane scrolls before sending them on their way.

With their alliances secured and their deception holding — for now — the party turned their attention to the Forge of Surtur, the industrial heart of the fire giants' operation in Szith Morcane. Murssvayas's mages transformed the party into drifting clouds of vapor, allowing them to slip silently through the tunnels and into the forge itself. What they found was staggering in scale: a cavern the size of a great hall, its ceiling lost in smoke, its floor carved with channels of molten rock tended by azer workers who moved through the heat as though born from it. Along the western wall stood rows of massive crossbow-like weapons shaped like serpent jaws — Hellfire projectors — some crated and ready for shipment, others still being assembled. The party understood immediately that destroying this place, or at least the people running it, would cripple the fire giants' ability to wage war on the surface world.

Standing in the Forge of Surtur, Felkur dropped out of gaseous form and cast an arcane eye to scout the area, discovering the sleeping Fire Giant King Dengor in his chambers below, guarded by fire giants and helmed horrors while enslaved stone giants sat in shackles nearby. He then recast gaseous form, and the party moved down into this palace. Felkur devised a plan: use the cover of their gaseous forms to position the party for a devastating surprise attack, conjure a field of writhing black tentacles to pin the sleeping king in place, and simultaneously free the stone giant prisoners to turn them into unexpected allies. The Owlbear was assigned the critical task of biting through the stone giants' chains. It was an audacious, multi-layered ambush that required everything to happen at once.

The attack unfolded with brutal efficiency. Felkur dropped out of gaseous form and unleashed a field of grasping, writhing tentacles that erupted from the ground around the sleeping king, ensnaring him before he could fully rouse. The Owlbear — a creature that had begun its journey as a random encounter in the woods and had since descended into the depths of the Underdark — shattered the first stone giant's shackles with a single powerful bite. Akritas followed with a bolt of lightning that struck the restrained king with devastating force, and Bramgrim summoned a spectral guardian that blazed with radiant light, striking at any enemy that drew near. The forge erupted into chaos as fire giants, helmed horrors, and salamanders scrambled to respond.

The battle was fierce and costly. The stone giants, once freed, turned on their captors with righteous fury, but the heavily armed fire giant guards began to wear them down. At first, Daein and Felkur hesitated, wondering if they should even care about the prisoners' fate or simply make their escape. Then, Felkur remembered that the stone giant king had promised to send his giants to aid their cause only if his kin were freed. Both realized that their larger plans in Maerimydra depended entirely on saving these stone giant prisoners. Acting quickly, Felkur cast a passwall spell to tear a shortcut through the stone wall, connecting Dengor's chamber directly to the area where the stone giants were fighting, while staying in the shadows to snipe at the fire giants from afar. Meanwhile, Daein, Bramgrim, and Akritas focused their combined might on King Dengor, ultimately slaying him. Bramgrim rushed through the newly created passage, casting a mass healing word to bolster the wounded stone giants and turn the tide of the battle.

As the last fire giant crumpled to the ground and the echoes of battle faded, the air in the chamber shifted. Velasta, the revenant, arrived, her eyes blazing with the fury of the wronged dead. She screamed a name — Dengor — and demanded to know who had killed him. The party stood in the wreckage of their hard-won victory, suddenly realizing that Velasta's thirst for vengeance extended to anyone who stole her kill by slaying Dengor. Having just delivered the killing blow to the fire giant king, they stood surrounded by the bodies of their enemies, knowing their most dangerous ally would now want to kill them.

## Locations
### Cathedral of Faces
A sacred stone giant site where a flawless crystal statue once stood, recently shattered by Felkur to break a cycle of madness that had gripped the stone giants for seven years.

### Stone Lodge
The residence of the Stone Giant King, filled with walls of carvings that were defaced during the giants' period of madness. The party met with the King here to finalize their alliance.

### Crypts of Szith Morcane
A dark passageway housing the remains of former drow governors, littered with the bones of Drow, Duergar, Quaggoths, and discarded Azer exoskeletons. It served as the meeting ground for the party and the followers of Kiaransalee.

### The Inverted Tower
A tower built into a large stalagmite in Szith Morcane, featuring octagonal rooms of glistening black rock and multiple levels protected by powerful arcane wards. The party discovered Solom Ned'razak's clone here on the fourth level.

### Forge of Surtur
A massive industrial cavern the size of a keep's great hall, filled with smoke and molten rock channels controlled by Azer workers. The fire giants used it to mass-produce Hellfire projectors and other siege weapons for an assault on the surface world.

### King Dengor's Palace
A massive underground chamber within Szith Morcane, located down a set of stairs from the main forge. It contained the sleeping quarters of the fire giant king and a section where stone giants were kept in shackles. The site of the party's climactic ambush.

### Szith Morcane
A drow outpost currently occupied by fire giants who are using its resources to prepare for a surface invasion. The party used it as a base of operations for negotiating alliances and planning their assault.

### Maerimydra
A drow city conquered by fire giants, discussed as the ultimate destination and focal point for the demonic influence of Graz'zt.
