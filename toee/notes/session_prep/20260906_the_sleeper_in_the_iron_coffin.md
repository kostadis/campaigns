# Dead Men Walk into Nulb

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging the first full Nulb session after **The Sleeper in the Iron Coffin**.

Party: level **9** — Calmer, Zephyr, Zinnia, and Sequoia; all player-run.

Register: **kinetic spy power fantasy with criminal color** — more *Black Widow* than Sherlock Holmes. Clues are visible, choices are bold, and nobody needs to understand an invisible web of organized crime before they can act.

> **Scope:** Open on the muddy river road, immediately after the Rhennee child shouted that family had returned and a dockhand gave Sequoia Skole's note: *“The captain is still telling people you're dead.”* Nulb is not ignorant: the party came back from the Scarlet Moon assault and recruited Dren Halveth here for the Earth Temple. Tolub's boast survives because everyone knows that openly doubting him can get a person killed. The intended order is **choose a friendly base → make the return undeniable → let Tolub's enemies settle scores → secure a supply route → shake a tail into Mother Screng's back room → learn who sleeps in the iron coffin**. If the party returns to the Temple tonight, stop on Dren saying that Hedrack is waiting. Do **not** pull Hedrack, Lucius, or the coffin expedition forward into Nulb.

## Two visible clocks

### Nulb Heat — how much attention the party has drawn

`[OVERLAY]` Start at **1 — noticed** because the Rhennee child shouted. Raise it when the party makes a spectacle or leads surveillance toward an ally; lower it by changing clothes, using a Rhennee wagon, or shaking a tail.

| Heat | What it looks like | Material effect |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — discreet** | Only chosen allies know where the party is right now | Advantage on the first entrance or ambush |
| **1 — noticed** | Rumor outruns them, but their destination is still uncertain | Selentis sends a polite invitation; Tolub has minutes, not hours |
| **2 — famous** | The taverns know the party is presently in town | Prices rise, Tolub prepares an exit, and a tail becomes visible |
| **3 — hunted** | Weapons or magic have been used openly | Every exit needs a decoy and the supply route will be watched |

### Tolub's Grip — how safe it feels to contradict him

`[TABLE]` `[OVERLAY]` Start at **2 — recognized**. Nulb has seen these faces before; the Rhennee child has named them again, but nobody yet knows whether the party will protect the first person who calls Tolub a liar. Lower Grip when the party blocks retaliation or publicly humiliates Tolub. Raise it if they withdraw, endorse his story, or let him punish a doubter.

| Grip | What the room permits | Material effect |
|---|---|---|
| **3 — enforced silence** | People recognize the party but repeat Tolub's story | Only chosen allies risk private contact |
| **2 — recognized** | Everyone watches the party and Tolub | Skole prepares to move, but waits for proof |
| **1 — permission** | Someone contradicts Tolub and remains protected | One grievance becomes action; Tolub runs or escalates |
| **0 — reckoning** | Tolub is visibly beaten, trapped, or abandoned | Old scores come due across the docks; every faction watches who inherits the space |

## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

`[OVERLAY]` This session lands if the players feel like the most dangerous people in Nulb, turn Tolub's coerced victory story into permission for his enemies to act, and leave with a **real supply solution** plus a **real outside ally**. The strongest close is Y'dey naming **Prince Thrommel** and telling them that waking the “sleeper” means rescuing a living man, not opening a monster's coffin.

`[OVERLAY]` Do not measure success by whether they understand every faction. Measure it by three visible changes:

1. Tolub can no longer punish people merely for admitting the party is alive.
2. Somebody has agreed to put food and pay on the road to Dren.
3. The party knows Y'dey is alive and the sleeper is Prince Thrommel.

# SCENE 1 — The Dead Get an Invitation

### Setup

- `[TABLE]` **Where:** the first muddy stretch of river road inside Nulb.
- `[TABLE]` **When:** seconds after Sequoia reads Skole's note.
- `[TABLE]` **Visible:** the Rhennee child is already sprinting toward camp. Dockworkers recognize the party, but the first one who says “they're alive” is elbowed quiet; nobody wants to be the first adult caught contradicting Tolub.
- `[OVERLAY]` **Hidden but shown:** a cleanly dressed runner with a black-and-white cord around one wrist waits beneath an awning.

The runner does not block the road. He waits for the child to disappear, gives a shallow bow, and offers a folded card.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Runner:** *“Madame Selentis extends her courtesy. The Old Mayor's Manor, after dark. She asks no oath and sends no escort. She assumes people of your reputation can find a door.”*

He leaves unless stopped. If threatened, he gives his name, repeats the invitation, and looks frightened without becoming insolent. The test is whether newly powerful people need to hurt a messenger.

### Give them the three bright doors

Do not make the players ask what exists in Nulb. Put the choices in the camera frame:

1. `[TABLE]` The Rhennee camp erupts into welcome upriver.
2. `[TABLE]` Skole's note points straight toward the Boatman's Tavern and Tolub.
3. `[OVERLAY]` Selentis's card points toward the Old Mayor's Manor after dark.

Mother Screng is not a fourth invitation. Eryk or a wound from the tavern scene points there later.

### Branch — they follow the runner

`[OVERLAY]` The runner is easy to follow and hard to catch doing anything dramatic. He passes one rope-wristed laborer, leaves a chalk mark beneath a bridge post, and enters the manor by the front. The party learns that Selentis is watching the roads; they do not uncover her whole network.

### Branch — they go straight for Tolub

`[OVERLAY]` Good. Tolub gets less warning. Start Scene 3 with him still holding court and let the Velkori welcome happen after the action.

### Branch — they choose family the Velkori

`[OVERLAY]` Best operationally and emotionally. Start Scene 2; Tolub learns they are back before they reach him, which enables the chase rather than removing the encounter.

### Failure is comedic and material

`[OVERLAY]` If they try to be perfectly anonymous after the child yelled “FAMILY HAS RETURNED,” a dockside boy begins selling descriptions of “Tolub's dead heroes” for copper while every adult loudly insists nobody is buying them. Tolub gets warning, and the party gains a terrible nickname; nobody loses the plot.

### NPC notes

- `[OVERLAY]` **Selentis's runner** — professional enough to deliver the card, young enough to be scared. Wants to complete one job and go home. Tic: wipes mud from one immaculate shoe after every answer.

# SCENE 2 — Family Before Business

### Setup

- `[TABLE]` **Where:** the Velkori camp beside the river.
- `[TABLE]` **Who:** Eryk, Serena, Vethra, and the ordinary life of the clan.
- `[OVERLAY]` **Tone:** loud, warm, fed. For ten minutes, nobody quizzes the party or asks them to prove anything.

Eryk embraces them. Serena steals Sequoia's note, reads it aloud, and laughs hard enough to make the nearby wagons join in without knowing why.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Eryk:** *“He killed you after you took Dren up the road? Good. Sit. Eat. When you are ready, you can go explain your death to him.”*

Give each character one uncomplicated sign of belonging: a dry coat, hot food, a drink, a child demanding a story, someone cleaning mud from a weapon without asking permission.

### The one problem Eryk puts on the table

`[TABLE]` The party already knows the Rhennee are being coerced into road ambushes. `[OVERLAY]` Make tonight's version concrete: a man wearing Selentis's black-and-white cord has ordered one covered Velkori wagon to report at dusk for a “short haul.” Eryk does not know what is being carried. He knows refusal costs wheels, animals, or people.

Place the token in Eryk's hand—a black-and-white cord cut from the last messenger. No criminal inference is required.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Eryk:** *“I do not need you to solve Nulb. I need this wagon to remain ours tonight.”*

### What the Velkori offer freely

- `[TABLE]` Safe beds, food, and family status.
- `[OVERLAY]` A change of river clothes for each PC, enough to drop Heat by one if they use them.
- `[OVERLAY]` A covered wagon, a fast skiff, or two local guides—but only one can be committed tonight.
- `[TABLE]` Eryk names Mother Screng as the honest source for medicine in town.
- `[TABLE]` Serena confirms that the Iron Lash and Boatman's upper floor remain dangerous, familiar territory.
- `[TABLE]` Vethra refuses another reading: *“The cards spoke. You have not finished listening.”*
- `[TABLE]` Vethra's crimson-barge image points toward Vesrek's *Crimson Trader*, not Tolub's *Iron Dagger*; do not merge the captains.

### Branch — they protect the wagon openly

`[OVERLAY]` They can stand beside it at dusk. The collecting team sees four level-9 adventurers and leaves to report. The party wins without combat, but Selentis knows they have taken responsibility for the Rhennee.

### Branch — they use the wagon as bait

`[OVERLAY]` Excellent spy play. Serena drives; one or more PCs hide inside. The collectors lead them to the manor's outer yard or the Wainwright route. This earns a clean entrance to Scene 4 and may expose one leg of the supply line.

### Branch — they tell Eryk they cannot help

`[OVERLAY]` He remains family. The welcome cools, and the wagon leaves at dusk. The party can still follow it or use Selentis's invitation, but the Rhennee will not lend another vehicle for Temple business tonight.

### Failure is comedic and material

`[OVERLAY]` If a disguise or ambush around the wagon goes badly, a goat eats the signal cord and the wrong cart rolls out first, loaded with wedding linens. The party loses surprise and must chase the correct wagon; nobody innocent disappears offscreen.

### NPC notes

- `[TABLE]` **Eryk** — expressive, loyal, concretely afraid for his people. Feeds family before asking the impossible.
- `[TABLE]` **Serena** — defiant and alive. Wants to take part, not stand as a symbol of a rescue already completed.
- `[TABLE]` **Vethra** — exhausted, exact, and done performing on demand. Wants action on the reading already purchased with her health.

# SCENE 3 — Walk into Tolub's Lie

### Setup

- `[MODULE §42/125/163]` **Where:** the Boatman's Tavern, with an escape line through its working rooms toward the docks.
- `[OVERLAY]` **Default:** Tolub is ashore and holding court until dusk. If he is at sea, put Grud in his chair and run the same scene with Tolub visible aboard the *Iron Dagger* at the end.
- `[TABLE]` **Party advantage:** the room remembers the party returning from the Scarlet Moon and taking Dren into Earth service. People repeat Tolub's later claim that he had them killed because they fear what happens to doubters.

Open on Tolub himself halfway through the boast. He says he had the party put in the ground after they marched Dren up the road. Each embellishment is less credible than the last; the room laughs anyway, and one man checks Grud before laughing.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Tolub:** *“They survived the fire. They took Halveth. Then I had all four put under honest dirt. Ask anyone here—carefully.”*

Then let the four “dead” people enter. Nobody needs time to recognize them. The tension is whether anyone dares admit recognition before Tolub decides what must be true.

Skole sets down four cups without looking surprised.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Skole:** *“Dead people drink free. Tolub says you're dead, so send him the bill.”*

Tolub's crew are not silent extras. They laugh when he is winning, and ordinary patrons copy them. When he is not, the room does not cheer the party immediately—it watches for proof that he can no longer punish the first person who moves. Grud watches the crew before he watches the party.

`[OVERLAY]` If any ordinary patron contradicts Tolub before the party checks him, Tolub points and Grud's men move to drag that person outside. Pause there. The party can intervene, and everyone in the room judges whether their protection is real. If they do nothing, the doubter vanishes by morning and the enforced story hardens.

### Three approaches, all strong

#### The public reveal

`[OVERLAY]` The party makes Tolub acknowledge them in front of the room. Give Zephyr the floor. A clean accusation does not reveal anything new; it demonstrates that somebody can call Tolub a liar and remain standing. Permission moves through the room one face at a time.

#### The quiet knife

`[OVERLAY]` The party uses Skole's back room, sends Tolub a message only a survivor could know, and makes him come privately. He offers money, supplies, or a name to preserve the public lie. This gains leverage but delays the public score-settling cascade until the party shows itself.

#### The smash cut

`[OVERLAY]` The party enters through a window, cellar, disguise, or rooftop and gets directly to him. Reward the audacity. Do not add guards just because their plan is good.

### What Tolub wants

`[MODULE §125/163–165]` He wants river power, wealth, and a patron. `[OVERLAY]` Tonight he needs his threat to remain more believable than the four people standing in front of him. He calls them doubles, ghosts, or actors wearing dead people's faces and watches to see whether the room will laugh.

> **Tolub:** *“Good likeness. But the four who took Halveth are dead, and everyone in this room knows how fond I am of being doubted.”*

If that fails, he offers the thing they came for: one supply run to the Temple, bought with his silence and theirs. His offer is useful and treacherous.

### How the old scores come due

`[OVERLAY]` Do not turn the room into instant allies. Each person acts for a private grievance only after the party makes retaliation look survivable. Reveal the cascade one beat at a time:

1. **Skole risks the first sentence:** *“I watched these four leave with Halveth.”* He puts Tolub's dispatch case within Sequoia's reach.
2. **Lodriss protects the tavern, not Tolub:** if he draws steel or orders a killing inside, she bars his cellar escape and presents every unpaid cost at once.
3. **The dockworkers collect:** men Tolub has beaten or extorted cut loose his prepared skiff, roll cargo into the pursuit lane, and point the party toward the *Iron Dagger*.
4. **The frightened talk:** a crew member or cheated local gives up one useful fact—Tolub's exit, Grud's signal, or the manifest—without pledging loyalty.

At **Grip 1 — permission**, one score is settled. At **Grip 0 — reckoning**, all four beats are available. Grud and the core River Wolves remain coherent until beaten, bought, or deprived of both leaders.

### Put the truth where they can grab it

`[OVERLAY]` A leather dispatch case hangs from Tolub's chair or belt. Inside is a cargo manifest bearing a blue-green seal associated with Belsornig's former network and a routing mark for the Old Mayor's Manor. Sequoia can steal it; Zinnia can spot Tolub protecting it; Zephyr can demand it; Calmer can simply take it after the room breaks.

The manifest establishes three things without a lecture:

1. Tolub was tied to Water, not Fire.
2. Selentis's manor controls the onward route.
3. A shipment of meat, lamp oil, and coin can move tonight.

### When Tolub runs — the kinetic set piece

`[OVERLAY]` Tolub flips the table and bolts when he loses the room, when somebody touches the dispatch case, or when capture becomes inevitable. Run three images, not a tactical grid:

1. **Common room:** River Wolves overturn benches. The party can go through, over, or around them.
2. **Cellar:** Tolub kicks loose stacked crates marked with the manor routing sign. Sequoia sees a narrow delivery hatch to the alley.
3. **Docks:** Tolub cuts a mooring line and jumps for a departing skiff. Catch **Tolub** or catch the dropped **dispatch case**; a brilliant plan can take both.

Checks decide cost, not whether the chase continues. On a failure, someone loses a cloak, weapon, coin purse, or position. The next image still happens.

### Branch — they catch him

`[OVERLAY]` Tolub trades one truth for his skin: *“Belsornig paid for the trap. Fire was where he wanted you to die.”* The party chooses whether to kill him, turn him, publicly humiliate him, or leave him owing a debt.

### Branch — they kill him

`[MODULE §126/166–168]` Grud keeps the River Wolves together. `[OVERLAY]` The party wins the room and dispatch case, but creates a pirate succession problem rather than erasing the crew. Raise Heat to 3 and drop Tolub's Grip to 0.

### Branch — he escapes

`[OVERLAY]` Let him reach the *Iron Dagger*, but the party gets the manifest and controls the story in the tavern. Tolub becomes a recurring enemy who knows they are much more dangerous than before.

### Branch — they recruit the room

`[OVERLAY]` Tolub's enemies use the opening to settle individual scores; they do not become the party's followers. Skole, a frightened crew member, an extorted dockworker, or a paid guide can help. The whole pirate faction requires both hard leaders to be neutralized or convincingly beaten.

### Failure is comedic and material

`[OVERLAY]` A failed intimidation lands as forced entertainment. The room applauds Tolub's joke too loudly; he points to the last man who joined in and asks why he hesitated. Grud's men seize that man unless the party intervenes. Either way, a terrible ballad about “the four dead lords” spreads by morning.

### NPC notes

- `[MODULE §125/163–165]` `[TABLE]` **Tolub** — loud, charming, and most dangerous while other people still laugh. His death boast is a loyalty test: doubt must be punished immediately or every buried grievance in Nulb becomes actionable.
- `[MODULE §126/166–168]` **Grud** — command continuity with a squint. Wants the crew to see that somebody remains in charge.
- `[TABLE]` **Skole** — chose the party once and is gambling that they can finally break Tolub's hold. Keeps his best lines dry and quiet.
- `[MODULE §130/178–180]` **Lodriss** — protects the tavern before Tolub. If he endangers her operation, she opens or closes an escape according to her own interest.

# SCENE 4 — The Woman Who Owns the Road

### Setup

- `[MODULE §54/221–223]` **Where:** the Old Mayor's Manor after dark—sagging portico, boarded windows, two lanterns lit together.
- `[MODULE §121/226]` **Who:** Selentis, Sir Torvel standing silent, and one forgettable clerk.
- `[OVERLAY]` **What is on the table:** the party's stolen cargo manifest, or Selentis's matching copy if they never acquired it; a black-and-white cord; a blank supply warrant.

Do not play this as a mystery. Selentis demonstrates what she controls by placing the answer on the table.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Selentis:** *“You need meat, lamp oil, and coin carried to a hole full of soldiers. I own the road between those nouns. Shall we pretend this meeting concerns anything else?”*

`[MODULE §221–232]` She runs Nulb logistics for Hedrack while keeping the Temple invisible. `[OVERLAY]` She does not name Hedrack, Lucius, or the Greater Temple unless the party does first.

### Her offer — simple enough to act on

`[OVERLAY]` Selentis can put one convoy on the road tonight and establish a weekly run afterward. The blank warrant makes her the recognized surface quartermaster for whoever now controls the upper Temple.

She asks only three questions:

1. Who signs orders in the upper Temple now?
2. Which old agents should still receive supplies?
3. Does the party want Tolub useful, frightened, or gone?

Each answer has an obvious consequence. Do not hide a fourth question inside them.

### Branch — they sign

`[OVERLAY]` The logistics crisis is solved immediately. A cart leaves tonight. Selentis gains a record of the party's consumption, personnel, and route; Hedrack's organization can see the shape of their household.

### Branch — they counteroffer

`[OVERLAY]` A one-run trial with no exclusive authority is a fair and clever counter. Selentis accepts if the party gives her one concrete concession: Tolub stops disrupting the docks, the Rhennee wagon completes no more forced work, or the party leaves her manor publicly unthreatened.

### Branch — they refuse

`[OVERLAY]` She does not punish them for being cautious. The party can build a smaller independent route from Eryk's wagon, Skole's dock access, and Otis's Ranger paths. It moves half as much and takes a day longer, but Selentis cannot inventory it.

### Branch — they threaten or attack

`[MODULE §223/227–230]` The manor has guards, a cracked-bell scatter signal, and a cellar escape. `[OVERLAY]` Selentis does not order a suicidal stand. Torvel gets her to the rat-run while the party chooses between pursuit, the manifest room, and getting out before Nulb's armed factions arrive. This is an extraction scene, not a boss fight.

### If they bring Tolub

`[OVERLAY]` Selentis appraises him like damaged cargo. She will take custody, but says exactly what that means:

> **Selentis:** *“If you leave him here, he becomes useful to me. Do not call that justice after the fact.”*

### Failure is comedic and material

`[OVERLAY]` A failed attempt to bluff their way into better terms produces an absurdly detailed requisition form in Calmer's new title. He must either sign “High Lord Master Calmer, Acting Authority for Meat” or lose tonight's convoy. The cost is dignity and a paper trail.

### NPC notes

- `[MODULE §120/221–230]` **Selentis** — patient, exact, and loyal to a hidden command. Turns threats into logistics. Wants a restored chain she can report and control.
- `[MODULE §121/151–153]` **Torvel** — devotion made visible. Says little; moves as soon as the room becomes unsafe.

# SCENE 5 — Shake the Tail, Find the Saint

### Setup

- `[MODULE §41/195–203]` **Where:** Mother Screng's herb shop, then Otis's forge, then the shop's back room.
- `[OVERLAY]` **How they get there:** Eryk recommends Screng for a tavern wound, or the party takes Falrinth's scarab to the one smith in town who knows old Temple work.
- `[MODULE §230]` `[OVERLAY]` **The visible problem:** the rope-wristed laborer from the road is following them badly enough for Zinnia to catch him in a bottle's reflection.

Do not roll to notice the tail. They are level-9 operators and the fun is losing him. A check can reveal that his backup waits near the Gallows; it cannot hide the scene.

Murfles meets them at the counter as Hruda, all impatience and stained sleeves.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Murfles:** *“Acid, fire, rot, or poison? Pick the thing that tried to kill you. I charge extra for speeches.”*

She puts a red bottle in Zinnia's hand, nods toward the street, and gives one job:

> `[OVERLAY]` **Murfles:** *“Take this to the smith. Lose the man behind you. If he arrives before the bottle, you were never here.”*

### Run the tail-shake in three images

Invite a plan, then give every PC one move. Competent ideas work; rolls decide Heat and collateral.

1. **Laundry line:** `[OVERLAY]` cloaks, sheets, and Rhennee colors turn the lane into a moving curtain. Zephyr picks the false silhouette or the ambush point.
2. **The Gallows crowd:** `[OVERLAY]` a dice game and public argument make a human knot. Calmer commands the space; Sequoia changes the package or steals the watcher's signal token.
3. **Otis's forge:** `[MODULE §195–197]` `[OVERLAY]` sparks, smoke, and two doors. Zinnia decides whether the watcher sees four people enter, two leave, or nobody arrive at all.

### Outcomes

| Result | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Clean** | The watcher follows a decoy to the river. Lower Heat by 1; Y'dey offers the cell's full support. |
| **Messy** | The watcher loses them but knows they used the forge district. Heat stays put; the cell helps, but moves its next dead drop. |
| **Caught** | The watcher reaches Otis's door. Otis stages a furious bulk-horseshoe dispute as cover. Raise Heat by 1; Y'dey still reveals herself, but offers no safehouse or Ranger route tonight. |

### What Otis can tell them

`[MODULE §195–197]` Otis looks at Falrinth's scarab as a craftsman, not an oracle. `[OVERLAY]` He gives three plain answers:

- It is a Greater Temple command key, not jewelry.
- It opens or commands something below, but he does not know which door or creature.
- The blue-green seal on Tolub's manifest belongs to Water's old network; Selentis's black-and-white cord belongs to the people who inherited the route.

Do not let him solve Lucius, Falrinth, or the coffin. He identifies what the objects **do in play tonight**.

### The back-room reveal

Back at the herb shop, Mother Screng closes the shutters. If Calmer offers the Cuthbertine phrase, Y'dey answers it. If he does not, she reveals herself anyway once she is satisfied the tail is gone.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Y'dey:** *“You are Calmer of the Earth Temple, they say. I am Mother Screng of the herb shop, they say. Both true. Both also incomplete.”*

Pause. Let Calmer react before she gives the mission.

> `[MODULE T1–4 §334]` `[OVERLAY]` **Y'dey:** *“There is a man beneath that Temple. Not a relic. Not a rumor. Prince Thrommel—Grand Marshall of Furyondy, Provost of Veluna, betrothed of her Ladyship Jolene. The kingdoms buried him as dead ten years ago. He is not dead.”*

Then make the ask clean enough for a teenager with a sword to understand:

> `[OVERLAY]` **Y'dey:** *“You can walk where I cannot. Bring him out alive. Do not trust what the Temple shows you when you find him.”*

That is tonight's payload. She does not make them pass a theological oral exam.

### Calmer's mask

`[OVERLAY]` Y'dey validates the mission, not every choice made under Earth Temple cover. She asks whether Calmer is still Calmer. If he demands a judgment, give him one line and return the decision to the player:

> **Y'dey:** *“A disguise is something you put on. It is not permission to become it.”*

Do not turn this into a sermon. His answer can be proud, angry, evasive, or sincere. Y'dey listens and then discusses the rescue.

### What the cell actually offers

| Ally | Tonight's useful support | First thing they ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Y'dey** | Healing, Cuthbertine authentication, and a covert Veluna channel once Thrommel is safe | Protect the cell and bring Thrommel out alive |
| **Murfles** | Dead drops, courier work, disguise notes, and field observation | Never walk directly from a mission back to this shop |
| **Otis** | Ranger paths, smithing cover, and practical identification of Temple objects | Do not summon open troops until there is a living prince to extract |

### Branch — full trust

`[OVERLAY]` Y'dey gives the name Thrommel, the broad prison description, the Ranger route, and a one-use Cuthbertine recognition phrase for the rescue.

### Branch — cautious cooperation

`[OVERLAY]` Good. Y'dey gives Thrommel's identity and the warning about appearances, but holds the route and recognition phrase until the party brings one verifiable report from below.

### Branch — hostility

`[OVERLAY]` The truth still lands. Otis opens the rear door; Murfles positions herself between Calmer and Y'dey; Y'dey refuses to beg. The party leaves knowing who the sleeper is but without the cell's logistical support.

### Failure is comedic and material

`[OVERLAY]` If the watcher reaches the forge, Otis loudly sells the party three hundred horseshoes for animals they do not own. The invoice circulates by morning. Selentis learns they met the smith, while Dren later asks where the Temple should stable three hundred horses.

### NPC notes

- `[MODULE §198–200]` **Y'dey** — quiet authority without mystery-box theatrics. Wants Thrommel alive and her cell intact. Gives the dangerous truth once, plainly.
- `[MODULE §201–203]` **Murfles** — quickest person in the room and mildly offended when others are slow. Wants to know whether the party can protect a secret while moving fast.
- `[MODULE §195–197]` **Otis** — broad, soot-blackened, practical. Wants action linked to a recoverable objective, not a heroic army charging at a rumor.

# SCENE 6 — What They Carry Back

This is the consolidation scene, not another investigation. Before the party chooses whether to return to the Temple, ask four questions in plain language:

1. Who supplies Dren now?
2. What happened to Tolub?
3. Who in Nulb now admits publicly that the party is alive?
4. What one fact will they **not** tell anyone in the Temple?

Write the answers down. They are the campaign state created tonight.

### Supply-route result

| Route chosen | What reaches Dren | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Selentis signed warrant** | Full load tonight; weekly deliveries begin | Selentis records the party's needs and reports the route |
| **Selentis trial run** | Full load once tonight | The negotiation stays open; no permanent authority granted |
| **Rhennee + Skole/Otis route** | Half load tomorrow | Private and loyal, but slower and smaller |
| **Tolub's deal** | Fast full load tonight | Tolub or Grud can sabotage, skim, or reveal it later |
| **No agreement** | Nothing new | Dren can improvise for two or three days; the clock keeps running |

### If they return to the Temple tonight

`[TABLE]` Dren meets them before anyone grander does. Give a short operational report based on their answers: food arrival, pay status, the necromancer's latest intrusion, and whether another body or clue was found. Do not improvise a whole new disaster to punish the Nulb trip.

Then Dren looks toward the lower passage.

> `[OVERLAY]` **Dren:** *“A man came up from below. No escort. No dust on his boots. Hedrack is waiting.”*

Hard stop. Do not begin the Hedrack audience.

### If they stay in Nulb

`[OVERLAY]` End on a visual consequence of their choices: the *Iron Dagger's* lanterns moving downriver if Tolub escaped, Selentis's paired manor lights going dark if she was threatened, or a supply wagon rolling silently toward the Temple if they solved the route. The next session opens on the party choosing when and how to return.

## Anticipated player questions

**Does Nulb actually believe Tolub killed us?**

No. People saw the party return from the Scarlet Moon and recruit Dren Halveth. Tolub claims he had them killed afterward, and people repeat it because doubt can get them killed. The current arrival matters because it may make contradiction safe.

**Who moves against Tolub once we show ourselves?**

Skole moves first. Lodriss protects her tavern if Tolub endangers it, extorted dockworkers interfere with his escape, and frightened locals trade information. They are settling their own scores, not joining the party.

**Can we just kill Tolub?**

Yes. Grud and the River Wolves remain, the abrupt violence raises Nulb Heat to 3, and Tolub's Grip drops to 0. Killing one pirate captain is a decisive act, not a magic delete key.

**Can we ignore Selentis?**  

Yes. Eryk, Skole, and Otis can assemble the smaller independent route.

**Do we have to solve all of Nulb?**  

No. One supply route and one trusted contact is a complete win for tonight.

**Why should we trust Y'dey?**  

They do not have to. She offers a verifiable identity, specific intelligence, and aid with clear limits. Thrommel's identity is true whether or not they join her cell.

**Can Y'dey summon an army?**  

Not safely. A living, extracted Thrommel is the trigger for open royal action. An army now burns her cell and may get the prisoner killed or moved.

**Can Otis fully identify Falrinth's scarab?**  

No. He can establish that it is a Greater Temple command key. Its exact lock and Lucius's connection remain future discoveries.

**Can we go straight back to the Temple?**  

Yes. Resolve the supply result if they made one, give Dren's report, and stop on Hedrack waiting.

**Can we bring these allies openly into the Temple?**  

They can ask. Y'dey's cell refuses until Thrommel's exact location and condition are confirmed; secrecy is the reason the cell survived.

**How do we learn Tolub really served Water?**  

The blue-green seal on the manifest or Tolub's own frightened admission. Put the proof in their hands; do not make them infer it from six NPC relationships.

## Stage directions

- Open with the three bright doors: family, Tolub, or Selentis.
- Give the Velkori ten real minutes of warmth before business intrudes.
- At the Boatman's, recognition is immediate; let permission to contradict Tolub cross the room one face at a time.
- Each time the party proves Tolub cannot retaliate, let one old grievance become an action.
- In each chase, failed checks add cost but never stop the camera.
- Selentis puts her answer on the table before negotiations begin.
- Make the tail visible. The challenge is shaking it with style.
- After Y'dey names herself, be silent and let Calmer answer.
- Say **Prince Thrommel** slowly. That name is the turn.
- Never decide what Calmer's Earth-priest cover means for him. Ask the player.
- If the boys stall, cut to motion: Tolub runs, the watcher appears, the wagon starts moving.
- Keep each scene to one secret and one decision.
- If they return, stop on Hedrack. The cliffhanger is the point.

## Open decisions for the GM

1. **Exact in-world date.** The campaign docs disagree after recent time jumps. Lock it before play if dates matter at the table.
2. **Tolub's starting position.** Recommended: still in the Boatman's until dusk so the confrontation can breathe before the chase.
3. **First score settled.** Recommended: Skole publicly confirms the Dren recruitment and puts Tolub's dispatch case within Sequoia's reach.
4. **Falrinth's scarab.** Ask whether Calmer carries it visibly; do not assume.
5. **How much Y'dey knows about the coffin's deception.** Recommended: she knows only “do not trust what the Temple shows you,” not the vampire mechanics.

7. **Grud's retaliation if Tolub dies.** Recommended: later, after Grud has chosen whether revenge or command matters more.
