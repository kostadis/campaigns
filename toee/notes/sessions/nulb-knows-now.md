# Nulb Knows Now

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging the continuation after Chapter 33, "The
Necromancer's Thank You Note."

The old `nobody-told-nulb.md` opening has now been played: the party negotiated with the
minotaur, discovered the necromancer's selective harvest, and recovered the shield from the
cistern. The Obsidian Edge are level **9**, still inside the upper Temple, with Barkinar's list
nearly complete and Falrinth's warning, the Nulb trip, and the hidden-good reveal still unspent.

This prep assumes the intended order is firm:

> **Temple business closes enough to leave → the party goes to Nulb → they meet Nulb's competing
> forces, including Y'dey's good-aligned cell → they return to the Temple → only then do they meet
> Hedrack.**

Do not bring Hedrack forward into Nulb. Do not have him summon them away halfway through the good
cell. If the table runs out of time, his audience becomes the next session's cold open; the order
does not change.

---

## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

This lands if the party leaves the Temple by choice, discovers that Nulb contains several
different power structures operating from mutually incompatible maps, and establishes at least a
workable contact with **Y'dey, Murfles, and Otis**. When they return, Hedrack receives them as
successful operators and quietly tests what Nulb changed in them.

The emotional sequence is:

1. **Competence:** they can leave because they have actually stabilized the upper Temple.
2. **Belonging:** the Velkori receive them as family.
3. **Leverage:** Nulb's abandoned Temple agents and Selentis each want something from the new
   management.
4. **Grace:** the hidden forces of Good prove the party are not alone — especially Calmer.
5. **The leash:** Hedrack congratulates them, and promotion becomes more frightening than blame.

The minimum viable close is Dren saying, *"Hedrack is waiting below."* The stronger close is
Hedrack saying, *"Lucius will find you,"* after the party has successfully kept Y'dey's cell out
of its report.

## The Default Trajectory (what happens if the party does nothing)

- **Dren keeps the upper Temple functional.** He can hold it for two or three days without the
  party. He cannot solve the necromancer, the Chest of Rao, payroll, or the long-term feeding of a
  minotaur.
- **The minotaur keeps its bargain.** It eats preserved meat and any undead that enter its room.
  It does not eat prisoners unless the party actually orders that; do not let Calmer's ugly joke
  become canon by administrative accident.
- **The necromancer keeps selecting.** Earth Temple bodies disappear; Fire and Water corpses rot
  in place. He remains below, and the party's short absence costs clues, not territory.
- **Nulb continues reporting to dead men.** Dick Rentsch waits for Romag, Wat waits for Alrrem,
  Tolub boasts that the party are dead, and Selentis collects contradictory reports without
  correcting anyone.
- **Y'dey's cell stays hidden.** Murfles will test the party if they enter the herb shop, but Y'dey
  does not reveal herself merely because famous adventurers are in town. Otis continues watching.
- **Selentis updates Lucius.** She may not know who now rules the upper Temple, but she knows the
  party survived and came back to Nulb. Every public disclosure improves her report.
- **Hedrack waits until they return.** He does not need to interrupt surface business. He wants to
  see what they bring back — facts, loyalties, lies, or all three.
- **If they refuse to return at all,** the campaign board changes honestly: their Temple cover
  begins to collapse, Dren must decide whether to withdraw, and Hedrack sends Lucius rather than
  an army. That is a branch, not a teleport back onto the rail.

## The Clever Play (what the optimal party would do — so you know when to nudge)

1. Leave Dren explicit orders: feed the minotaur, burn or guard the dead, keep the Chest secure,
   and tell nobody where the party went.
2. Enter Nulb without Earth Temple regalia. Let people keep believing the old factional map until
   their ignorance stops being useful.
3. Go first to the Velkori camp. It is the one place in Nulb where hospitality is not an
   interrogation disguised as a transaction.
4. Listen to Rentsch, Wat, and Skole before correcting them. Dead principals have made each man a
   free agent, and free agents explain themselves when allowed to talk.
5. Treat Selentis as a state, not an NPC: trade with her if useful, reveal nothing she has not
   already demonstrated, and assume every bargain becomes a report.
6. Bring Calmer to Mother Screng's shop, let Murfles conduct her test, and give Y'dey enough truth
   to establish mutual purpose without handing her every Temple name.
7. Accept the good cell's help without asking it to march. Their value is that they remain
   invisible: healing, messages, identification, extraction, and an honest outside channel.
8. Return normally. Vanishing immediately after meeting Good tells the Temple that Nulb changed
   them. Going back and attending Hedrack's audience preserves the access only they possess.
9. In Hedrack's room, tell the truth about the **itinerary** and lie only about the **substance**.
   He can learn where they went; he should not learn what Y'dey said.

## The Four Maps of Nulb (keep this beside you)

| Network | What it thinks is true | What it wants from the party | What it can offer | What exposes the party |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Velkori Rhennee** | The party are clan; Selentis is the danger; Tolub is still active | Relief from the coercion forcing Eryk's people into road ambushes | Safe beds, river movement, local trust, an honest referral to Mother Screng or Otis | Boasting where children, teamsters, or camp followers can repeat it |
| **Orphaned elemental agents** — Rentsch, Wat, Tolub, Skole | Their old patrons still matter, or at least nobody has formally replaced them | A new patron, safe exit, revenge, or room to become independent | Tavern intelligence, recruits, dock schedules, old faction routes | Announcing that all four prophets are dead or that the party now owns the Temple |
| **Selentis's operation** | The elemental war changed something; the Greater Temple still owns the board | Restore a usable chain of command and learn how controllable the party are | Fencing, clean coin, labor, supply routes, controlled access to Nulb | Giving her names, inventories, or a complete account of the new Temple hierarchy |
| **Y'dey's good cell** — Y'dey, Murfles, Otis | The Temple is resurgent; these may be the first insiders worth trusting | Proof of purpose, protection of the cell, and ultimately Prince Thrommel alive | Healing, Ranger routes, safe messages, item identification, Veluna/Furyondy escalation | Speaking Y'dey's name outside the back room or bringing a watcher to the shop |

> **GM-only:** These are not four teams waiting for the party to pick a color. The best play is to
> take something useful from each without letting any one network own the whole truth.

## The Monster Hierarchy Overlay (who actually obeys whom)

The monster hierarchy adds a second axis to the human faction map. Robes and Temple affiliation
describe doctrine; **tribe, pay, and immediate boss describe behavior.** Do not play every bugbear,
pirate, ogre, or troll as interchangeable Temple muscle.

The `HARD` / `NOMINAL` distinction is GM-facing social information, not a label to announce:

- A **HARD boss** is personally far above the rank and file. The group coheres around fear of or
  dependence on that individual; removing the boss is a genuine decapitation.
- A **NOMINAL boss** leads near-peers. The group survives the boss and is susceptible to
  contracts, succession fights, and separate bargains.
- **NO BOSS** means the group is a crowd, ecology, or convenience coalition. It responds to
  immediate incentives rather than a command chain.

| Monster society | Real chain | Hierarchy read | What that means this session |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nulb Militia** — Clogg the Crier, Dame Yarl, Pike-Tom | No standing boss; 10–60 locals turn out when fear or loot gives them a reason | **NO BOSS** | Selentis can point the crowd but does not own it. A public fight can attract a mob without creating disciplined opposition. |
| **Waterside Mercenaries** — Captain Vesk “Cross-Bow,” Serjeant Holl, Spear-Mara | Whoever pays Captain Vesk and keeps the drinks coming | **NOMINAL** | They are an employment market, not Rentsch’s loyal household. The party can hire, split, or alienate them independently of the tavern agents. |
| **River Wolves** — Tolub, Grud Squinteye, Mate Yarrin, Bosun Greel, Hessa | Tolub; Grud commands when Tolub is absent | **HARD** | Tolub is not one loud pirate surrounded by disposable extras. His roughly forty-person crew remains a coherent faction; no more than about ten are normally in port. |
| **Broken Tower brigands** — Captain Garroth “Black-Plate,” Sael, Bren | Garroth over a separate 26–28-person tower company | **NOMINAL** | Garroth can bargain, but his lieutenants and sergeants can survive or replace him. They work with the Upper Works band; they are not the same unit. |
| **Upper Works band** — Captain Tarn “the Pale,” Lieutenant Vox | Plunder and local command, separate from Garroth | **NOMINAL** | A second brigand faction exists behind the word “bandits.” Selentis or Feldrin can play the two companies against each other. |
| **Greywind Devotees** — six bugbears and four goblin servants | Personally dependent on Kelno | **HARD boss** | Kelno’s little Air faction is weak in numbers but tightly personal. His followers do not automatically recognize Calmer, Dren, or Hedrack’s black-plate officers as their immediate boss. |
| **Blackplate Wardens** — Grim the Post, Vex the Hall-Warden, Blackshield Gorl, Hush-Brek | Central Temple command; ultimately Hedrack | **HARD boss over them** | These black-armored bugbears are deliberately outside the four elemental cults. They are Hedrack’s eyes in the upper levels and should not be filed as “Kelno’s bugbears.” |
| **Moathouse Raiders** — Varric the Steady, Serjeants Hull and Wren, rank-and-file looters | Feldrin, but loyalty is primarily to plunder | **NOMINAL / borderline** | They are a mobile bridge between the Temple and Nulb supply system. A deserter is normal evidence of weak cohesion, not an anomaly. |
| **Temple Host** — trolls, hill giants, ettins, ogres, gargoyles, bugbears | Hedrack through Barkinar and Deggum | **Hedrack NOMINAL over the elite tier** | The trolls and giants are near-peers in raw danger. Hedrack rules them through fear, privileges, ritual authority, and a functioning command structure—not because each is helpless before him. |

Two current party assets sit **outside** this inherited hierarchy:

- **The minotaur** appears nowhere in the established command tree. Its food-and-guard bargain
  makes it an independent household faction of one, not a newly obedient member of the Temple
  Host.
- **Vurakhal** follows his negotiated contract and its clauses. Fire Temple imagery does not
  supersede the document, which is why asking him to recognize a new priest is less effective than
  reading what he actually signed.

> **GM-only — the Chapter 32 bugbears:** Area 231 belongs to the Blackplate Wardens in the monster
> hierarchy. The party read its four black-armored bugbears as Kelno’s expansion and took 20 gp
> from them. Unless table canon established an unmistakable Air sigil, treat that as an in-character
> misread: they extorted Hedrack’s direct troops. The old `nobody-told-nulb.md` open decision is now
> strongly resolved toward **Blackplate**, with the hierarchy as the reason.

### Fast color rule

Whenever an unnamed monster speaks, give it **one chain-of-command tell** before a combat tell:

- A River Wolf glances toward Grud before reaching for steel.
- A Waterside mercenary asks who is paying before asking whom to hit.
- A Greywind bugbear touches a looted wind-chime or invokes Kelno.
- A Blackplate Warden records a name, room, or toll in chalk for central command.
- A Temple Host troll complains about its post but goes quiet when Hedrack’s door opens.

That one beat turns a stat block into a faction member.

# SCENE 1 — The Cost of Owning a Dungeon

### Setup

- **Where:** the cistern and the walk back to the party's quarters.
- **When:** immediately after Chapter 33. Calmer has the slime-cleaned shield +1; the demented
  ghoul is dead; the necromancer's note is in Calmer's possession.
- **Who:** the party first; Dren when they return to their headquarters. The minotaur and Vurakhal
  remain offstage unless called for.

Do not open with another monster. Open with the administrative consequences of the monsters they
already acquired.

> **Dren, with a short written list:** *"The minotaur has food for three days. The men have pay
> for four. The dead have started leaving without permission. I can solve any one of those. I
> cannot solve all three while pretending this is a normal command."*

Let the players decide what matters. Dren's list should make leaving feel like the decision of
competent management, not GM extraction:

- Food fit for a minotaur rather than prisoners or necromancer-tainted corpses.
- Coin or a fence for the party's unsellable valuables and the garrison's payroll.
- Healing stock after Calmer's recent performances.
- A safe expert for the **TZGY scarab**, whom Lucius and Senshock must not see examining it.
- A possible courier or hiding place for the Chest of Rao.
- News from the Velkori and Tolub's known ship schedule.
- A decision about four black-plated bugbears asking Dren who authorized a twenty-gold “Air
  Temple toll.” Dren does not recognize them as Kelno’s people; their armor bears no wind sigil.

If the party follows that last detail, Dren can supply the crucial distinction without naming the
whole hierarchy:

> **Dren:** *"Kelno’s lot wear grey and steal kitchen bells. These wear black plate, keep a duty
> book, and look at me like my employment is temporary. Different problem."*

### Falrinth's unspent probe

The Chapter 33 summary does not record Falrinth's candle scene, so this prep assumes it has **not**
fired. Run it that night, after the party has had time to feel the necromancer watching them.

Use the full version in `nobody-told-nulb.md`, Scene 3. The shortened table version is:

> *"The flattering man has already been to you. Golden eyes, soft hands, very good wine. Lucius
> Graeme. Write down the name; he collects the ones he gives you.*
>
> *The scarab your cleric carries is not jewelry. Lucius will know it. Senshock will know it. They
> will use that knowledge differently, and neither will tell you they noticed.*
>
> *There is a room below, on the level beneath your feet. The holy gentlemen keep a sleeper in an
> iron coffin. Wake it and Verbobonc, Veluna, and Furyondy will remember this Temple exists. Ask
> your golden-eyed friend why that frightens him. Then signal me."*

He fades before questions. He still does not say *prince*, *Thrommel*, *Lolth*, or what he really
wants.

> **GM-only:** Falrinth makes them curious. Y'dey will make them care. Hedrack, after the return,
> will make them afraid of showing either emotion.

### Branch — they hunt the necromancer instead

Let them spend a scene on it. Reward the choice with the selective pattern and the swept corridor
outside the Bronze Door: Earth dead missing, Fire and Water dead untouched, an intact glyph on a
door somebody has been opening rather than breaking.

They do not find the necromancer. Dren's surface problems remain. Nulb is still the place where
those problems can be solved.

### Branch — they send Dren or a proxy to town

Dren can buy meat and lamp oil. He cannot safely test the scarab, meet the Velkori as family, or
unlock Y'dey. A proxy comes back with goods and rumors but no alliances. That is useful, not
sufficient.

### Branch — they choose Hommlet instead

It is possible but louder. Hommlet means explaining their reappearance to Jaroo, Burne, Rufus,
Terjon, and perhaps Elmo. Nulb is closer, already accepts dangerous people without paperwork, and
contains their existing Rhennee allies. Leave Hommlet open; make its political price clear.

### NPC notes

- **Dren Halveth** — wants a command he can actually execute and soldiers he can pay. Tic: reduces
  supernatural horror to a staffing problem. Would never ask the party to solve something he can
  solve himself.
- **Falrinth** — curious, oily, never physically present. Wants the party looking where Hedrack
  would rather they did not. Theatrically apologizes for every theatrical choice.

# SCENE 2 — Leave Someone Holding the Knife

### Setup

- **Where:** party headquarters, then the road from the Temple to Nulb.
- **Time:** morning after Falrinth's contact, or whenever the party commits to surface travel.
- **Offstage clock:** Barkinar's week is nearly complete. Do not have Hedrack summon them yet.

Before they leave, ask plainly: **"What are Dren's orders while you are gone?"** Write the answer
down. It becomes the state they return to.

The four decisions that matter:

1. **Minotaur:** preserved meat, undead only, prisoners, or no standing rule.
2. **Bodies:** burn them, guard them, inventory them, or leave them where they fell.
3. **Chest of Rao:** carry it, conceal it, leave it under guard, or substitute something.
4. **Authority:** may Dren make decisions in their absence, or must he wait for them?

> **GM-only:** These are Hedrack-profile questions in disguise. He will hear how their household
> behaved while they were gone even if he never learns what happened in Nulb.

### Failure is comedic and material

If their orders are vague, do not kill a prisoner offscreen. Have Dren solve the ambiguity in the
most defensible mercenary way: he spends real party coin on two sides of beef, appoints the
minotaur "acting reception officer," and has a clerk produce a food ledger requiring Calmer's
signature on every page. They lose money, dignity, and a little authority — not a life by fiat.

### The road

Skip a random encounter unless the table desperately needs action. The important choice is the
costume:

- **No Temple insignia:** easier movement; Nulb sees adventurers who survived.
- **Earth badges concealed but carried:** best tradecraft; credentials available if needed.
- **Lord Master regalia openly worn:** every Temple observer in Nulb knows the management changed
  before sunset.

Describe the first sight of Nulb as a place that has not received the news:

> *"Smoke lies low over the river. The same barges lean at the same rotten piers. At the tavern
> doors, men are still arguing over which elemental prophet will win a war that ended beneath
> their feet days ago. Nulb has not changed. That is how you know nobody told it."*

# SCENE 3 — Three Invitations Before Their Boots Dry

### Setup

- **Where:** bridge, river road, and the first muddy stretch of Nulb.
- **Who notices:** the Velkori first, Skole's people second, Selentis's watchers whether or not the
  party sees them. **Watcher-Grey**, one of the Broken Tower's giant raven sentries, circles three
  times before settling on a roofline if their arrival has already been flagged.

Give the party choices, not a receiving line:

1. A Velkori child recognizes them and runs for the camp shouting that family has returned.
2. A dockhand presses a folded scrap into Sequoia's or Zinnia's hand: *"Skole says the captain is
   still telling people you're dead. He thought you might enjoy the update."*
3. Before dusk, a cleanly dressed runner offers Madame Selentis's courtesy: the Old Mayor's Manor,
   after dark, if they wish to discuss business. It is explicitly an invitation, not an arrest.

If they arrive in regalia or make a spectacle, **Clogg the Crier** rings the militia muster bell
because a crowd is profitable, not because anyone declared an emergency. **Dame Yarl** arrives
with her cleaver to see who needs supporting; **Pike-Tom** arrives to see what can be pocketed.
This is the leaderless Nulb militia in miniature: a weather system made of people.

Mother Screng sends no invitation. The party reaches the good cell through their own need —
potions, the scarab, a discreet buyer, or a referral from the Velkori.

### Information discipline — let the town update in layers

| What the party says publicly | What changes before nightfall |
|---|---|
| "We survived Tolub's trap." | Tolub is embarrassed; Skole is delighted; no Temple secret is lost. |
| "The elemental war is over." | Rentsch and Wat start seeking new patrons; Selentis asks who won. |
| "The four prophets are dead." | Every old agent panics; Lucius receives the news before the party leaves town. |
| "We command the upper Temple." | Nulb's stale map disappears. The party loses its best information advantage. |
| Names of Barkinar, Hedrack, or Greater Temple routes | Selentis treats them as insiders and begins a full operational debrief disguised as hospitality. |

Do not call for one giant Deception check. Let each disclosure have its own audience and
consequence.

# SCENE 4 — Nulb's Rooms (modular; follow the party's order)

The party can visit all four rooms, skip one, or return to one twice. The room they choose first
determines who gets the cleanest read on them.

## 4A — The Velkori Fire (belonging before business)

### Setup

- **Where:** the Rhennee camp by the river.
- **Who:** Eryk, Serena, Vethra if her health permits, and the ordinary life of the clan.
- **Tone:** warm, loud, fed. This is the one scene where nobody begins by testing them.

Eryk embraces them as family, then asks the question he has been avoiding: can they do anything
about the people forcing his caravans into road ambushes? He does not know the whole chain, but he
knows demands arrive through Selentis's orbit and that refusing costs wheels, animals, or people.

What Eryk can give them:

- Selentis's runners have become more active, not less, despite the supposed faction war.
- Tolub is still boasting; whether his ship is in port is an Open Decision below.
- **Captain Garroth “Black-Plate”** has been pressing Rhennee wagons into short-haul work for the
  Broken Tower company. Eryk distinguishes Garroth’s men from **Captain Tarn the Pale’s** Upper
  Works band: same trade, different captains, and they compete over plunder.
- The best honest potions in town come from Mother Screng.
- The best smith who does not ask foolish questions is Otis.
- A Rhennee wagon or barge can hide a person or object leaving Nulb, but not an army.

> **Eryk:** *"You saved my sister and became family. Family means I feed you before I ask for the
> impossible. Sit down. The impossible will still be there after stew."*

Do not make Vethra perform another costly reading. If asked, she refuses or says the last cards
are still speaking. The useful connection is already on the table: the Chain is the supply chain,
the Betrayer has not yet shown his face, and the crimson barge is Vesrek's slaver ship.

### Branch — they tell Eryk everything

He believes them, but the camp is not a secure briefing room. He moves the conversation into his
wagon and assigns two adults to keep people away. The party gains trust but loses time; if they
spoke first by the fire, one sentence becomes rumor by evening.

### Branch — they refuse his coercion problem

He remains family. The warmth dims, and the Rhennee will still shelter them, but they will not
risk a barge or courier on Temple business until the party addresses the debt being extracted
from them.

### NPC notes

- **Eryk Velkori** — expressive, loyal, frightened of Selentis for concrete reasons. Wants his
  family out of forced bandit work. Uses hospitality to make hard conversations survivable.
- **Vethra Velkori** — exhausted seer, not a vending machine for prophecy. Wants the party to act
  on the reading already purchased with her health.
- **Serena Velkori** — living proof the party keep promises. Let her presence do more than another
  exposition speech.

## 4B — The Orphans of Dead Prophets (the tavern map)

### Setup

Run this at the Waterside Hostel, Boatman's Tavern, or across both in short cuts. The scene is not
about another tavern brawl. It is about watching men discover that their chains of command may no
longer exist—and noticing that the armed people in the room have chains of command of their own.

Use only the NPCs the party seeks:

- **Dick Rentsch** still expects direction from Romag. He wants to know who signs orders now and
  whether his recruiting operation still has value.
- **Wat** is canonically an Alrrem/Fire agent, though the party currently reads him as Water. His
  principal is dead. He wants safe passage, employment, or a new lie convincing enough to survive.
- **Skole** already chose the party once. He wants Tolub's leverage broken and will give them a
  private room, dock gossip, and the most recent version of Tolub's schedule.
- **Tolub** wants to preserve the fiction that he killed them. If present, the sight of them is a
  public detonation.

Then populate the room from the monster hierarchy:

- At the **Waterside**, **Captain Vesk “Cross-Bow”** sits with five to eight mercenaries. Serjeant
  Holl is present only if the company is large tonight; Spear-Mara watches the door. They take
  work through the Hostel but are loyal to their contract, not to Rentsch’s religion.
- At the **Boatman’s**, use two or three River Wolves—Mate Yarrin with the hook hand, Bosun Greel
  with the serpent tattoo, or Hessa, the most dangerous hand aboard. They look toward Tolub or
  Grud before committing themselves. They are crew, not tavern scenery.
- **Grud Squinteye** is a HARD second command, not a comic lieutenant. If Tolub is away, Grud can
  keep the River Wolves coherent and make decisions in his name.

> **Skole, setting down four cups:** *"Dead people drink free. Tolub says you're dead, so send him
> the bill."*

> **Rentsch, before he understands:** *"If Romag wants a new report, he can send a new runner."*

Let that sentence sit. The party controls when his world ends.

### The employment market inside the faction scene

Captain Vesk can be hired separately from Rentsch. His immediate questions are practical:

1. Who pays the first tenday in advance?
2. Is the work guarding, raiding, or dying in a hole?
3. Does Dren Halveth know the offer exists?

Hiring him gives Dren extra surface reach but creates an internal mercenary rivalry. Failing to
hire him does not make him hostile; it leaves him available to Selentis, Garroth, or the next
person with clean coin.

### Branch — they reveal the new order privately

Rentsch offers his operation to the party. Wat offers information on Tolub to avoid being handed
to Rentsch. Skole distrusts both but becomes more useful. The party gains assets and also creates
three people who now know the truth.

### Branch — they leave everyone on the old map

They collect excellent intelligence. The cost is that none of these men can act reliably on the
party's behalf. Rentsch may send a report to a dead Romag; Selentis will intercept it.

### Branch — Tolub is present and the party attacks

Let it happen. The grudge is earned. The material cost is the session clock and the town's
ignorance: a public fight announces the party's survival and gives Selentis legal-looking grounds
to intervene. It also activates a **HARD-led pirate faction**: up to ten River Wolves may be in
port, while the rest guard the ship or sail. Killing Tolub without neutralizing Grud transfers the
war; it does not dissolve the crew. If keeping the Nulb coalition scene is the priority, have the
**Iron Dagger** visible downriver and due back at dawn rather than at the dock tonight.

### Branch — they recruit below the bosses

This works on nominal hierarchies and fails differently on hard ones. Vesk’s mercenaries or
Garroth’s lieutenants will entertain a better contract. A River Wolf may sell information, but
trying to buy the whole crew out from under Tolub or Grud gets reported upward unless the party
first demonstrates that the hard boss cannot protect them.

### Failure is comedic and material

A failed attempt to intimidate the whole tavern does not start combat automatically. The room
cheers because everyone assumes it is a performance, Skole charges an entertainment fee, and by
morning the party's exact costume and claims have become a popular, badly rhymed dockside song.
They lose secrecy and gain an unhelpful reputation.

### Monster-faction notes

- **Captain Vesk “Cross-Bow”** — a contractor with a bolt scar through one earlobe. Wants the next
  tenday paid and a job his people can survive. Tic: prices danger aloud while other people talk.
- **Grud Squinteye** — Tolub’s command continuity. Wants the River Wolves feared even when their
  captain is absent. Tic: looks to the crew before answering, ensuring they heard the answer too.
- **Hessa** — the deadliest ordinary River Wolf. Wants to know whether the party can actually
  break Tolub’s protection or only embarrass him. She is the crew’s temperature, not a secret
  rebel waiting to defect.
- **Clogg, Dame Yarl, and Pike-Tom** — three faces of a bossless militia: alarm, appetite, and
  opportunism. None can promise what the whole crowd will do.

## 4C — Madame Selentis's Courtesy (the town as an intelligence service)

### Setup

- **Where:** Old Mayor's Manor after dark.
- **Who:** Madame Selentis, Sir Torvel Hawkes standing silent, a clerk with no name worth learning.
- **Visible security:** enough to show control, not enough to imply an ambush.

Selentis does not ask whether the party won. She asks what kind of order replaces the war.

> **Selentis:** *"Nulb can survive four temples fighting. It cannot invoice uncertainty. Who owns
> the road now?"*

Her goals tonight:

1. Confirm that the party survived.
2. Learn which old faction agents remain useful.
3. Discover whether the party recognizes a Greater Temple logistics network when standing inside
   one.
4. Offer a fence and supply line so attractive that refusal itself becomes information.

Her offer is real: clean coin for loot, food and equipment delivered to the Temple, labor hired
without questions. The price is not a villain surcharge. The price is a perfect inventory of the
party's wealth, consumption, personnel, and route.

Her monster-facing power is **coordination**, not personal ownership. She can:

- Have Clogg ring up a leaderless militia by promising lawful seizure and salvage.
- Put clean coin in front of Captain Vesk’s contract mercenaries.
- Send work separately to Garroth’s Broken Tower company and Tarn’s Upper Works band, ensuring
  neither knows the whole job.
- Exchange information with the River Wolves while leaving Tolub and Grud enough independence to
  believe they are partners.
- Use the giant ravens as visible sentries and human clerks as the actual record-keepers.

This is why attacking Selentis does not make “her army” roll initiative. It makes several distinct
groups decide whether fear, profit, or freedom matters most.

> **GM-only:** Selentis is Hedrack's logistics chief in Nulb, installed and protected through
> Lucius. She does not name either man tonight unless the party already does. She reports facts,
> not theories.

### Branch — they accept

They solve the food, payroll, and fencing problems at a fair price. On the return journey, Hedrack
knows exactly what they bought and sold. That is not punishment; it is what they purchased along
with the service.

### Branch — they refuse gracefully

Selentis respects disciplined independence and keeps the door open. She still records their
route, companions, and visible equipment.

### Branch — they threaten or expose her

She does not order a suicidal fight against four level-9 adventurers in her own drawing room. She
ends the meeting, rings once for the door, and lets Nulb become expensive: no clean fence, higher
food prices, vanished messengers, stable space suddenly unavailable. Failure costs logistics and
time.

### NPC notes

- **Madame Selentis / Lady Nysera Krivaltis** — patient, exact, never spends violence when an
  invoice will work. Wants the chain restored and her control preserved. Tic: turns threats into
  administrative vocabulary.
- **Sir Torvel Hawkes** — devotion made visible. Says almost nothing. Wants nobody to make Nysera
  choose between dignity and survival.

## 4D — Mother Screng's Back Room (the forces of Good)

This is the payload. Do not run it as a faction briefing from the first sentence. The party must
first encounter the cover, then the test, then the people beneath it.

### Beat 1 — Murfles tests the customers

The herb shop is cheap, clean, and busy in a town that is none of those things. Murfles, playing
Hruda, asks questions that are slightly too competent:

- What caused the wounds — acid, fire, rot, or poison?
- Where they expect to use the potions.
- Whether the black scarab was worn against skin.
- Why an Earth cleric needs a Cuthbertine healing compound.

> **Murfles, not looking up:** *"Acid, then. Not fire. Different salve. Most people learn that
> after it matters."*

She never asks who they are. She is confirming events, not identities.

### Beat 2 — Y'dey recognizes Calmer

If Calmer speaks, Mother Screng comes out and watches him one beat too long. While wrapping a jar,
she offers the deniable half of a Cuthbertine call-and-response:

> *"Cudgel and truth, then, and the road be short."*

If Calmer answers by reflex, she sets the jar down. Murfles locks the front door.

> **Y'dey:** *"You are Calmer of the Earth Temple, they say. I am Mother Screng of the herb shop,
> they say. Both true. Both also incomplete."*

Then stop. Do not explain the moment over it.

If Calmer is GM-run rather than player-run, do **not** make your own character the gate. Let
Zinnia notice the test, Sequoia remember the missing Canoness, or Murfles decide the party's record
is enough. Calmer's presence adds warmth; it must not turn the reveal into the GM talking to
himself.

### Beat 3 — Calmer's faith is not waved clean

Y'dey knows what long cover does to people. She validates the mission, not every choice made in
its name.

If Calmer admits the minotaur/prisoner proposal:

> **Y'dey:** *"Cover is a thing you wear. It is not a thing you feed. Decide whether you meant it
> before the Temple decides for you."*

If he speaks about Terjon abandoning him:

> **Y'dey:** *"Terjon kept my name out of mouths that would have killed me. He also left you alone
> too long. Both can be true. We will settle the Church's accounts when people are no longer dying
> under this hill."*

She offers recognition, confession, and a way back — not automatic absolution. This is the first
time Calmer has stood before a senior Cuthbertine who understands the actual operation.

### Beat 4 — Otis arrives after dark

Otis does not emerge from the pantry. Murfles fetches him only after the party has passed the
first test. He enters by the rear with smith's soot still on him, establishing that the good cell
has compartments and ordinary covers.

The three offers are distinct:

| Ally | Immediate offer | First ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Y'dey** | Healing, church authentication, a covert route toward Veluna, possible custody of the Chest of Rao | Tell her enough about the Temple to judge whether the party truly controls access |
| **Murfles** | Field observation, courier work, a dead-drop, one mobile ally inside Nulb | Never walk directly from a sensitive meeting to her shop again |
| **Otis** | Temple-metal identification, Gnarley Ranger paths, extraction planning, a safe smithy cache | No open call for troops until there is a target worth exposing the network for |

Otis can identify the TZGY scarab as a **command key of Greater Temple workmanship**, not a
trinket. He does not know every mechanism it controls. Do not turn him into an encyclopedia.

> **Otis:** *"This opens something built by men who expected the keyholder to be obeyed. I can
> tell you that without putting it on. I recommend we keep it that way."*

He can also correct one dangerous visual shorthand. If shown a rubbing, insignia, or description
of the four bugbears the party taxed:

> **Otis:** *"Grey cloth, bells, wind-scratches—that is Kelno’s kennel. Black plate with no
> element on it is central command. Those are not converts. They are inspectors."*

This is valuable good-cell intelligence because it changes the party's internal map without
requiring Y'dey to know dungeon room numbers. Otis understands equipment, organization, and what
wear patterns say about patrol routes. He can sketch three actionable distinctions:

- **Greywind Devotees:** a small personal Air faction that may collapse or bargain if Kelno falls.
- **Blackplate Wardens:** Hedrack-loyal observers who record rather than evangelize.
- **Moathouse Raiders:** Feldrin’s plunder-driven humans, likely to defect or sell routes because
  their boss is only nominally above his veterans.

### Beat 5 — what Y'dey knows about the sleeper

Once Y'dey surfaces, she gives the name Falrinth withheld. **This is the indispensable
`the-sleeping-prince.md` handoff; do not gate it on Falrinth having appeared, a full confession,
or a second visit.** Falrinth changes whether the party recognizes the competing-handler pattern.
Calmer changes the warmth and intimacy of Y'dey's reveal. Neither changes whether the Prince
vector lands.

> **Y'dey:** *"There is a man beneath that Temple. Not a relic. Not a rumor. Prince Thrommel —
> Grand Marshall of Furyondy, Provost of Veluna, betrothed of her Ladyship Jolene. The kingdoms
> buried him as dead ten years ago. He is not dead.*
>
> *For ten years I have sat in this shop because I could never get in. You hold the upper halls
> now. You can walk where the Church of St. Cuthbert cannot. Bring him out alive and you do not
> merely wound the Temple. You give Furyondy and Veluna a reason to remember exactly what was
> taken from them. That is why we are speaking."*

She knows he is hidden by the Temple's foulest art and warns them not to trust their eyes. How
much she knows about the vampire illusion remains an Open Decision. She may also name Fragarach:
*"Find the man, find the sword."*

> **GM-only:** Y'dey wants the Prince rescued, full stop. Falrinth wants the resulting coalition
> war. Lucius and Hedrack want to know whether the party has discovered the hostage. Three
> handlers now point at the same room for incompatible reasons.

### The alliance terms

Y'dey does **not** ask the party to abandon their cover tonight. She asks them to go back.

> **Y'dey:** *"If you vanish after entering this shop, they learn exactly where your courage came
> from. Go back. Attend whatever audience success has bought you. Learn what they want. Then come
> to us by a route Murfles gives you, not by the front door."*

This is the in-fiction reason Hedrack comes after Nulb rather than replacing it.

### Branch — the party gives full trust

The good cell reciprocates, but not with an army. They receive a dead-drop, an emergency healing
route, Otis's smith mark for Ranger recognition, and a serious discussion of moving the Chest of
Rao. Y'dey gives them her best extraction intelligence about Thrommel.

### Branch — the party remains cautious

The **Thrommel truth still lands**, but colder and in fewer words. Y'dey may remain behind the
Mother Screng manner while Murfles passes parts of the intelligence. What they withhold is access:
no safehouse, no Ranger route, no offer to move the Chest, and no full extraction plan. Murfles
gives them one test instead: bring back a fact from Hedrack's audience that Nulb's Temple network
could not have supplied. Trust can grow on the second visit.

### Branch — the test goes badly

If the party simply leaves, let them leave; Y'dey does not chase them down the street. If they
stay but remain hostile or fail Murfles's social test, suspicion does **not** delete the Sleeping
Prince vector. Y'dey proves enough of her identity to name Thrommel and state that he lives, then
ends the conversation. The material loss is operational support: no safehouse, courier, Ranger
route, or detailed extraction help until they establish trust.

### Failure is comedic and material

If the party attracts attention leaving, Otis turns the meeting's cover into an actual bulk order
and makes them carry two loud sacks of horseshoes through town. They keep the alliance but lose
subtlety, speed, and dignity; every watcher records a boring commercial errand.

### NPC notes

- **Y'dey / Mother Screng** — tired, faithful, operationally patient. Wants Thrommel alive and the
  party still capable of reaching him. Her Cuthbertine plain speech leaks through the Screng mask.
- **Murfles / Hruda** — protective, sharp, mobile. Wants evidence before intimacy. Never lets a
  stranger stand between Y'dey and an exit.
- **Otis** — evaluates before equipping. Wants a target worth spending the Gnarley Rangers' cover
  on. Talks about people the way a smith talks about metal: stress, temper, hidden cracks.

# SCENE 5 — What They Carry Back

Before leaving Nulb, ask the players to state four things. This prevents the Hedrack scene from
turning into a memory quiz:

1. **What did they buy, sell, or visibly carry?**
2. **Who saw them?**
3. **What is their innocent explanation for each stop?**
4. **What single fact are they absolutely not telling Hedrack?**

Recommended innocent account:

- Velkori camp: family obligation and lodging.
- Waterside/Boatman's: old business with Tolub and Dren's recruiters.
- Selentis: supplies and fencing.
- Herb shop: acid salve, healing potions, and Calmer's wounded pride.
- Otis: identify or repair Temple metalwork.

All of these are true. That is why they are good cover.

### What the good cell sends with them

Keep it small and real:

- Potions or salves they actually purchased.
- A dead-drop protocol from Murfles.
- Otis's unobtrusive smith mark or spoken Ranger recognition phrase.
- Y'dey's instruction to return normally and preserve their access.
- Once Y'dey surfaces, the name **Prince Thrommel** and the warning, *"Do not trust your eyes."*
  Trust determines how much support accompanies the truth, not whether she tells it.

Do not send Y'dey, Otis, or a squad of Rangers into the Temple. The party's unique value is that
they can walk in without a battle.

# SCENE 6 — The Summons Waited for Them

### Setup

- **Where:** the party's upper-Temple headquarters.
- **When:** after they physically return from Nulb. Not by messenger on the road; not in Selentis's
  manor; not while Y'dey is speaking.
- **Who:** Dren, then a Greater Temple escort or sealed message.

Dren gives the operational report first. Pay off their departure orders: how much meat remains,
whether another body disappeared, whether the Chest stayed put, and what the minotaur has decided
"reception officer" means.

Also pay off the monster hierarchy. **Vex the Hall-Warden** and **Hush-Brek** came through in black
plate, checked the doors, counted Dren’s people, and wrote down the party’s absence. They did not
ask Kelno for permission and did not acknowledge Greywind rank.

> **Dren:** *"The black-plate pair counted everything except themselves. The Air lot hid the
> kitchen silver until they left. So: definitely different management."*

If the party gave good orders, Dren handled this as a routine inspection. If they left no chain
of authority, Vex has temporarily countersigned the food stores and the party returns to discover
that Hedrack’s faction acquired administrative access without drawing a weapon.

Only then:

> **Dren:** *"One more thing. A man came up from below. He did not give me his name because he
> assumed I knew it. Hedrack is waiting for you."*

No attack, no drumroll, no chance to pretend this is a casual invitation. Let the name do the
work.

### Branch — they delay to prepare

Reasonable. Hedrack permits a short rest, bathing, changed clothes, and private discussion. That
permission is part of his confidence. A delay beyond the same day brings Deggum or an escort and
costs the party the privilege of choosing how they enter.

### Branch — they refuse

Hedrack does not throw troops into the party's fortified upper levels. Lucius arrives with wine
and an offer to "clear up the misunderstanding." Dren's supplies begin requiring Greater Temple
countersignatures. Their autonomy shrinks materially while the audience remains open.

### Branch — they abandon the Temple instead

Honor it. Dren must choose whether to evacuate; the minotaur's bargain becomes a real problem;
the good cell becomes their new operational base. Hedrack's meeting turns into an approaching
threat rather than a social scene. This is a campaign pivot, not failure.

# SCENE 7 — Hedrack's Congratulations

### Setup

- **Where:** Hedrack's quarters, area 417, in the Greater Temple.
- **Who:** Hedrack; two servants who behave as though fear is etiquette. Barkinar and Lucius are
  conspicuously absent. Deggum waits outside unless the party forced an escorted entrance.
- **What the room says:** brass braziers, fireplace, sablewood furniture, leadership and battle
  books that look less used than the room's rugs, and a painting of the Greater Temple's united
  forces with Hedrack unmistakably at the center. Two badly hand-painted ceramic plates sit on
  the mantel among objects worth fortunes.
- **What not to touch:** the ebony horned-skull carving on the mantel can draw Iuz's attention.
  Do not volunteer its function; describe it clearly if anyone approaches it.

### The walk in is part of the audience

Do not fill the approach with generic guards. Let the hierarchy escort them:

- **High-Guard Sniff**, a troll whose regrown ear sits crooked, watches the corridor outside the
  commanders’ wing. At night, his louder twin **Night-Maw** is there too.
- A black-plated bugbear checks the escort’s mark and records the party’s names. If Vex came
  upstairs during their absence, he already has them written down.
- An ettin sentry argues between its two heads over which title to use for Calmer, then falls
  instantly silent when Hedrack’s inner door opens.

The monsters do not behave like charmed pets. The troll looks bored, the bugbear officious, the
ettin irritated. What changes around Hedrack is not personality but **permission**.

Hedrack is in bejeweled silks, not armor. That is not vulnerability. It is his way of showing that
he does not expect to need protection from them.

> **GM-only:** This is their first audience with the actual architect. Hedrack presents the Iuz
> cover story: disciplined Greater Temple commander, cultivating useful outsiders. Secretly he is
> assessing potential Divine Proxies for the Zuggtmoy plan. He needs them alive, capable, and
> convinced they retain independent judgment.
>
> The monster hierarchy adds a useful contradiction: Hedrack is a HARD authority over his
> Blackplate bugbears but only a narrow personal step above his elite trolls and hill giants. His
> real weapon is the institution that makes near-peers wait outside his door. If that institution
> visibly cracks, some of those creatures will renegotiate rather than die for him.

## Hedrack's goals in the room

1. Reward their stabilization of the upper Temple sincerely.
2. Learn what Nulb changed without revealing the depth of his surface network.
3. Test whether Calmer's faith, the Chest of Rao, or a new outside alliance can be used as
   leverage.
4. Make the party answerable to him without making them feel like ordinary subordinates.
5. Put Lucius in their future as his authorized intermediary.
6. Learn whether they understand the difference between Kelno’s Greywind followers and his own
   Blackplate Wardens.
7. Say nothing about Zuggtmoy's release, ascension, the Orb-restoration lie, or Thrommel.

## Beat 1 — competence recognized

He does not make them kneel unless they choose to. He studies them beneath the painting, then:

> **Hedrack:** *"Four temples spent years failing to govern four corridors. Barkinar gave you a
> week. You required less."*

He names concrete successes: Dren installed, Fire and Water pacified, trolls removed, Kelno
contained or allied, the upper levels supplying reports again. He treats the necromancer as an
unfinished professional problem, not a mark of failure.

Then he corrects the twenty-gold misunderstanding without anger:

> **Hedrack:** *"Vex reports that four of my Wardens paid an Earth toll in a corridor I own. He
> thought this humiliating. I thought it an inexpensive way to learn whether you look at armor or
> allegiance."*

If the party already identified the Wardens through Dren or Otis, they can answer cleanly and win
real respect. If not, the correction establishes that Hedrack has had eyes in their territory the
entire time.

> **Hedrack:** *"Competence is rare. Loyalty is common. I have always preferred the rarer
> commodity."*

This is flattering and diagnostic. He wants them to believe he values independent thought.

## Beat 2 — the Nulb report

Hedrack asks three questions and allows silence after each:

1. *"Which part of Nulb still believes it commands this Temple?"*
2. *"Whom did you permit to keep believing it?"*
3. *"What did you bring back that cannot be entered in a quartermaster's ledger?"*

The first two reward an honest operational report. The third is a trap without one correct
answer. Intelligence, alliances, enemies, doubt, and nothing are all revealing.

### What Hedrack knows

Scale his knowledge to what the party exposed; do not make him omniscient.

| Party conduct in Nulb | Hedrack knows at the start of the audience |
|---|---|
| Entered quietly, used Velkori cover, avoided Selentis | They went to Nulb and returned; little else. |
| Visited public taverns or fought Tolub | Exact public claims, clothing, companions, and visible magic. |
| Met Selentis | Everything bought, sold, promised, and openly discussed in the manor. |
| Visited the herb shop as ordinary customers | The stop occurred. Potions and acid treatment make the reason plausible. He does not know the back-room conversation. |
| Walked directly from the herb shop to Otis or named Y'dey publicly | The good cell is in danger; Hedrack knows there is an unexplained connection worth watching. |

If they conceal Y'dey successfully, let them succeed. Hedrack's power comes from good networks,
not GM omniscience.

## Beat 3 — the Chest of Rao

If the Chest remains in party holdings, Hedrack asks where it is. He does not demand it tonight.
His restraint is the test.

> **Hedrack:** *"A holy box has inspired more bad decisions in this Temple than any demon. I am
> interested in your decision, not your haste. When you decide what it is worth, tell me what you
> paid."*

> **GM-only:** Hedrack does not need the Chest. Handing it over records compliance; hiding it gives
> him leverage; substituting a fake identifies useful trickery. The house benefits either way.

If Y'dey offered to move it to Veluna, this question should feel like the walls closing around a
choice the party made less than a day ago.

## Beat 4 — promotion as a leash

Hedrack gives no grand rank. He removes a layer of hierarchy.

> **Hedrack:** *"Barkinar will continue to command troops. You will continue to command results.
> When Lucius Graeme brings you a matter in my name, hear him as you would hear me. Agree when he
> is right. Correct him when he is not. I have no use for echoes."*

This authorizes Lucius's later manipulation arcs while making obedience sound like intellectual
freedom.

If you want the next hook tonight, Hedrack adds only:

> *"The Lord Wizard Senshock has become an instability. Lucius will explain what can be known
> outside this room."*

Do not pitch the assassination here. Per `hedrack_strategy.md`, Lucius delivers Arc A and its
manufactured evidence.

## Beat 5 — the closing line

Hedrack dismisses them warmly:

> **Hedrack:** *"You have made yourselves indispensable. That is a dangerous thing to do in
> another man's house."*

Then, as they reach the door:

> *"Lucius will find you."*

If Falrinth named Lucius before Nulb, this is the session's closing barb.

### Branch — they report Falrinth

Hedrack appears mildly amused and asks exactly what Falrinth said, especially about Lucius, the
scarab, and anything below. He thanks them. Falrinth will not be where the Temple looks. Lucius's
approach accelerates and becomes more careful.

### Branch — they report Y'dey, Murfles, or Otis

Hedrack does not lunge at the name. He asks the party to preserve the contact and report what the
cell knows. This is worse: the good allies remain alive but are now bait unless warned. If the
party realizes the mistake, they have a race back to Nulb.

### Branch — Hedrack catches a consequential lie

No initiative. No lightning. He compliments the attempt and assigns a "liaison from Deggum's
command" to the upper Temple. **Vex the Hall-Warden** arrives instead of a human clerk. Vex
installs himself at the pink desk, logs every visitor in chalk, countersigns supply orders, and
makes private operations substantially harder. Failure is funny at first and material every day
afterward: the party has acquired a bugbear compliance department that reports directly below.

### Branch — they openly refuse his authority

Hedrack does not need a confession of loyalty tonight. He asks whether they are resigning the
upper Temple or negotiating their terms. A negotiation preserves the campaign's political game;
resignation begins the collapse of their cover. He respects clarity more than performative
submission.

### Branch — they attack

The quarters are not a fair boss room. Hedrack can seal exits with his instant fortress, summon
guards, and potentially draw Iuz's attention through the ebony skull. If they choose violence,
the objective becomes escape with their secrets and allies intact, not grinding through a
surprise stat block. Make the loss political and positional: cover, access, Dren's garrison, and
the chance to reach Thrommel unseen.

### NPC notes

- **Hedrack** — absolute confidence, theatrical restraint, genuine admiration for effectiveness.
  Wants the party alive and self-convinced. Tic: asks a question, then lets silence force the
  answer. Would never explain the real reason he needs them.
- **Vex the Hall-Warden** — Blackplate institutional memory in armor. Wants every anomaly entered
  under the right name. Tic: chalk tally before conversation. Would never accept an elemental
  priest’s authority without a central writ.
- **High-Guard Sniff** — elite troll whose obedience is professional rather than devotional.
  Wants the easy, prestigious post outside Hedrack’s room to remain easy. Stops complaining when
  Hedrack appears; does not pretend to love him.
- **The servants** — fear performed as perfect etiquette. Their stillness demonstrates his power
  more effectively than armed guards.

## Continuity Gate — Hedrack Does Not Replace the Third Handler

`the-sleeping-prince.md` depends on a precise escalation:

> **Falrinth makes them curious → Y'dey makes them care → Lucius makes them afraid.**

The required Hedrack audience changes the calendar, not those functions. The revised sequence is:

> **Falrinth → Y'dey in Nulb → return → Hedrack → Lucius's sleeping-king probe.**

Hedrack authorizes Lucius and says, *"Lucius will find you."* Then, later that night or at the
next session's opening, run `the-sleeping-prince.md`, Scene 3. Lucius congratulates them, casually
mentions that Nulb fills people's heads with stories of *"buried treasures and sleeping kings,"*
and watches to see whether they react.

This is still the scene where the Thrommel path can become harder:

- **They stall or play dumb:** Lucius relaxes; their deep-level access remains usable.
- **They reveal Thrommel or Y'dey:** Lucius's warmth never drops, but he reports them to Hedrack;
  access tightens and the rescue becomes a race.
- **They feed him a different Nulb truth:** he leaves with something plausible, and they preserve
  both their cover and the Prince operation.

Do not let Hedrack conduct this specific probe during his audience. Hedrack assesses the party as
assets; Lucius tests whether they know about the Temple's crown-jewel hostage. Keeping those scenes
separate preserves the three-handler architecture.

## Anticipated Player Questions

- **"Why can we leave while the necromancer is active?"** → Dren can hold for days, the selective
  disappearances are slow, and the party needs food, coin, healing, expertise, and surface
  intelligence. Their absence costs clues, not the Temple.
- **"Can we bring the minotaur to Nulb?"** → Yes, but every stealth branch ends immediately. It
  also abandons the post it just agreed to guard. Leave the option open and let the consequences
  be obvious.
- **"Why should we return after finding real allies?"** → Because their inside access is the one
  capability the forces of Good cannot reproduce. Disappearing now tells Hedrack exactly when
  they turned. Y'dey explicitly recommends returning.
- **"Can Y'dey call an army?"** → Eventually, with proof and a target. An army now destroys the
  party's cover, drives the Greater Temple deeper, and may get Thrommel killed or moved.
- **"Can Otis identify TZGY completely?"** → He recognizes a Greater Temple command key and the
  danger of wearing it. Exact mechanisms require observation, a captured record, or someone like
  Senshock/Falrinth.
- **"Can the Rhennee move the Chest of Rao?"** → Yes, if the party accepts the risk to their
  adopted family. That is a moral choice, not free shipping.
- **"Can we kill Tolub?"** → Yes. If he is in port, it consumes time and updates all of Nulb. If
  he is at sea, they can plan the ambush without sacrificing the coalition scenes. Killing him
  does not automatically dissolve the River Wolves while Grud remains able to command.
- **"Can we hire the armed people at the Waterside?"** → Captain Vesk’s mercenaries, yes; they
  are contract-loyal and only nominally led. Hiring them may create friction with Dren and makes
  the party’s cash needs visible.
- **"Were those four bugbears actually Kelno’s?"** → The hierarchy says no: area 231’s black plate
  and lack of elemental sigil mark Hedrack’s Blackplate Wardens. Kelno’s Greywind Devotees wear
  grey and use Air devotional clutter. The party made an understandable in-character misread.
- **"Do all the monsters obey Hedrack absolutely?"** → No. Blackplate Wardens are directly bound
  to central command; elite trolls and hill giants are near-peers held by status, fear, pay, and
  structure. Breaking the structure creates bargains and succession, not instant friendship.
- **"Does Selentis know Hedrack?"** → The party does not know. She never volunteers the name.
  GM-only: she is Hedrack's logistics chief through Lucius.
- **"Does Hedrack know we met Y'dey?"** → Only if the party's conduct exposed more than an herb
  purchase. He may know where they went; he does not automatically know what was said.
- **"Can we refuse Hedrack's meeting?"** → Yes. Their supplies acquire oversight, their access
  narrows, and Lucius comes to them. No invisible wall forces the audience.
- **"Is Hedrack asking us to kill Senshock?"** → Not yet. He names an instability at most. Lucius
  will make the actual pitch later.
- **"What about the sleeper?"** → Falrinth hints; Y'dey names Prince Thrommel. Hedrack never
  mentions him unless the party makes the catastrophic choice to do so first.

## Stage Directions (timing and silence)

- Start with Dren's mundane list, not another threat. The comedy of ownership is the bridge out.
- Falrinth's sleeper line arrives as the image is already fading. No follow-up questions.
- On entering Nulb, let the party enjoy knowing everyone else's map is obsolete. Do not correct
  NPCs for them.
- Let Watcher-Grey circle before any human messenger arrives. Nulb’s monster factions should feel
  embedded in the town’s information system, not rolled from a random table.
- In each armed group, show one obedience tell before anyone reaches for a weapon: a glance to
  Grud, a price quoted by Vesk, or a chalk tally from a Blackplate Warden.
- Give the Velkori ten real minutes of warmth before asking for anything.
- Rentsch's *"Romag can send a runner"* deserves a beat. The party chooses when to tell him he is
  waiting on a corpse.
- Selentis never threatens. The more politely she prices consequences, the more powerful she
  feels.
- Murfles never asks who the party are. She asks what happened.
- After Y'dey says, *"Both true. Both also incomplete,"* stop talking.
- Otis comes through the rear only after trust. The cell should feel compartmentalized, not
  conveniently assembled.
- Do not have Hedrack's summons arrive in Nulb. It is waiting only after they return.
- In area 417, describe Hedrack's enormous portrait before Hedrack himself speaks. Let the party
  notice the two badly painted plates without explaining them.
- On the walk to area 417, let the troll complain and the ettin argue. Their abrupt silence when
  Hedrack’s door opens is the demonstration of his hierarchy.
- Hedrack pauses after every diagnostic question. Do not rescue the players from silence.
- If they lie successfully, move on. Do not hint that a secret die roll saved them.
- End on *"Lucius will find you"* if at all possible.

## Pacing by Table Clock

- **First hour:** close Temple business, Falrinth, departure, arrival.
- **Second hour:** Velkori plus one of taverns/Selentis.
- **Third hour:** herb shop, Y'dey, Otis, terms of alliance.
- **Final hour:** return, Dren's payoff, Hedrack audience.

If time compresses, cut the tavern and let Selentis's invitation remain unanswered. Do not cut the
