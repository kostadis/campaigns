# Nobody Told Nulb

Co-GM working doc. Not canon yet — staging for the session after Chapter 32 ("The Minotaur").

Party: the Obsidian Edge — **Calmer** (War Cleric of St. Cuthbert, undercover as Prophet of the
Earth Temple), **Zephyr** (tiefling assassin, holds Vurakhal's contract), **Zinnia** (elf monk,
he/him, the party's eyes), **Sequoia** (halfling rogue, Uncanny Dodge, Lucky). Level **9**.

**Where we are:** mid-tenday of the week Barkinar granted them, with a few days still on the
clock. **Barkinar's List is essentially done** — Dren formally installed, Fire Temple pacified,
Water Temple pacified, troll contract resolved by force. Two items remain: the **necromancer**
(unlocated) and the **Chest of Rao** (undecided). The party is deep in former Water/Air territory
on Level 2 and a minotaur has just stood up off its throne.

**What this session is actually for:** getting them out of the dungeon and into **Mother Screng's
herb shop in Nulb.** Everything else is the delivery system.

Companion docs: `the-sleeping-prince.md` (the arc this opens — Scene 1 fires here),
`nulb_allies.md` (Y'dey / Murfles / Otis, the payload), `nulb_network.md` (the town's operational
map), `falrinth_strategy.md` (the probe), `upper_temple_network.md` (who the party owns),
`phase3_endgame.md` (Barkinar's List), `tzgy_scarab_urgent.md` (the scarab).

## The Win Condition (state plainly to yourself)

This session lands if the party **chooses to leave the Temple and walk into Nulb**, and ends the
night in the herb shop with Murfles asking too-specific questions — ideally with Calmer in the
room and the disguise starting to slip.

That's it. That's the whole target. The minotaur, the sweep of Level 2, and Falrinth's midnight
visit exist to manufacture a reason to go to town that the players believe was *their idea.*

Secondary wins, in order of value:

1. **Falrinth's probe lands** — the TZGY scarab becomes a known-dangerous object, and the name
   **Lucius Graeme, golden eyes** goes on the record at the table.
2. **The party notices the fencing problem** — their wealth is in unsellable curios, and their
   only buyer is the man they've just been warned about.
3. **They learn Nulb still thinks the old Temple runs the Temple.** Nobody down there has been
   told. That ignorance is an asset and they should feel it.

> **GM-only:** You do not need Y'dey to fully unmask this session. Murfles testing them and the
> *first crack* in Mother Screng is a complete, satisfying close. The full reveal can be the cold
> open next time. Don't rush the best scene in the campaign to fit a session boundary.

## The Default Trajectory (what happens if the party does nothing)

- **The minotaur is a fight either way.** It's the one thing on this list that resolves itself.
- **Falrinth probes regardless.** He doesn't need their cooperation and he isn't waiting for an
  invitation. If they ignore it, he simply has more information than they do and they walk into
  Lucius's next visit blind.
- **Barkinar's clock keeps running, and the party is going to pass.** Dren installed, both temples
  pacified, trolls handled. Left alone, the check-in is a *commendation* — and a commendation is
  how the leash gets shorter. Only the necromancer and the Chest of Rao are still outstanding, and
  only one of those is a thing the party can point at and say "we handled it."
- **Nulb is never told.** Wat is still filing reports to a dead Water prophet. Tolub is still
  telling the docks he killed these people. Madame Selentis still thinks the Temple's four
  factions are at each other's throats. **Every day the party doesn't go to town, that ignorance
  is worth more — and it's also aging.** Eventually somebody walks up the road and looks.
- **Y'dey waits.** She has waited ten years. She will not come to them, will not send for them,
  will not make it easy. If the party never goes to town, this vector simply doesn't fire — which
  is a real outcome, not a failure. It just means you run it next session instead.
- **The thing in the acid pool keeps whispering.** It is undefeated, intelligent, and it plants
  suggestions. It has not stopped being in their basement.
- **The necromancer keeps working, and keeps selecting.** More Earth Temple dead get up; the Fire
  and Water dead lie where they fell. He is on Level 3 and he is not in a hurry. Nothing the party
  does on Level 2 will find him.

## The Clever Play (so you know when to nudge)

1. **Look at the minotaur before swinging.** It was *sitting on a throne* in a reception hall. A
   minotaur that sits is a minotaur that might be garrisoned, or bound, or waiting for someone.
   The party that opens with a question gets more than the party that opens with a crossbow.
2. **Take Falrinth's warning as intelligence, not a command.** Stop wearing the scarab. Do *not*
   report him to Barkinar yet. Do not light the candle yet either — verify first.
3. **Notice that verification means leaving.** Nobody inside the Temple can be asked about the
   scarab without telling them you have it. That's the pivot.
4. **Realise they can't fence their own loot.** Their liquid wealth is a fire opal necklace, a
   silver service, elven chain mail, six books of lore, a coral box of seed pearls, and a
   pink desk. Their one buyer is Lucius — and Lucius was just described to them as the most
   dangerous man in the building. **Going around their own quartermaster is the smart move and
   the reason to walk to Nulb.**
5. **Go to town without the regalia.** "Lord Master Calmer" is a title for the dungeon. In Nulb
   it's a death sentence and a broken cover.
6. **Buy potions.** Calmer's healing has been a running joke at the table for two sessions
   (Ch. 32: two casts, both embarrassing, open mockery). An herbalist is the obvious answer, and
   it's the party's own established grievance that walks them through Mother Screng's door.

# SCENE 1 — The Minotaur on the Throne (cold open)

### Setup
- **Where:** the reception chamber at the end of the long hallway — 40' × 50', murals of the
  Temple's former hospitality, battered furniture, scattered bones, a dry fountain.
- **When:** immediately. Sequoia has just picked the lock, walked in, studied the "statue," and
  realised it is breathing. Pick up on his realisation.
- **Who:** the minotaur, seated. The party, spread across a doorway.

> **Area number:** this room doesn't map cleanly onto a canonical Level 2 area (it's not 227, 231,
> or 232 — see Open Decisions). Treat it as yours. That's freeing: nothing in the module
> constrains what this thing is.

### Approach — give them the beat they're owed
End of last session was a held breath. Don't waste it on initiative. Read the tell first:

> "The dust on its shoulders is thick. The dust on its forearms is not. Something in this room
> has been moving, and slowly, and not for a long time — but not never."

Then let it open its eyes.

**The question the scene asks: is this a guard, a prisoner, or a tenant?** All three are playable
and all three are good. Recommended read below, but pick before the table sits down.

**Recommended — a garrison nobody stood down.** It was posted here. Its officer is dead (one of
four prophets, take your pick). It has not been relieved, has not been fed properly, and has
been sitting in a reception room for a war it was never called into. It is not stupid. It is
*bored, hungry, and technically still on duty.*

That makes Calmer's entire management shtick load-bearing: the party doesn't have to kill it.
They have to **issue it an order.** They are, after all, the new command.

> **Lift verbatim (slow, flat, enormous):** *"You are not the one who put me here. Are you the
> one who takes me off?"*

### Branch — the party attacks immediately
Fine, and likely. A CR-appropriate minotaur at level 9 is a solid fight but not a threat to four
level-9 operators with Vurakhal available. **Make the loss material, not mechanical:** they kill
the one thing on this level that could have told them what the black-armoured bugbears are
actually doing here. It had been watching them come and go for a week.

### Branch — the party talks to it
Best outcome. What it can give them:
- **Who has been walking these halls in black armour**, and that they aren't Air Temple.
- That **nobody has come down from Level 3 to relieve it in a very long time** — and that before
  the war, someone did, regularly.
- That it has heard **something moving at night** in the direction of the dead. (The necromancer.
  A loose thread, not a solution.)
- It wants: food, a post that means something, and to not be here.

### Branch — the party recruits it
Entirely in keeping with this campaign. They've collected a salamander, a water priest, a
mercenary company, and a sentient sword. **Let them.** The cost isn't mechanical, it's
managerial — Dren will have opinions, and a minotaur is a *conspicuous* retainer to walk past
Barkinar's ogre.

### NPC notes
- **The minotaur** — slow, literal, not stupid. Tic: answers the question actually asked, never
  the one implied. Wants: to be relieved of a post. Would never: beg.

# SCENE 2 — The Rest of Level 2 (modular, player-driven)

Offer these; don't push. Any of them can be skipped entirely. Their real job is to burn a
few hours of in-fiction time and hand the party two or three things they can't resolve without
leaving the building.

### 2a — The four bugbears, revisited
They took 20 gp off four bugbears last session and read them as Kelno's. **Canon area 231 holds
four bugbears in black armour — Greater Temple, deliberately aloof from all four elemental
factions.** If that's the room, the party shook down Barkinar's own men and filed it as Air Temple
expansion.

This is the best piece of dramatic irony currently on the board and it costs nothing to play:
- Nobody corrects them now.
- At Barkinar's check-in, he mentions — mildly, without heat — that some of his people were
  approached for a toll. He is *amused.* That's worse than angry.
- If they go back and look properly: black armour, no elemental sigil, spears meant for throwing,
  and a discipline the elemental bugbears never had.

> **GM-only:** decide before play whether they were Barkinar's or genuinely Kelno's (Open
> Decisions). Barkinar's is funnier and tightens the leash. Kelno's is a straightforward
> territorial problem and much less interesting.

### 2b — The Hall of Triumphal Evil (227) and the stair down
The big echoing hall of skulls and torn standards, black stone steps descending north. **The
party has been here** — this is where the self-opening door was, back before the war.

Two things they can learn if they look:
- The steps carry **antipathy runes**, the same craft as the sealed main entrance. *No creature of
  evil alignment can set foot on that stair.* The bronze doors at its base (area 340) are
  triple-chained and triple-barred with cold iron.
- The **normal door on the south wall by the prison room** opens on a dusty alcove and a 10'-wide
  stairway down to **area 328a — Dungeon Level Three.** That one has no runes on it.

> **GM-only — this is the road to the coffin, and it's worth letting them find it now.** Knowing
> the way down is a *loaded gun*, not a spent one. They can't use it yet (Barkinar's clock,
> and they have no reason to go), but when Falrinth says "a room below, on the level beneath your
> feet," they will already know exactly which stair he means. That's the moment the hook sets.
>
> The antipathy runes are also a quiet, devastating character beat for Calmer. If he sets foot on
> those steps and *nothing happens* — he's still good, whatever he's been calling himself — that's
> a gift. If he hesitates before trying, that's better. Don't call for a roll. Just describe the
> stair and let his player decide whether to test it.

### 2c — The Hall of Verdigris, still occupied
The acid pool is not solved. The thing in it is intelligent, plants *Suggestion*, and burned two
party members. Ritual texts imply Brother Eelrich knew and said nothing.

Offer, don't force:
- The party can go back at it with better tools (they now have a scroll of *true seeing*, potions
  of water breathing they correctly clocked as useless, and Vurakhal — who **will not enter
  standing water, under any order, per clause II.1 of his contract**).
- **The Vurakhal beat is the point.** They will ask him. He will decline. He will not apologise or
  explain beyond the terms. *"The terms are in the document. If you have questions about the
  terms, read the document again."* Flat, no emotion. The party may find that more unsettling
  than defiance — which is exactly right.
- If they lean on Eelrich about what he knew: he folds, partially, and gives up something
  genuinely useful about the pool while conspicuously not explaining why he didn't mention it.

### 2d — The necromancer (identified, but not found)

> **GM-only — who he is. The party has already been told, twice, and dismissed it as gossip.**
>
> He is **Romag's predecessor as Prophet of the Earth Temple.** Romag told them about him himself,
> resentfully, on two separate occasions:
>
> > *"...Romag is a new and inexperienced leader trying to prove himself, known for micromanaging
> > and lacking the cleverness of his predecessor, who had been **promoted to the deep temple**."*
> >
> > *"Ever since his predecessor had gone to work for the Deep Temple, **the undead refused to work
> > for him**."*
>
> Note the word Romag used: *promoted.* Not defected, not fled — **promoted.** He went downstairs
> because the Greater Temple wanted him, and Romag was the consolation prize the Earth Temple got
> instead.
>
> **This answers the one detail nothing else does: why it is always Earth Temple corpses that rise.**
> The undead never obeyed the Earth Temple. They obeyed *him.* He kept that when he went down, and
> the war just handed him a few hundred bodies he already has the keys to. Romag spent his whole
> tenure complaining about a problem he never understood: the ghouls and harpies who "refused to
> bow to him" weren't rebellious. They were **already employed.**
>
> And the party cleared those creatures out for Romag. They have been working against this man for
> months without knowing his name.

**What he is for the campaign:** the first face of the Deep Temple, arriving before Hedrack and
before Senshock. He is what "promoted downstairs" actually looks like — and the party currently
holds the job he used to have, staffed by people he outranks, in territory he knows better than
they do.

**What he wants** (pick one before play, or leave it open):
- **His old house back**, and he is unimpressed by the amateurs squatting in it.
- **Nothing from them at all** — he is quietly harvesting a battlefield and they are incidental,
  which is far more insulting.
- **To recruit them**, the way he was recruited. He got promoted for being clever. He may
  recognise cleverness.

### What they actually get this session — a smell, not a solution
**Do not resolve this.** He is not findable on Level 2, because he isn't on Level 2. Give them
data points and let it itch:

- A body from the war that should be four days cold and isn't.
- Drag marks toward a door nobody has opened — heading **down**.
- One of Dren's soldiers who "saw someone in the dark who wasn't one of ours" and can't say more.
- **The good one:** Earth Temple dead are missing. Fire and Water dead, in the same corridors,
  rotting where they fell, are untouched. Whoever is doing this is *selecting.*
- If they killed the minotaur, they don't get its testimony about night movement. Their loss.

> **Dren, if they ask him** (he was there for none of it, but his men talk): *"Ours are going.
> Theirs aren't. I've had two men say it and I told them both to shut up because I didn't want it
> spreading." (beat) "It's spreading."*

**If a player connects it to Romag's complaint** — that the undead stopped obeying after his
predecessor left — give it to them immediately and without a roll. It's a genuinely clever catch
built entirely from things they were told at the table, and rewarding it is worth more than the
mystery.

### Where he is — he isn't hiding, he's commuting

> **GM-only.** Three locations, all canonical, none invented. He has a larder in the party's
> territory, a workshop below, and a private stair between them that somebody *sweeps.*

**1. The larder — Area 141, the Bronze Door. Dungeon Level One. The party's own territory.**

Twenty feet from where they murdered Romag. Between his Sleeping Apartment (137) and the Black
Chamber (142), in the corridor they now walk every day.

> *"This portal is shut fast by a massive lock. Upon its surface are runes... **oll err JK**"*
>
> The runes are a **glyph of warding** — 20 cold damage to anyone touching it. The door is solid
> and **takes 300 points of damage before breaking open**; non-magical weapons have a 1-in-6
> chance of shattering against it.
>
> Behind it: a rough-hewn crypt with a passage north to a secondary burial place. **Eleven crypts,
> each holding the body of a cleric who died in the service of the Earth Temple.** Each still
> wears a bronze ring with a triangular jet setting. One wears a *cloak of protection +1*. The
> vaults themselves are relatively easy to open.

**Eleven dead Earth Temple clerics. Behind a door only an Earth Temple prophet has the key to.**

He commissioned that door. He knows the glyph. Those are his own clergy — men he ordained,
promoted, and buried — and they are precisely the corpses he still holds the obedience of.

> **The gut-punch:** the party has almost certainly *seen this door* and declined to open it,
> because 300 hit points and a cold glyph is a "later" problem. It has been "later" for months.
> They have been living next to his pantry.

**2. The workshop — Dungeon Level Three,** reached through the concealed trapdoor in **Area 313,
the Circular Shaft.** Not his lair as such; his working space, where the fresh war dead go.

**3. The commute — and the clue that gives him away.** Area 313's trapdoor is the tell, and it is
verbatim module canon:

> *"The trap door in the northwest part of the floor is well hidden. A small stone flag must be
> lifted to reveal the door's opening ring. Steep steps lead down to a passage... **The steps are
> worn and relatively clean. A small twig broom lies at the bottom of the steps, used to maintain
> the trap door's obscurity.**"*

**Somebody sweeps that stair.** A man who has held a senior post for years, who was *promoted* for
being clever, does not leave dust on the route he uses nightly. The broom is the single most
characterising object he owns: he is tidy, he is patient, he has been doing this a long time, and
he does his own housekeeping because he does not trust anyone else with the door.

> There is also a second vertical route in this earth-themed column — the stairs at **312a** climb
> from the Earth-Floored Area on Level 3 up to **222a on Dungeon Level Two**, and descend the other
> way toward Level Four. He has options. He is not sneaking; he is using the building.

### What this lets you do *this* session, without him appearing

- The drag marks in 2d lead toward **the Bronze Door** — and stop there, at a lock nobody has
  opened, with no dust in front of it.
- The corridor outside 141 is **clean.** Everything else on Level 1 is a week of war-filth. That
  stretch is swept.
- If they get a light on it: the glyph is **intact and undisturbed**, which is the wrong answer.
  Whoever is going in and out isn't breaking it. They're *opening* it.
- **If they try the door:** let them. 300 HP and a 20-cold-damage glyph is a genuine afternoon's
  work at level 9 and entirely doable — but it is loud, slow, and it announces them. He will
  simply not be there, and neither will the two freshest bodies. Reward the effort with an empty
  larder, a swept floor, and eleven opened, empty vaults. *That* is scarier than a fight.
- **If they ask Dren or Eelrich about the door:** Dren has no idea, it predates him. **Eelrich
  might know it exists** — Water and Earth were rivals but the Temple's senior clergy all knew
  where each other's dead went. He'll be evasive about how he knows.

> **Don't let them corner him this session.** He is the first face of the Deep Temple and he should
> arrive on his own schedule. What they get now is a clean corridor, an unbroken glyph, and the
> slowly dawning understanding that the thing they've been hunting has been living in their house,
> using a door they walked past, tidying up after itself.

**A name is not established in canon.** Three candidates, pick one before play — it will stick:
**Oreth Vale** (dry, administrative, sounds like a man who was promoted); **Corvan Ashgrave**
(more gothic, wears its function); **Brother Tallow** (a Temple honorific he never gave up —
quiet, and the most unsettling of the three).

### 2e — The List is done, and that's the problem
Dren is installed. Fire is pacified under Varek and Maeris. Water is pacified under Eelrich. The
trolls are dead. **The party has, in four days, completed almost everything Barkinar asked for in
a week.**

Play the competence back to them. Let them feel good about it — Dren reporting clean, Varek's
dry status update, Maeris in Alrrem's too-big robe running a functioning temple. They earned it.

> **GM-only — the trap in the good review.** Barkinar's warmth is genuine *and* instrumental, both
> at once. A party that overperforms doesn't get left alone; it gets **promoted**, and promotion is
> how Hedrack's leash gets shorter. The correct emotional shape for the check-in is not "you're in
> trouble" — it's *"you're better than I expected, so I'm going to need more from you."*
>
> Don't play this yet. Just don't let the party's success read as safety.

**Two things are still open, and they are not the same kind of thing:**

- **The Chest of Rao** — a decision, not a task. No amount of dungeon-crawling resolves it. See
  the note below.
- **The necromancer** — a task with no location. See 2d.

### 2e-i — The Chest of Rao (the one that actually matters)
This is now the single heaviest item on the board, and it's Calmer's.

The history: Brother Eldin begged them to return it to the Archclericy of Veluna. They handed it
to Romag instead. Romag died; Hartsch inherited it; Hartsch died. It is sitting in Earth Temple
holdings, and the party are the Earth Temple.

Barkinar said the Greater Temple "has interest in" it. He did not demand it. **That restraint is
the pressure** — he's letting them arrive at it.

**Three options, no clean one:**
- **Hand it over.** Compliance. They surrender a genuinely powerful artifact of Good to the actual
  enemy, and they do it voluntarily, and Calmer has to watch.
- **Hide it.** Leverage — *"we know you have it"* cuts both ways, and Barkinar's people will look.
- **Substitute a fake.** Trickery. Requires a forger and a plausible account of the original's
  loss. **Note this is another reason to go to town** — nobody in the Temple makes fakes for you.

> **GM-only:** Hedrack does not actually need the Chest. Hand over = compliance logged. Hide =
> leverage acquired. Fake = useful trickery noted. **The house wins on all three.** Do not
> telegraph this. Let them agonise; the agony is real even though the outcome isn't.

**Why it belongs in this session:** if the party decides to fake it, or to move it, or to get it
out of the Temple entirely, the answer is *outside* — a forger, a courier, a Rhennee barge, a
Cuthbertine who could get it to Veluna. Every one of those is in Nulb. **The Chest can be the
reason they go to town all by itself.** If they raise it, follow it there and let Scene 4's
fencing problem ride along behind it.

> **Voice — Calmer's conscience.** This is the beat that should needle *him* specifically, given
> how far into "Lord Master Calmer" he has gone. Zinnia's standing warning — *you're going in too
> deep* — has teeth here, and it's the last time it will be cheap to say.
>
> And if they get as far as the herb shop: **Y'dey is a Canoness of St. Cuthbert.** She is exactly
> the person who could get the Chest of Rao to Veluna. Neither she nor the party knows that yet.
> Don't force the connection — but if a player makes it in the shop, that's the session's best
> possible accident.

# SCENE 3 — Falrinth's Candle (night, the hinge)

This is Scene 1 of `the-sleeping-prince.md`, firing now. **Read that doc's Scene 1 before play.**
The one thing here that doc doesn't cover: the script assumed Falrinth would reach them *before*
Lucius. He didn't. Lucius already came, poured the wine, and left. **The script needs the
adjustment below and it is stronger for it.**

### Setup
- **When:** late, the party's claimed quarters, after whatever they did on Level 2. Everyone's
  asleep or nearly.
- **Who:** an illusory image of a hooded man. He addresses **Zinnia** by name — most perceptive,
  correct read per his dossier. He never risks himself.
- **What the party already has:** Barkinar named Falrinth to them in person as "a wizard hiding
  from the Greater Temple, to be discussed later." So the name lands as one *their own boss*
  already flagged.

### The beat — revised for post-Lucius
Falrinth's original pitch says *"the man called Lucius Graeme will come to you."* Change it to the
past tense. It's a better line and it proves his surveillance is real:

> "Forgive the theatrics. I cannot be seen sending a runner and you cannot afford to be seen
> receiving one.
>
> The flattering man has already been to you. Golden eyes, soft hands, a great deal of wine. He
> gave you presents and asked you what you were short of. **Lucius Graeme.** Write the name down;
> he collects the ones he gives you.
>
> The Orb this Temple frets over — yes, I have it. I am not giving it to Hedrack, nor to Senshock,
> nor to any of the holy gentlemen who would like me dead. I am not giving it to you either, but
> that is a separate conversation.
>
> The trinket your cleric carries. The scarab with the four letters. **You should be far more
> careful with it than you have been.** Lucius will know it on sight. Senshock will know it on
> sight. They will use it against you in different ways and neither will tell you they noticed.
> That is the sort of thing I see and they have not. *Yet.*
>
> Lareth was a friend. I am told you handled him with discretion, which I appreciate more than
> you would expect. But there are other friends, and not all of them are as patient as I am.
>
> So. When the flattering man comes again — and he will — do not commit. Stall. A candle in the
> west window will reach me.
>
> Or don't. Report me to Barkinar; he'll believe you. I simply will not be where you tell him I
> am. But you will have made your choice."

And then, as the image is already fading — **this is the barb, and it must come too late to
question:**

> "One more thing, since you hold these wretched halls now. There is a room below. Deep, forgotten,
> on the level beneath your feet. The holy gentlemen keep something in it. A *sleeper.* My leash is
> short and the gates are watched, but you — you have keys I never did. If you ever want Verbobonc
> and all its banners to come down on this Temple, and oh, they would come, for *this* — wake what
> sleeps in the iron coffin.
>
> Ask your golden-eyed friend what he's so afraid you'll find. Then signal me."

Gone before Zinnia can answer.

> **GM-only — what Falrinth actually wants.** Not the Prince. He wants Veluna and Furyondy to march
> on the Temple, because a coalition siege keeps every eye in the region fixed *here* and away from
> Lolth's real work. Freeing Thrommel is a detonator, not a rescue. He won't say "prince" — partly
> to keep them hungry, mostly because the less he commits, the safer he is. He also genuinely does
> not know yet whether they're already Lucius's tools. This is still a probe.
>
> Do **not** mention Lolth. Not by name, not by implication.

### Branch — they interrogate the image
It's a one-way projection with three or four pre-loaded vague answers. Press harder and it repeats
"signal me" and fades. **Don't punish the attempt — just give them nothing.** The point is to send
them looking elsewhere, and elsewhere is town.

### Branch — they report Falrinth to Barkinar immediately
Viable, and Barkinar would be genuinely interested. But it puts them on record as having received
a fugitive's contact, and Lucius's network will hear. The check-in gets colder. Let them; don't
warn them out of character.

### Branch — they light the candle that night
Eager, and premature. Falrinth doesn't answer the first signal — he waits, watches whether the
room fills with Barkinar's ogres in the next day. **Give them the anticlimax.** It teaches them
he's careful, which is the correct read of him.

### Branch — they go straight down 227a
Bold. They own the upper levels and now know the stair. But the bronze gates are watched, they
have no cover story, and the clock accelerates hard. Reward the boldness with *danger, not a
closed door* — and note that this trades away the Nulb session. If they commit, follow them and
run `the-sleeping-prince.md` Act Two early; Nulb keeps.

### NPC notes
- **Falrinth** — oily, transactional, evaluating not befriending. Tic: theatrical apology for the
  theatrics. Wants: information and a catspaw. **Never says "prince."** Never raises his voice.

# SCENE 4 — The Fencing Problem (the engine that drives them to town)

This is the mechanical scene that makes leaving the dungeon the party's own idea. It is not a
set-piece; it's an accounting problem you let them discover.

### The setup
They are, by their own reckoning, rich. Lay out what they actually hold:

| What they have | What it's worth | Can they spend it? |
|---|---|---|
| Fire opal necklace | ~3,500 gp | No |
| Silver service (Lareth's) | ~2,000 gp | No |
| Elven chain mail | Held for sale | No |
| Six books of Water Temple lore | 250 gp each | No |
| Coral box, 100 seed pearls | Substantial | No |
| Ivory tray, crystal decanters, stemmed cups | Substantial | No |
| Belsornig's getaway chest | 3,000 gp + gems | **Yes** |
| A desk that is now pink | Priceless | No |

And what they want: a **Ring of Invisibility (10,000)** and a **Ring of Free Action (20,000)**.

**Ruling: they did not pay the deposits.** Lucius offered to hold both for 4,000 gp apiece and the
party declined. So the money is still in their pocket — and **neither ring is reserved.** They are
sitting in a middleman's chest, available to whoever brings 30,000 gp first, and Lucius sells to
clever people generally, not to these clever people specifically.

That's a better squeeze than a sunk deposit. A deposit would have bought them time. Declining
bought them nothing but the full price and a queue they can't see.

> **If they ask whether the offer stands** — it does, warmly, unchanged, no reproach whatsoever:
> *"Of course it stands. I said I'd hold them and I meant it, though I'll be honest with you and
> say I meant I'd hold them for a deposit. Bring me the coin and they're yours. Bring me the coin
> before someone else does and they're yours faster."*

> **The gap is the point.** They ended Ch. 31 "newly and powerfully motivated to plunder every last
> secret the temple had to offer." Good. Now show them that plunder isn't the constraint —
> **liquidity** is. A ceremonial ivory tray is not money. It is a tray.

### The trap they should notice
Their one buyer is **Lucius**, whose Pipe 2 *is* the Nulb funnel — Selentis's people, the Broker
at the Boatman's. Selling through Lucius means Lucius gets a complete, itemised inventory of
everything the party owns, at the exact moment Falrinth has told them he is the most dangerous man
in the building.

**A party that thinks about this for ten seconds wants to fence independently. In Nulb.**

If nobody makes the connection, hand it to them through **Dren** — he's the honest counterweight
and he's allowed to be practical:

> **Dren (lift verbatim):** *"Boss. The nice man's got a warehouse and a smile and he'll take the
> lot off you at a fair price. He'll also know to the copper what we've got and where it sleeps.
> That's how I'd do it, if I were him." (beat) "There's men in Nulb who'll rob you honest."*

### The other reasons to go, if they need more
Stack these; don't deploy all of them. One is a hook, three is a shove.
- **Potions.** Calmer's healing has been a punchline for two sessions. An herbalist fixes that,
  and the party has been openly mocking him for it. Let a *player* raise this if you can.
- **The scarab.** Falrinth just told them it's dangerous and named two people who'd recognise it.
  Nobody inside the Temple can be asked about it without revealing they have it.
- **Staffing.** They're management now with a skeleton crew. Nulb is the hiring hall.
- **The tithe.** Barkinar expects coin, not curios. They need a money-changer regardless.
- **Old business.** The Velkori clan adopted them and is camped at Nulb. Tolub is still alive and
  still telling people he killed them. Jorin Pike Kale gave them Tolub's ship schedule.

# SCENE 5 — Nulb Doesn't Know (arrival)

### The central fact of this scene
**Nobody in Nulb has been told the Temple changed hands.** No runner went down the road. No
proclamation. The war happened underground and stayed there.

So:
- **Wat**, at the Waterside Hostel, is a Water Temple agent still filing reports to Belsornig,
  who has been dead for days.
- **Tolub** is on the docks telling anyone who'll listen that he disposed of the party.
- **Madame Selentis** believes four elemental factions are still clawing at each other.
- **Dick Rentsch's** Earth Temple recruiting pitch is now a pitch for an organisation the party
  personally owns.

The party walks into a town operating on a map that is a week out of date, and they are the
only people who know it. **Play that straight and let them feel it.** Don't have anyone recognise
the change. The pleasure is entirely in the dramatic irony.

### The costume problem
Calmer has spent two sessions being *Lord Master Calmer, Prophet of the Earth Temple.* In the
dungeon that's an asset. In Nulb it identifies him to every Temple informant in town — and to
Tolub, who believes he's dead.

Don't rule on it. **Describe the road, describe the town gate, and let them decide what they're
wearing.** If they walk in dressed as management, that's a live choice with consequences, not a
mistake to correct.

### Tolub — the loaded gun
Tolub's ship schedule is *known to the party.* Decide before play whether he's in port.

- **In port (recommended):** they can see him before he sees them. That's a gift — the party
  chooses whether tonight is the night. If they take him, it's satisfying and it eats the session.
  If they walk past him to get to the herb shop, that restraint *is* a character beat and you
  should let it land in silence.
- **Out on the water:** removes the temptation and keeps the session pointed at Y'dey. Cleaner, but
  you spend a payoff the party has been owed since Chapter 15.

> **GM-only:** if the session is running long, put him out on the water. Getting to the herb shop
> matters more than getting to Tolub, and Tolub isn't going anywhere.

### Branch — the party goes to the Velkori camp first
Very likely, and good. They're adopted clan. Eryk's coercion into road ambushes is still open and
unaddressed; **Vethra's Tarokka reading is still unresolved** — the Chain, the Betrayer, the
Flooded Gate, the crimson barge heavy with souls. The Flooded Gate reads very differently now that
they've drowned the Water Temple.

Use the Velkori as the *soft* entry to town: warm, safe, and a natural place to ask "who in this
town sells decent potions" and "who'd buy a fire opal without asking questions." **Vethra or Eryk
can name Mother Screng.** That's the cleanest possible route to the herb shop — a trusted ally
pointing at the door.

### Branch — the party sends someone else
Entirely reasonable tradecraft. Murfles is a bard; she'll test whoever walks in. But
**Y'dey does not surface for a proxy.** If they send Zephyr and Sequoia without Calmer, they get
Beat 2 and stop there — a surly herbalist's daughter asking sharp questions and nothing more.

That's not a failure. It's a *reason to come back with the cleric,* which is a better second visit
than a first one. Don't force Calmer into the shop; make the shop worth returning to.

# SCENE 6 — The Herb Shop (the payload)

Run this off `nulb_allies.md`, Beats 2 and 3. Summary here; the detail is there.

### Setup
- **Where:** Mother Screng's herb shop. Dried bunches, jars, a counter, a back room. Cheap and
  clean and busy in a town that is neither.
- **Who:** **Murfles**, playing "Hruda" — plain, surly, sharp. **Mother Screng** in the back, or
  behind the counter and beneath notice.
- **Why they're here:** potions. Or a question about a scarab. Or both.

### Beat 1 — Murfles tests
She's rude in a way that's *slightly wrong.* Her questions are too specific for a shop girl:

- What the potions are *for* — not "how many," but **"for what."**
- Where they took the injuries.
- Whether the burns are acid or alkali. (She knows the difference. A shop girl might. A shop girl
  wouldn't ask so precisely.)
- Whether they want the cheap ones or "the ones that work," and she prices the second honestly.

> **Murfles (lift verbatim):** *"Acid, then. Not fire." (she doesn't look up) "You'd want different
> salve for fire. Most people don't know that until it's too late to matter."*

**What she's actually doing:** confirming to Y'dey that these are the people. Her tell is that she
never asks who they are — she asks what happened to them, which is the question an intelligence
officer asks and a merchant doesn't.

### Beat 2 — the shape of the room changes
If Calmer is present and speaks, Mother Screng comes out. She looks at him a beat too long.

Then she probes — **Cuthbertine, and deniable.** A saint's-day reference dropped as small talk. A
half-line of the Brilliant Acclamation offered as a country blessing over the sale. Something a
real Cuthbertine would complete without thinking and a cultist would let pass.

> **Mother Screng, wrapping the jars:** *"Cudgel and truth, then, and the road be short."*

The correct response is reflex. **Do not tell Calmer's player it's a test.** Let them either catch
it or not. If they catch it, everything below fires. If they don't — she wraps the jars, takes
their coin, and they leave, and she watches them go, and you run it again next visit. **That is a
fine outcome.** Don't rescue it.

### Beat 3 — the slip
If Calmer answers right, she stops working. She sets the jar down. She looks at the door, and then
at Murfles, and Murfles moves — not to attack, to *close the shop.*

> **Y'dey (lift verbatim, and then stop talking):**
>
> *"You are Calmer of the Earth Temple, they say. I am Mother Screng of the herb shop, they say.*
> *Both true. Both also incomplete."*

**Then be quiet.** Do not fill it. This is the campaign's first piece of genuine good news and
it needs the air.

### What she is and what it closes
- **Y'dey was never missing.** She went undercover, sent by the Church of St. Cuthbert. She's been
  in this shop the whole time.
- **Terjon isn't a villain.** He's the Hommlet end of her channel. His evasiveness about her
  disappearance was *protection*, and the party has quietly suspected him for most of the
  campaign. That's a clean, satisfying acquittal.
- **Calmer is no longer the only Cuthbertine in the room** — for the first time since the campaign
  started. Given how far into "Prophet Calmer" he's gone, and Zinnia's standing warning that he's
  going in too deep, this is the scene where somebody with actual authority can tell him what he
  still is.

### How far to go tonight
Depends on the clock:
- **Minimum viable close:** the slip, the silence, and *"come back after dark and bring no one you
  don't trust with your lives."* End there. Perfect cliffhanger.
- **If there's time:** she gives them the Prince. The full speech is in
  `the-sleeping-prince.md` Scene 2 — Thrommel named, Grand Marshall of Furyondy, Provost of
  Veluna, buried as dead ten years and not dead. *"For ten years I have sat in this shop and
  waited for someone who could get in. This is the opportunity of a generation."*

> **GM-only — the irony to hold onto.** Y'dey believes she is the only person alive who knows the
> Prince is down there. She is wrong. Falrinth pointed the party at that same room three nights
> ago for the opposite reason, and Lucius is actively hunting whoever knows. **Three people are
> steering this party toward one coffin and each thinks they're alone.** Don't let the party see
> the shape of that yet. Let them feel it later, when they line the three conversations up.

### What she can warn them about
- **"Do not trust your eyes down there."** She knows he's hidden by "the Temple's foulest art."
  Recommended: she does **not** know the specific vampire-illusion (see Open Decisions) — that
  preserves the Act Two reveal while justifying the party packing *true seeing* and *dispel*.
- **Fragarach.** She may know the Prince carried a holy blade lost with him. *"Find the man, find
  the sword."*
- **Murfles and Otis** as topside support — extraction routes, healing, a smith who can look at
  a scarab without reporting it.

### NPC notes
- **Y'dey / Mother Screng** — tired, patient, fiercely faithful, ten years deep in a cover that has
  eaten her life. Tic: St. Cuthbert's plainspoken cadence bleeds through the Screng voice, and she
  doesn't always catch it. Wants: the Prince out. Full stop. No hidden agenda — which in this
  campaign is genuinely disorienting.
- **Murfles ("Hruda")** — surly, plain, capable, armed. Tests before she trusts. Protects Y'dey
  above all. Would never: let a stranger get between Y'dey and the door.

## Anticipated player questions

- **"Why would we leave the dungeon? We own it."** → Because they can't spend what they own, can't
  ask about the scarab inside the building, and can't get a decent healing potion out of a cleric
  who keeps rolling threes. Their empire has no cash and no pharmacy.
- **"Can't Lucius just get us the potions / fence the loot?"** → Yes. Completely, cheerfully, at a
  fair price. And then he'd have a full inventory of everything they own. Let them decide whether
  that's fine. It might be! Just make sure they *decided* it.
- **"Is the herbalist a Temple trap?"** → No, and let them stay paranoid — this campaign has earned
  their distrust. Her proof is Cuthbertine liturgy and things only a real canon would know.
  **Calmer's player adjudicates whether it rings true.** Players are the authority on their own
  character's read.
- **"Should we tell Barkinar about Falrinth?"** → Don't answer. It's a real choice with real costs
  either way, and it's the test Falrinth explicitly set.
- **"What's TZGY mean?"** → Nobody in the room knows yet. Falrinth said it's dangerous and named
  two men who'd recognise it. That's all they have. Otis or Spugnoir are the safe consultants;
  Y'dey might know the craft-tradition but not the specific owner.
- **"Can we kill Tolub tonight?"** → Yes, if he's in port. It's satisfying and it will eat the
  session. Let them; just know what you're spending.
- **"Do we have to decide about the Chest of Rao?"** → Not tonight. Barkinar grades it at the
  check-in, not now. Deferred pressure is scarier than a deadline.

## Stage directions (timing / silence cues)

- **Open on the minotaur's eyes, not on initiative.** They ended last session on a held breath.
  Give them one beat of the thing being *awake* before anyone rolls anything.
- **Falrinth's "one more thing"** — drop it as the image is already going. They must not be able to
  interrogate it. Curiosity is the hook; the denial of follow-up is the barb.
- **When Falrinth names Lucius Graeme, let it sit.** The party has met that man. He poured them
  wine. Don't editorialise; just move on and let them look at each other.
- **The antipathy stair** — if Calmer steps on it, describe nothing happening, and say nothing
  more. No roll, no comment. Let the silence do it.
- **Don't have anyone in Nulb notice the world changed.** The entire pleasure of Scene 5 is that
  the party is the only informed person in the room. Every NPC should be a week out of date and
  totally unbothered.
- **Murfles never asks who they are.** Only what happened to them. If a player clocks that, give it
  to them.
- **Y'dey's slip lands in silence.** "Both true. Both also incomplete." Then stop. Don't fill it,
  don't move to the next beat, don't ask for a roll. Let Calmer's player sit in it as long as they
  want to.
- **If the party sends a proxy and Y'dey stays hidden — do not compensate.** A colder, smaller
  scene now buys a much better one later. Trust the second visit.

   the party extorted their own boss's troops and doesn't know it) or genuinely Kelno's. This
