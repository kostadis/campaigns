# Oohlgrist, Troll Chief of Area 219 (Room of Bones)

> Canon source: T1-4, Area 219 "Room of Bones." Wily, cunning, mercenary — the neighboring trolls of Areas 203 and 218 both serve rival Temple factions and both court Oohlgrist's favor, since he can bring "a score or more of great troll" to whichever side he backs. He has no intention of dying for either of them. Per `notes/eelrich_vane.md` and `notes/phase3_endgame.md`, Belsornig recruited him with an unpaid contract before dying — that debt is now the party's problem to resolve (pay, fight, or hire onward) as part of Barkinar's list.

## Full Stat Block

**Oohlgrist** — Named troll chieftain, mercenary negotiator dressed for war. Large giant, chaotic evil.

**AC** 16 (natural hide + bone armor, see Treasure) | **HP** 152 (16d10 + 64) | **Speed** 30 ft.
**STR** 20 (+5) **DEX** 13 (+1) **CON** 19 (+4) **INT** 8 (-1) **WIS** 10 (+0) **CHA** 10 (+0)
**Skills** Perception +4, Intimidation +4
**Damage Resistances** fire *(ring of fire resistance, see Treasure)*
**Senses** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 14
**Languages** Giant, Common (rudimentary)
**CR** 7 (2,900 XP)

***Regeneration.*** Oohlgrist regains 15 hit points at the start of his turn. If he takes acid or fire damage, this trait doesn't function at the start of his next turn. He dies only if he starts his turn with 0 hit points and doesn't regenerate. *(His ring of fire resistance halves incoming fire damage but does not stop it from suppressing Regeneration — a burning troll still can't knit a burning wound. Ask the table if they want this ruled otherwise; it's the one place the ring and the canon regeneration clause visibly argue with each other.)*

***Old Ferocity (canon boost).*** Oohlgrist fights with the accumulated meanness of a long life playing bigger predators off each other: +2 to attack and damage rolls on all his attacks (already included in the block below).

***Cunning Retreat.*** If Oohlgrist is bloodied (at or below half HP) and no ally remains fighting alongside him, he can use his reaction to Disengage instead of taking an opportunity attack against him. He will use this the instant the fight stops favoring him — see Tactics.

## Actions

***Multiattack.*** Oohlgrist makes three Rend attacks.

***Rend.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +9 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 13 (2d6 + 6) slashing damage.

***Hurl Debris (recharge 5-6).*** Oohlgrist snatches up a chunk of the room's bone-and-rubbish floor and hurls it. *Ranged Weapon Attack:* +9 to hit, range 30/60 ft., one target. *Hit:* 15 (3d6 + 5) bludgeoning damage, and the target must succeed on a DC 16 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.

## Bonus Actions

***Charge.*** Oohlgrist moves up to half his speed straight toward an enemy he can see.

## Reactions

***Cunning Retreat*** *(see above; only usable once bloodied and abandoned).*

## Lair Actions

*(Room 219 only — the Room of Bones. On initiative count 20, losing ties, Oohlgrist can take one of the following. He forgets these exist the moment he's decided to flee or parley — they're a fighting animal's instincts, not a strategist's plan.)*

- **Bone Slide.** The refuse-and-bone floor shifts underfoot. Each creature other than Oohlgrist standing on the floor must succeed on a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw or fall prone.
- **Skull Shelf Collapse.** A shelf-ledge of stacked bones (see room description) crashes down in a 10-foot cube Oohlgrist chooses within the room. Each creature in that area must make a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw, taking 9 (2d8) bludgeoning damage on a failure, or half as much on a success.
- **Choke the Air.** A cloud of old bone-dust and ordure kicks up in a 15-foot radius centered on Oohlgrist. The area is heavily obscured until the end of Oohlgrist's next turn (he's used to the smell; nobody else is).

## Tactics

Oohlgrist does not want to die for anyone — not Belsornig, not whoever holds his contract now. Per canon, **he parleys before he fights**: ransom in gems or coin first, then a dismissive "go away, Great Ugly Troll Chief spare you," then the ruby necklace, then the emerald belt (never the ring or the necklace of adaptation — those don't leave his person short of his corpse). If none of that lands and the party still looks like a fair match for the Temple factions he's used to sizing up, his last offer is service: aid against one of the elemental temples for a quarter of the loot (he'll open by asking for half).

If it comes to blows, he fights for real *only as long as he perceives no treachery and the fight looks even*. The instant the party tries to cheat him, gang up unfairly, or he drops below half HP with no one covering his flank, he uses Cunning Retreat and looks for the door — he does not fight to the last hit point for anyone, including himself out of spite. He is not a fanatic; he is a survivor who has outlasted four Temple factions' worth of shifting alliances by never being the one who dies for the cause.

**If the party brings fire or acid,** his Regeneration is genuinely in trouble — call this out at the table once someone tries it; it should visibly worry him and accelerate his move toward parley or flight.

## Treasure

- **Bone armor** — crude but functional; a valuable trade good to another troll or ogre (+1 AC, already priced into the block above).
- **Ring of Fire Resistance** — worn on his little finger; grants resistance to fire damage (already applied above). Loses it if his hand is taken as a trophy, which is exactly the kind of thing this party does.
- **Necklace of gold with four rubies** (4,750 gp) — a gift from the Fire Temple priesthood, currying his favor.
- **Jeweled silver belt with four emeralds** (5,250 gp) — a gift from the Water Temple priesthood, same logic, opposing patron.
- **Necklace of Adaptation** — kept in a belt pouch, also a Water Temple gift. He has never had reason to wear it; he doesn't fully understand what it does and would trade it away readily if pressed, unlike the ring.
- **Potion of Superior Healing** — hidden in a leather pouch under the pile of old cloth/tapestry scraps in the southeast corner. He doesn't advertise this one.

*GM note: per canon, make a saving throw for the jewelry and ring if Oohlgrist is struck by damaging magical effects — they're not indestructible, and a fire-based kill could plausibly cost the party the ring specifically.*

## CR Calibration

Intended CR 7, calibrated as a **solo elite encounter** for a 4-person, 9th-level party (Calmer: Cleric, War Domain; Zephyr: Tiefling Rogue/Assassin; Zinnia: Elf Monk; Sequoia: Halfling Rogue) — a burst-damage-heavy, control-light party with no dedicated front-line tank. Canon rates a stock troll (CR 5) as merely "Hard" for a 6th-level party; against four 9th-level characters a stock troll dies in one or two rounds to focused rogue/monk burst before Regeneration matters. This block roughly 1.6x's his HP, adds his canon +2 attack/damage "ferocity" bump, gives him a ranged option (Hurl Debris) so he isn't trivially kited, and adds lair actions plus one reaction so a single monster has a fighting chance of surviving more than two rounds of four attackers acting before he does. Expected outcome: a genuinely dangerous fight *if the party is loud, greedy, or breaks the parley* — but the design intent (and his personality) point toward a negotiated resolution most tables will actually reach, per `notes/phase3_endgame.md`'s framing of the troll contract as a choice, not a boss fight.

## Source / Chassis

Base chassis: **Troll (2024 Monster Manual / XMM, p.310)** — CR 5, Multiattack/Rend, Regeneration, Charge bonus action, retrieved via 5etools MCP. Modifications for this campaign: HP increased (94 → 152), attack/damage bonus increased +2 per T1-4 canon ("+2 to attack and damage rolls to reflect his exceptional ferocity"), added Hurl Debris (a reflavored improvised ranged attack, standard troll-adjacent giant behavior, not from a specific spell), added Cunning Retreat (reflavored Disengage-as-reaction, a common "boss escape" pattern), and three lair actions built directly from the room's own canon description (the bone-and-refuse floor, the skull shelves, the general stench) rather than from any spell list. Loathsome Limbs (present on the stock 2024 block) was cut — it reads as a chaotic-battlefield mechanic that doesn't suit a negotiator who avoids taking that much sustained damage in the first place; reintroduce it if you want a bloodier fight.
