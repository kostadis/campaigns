### Voss

**Current status:** Alive  
**Current location:** At the Moathouse (last known location)  
**Allegiance/faction:** Formerly affiliated with the Temple (cult linked to the Church of St. Cuthbert’s purge); now a desperate, unaffiliated fugitive  
**Current possessions / notable items:** A flat river rock inscribed with an eye and a tear (given by his boss as a token of belonging)  
**Current assignment / role:** Former bandit; currently hiding and terrified of capture by the Church of St. Cuthbert  
**Defining recent actions:**  
- Expressed deep emotional distress upon seeing the symbol (eye and tear on the river rock), crying hysterically.  
- Confirmed under questioning that he was coerced into service with the Temple due to gambling debts, alcoholism, and the forced sale of his property.  
- Acknowledged the Moathouse was chosen as a hideout because it was abandoned and avoided by others after the Temple’s fall and looting by adventurers.  
- Revealed awareness that membership in the cult is punishable by death, and is now terrified of execution.  

**Revealed motivations or secrets:**  
- Did not choose to become a bandit; his descent was precipitated by financial ruin and addiction.  
- The river rock symbol was not a mere trinket but a sacred token of belonging within the cult hierarchy — its presence triggers trauma and panic.  
- He believes the symbol’s origin traces back to a “big boss,” implying a chain of command he was never fully part of but is now condemned by.

## Uncertainty
- The exact nature of the “Temple” and its connection to the Church of St. Cuthbert is implied but not fully defined — is it a cult *of* St. Cuthbert, or a heretical offshoot?  
- Whether Voss is still physically at the Moathouse or has fled since the confrontation is not confirmed in later facts.  
- It is unclear whether the “big boss” who gave the symbol is alive, dead, or still active — Voss’s statement implies a hierarchy, but no names or current status are given.  
- The emotional trigger (the river rock) is tied to his fear of execution, but whether he is currently being pursued or has been formally charged by the Church is not stated.  
- The quote “He explains…” appears twice with near-identical wording — it is unclear if these are two separate utterances or repetitions in the record.
