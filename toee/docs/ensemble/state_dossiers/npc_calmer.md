### Calmer

**Current Status:** Alive, severely wounded, and psychologically fractured.  
**Current Location:** Upper Temple, under the care of Dren Halveth’s mercenaries, stationed behind a curtain to prevent re-entry into combat.  
**Allegiance/Faction:** Self-proclaimed Chief Cleric and supreme leader of the Upper Temple, now fully embracing the identity of the Prophet of the Earth Temple — though his true allegiance to St. Cuthbert is in catastrophic collapse.  

**Current Possessions / Notable Items:**  
- Mace of Smiting (actively wielded)  
- Plate mail (damaged but still worn)  
- Holy symbol: Cudgel (still dangling from wrist, though its divine efficacy is now contested)  

**Spells / Feats / Abilities:**  
- Capable of casting *Toll the Dead*, *Guiding Bolt*, *Sacred Flame*, *Cure Light Wounds*, *Healing Word*, *Guidance*, and *Spectral Guardians*  
- Possesses the ability to invoke divine radiance and the Rite of Turning (used to destroy the wraith)  
- Can channel necrotic resonance — now used as a weapon of assassination and destruction  
- Has lost the ability to utter the name of St. Cuthbert in the temple; prayers manifest as complaints and despair  

**Current Assignment / Role and Key Relationships:**  
- **Role:** Chief Cleric and supreme leader of the Upper Temple; self-declared Prophet of the Earth Temple  
- **Reports to:** No one — he is now the authority. He has installed Dren Halveth as “supreme commander of the Earth Temple Forces,” placing him as subordinate.  
- **Key Relationships:**  
  - Zephyr: Tolerates Calmer’s authority as a useful illusion; Calmer defers to Zinnia’s judgment on alliances.  
  - Zinnia: Calmer seeks his counsel and trusts his judgment — the last vestige of his former self.  
  - Dren Halveth: Calmer’s appointed subordinate, who now physically carries and guards him.  
  - Frostbrand: Accused Calmer of being “St. Cuthbert’s” in a forbidden temple — triggering visible panic.  
  - The party: Viewed as expendable assets; Calmer now sees them as “henchmen” under his command.  

**Defining Recent Actions:**  
- Slew the wraith with a final, self-destroying blast of divine radiance, leaving him with permanent necrotic trauma and a void within.  
- Declared himself Chief Cleric and Prophet of the Earth Temple, asserting absolute authority over all surviving temple factions.  
- Installed Dren Halveth as commander of Earth Temple Forces, formalizing a new hierarchy.  
- Corrected Zephyr’s phrasing to “Our subordinate” when referring to the Fire Temple cleric, enforcing doctrinal supremacy.  
- Demanded to be called “Prophet Calmer, Prophet of the Earth Temple,” signaling full psychological assimilation into his cover.  
- Refused to participate in physical embraces (“I do not wrestle”), insisting on ritual purity amid tactical victory.  
- Attempted to invoke a death-knell against a salamander — failed, but used the moment to interpret its surrender non-verbally, establishing a pact.  
- Approved Zephyr’s decision to open a secret door — the first sign of tactical acquiescence, not leadership.  

**Revealed Motivations / Secrets:**  
- Calmer no longer believes in St. Cuthbert — he speaks the god’s name only in failure, and his prayers now come out as complaints.  
- He is haunted by the wraith’s laughter and the cold void inside him — a wound no healing magic can close.  
- His identity as the Prophet is no longer a cover — it is writing his objections for him. He is becoming the role he pretended to be.  
- He clings to titles and hierarchy not out of faith, but to avoid the terrifying silence of having lost everything he once believed in.  

## Uncertainty  
- Contradiction: Calmer is repeatedly referred to as a cleric of St. Cuthbert, yet in Chapter 20 he claims to have converted to the Earth Temple. Later chapters treat him as a full Earth Temple leader — but no source confirms whether he *actually* converted or is still secretly a St. Cuthbert cleric pretending to be one. The later chapters (27–31) treat his St. Cuthbert identity as broken, not abandoned — suggesting internal conflict, not conversion.  
- Ambiguity: Was Calmer truly resurrected after being killed by Thalsor in Chapter 11? The text says his body was “intact and could be raised,” but never confirms whether he *was* raised. His survival into Chapter 12 onward is assumed, but the mechanism is unrecorded.  
- Attribution: In Chapter 27, Calmer is called “a cleric of St. Cuthbert” even as he kills Romag with necrotic power — suggesting either the narrator is unaware of his shift, or Calmer’s identity is still perceived ambiguously by others.  
- Unclear State: Is Calmer’s inability to say “St. Cuthbert” a magical curse, psychological trauma, or divine abandonment? The text describes it as “coming out sideways as complaint,” but never explains its origin.  
- Ambiguous Power: Calmer’s necrotic power (used to kill Romag and the Chimera) is never labeled as a spell, feat, or divine gift — is it a corruption of St. Cuthbert’s power, a gift from the Earth Temple, or something else? The source remains silent.
