# Lucius Graeme

> **Naming note (resolved 2026-08-22).** The canonical name is **Lucius Graeme**, who introduces
> himself using both his names at once — *"I'm Aldren — well, Lucius, Aldren when I'm working, it's
> a long story and a dull one."* That deliberate self-garble is why the session summaries disagree:
> Chapter 31 recorded **"Lucius Alrrem"** (contamination from Alrrem, the dead Fire Temple prophet)
> and Chapter 32 recorded **"Lucius Graham."** Both are transcription drift. **Graeme** is correct;
> **Aldren** is his working alias, including in Nulb. Chapter 31's account of this visit contains at
> least one other confirmed garble ("Sequioa and *Romag* each purchase a shortsword" — Romag being
> dead by then), so treat Chapter 32 as the more reliable record of the encounter.

## Identity
- **Role:** Courier, fixer and quartermaster-of-favors to the Upper Temple's new management. In
  truth, **Hedrack's spymaster** — runs the Temple's external whisper-web across Verbobonc, Nulb
  and Hommlet. *Also known as: Aldren (working alias, incl. a gray-clad gambler's cover at the
  Waterside Hostel in Nulb).*
- **Faction:** Greater Temple. Tier 1 in Hedrack's coup — one of the few who knows it exists.
  **GM-only:** his actual allegiance runs past Hedrack to **Zuggtmoy**, with whom he communes
  directly.
- **First Appearance:** Chapter 31 (2026-06-28), the party's lair on Dungeon Level One, the morning
  after the Water Temple fell. Sent by Barkinar to "see to the household."

## Personality & Motivations
- **Core goal:** to be indispensable. He is buying the right to be the party's first phone call,
  and he is paying for it in gifts.
- **His creed** — the only thing he actually believes: *civilization is a thin mask over elemental
  chaos, and the man who embraces the chaos is the only man ever truly in control.* He was a lord
  once — vain, cruel, comfortable — and a fall took it from him, along with a scar across the face
  that he tells at least four different lies about.
- **Plays the fool on purpose.** Portly and soft, plain gray clothes, a low gray hat, a manner
  faintly buffoonish. **A man others underestimate hears everything**, and he delights in it — he
  plays the fool the way a card-sharp plays the drunk. Behind it is a calculating engine, and lower
  still a banked rage he unleashes without mercy on the rare occasion someone earns it.
- **Warmth as a weapon, and the warmth is also real.** He genuinely enjoys competent people and
  genuinely enjoys giving them things. That both things are true at once is what makes him
  effective and what makes him dangerous.
- **Tics:** fills cups; undersells himself; turns every compliment back on the person he is
  complimenting; compliments that are also probes.
- **Never:** raises his voice in negotiation; makes a threat he intends to carry out personally
  (his menace is always something that will happen *to* you, arranged by hands that aren't his);
  lets the fool-act slip where it matters; admits the warmth is strategic.

## Mechanical note — he cannot be detected
He wears an **Amulet of the Shrouded One**. *Detect evil and good*, *scrying*, *commune* and
*divination* all fail against him. If the party tries to magic-sense him, it comes back **clean** —
which a clever player should find suspicious in itself. **He has to be read with plain tradecraft,
not magic.** That limitation is the whole game with him and should be made explicit at the table
the first time they try.

## History with the Party
1. **Ch. 31 — Arrival (the party's lair, Level One).** Dren announced him with visible distrust —
   a soft-looking gray man who smiled too much, claimed Barkinar sent him, and *laughed* when
   patted down for weapons. ("I don't trust harmless.") The party let him in.
2. **Ch. 31 — The gift.** He introduced himself as a facilitator and middleman — *"a carrier of
   things from clever people to clever people"* — and produced a cask of genuinely excellent wine
   plus a **Potion of Superior Healing for every member of the household**, courtesy of Barkinar
   and the Temple's supply chain. He asked for nothing.
3. **Ch. 31 — The chest.** Asked about magical wares, he snapped his fingers and had Dren bring in
   a chest of enchanted goods (Dren warning, gamely, that chests sometimes turn out to be mimics).
   He named **Lord Hedrack** as the authority who had made clear that magic items are not given
   away free — the first time that name has ever been spoken to the party.
4. **Ch. 31–32 — The sale.** **Sequoia and Zephyr each bought a +1 shortsword for 1,000 gp.** He
   then showed them the two items they actually wanted — a **Ring of Invisibility (10,000 gp)** and
   a **Ring of Free Action (20,000 gp)** — and offered to hold both in reserve for a **4,000 gp
   deposit apiece**. **The party declined.** Both rings remain unreserved and at full price. The
   party left the encounter powerfully motivated to find more money.

## Current Status
- **Last Known Location:** departed the party's lair. Operates between the Greater Temple, Nulb,
  Hommlet and Verbobonc; reachable through Dren, or by sending word downstairs.
- **Active:** yes, and watching. He did not choose these four and Barkinar handed them the Upper
  Temple without consulting him — which unsettles him more than he shows. **This first visit was
  reconnaissance disguised as generosity, run entirely warm.** Every probe was holstered.
- **What the party knows:** `[KNOWN]` a soft, gray, perpetually-smiling man sent by Barkinar; a
  self-described facilitator with access to wine, potions and a chest of magic items; he says the
  name **Lord Hedrack** carries weight above him; he is holding two rings they cannot afford.
  `[SUSPECTED]` nothing. No Insight has landed, no external warning has fired. **The asymmetry is
  total — he reads them and they do not read him.**
- **Pending:** Falrinth's contact is scheduled to name him to the party as *"a flattering man with
  golden eyes,"* with the instruction *do not commit, stall* — and to warn them that Lucius will
  recognise the TZGY scarab on sight. That warning arriving *after* they have already met and liked
  him is deliberate (see `notes/sessions/nobody-told-nulb.md`, Scene 3).
- **What remains hidden:** `[GM-ONLY]` Hedrack's coup and his Tier-1 place in it; that his true
  allegiance is to Zuggtmoy rather than to Hedrack, and that he would abandon the coup the instant
  the demon's interest moved; the Amulet; that he holds the Selentis leash; that he is
  simultaneously playing Senshock's "informant"; that he already knows the party were in Nulb; and
  that the one thing he most needs to know is **whether they have heard about the sleeper below.**

## What He Knows About the Party
- Four prophets dead in a single day (Romag, Hartsch, Alrrem, Belsornig) and the Upper Temple handed
  to unknowns he did not pick.
- **They were in Nulb.** His network flagged the visit. Recommend he does **not** yet know *who they
  saw* there — that is what keeps Y'dey's cover alive.
- They are loot-rich and gear-poor, and have been hauling unidentified Temple loot for months.
- **Calmer carries the TZGY scarab** — Senshock's personal control device. Lucius would know it on
  sight. Recommended play: he clocked it silently in Chapter 31 and said nothing.
- **He does NOT know about Prince Thrommel being on the party's radar** — and finding that out is
  the entire purpose of his next visit.

## His Three Supply Pipes
- **Pipe 1 — Temple-confiscated gear (immediate).** Four dead prophets' effects, the Water Temple
  treasury, Greater Temple quartermaster stores. He isn't giving them his things; he's giving them
  *their own conquered things, sorted and identified* — which feels like a gift and is really an
  information advantage.
- **Pipe 2 — the Nulb funnel (days).** Selentis's people → the Broker (Lorveth), who runs discreet
  auctions and laundering above the Boatman's. Sourcing through this pipe quietly routes the party
  into Madame Selentis's logistics.
- **Pipe 3 — his own couriers (a session or more).** For the genuinely rare ask: *"let me make some
  inquiries."* This is the pipe that explicitly converts a purchase into a future obligation. The
  build-defining items live here and are **never** a freebie.

## Relationships
- **Hedrack (Supreme Commander):** nominally his master; Lucius is the operational hand that
  delivers the coup's manipulation arcs. **Hedrack believes Lucius is loyal to him. He is not.**
- **Zuggtmoy:** his actual allegiance. He hears her directly, which is more than most of the holy
  men above him can say.
- **Barkinar:** his sincere, deceived colleague. Barkinar genuinely does not know the coup exists
  and genuinely believes he is serving Iuz. Lucius does not correct him.
- **Senshock (Lord Wizard):** believes he controls Lucius by feeding him crafted intelligence.
  Lucius lets him believe it. **This double-game is the seam the party can eventually pull** —
  Lucius tells Senshock one story and the party another, and either thread leads to the other.
- **Madame Selentis / "Lady Nysera Krivaltis":** his protégée and logistics chief in Nulb. He holds
  her leash.
- **Zert (Inn of the Welcome Wench, Hommlet):** his personal enforcer. He would sacrifice Zert
  without blinking to protect the larger game.
- **Falrinth:** the rival who is about to name him to the party. Lucius does not know Falrinth has
  made contact.
- **Dren Halveth:** his delivery vector into the party's household, and a quiet test of Dren's
  loyalty every time he uses it. Dren distrusts him on instinct and has said so out loud.
- **Calmer:** carries the scarab. Lucius has almost certainly noticed and has said nothing.
- **The party:** he made them richer, better-armed, and glad to see him. That is the leash, and
  they cannot feel it yet.

## Voice
- *(warm, expansive, filling cups)* — "Forgive the fuss. I'm only a courier; I carry things from
  clever people to clever people. And you four are the cleverest thing to happen to this miserable
  rock in a year. Sit. Drink. Tell me what you're short of and I'll see it carried."
- *(the probe, light as air, eyes steady)* — "Been down to Nulb, I hear. Restocking; war's thirsty
  work. Did some herb-wife fill your heads with the usual? Sleeping kings, buried gold under the
  altar?" *(a soft laugh)* "People do love a bedtime tale. There's nothing below but rock and the
  faithful's work."
- *(when the party asks what the catch is)* — "The catch? You're suspicious of a gift. Good. Stay
  that way; it'll keep you alive down here. There's no catch this morning. There may be a day I ask
  a favour of clever friends. This isn't it. Today I'm just the man with the warehouse."
- *(his creed, when he thinks nobody important is listening)* — "Civilization is a fragile mask over
  the face of the elemental chaos. It's a shame when it breaks. Or a delight."
- *(rare — the rage off the leash)* — "You think the soft voice is the whole of me. Everyone does.
  It's the last thing they think."

**What he would never say:** he would never name the Prince, the Orb's true fate, or the coup. He
would never admit he hears Zuggtmoy directly, or that his loyalty runs past Hedrack. He would never
be caught *seeming* to mind being underestimated — the fool-act only works while it looks like the
truth.

## Notes for the GM
- **He cannot be killed or flipped in a friendly scene.** Attempts cost the party goodwill, tell him
  they're a problem, and accelerate the Barkinar clock. Hold any real Lucius confrontation for the
  unmasking, deep in the Hedrack arc.
- **Give the Insight flicker when someone earns it** — a *thread, not a confession*: "he's sharper
  than he's pretending, and he's working very hard to be liked." Then have him be warmer than ever.
  The contrast is the unease.
- **If the party ever reveals they know about Thrommel:** his warmth does not drop. That is what
  makes him frightening. He finishes the meeting graciously and files it — and within a day their
  deep-level access quietly tightens, a time-consuming mission is proposed, and he begins arranging
  for the Prince to be moved, re-hidden, or "martyred." This is not a fail-state; it is a harder
  game against a closing net.
- **Cornered, he does not martyr himself for another man's coup.** He runs, lies, or flips. He has
  *Pass Without Trace*, *Nondetection*, and a lifetime of escape routes into the network he built.
