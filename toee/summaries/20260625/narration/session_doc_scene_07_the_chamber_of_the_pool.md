Zephyr had finished dividing the treasure — he always finished dividing the treasure — and then he turned to the door on the south wall, the one Brother Eelrich had been standing in front of the whole time like a man guarding a relative's grave.

I asked what was behind it. That is the sort of thing the Prophet of Stone asks, and I have learned the cadence of it.

"Behind the door to the south," Eelrich said, and his voice did a small reverent thing. "That is where the high priest retained all of its powers. Inside is a creature of unparalleled power."

I nodded like a man being told where the good wine was kept.

"A creature of unparalleled power," he said again, warming to it. "One that we worshipped, and one that we served, and one that served us."

*Worshipped.* The word sat wrong in my chest. I have stood in a lot of rooms in this place and swallowed a lot of words. I have watched Romag toast me as a convert and clinked my cup against his. But *worshipped and served and served us* is a particular kind of sentence, and something in me that has been quiet for weeks stirred at it and did not go back to sleep.

"Can we see it?" Sequoia said.

"You wish to see it?" Eelrich asked.

"Yeah."

"Sure," said Zinnia, easy, the way he says everything, and Eelrich went, "well," and opened the door.

The room past it was carved from great blocks of polished serpentine, green-black and wet-looking though it was dry, and the ceiling above was a dome of crystals cut to throw everything back at you crooked — my own plate armor smeared across a hundred facets, a hundred small broken Calmers. All of that reflected light bent down toward the center, toward the pool.

The pool gave off a blue-green glow. That was the first wrong thing. The second was that it also took light in — I watched the pale flames from the cressets lean toward it and vanish somewhere under the surface, swallowed, held. Eight bronze cressets, four along the east wall and four along the west, all burning a sick green fire that shed light and no heat at all. I have blessed enough fires to know when one is a lie.

The tapestries I did not look at long. The mosaic on the far wall I could not stop looking at. Ten arms of dark purple tile, reddish along the edges, two green orbs for eyes, and in each of the ten tentacles a human figure held up like an offering. I did not have to guess what I was seeing. I have read enough scripture, real scripture, to know a sacrificial tableau when a temple puts one on its wall to be proud of. This was not a warning. It was a menu.

And this was the thing they worshipped. This was what St. Cuthbert—

I closed my mouth on the name. I felt it come up my throat the way it had in the Air Temple corridor, the way it does when the wrongness in front of me is total and old and pleased with itself, and I held it behind my teeth and let it go back down. Not here. The mercenaries were not in the room, but Eelrich was, and a man's cover does not survive his god's name spoken over an altar to a kraken.

The pool was thirty feet long and wide across, set in a basin of small blue lapis tiles, and the water was so clear it lied about its depth. The edges came up shallow. Then the floor fell away toward the center, down and down to where the water went dark, and at the very bottom, thirty feet down, chained where the light finally died, there was a figure. Humanoid. Bound. Alive.

Then it spoke inside my head.

Not to me alone — I saw Zinnia's chin come up, saw Sequoia go still. A Triton, the voice said, pleading, fast, frightened in the way that only true things are frightened. *Release me. When the Kraken shown on the wall materializes, it will come for you, and it will come at any moment. As many of you as can, jump into the pool. Help me break these chains. Soon it will be too late.*

I stood there in my looted plate, the Prophet of Stone, the man who runs this temple now whether I meant to or not, and I looked at a good creature chained at the bottom of a shrine to a monster, begging.

Something in me wanted to be in the water already. The same thing that has always wanted to charge the wrong thing alone.

And something colder and more careful stood behind it and said this is a shrine, and shrines set traps in the shape of exactly what you cannot walk away from.

The Kraken had not come yet. The Triton was still chained. And I had not yet decided which of the two men I have become was going to move first.
