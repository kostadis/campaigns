The idol woke up.

I had been watching it the whole fight: a slab of stone that had no business moving, and then it moved. Projections came out of it. Pseudopods, reaching, unfolding like a thing deciding it was alive after all. It rolled. Stone that rolls. *I've heard of worse in the Abyss, but this is bad.*

Behind me Calmer was getting swarmed, gargoyles peeling at his plate like they meant to open a tin. "Gentlemen," he said, in the voice he uses when he wants everyone to know he's still the smartest man in the room, "don't wish to be rude, but perhaps some protection might be in order?" And then, because one line is never enough for him: "Don't forget, I am the Chief Cleric. If I die, bad things will happen to all of you."

He's not wrong. The healer is the one asset you don't spend. I sighed. "Okay. Let's protect Calmer."

Sequoia was riding a gargoyle. Actually on its back, legs around it, telling anyone who'd listen he was *kind of on top of something right now* and couldn't tell if the rest of us were idiots or not. Zinnia went up off the altar, came down on a gargoyle, killed it, and landed light on the corpse like it was a springboard. The dance was going fine.

Then the idol reached out and hit Calmer with three of those stone limbs at once, a blow like a wall falling on him, and he sagged, and held, and did not go down. The working he was holding never slipped. Good man. Louder-than-necessary man. Held.

That was when I understood the idol was the whole problem. A Juggernaut. Everything else in the room was noise.

I put a bolt into it from the pistol and slid back toward the altar before it could answer, keeping myself near Calmer. Sequoia's gargoyle dropped under him and he stepped off the falling stone clean, like a man walking off a gangplank onto solid ground. The room was almost ours.

Then the thing came for me.

It rolled, the entire weight of it, a mountain deciding to lie down where I was standing, and I moved before I thought about moving. The stone passed and I wasn't under it. *Come and get it.* It missed completely.

So I tried to take it apart the way I take things apart. Trip it: nothing, it's a wheel, you can't topple a wheel. Poison the blade: nothing, the venom found nothing in it to kill. Every tool in the box and none of them fit the lock. *This again.* It rolled through the altar behind us and turned it to rubble, a whole sacred slab reduced to gravel, and some cold accountant's part of me noted that the stone had probably meant something to the Water Temple and meant nothing at all to me. Move on.

Sequoia and I traded positions, keeping each other's angles clean, and his dagger bit deep. We were making progress. We were.

Then it came back for me and this time it did not roll. It just hit. Three times, stone on flesh, and then there was no floor.

Dark. No pain, exactly: pain is for the living and I was somewhere adjacent to that. Somewhere far off there was a tug, a hook set in whatever's left of a man when the body quits, pulling, and something in me pulled back and held. The first pull, resisted, and I knew somehow there'd be others. And through the dark, dimly, a hand at my collar, my heels dragging over rubble, someone hauling me like a sack of grain through a doorway. *The assassin. Cargo now.* The blow that put me down should have been worse than it was, later I'd learn Sequoia had bent something so it landed softer, but not soft enough. I stayed under. The wraith had promised one of us would die today, and for a black stretch I wondered, with no drama left in me, whether this was the debt coming due.

Then warmth. Calmer's warmth, holy fire poured into a corpse until it remembers it isn't one, and I was back on my feet with the taste of stone in my mouth and a very short list of grievances.

I looked at the gargoyle still worrying the door. "Now, I will exact my revenge on the juggernaut." Wrong target, right sentiment. Sequoia was at my shoulder, and I put my blade through the gargoyle, and it died.

The Juggernaut was in bad shape; the whole party had been carving at it while I was drooling on the floor. Calmer was at the door asking nobody in particular, "do you think the doors are gonna stop him?" and answering himself: "maybe it will, maybe it won't, but if I slow him down, he can't race as fast after us." Fine. Slow it. Everyone had a job.

Then Sequoia turned to me with that flat halfling honesty. "Would you mind being the sacrificial lamb again?"

Me. The one who sends other people into the water. Stand still, bleed, draw the mountain.

"Yes, yes."

I stood in front of it. It came, and Calmer walked into the fight and finished it, and the great rolling weight went still.
