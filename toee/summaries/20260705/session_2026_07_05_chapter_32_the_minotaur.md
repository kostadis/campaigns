The party's escape from the Hall of Verdigris was a harrowing affair. Zephyr had taken the bait of a desperate mental suggestion and thrown himself into what he believed was a pool of water, only to find himself seized by a sentient, acidic intelligence that locked him in place as acrid smoke curled off the surface. Sequoia attempted to lash a rope around his waist to haul Zephyr free, but standing only three feet tall against a four-foot-deep pool, the effort was doomed from the start — a fact Calmer was quick and unsympathetic in pointing out. It was Zinnia who ultimately tied off a rope, leapt in without hesitation, and dragged Zephyr out alive, though coated head to foot in acid. Sequoia, not to be outdone in poor decisions, jumped in a second time before realizing his mistake and scrambling clear, burning as he went.

Retreating behind a curtain just outside the hall, Calmer pieced together the most unsettling truth of all: the thing in the water could plant suggestions in their minds. The party fell back to catch their breath, and their thoughts turned quickly from survival to opportunity. Brother Eelrich Vane's chambers awaited, now claimed in the name of the Prophet of Earth, and the party wasted no time. From a desk they recovered a collection of powerful scrolls, and from locked chests in the bedroom they pried free ritual texts on elemental water, a ceremonial bronze mace, a gold ring set with pearl, and potions of water breathing. As they leafed through the ritual texts, however, an unsettling suspicion took root — the writings hinted strongly that Vane had known all along about the sentient, living nature of the water elemental, and had said nothing. Before leaving, the party made their position crystal clear to the defeated priest: the next surprise found in these halls would be meant for him.

Returning to their lair on the first level to rest and recover, Calmer took the opportunity to formalize the new order. He named Zinnia as the head of all intelligence operations, Zephyr as chief of day-to-day operations, and Sequoia as the one responsible for ensuring valuables found their way into the right hands. Calmer himself declared that his concerns lay with the spiritual matters of the elemental gods, and that petty operational details were entirely beneath him — a declaration delivered with the full gravity of a man who had just forced a priest to call him Lord Master. Their rest was soon interrupted by a knock at the door, as Dren reported the arrival of a soft-looking grey man who smiled far too much and claimed to have been sent by Barkinar.

The man introduced himself as Lucius Graeme — a facilitator, a middleman, a carrier of things from clever people to clever people — and he arrived bearing gifts. He produced a cask of genuinely excellent wine and presented each member of the household with a potent healing potion, all courtesy of Barkinar and the temple's new supply chain. When the party inquired about magical wares, Lucius snapped his fingers and had Dren bring in a chest of enchanted goods, revealing an assortment of items available for purchase. Zinnia and Zephyr each walked away with finely enchanted blades, but the items that truly captured the party's imagination carried price tags that dwarfed everything they had accumulated. Lucius offered to hold the most coveted pieces in reserve for a deposit, but the party declined, left instead with a powerful and very practical motivation to plunder every last secret the temple had to offer.

## Summary
Before setting out, Dren raised the matter of the trolls still lurking somewhere in the temple's depths — creatures he was decidedly not equipped to handle. He explained that trolls were powerful, regenerating beasts, and that the only reliable way to put one down for good was fire. The party immediately thought of their fire salamander ally on the second floor and went to pay him a visit. Vurakhal, who did not speak Common, communicated through Frostbrand, the party's sentient and deeply opinionated magical short sword. When Frostbrand enthusiastically declared himself "Frostbrand, the evil sword" as part of the party's cover story, the performance landed with all the menace of a damp cloth — but Vurakhal was convinced, and agreed to join the hunt.

On their way to the troll lair, the party passed through a southwestern chamber and came face to face with a very large, very hungry, and very chained owlbear. Sequoia offered it food, and Zinnia approached with calm confidence; the creature's fury gradually subsided into wary tolerance. Sequoia, drawing on hard-won experience, recognized that this particular owlbear was the meanest of its kind and that releasing it would be an unpredictable gamble at best. The party left it chained and moved on, quietly relieved to avoid another ill-fated attempt at an exotic pet.

Sequoia and Zephyr crept ahead to scout the troll lair and found two of the creatures feasting on the remains of fallen soldiers, their colorful tribal garb doing nothing to soften the grim scene. The party launched a devastating surprise assault, with Sequoia and Zephyr striking hard and fast before disengaging into the shadows. The trolls, however, proved their reputation — one of them manifested grotesque additional limbs and lashed out in a frenzy, leaving Sequoia grievously wounded. Vurakhal strode into the fray, his searing heat aura cutting off the trolls' ability to regenerate, and drove his flaming spear through one of them with lethal efficiency. The second troll fell moments later, and Calmer attempted to mend Sequoia's wounds with divine healing — twice — producing results so underwhelming that the party could barely contain their disbelief.

Pressing deeper, Zinnia's sharp eye caught a secret door that opened into a hallway leading further into the temple. At the end of it, the party discovered a chamber that could only be described as a monument to depravity: walls plastered with excrement and bones arranged into patterns, skulls of humans, dwarves, and various humanoids leering from shelf-like ledges, and furniture fashioned entirely from skeletal remains. A massive troll chieftain rose from a heap of torn rags and tapestries in the corner, enormous and oblivious to the party's presence. The group coordinated a stealthy approach, bringing Vurakhal forward to neutralize the creature's regeneration before it could even react. Zephyr tripped the chieftain and sent him crashing to the ground, and Sequoia stepped in and delivered a single, perfectly placed strike — one stab, clean and lethal — that killed a creature of terrifying size before it could take a single action. The whole party stood in stunned silence for a moment before the reality of what had just happened sank in.

The chieftain's remains yielded a handsome reward: a substantial sum of gold and platinum, a ring of fire resistance the troll had never had the chance to use, a potion of greater healing, and a curious magical wood totem. When the totem was tested on a nearby desk, it turned the furniture a vivid shade of pink — a permanent change, usable once a day, and immediately claimed by the party as a novelty too useful to leave behind.

Continuing their exploration, the party pushed into a room that had once belonged to the Water Temple and found four bugbears dressed in the colors of the Air Temple. Kelno had been busy. The border between the water and air territories had quietly shifted while the party was occupied elsewhere, and these guards made it clear they answered to the Air Temple now. Calmer drew himself up to his full height and announced that he was Lord Master Calmer, demanding tribute as the new ruling authority of the area. The bugbears stared at him in confusion, but Calmer's declaration landed just well enough to shake twenty gold pieces loose from them before the party moved on.

At the end of a long hallway, Sequoia picked the lock on a heavy door and the party stepped into what had once been a grand reception chamber. Murals on the walls depicted the temple's former hospitality in vivid detail, though the room itself was now a ruin of battered furniture, scattered bones, and a dry, neglected fountain. A dirty, decorated statue of a minotaur sat upon a throne at the room's center, dull and motionless. Sequoia studied it carefully — and then realized, with a cold certainty, that it was not a statue at all. The session drew to a close with the party staring down a living minotaur, poised on the edge of yet another deadly encounter in the depths of the temple they now called home.

### Lucius Graeme
A soft-looking grey man who smiles constantly and arrived claiming to be sent by Barkinar. He is a facilitator and middleman who brought gifts of wine and healing potions, and presented a chest of enchanted items for sale.

### Barkinar
Commander of the Greater Temple, and the authority who handed the Upper Temple to the party after four prophets died in a single day. He sent Lucius Graeme as their quartermaster and conduit for supplies and magical wares. The one-week deadline he set for the party to organize the temple is still running through this session.

### Vurakhal
A fire salamander ally who does not speak Common. He is contracted to the party and agrees to help eliminate the trolls. His fire aura proves vital in preventing troll regeneration, and he wields a flaming spear with deadly efficiency, though he is injured by the end of the troll encounters.

### Varek Solain
The Captain of the Fire Temple who arrives during the party's meeting with Vurakhal. He informs the party about the trolls' history — naming **Ulgrist**, whom both Alremm and Belsornig had tried to recruit, and describing how the trolls refused both sides, waited out the war, and scavenged the bodies afterward — and provides their location.

### Frostbrand
A sentient, opinionated magical short sword that serves as a translator between the party and Vurakhal. It enthusiastically embraces the party's cover story of overthrowing the temple from within, even attempting to adopt an 'evil face.'

### Air Temple Bugbears
Four guards dressed in the colors of the Air Temple who have moved into the Water Temple's former territory on Kelno's orders. They are confused but compliant when Calmer demands tribute, offering twenty gold pieces.

### Kelno
Prophet of the Air Temple and the party's ally — he took their deal rather than fight them in Chapter 30, and now serves as their number two in the Air Temple. That alliance is what makes this session's discovery pointed: his bugbears have quietly moved into the Water Temple's former holdings while the party was occupied elsewhere, annexing territory the party had just taken.

### Troll Chieftain (Ulgrist?)
A massive, particularly ugly troll who decorated his lair with the skulls and bones of his victims and wore bone armor. Varek Solain named the trolls' leader **Ulgrist** earlier in the session; whether this chieftain is Ulgrist himself was never established at the table. He was caught completely off guard and slain by Sequoia before he could take a single action.

### Minotaur
A creature disguised as a dirty, decorated statue sitting on a throne in the reception chamber at the end of the session, revealed by Sequoia's investigation just as the session ends.
