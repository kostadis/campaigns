# The Sleeper in the Iron Coffin

Date: 2026-08-30

## Summary

Four prisoners were still chained somewhere in the temple, and the party had left them without food for several days. When they finally reached the holding area, they found the chains torn apart and the captives gone. An Investigation check of 22 revealed that they had not escaped; their corpses had been claimed by the necromancer and added to his growing arsenal of the dead. The necromancer’s activities were becoming increasingly irritating, and the party resolved to track him down.

Zephyr proposed posting notices throughout the temple ordering anyone with information about the necromancer to report to the party. Calmer enthusiastically approved: “I think that’s a fantastic idea, Zephyr. As the Chief of Staff, I think you are responsible for all matters related to security operations. Produce an appropriate note, and I will sign it.” He then reconsidered and asked whether the initiative should properly belong to Zinnia as Chief of Security. Sequoia, meanwhile, was reminded that he was in charge of supplies — or, in his own words, “I’m in charge of getting shit done.” With Zinnia’s approval, Calmer instructed Zephyr to bring him something to sign.

Rather than immediately returning to their offices, the party chose to finish exploring the remaining sections of their domain. Sequoia declared, “I’m a completionist,” and led them west into a refuse-filled chamber that had once been a secluded place of relaxation. It was now heaped with rotting garbage, dung, bones, and skulls, with parts of a bronze-bound coffer visible near the center. Concerned that the necromancer would use the remains, the party decided to burn the entire room.

No one had a suitable fire spell — Calmer admitted this was a mistake, and laughed when Zephyr asked about Meteor Swarm — so Zephyr and Sequoia headed to the Fire Temple while Zinnia stayed behind to keep an eye on Calmer. Taking an unexplored dark corridor on the way, they emerged into familiar territory. Offered the choice of approaching Vurakhal directly, they went instead to the captain of the guard — Captain Varek Solain.

Varek Solain, no longer defeated in demeanor, received them and confirmed that the refuse room was where the Fire Temple habitually dumped its garbage. When told that a necromancer was quietly taking control of the skeletons, he finally connected the threat to an old political grievance. The necromancer had served under Barkinar and disappeared after Romag received the promotion he believed should have been his. Varek did not know where the necromancer was hiding — or even whether he remained inside the temple — but insisted that if undead were appearing, “he’s around.” Varek had warned Alrrem about the danger, but Alrrem had ignored him instead of finding the necromancer and making an alliance.

Zephyr pressed Varek to find the necromancer, only for the captain to answer, “With what army? I need supplies. I need food, I need troops,” noting that the party had taken his money and resources. He asked Sequoia, as head of logistics, to send someone to Nulb and repair the supply chain. In the meantime, he provided several fire arrows and explained their use: throw a flask of oil at the target, then shoot it with a burning arrow. He stressed that his supply was limited. Offered the fire arrows or Vurakhal’s help with the burning, Zephyr took the arrows; the party routed around their own salamander retainer a second time.

Zephyr then granted the Fire Temple control over the northern food depot. Varek was visibly delighted. “Food is always a good thing,” he said. “He who controls food controls everything.” Zephyr immediately corrected him: “We control the food, not you. You’re simply guarding it.” Varek accepted the distinction, saying it demonstrated trust in the Fire Temple, but could not resist proclaiming, “And fire shall burn again!” Zephyr responded, “Okay. Maybe don’t,” and suggested placing an Earth Temple infiltrator nearby to keep the Fire Temple in check.

When the party returned and set the refuse ablaze, a horrific creature emerged from the trash and furiously tried to douse the flames. The party chose to defend its territory rather than walk away. Calmer cast Hold Monster, successfully paralyzing the creature. Zinnia struck it repeatedly for automatic critical hits, Zephyr dealt 52 points in a devastating sneak attack, and Sequoia rolled twelve damage dice on a critical — only to average poorly and curse Frostbrand for having “one job.” The creature remained paralyzed through its saving throw. Calmer cast Toll the Dead and proudly remarked, “This new spell of mine is very effective.” Zephyr eventually landed another 57-point critical and killed the creature. The party stepped outside and allowed the refuse chamber to burn for several hours.

Continuing onward, they found another room filled with broken containers whose disturbed litter showed recent traffic, probably from people carrying off the remaining foodstuffs. The temple’s food supplies were plainly running low, and nobody was currently managing them. Beyond that lay an oddly shaped chamber packed with jars, boxes, barrels, crates, chests, sacks, casks, bales, bags, and hampers. Some were broken or empty, but many remained full. Bales held black cloth and finished garments. Zinnia’s Insight roll of 24 identified these as supplies and uniforms belonging to the Greater, or Deep, Temple.

The party’s Arcana checks failed to reveal anything further. Sequoia nevertheless looked up, explaining, “The answer is always on the ceiling.” An Investigation result of 21 vindicated him: four creatures hung overhead by their tails, claws poised. “I was right,” Sequoia said in disbelief. “I was saying, as a joke, oh my god.”

The creatures were nightmarish, gaunt, ape-like predators with ash-gray hides, four long muscular arms ending in hooked black claws, powerful jaws filled with uneven fangs, three alert eyes, and thick prehensile tails wrapped around the stonework. Their limbs were unnaturally jointed for climbing and ambushing, and faint violet psychic energy pulsed around their skulls.

Zephyr acted first but missed both pistol shots. Zinnia and Sequoia concentrated their attacks on the same creature, while the predators unleashed psychic blasts against the entire party. Zinnia, Zephyr, and Calmer resisted; Sequoia failed the DC 17 Wisdom save, suffered 17 points of psychic damage, and was stunned. Calmer cast Spirit Guardians, creating a fifteen-foot aura that damaged and slowed several attackers.

Zephyr’s next pistol attack, including sneak-attack damage, killed the already wounded predator. The others swarmed Calmer with as many as five claw attacks apiece, but his armor class of 21 — bolstered by the new Shield +1 — allowed only one blow through. He suffered 8 slashing damage and lost concentration on Spirit Guardians, while another creature missed him five times in succession. Calmer healed Sequoia after the wounded logistics chief pointedly suggested that the cleric could save him, then used Word of Radiance against the enemies clustered around him.

Zinnia slew a second creature, while Calmer continued alternating healing and radiant blasts. “Are you okay, Sequoia?” he called. When Sequoia answered, “I guess,” Calmer replied, “My guess is not an answer, yes or no.” Sequoia finally answered, “Maybe,” and warned, “Just don’t blame me when your logistics breaks down.” Calmer cast another Healing Word, restoring 14 hit points, then struck both nearby predators with another Word of Radiance.

Zephyr used Chill Touch against one survivor, preventing it from regaining hit points, while Zinnia, newly reminded of his Stunning Strike technique, and Sequoia continued the assault. The two remaining predators finally disengaged and fled to a shadowed corner. Zephyr warned them, “You know where the boss is, okay? So, don’t ever… try to… anything again.” He then tried Undercommon, the one relevant tongue he has; no one in the party had Deep Speech, which is what these aberrations would have answered to. The creatures merely sneered, howled, hissed, and curled into the darkness.

An exhausted and flustered Dren Halveth arrived shortly afterward, notepad in hand, to deliver a perfectly deadpan summary of the temple’s cascading administrative disasters. Addressing Zephyr, he reported, “I’ve been doing the supply check. The Minotaur has food for 3 days. The men have pay for 4. The dead have started leaving without permission. I can solve any one of these. I cannot solve all three while pretending this is a normal command.”

Dren explained that supplies ordinarily came through Nulb, meaning someone would have to travel there and restore the supply chain. The party issued its orders: assign the armor depot to the Water Temple, assign the firewood depot to the Air Temple, burn corpses before the necromancer could claim them, and feed usable bodies to the Minotaur. Dren requested permission to use the Fire Temple’s salamander to help with the burning, and the party agreed. He also reminded them that Barkinar was still waiting for a decision about the Chest of Rao.

The group resolved to travel to Nulb the following morning. Dren was left with broad authority to keep order and the Minotaur fed, though Sequoia warned him, “We trust you, but if you betray that trust, you know…” They retired to their quarters, where Calmer relaxed with evident satisfaction and admitted, “I like being in charge.”

That evening, as the party drank and rested, an illusory image of a hooded man appeared. “Forgive the theatrics,” he began. “I cannot be seen sending a runner, and you cannot afford to be seen receiving one.” The figure’s message and the party’s previous knowledge identified him through implication as Falrinth, a wizard hiding from the Greater Temple whom Barkinar had considered important enough to mention.

Falrinth warned them about Lucius Graeme: “The flattering man has already been to you. Golden eyes, soft hands, a great deal of wine. He gave you presents and asked you what you were short of. Lucius Graeme, write the name down. He collects the ones he gives you.” Lucius had already visited the party carrying gifts, and Falrinth advised them not to commit when he returned, but to stall him.

Falrinth then revealed that he possessed the Orb of Golden Death. “The orb this temple frets over, yes! I have it. I’m not giving it to Hedrack, nor to Senshock, nor to any of the holy gentlemen who would like me dead. I’m not giving it to you either, but that is a separate conversation.” When Sequoia asked how he had acquired it, Falrinth answered, “That is a conversation for another year.” Romag had previously described the fully assembled orb as a source of tremendous power capable of summoning monsters and destroying virtually anything, raising the question of why Falrinth possessed it but did not appear to be using it.

Falrinth next turned his attention to Calmer’s scarab, inscribed T-Z-G-Y. “Yes, you should be more, far more careful with it than you have been. Lucius will know it on sight. Senshock will know it on sight. They will use it against you in different ways, and neither will tell you they notice.” He also revealed that Lareth had been his friend and said, “I’m told you handled him with discretion, which I appreciate more than you would expect. But there are other friends, and not all of them are as patient as I am.”

Falrinth explained how to reach him: “A candle in the West will reach me.” Reporting him to Barkinar was another option, but Falrinth predicted that Barkinar would believe the party and simply fail to find the wizard where they claimed he was. Either decision would reveal the party’s choice of allegiance.

As the illusion began to fade, Falrinth delivered his most chilling revelation: “There is a room below, deep, forgotten, on the level beneath your feet. The holy gentleman keeps something in it. A sleeper. My leash is short, and the gates are watched. But you, you have keys I never did. If you ever want Verbobonc and all its banners to come down on this terrible… hell… on this temple, and oh, they would come for this, wake what sleeps in the iron coffin. Ask your golden friend what he’s so afraid you’ll find, then signal me.”

Only after trying to question him did the party realize that the projection was a one-way message whose creator had merely anticipated some of their likely responses. It faded away, leaving the party with the revelation that Falrinth either possessed the Orb of Golden Death or was lying about having it, and that something beneath their feet could provoke a full military response from Verbobonc.

The next morning, the party traveled to Nulb dressed discreetly, choosing not to announce their new status as lords of the temple. Zephyr summarized their philosophy as “Power and subtlety.” Calmer approved: “Good choice. This is why I have made you Chief of Staff.” Zephyr added, “We don’t have to say that we’re powerful, we just know,” prompting Calmer to puff himself up and answer, “We just know. I like it!”

Smoke lay low over the river. The same barges leaned against the same rotting piers, and men at the tavern doors continued arguing over which elemental prophet would win a war that had ended beneath their feet ten days earlier. Nulb had not changed, which was how the party knew nobody had told it.

On the first muddy stretch of the river road, a Rhennee child from the river folk the party had previously aided recognized them and ran shouting, “FAMILY HAS RETURNED!” As they entered the village, a dock hand pressed a folded note into Sequoia’s palm. It read, “Skole says the captain is still telling people you’re dead. He thought you might enjoy the update.” Captain Tolub — who had previously tried to kill them — remained convinced, or wanted everyone else convinced, that the party was dead. The game, it seemed, was far from over.

## NPCs

### The Minotaur

Installed in Chapter 33 as guard of the ruined reception hall, in exchange for regular meals and protection from black-coated bandits. Its own tongue is Abyssal, though it understands and speaks broken Common. It can eat undead flesh but prefers fresh meat. This session it is a standing logistics problem rather than a presence on stage: Dren Halveth reports it has food for three days, and is left with authority to keep it fed. Its agreement carries a direct threat to find, kill, and eat the party if it is attacked and they fail to protect it.

An unseen antagonist who left a mocking note for the party, claiming the temple as his home and vowing to use every corpse they create against them. ### Alrrem

The former head of the Fire Temple, now deceased. Varek Solain says he explicitly warned Alrrem about the necromancer and urged caution, but Alrrem ignored the threat rather than locating the necromancer and attempting an alliance.

### Dren Halveth

An exhausted and flustered administrator who manages the temple’s day-to-day logistics. He arrives carrying a notepad and reports three simultaneous crises: the Minotaur has food for three days, the men have pay for four, and the dead are leaving without permission. He explains that the temple depends upon supplies from Nulb and cannot manage every crisis while pretending the command is normal. The party leaves him in charge with orders to feed usable corpses to the Minotaur, burn the rest with help from the Fire Temple salamander, and maintain order. Sequoia grants him trust while making the consequences of betrayal clear.

### Psychic Predators

Four gaunt, four-armed, ape-like creatures with ash-gray hide, three eyes, hook claws, powerful jaws, and prehensile tails. Their limbs are unnaturally jointed for climbing and ambush, and violet psychic energy pulses around their skulls. They clung upside down to the Greater Temple storeroom’s ceiling and attacked with both psychic blasts and as many as five claw strikes at once. Their psychic power stunned Sequoia and dealt 17 damage. Two were slain; the other two disengaged, retreated to a shadowed corner, and rejected Zephyr’s attempted warning with sneers, howls, and hisses.

### Falrinth

A wizard hiding from the Greater Temple who communicated through a one-way illusory hooded projection. Barkinar had previously mentioned him as an important figure, though without explaining why. Falrinth claims to possess the Orb of Golden Death and refuses to surrender it to Hedrack, Senshock, the party, or the temple’s other powerful factions. He warned the party about Lucius Graeme, cautioned Calmer about his T-Z-G-Y scarab, and revealed that Lareth was his friend. He can allegedly be contacted with “a candle in the West.” Falrinth also revealed the existence of a sleeper in an iron coffin on the level below and suggested that waking it would provoke a full response from Verbobonc.

### Lucius Graeme

Described by Falrinth as a flattering man with golden eyes, soft hands, and “a great deal of wine.” He has already visited the party bearing gifts and asking what they lack. Falrinth claims Lucius catalogs what he gives and later “collects” the recipients, implying that every gift creates leverage or an obligation. He will recognize Calmer’s scarab immediately but conceal that knowledge. Falrinth advises the party to avoid making commitments and stall him when he returns.

### Captain Tolub

A captain who previously attempted to kill the party and is currently telling people in Nulb that they are dead. The information reaches the party through Skole’s covert message, giving them an opportunity to exploit Tolub’s false belief or confront his continuing deception.

### Skole

An informant in Nulb who passes a covert note to Sequoia through a dock hand. The message warns that Captain Tolub is still telling people the party is dead and dryly suggests that Sequoia might enjoy the update.

## Locations

### Prisoner Area

A section of the temple where four captives had been left chained without food for several days. The party found it empty, with the chains torn apart. Investigation indicated that the prisoners had not escaped; their corpses had been taken by the necromancer.

### Garbage Room

A rotted chamber once used as a secluded place of relaxation, now a heap of stinking refuse, dung, bones, and skulls. Parts of a bronze-bound coffer were visible in the central pile. The Fire Temple had traditionally used the room as a dumping ground. The party incinerated it to deny the necromancer use of the remains, drawing out a furious creature that tried to extinguish the flames.

### Fire Temple

A section of the temple complex under the command of Captain Varek Solain. Its forces are short of food, troops, money, and equipment, and depend on supplies routed through Nulb. The party obtained fire arrows here and delegated control of the northern food depot to Varek, while considering the placement of an Earth Temple observer to keep him in check.

### Greater Temple Storeroom

An oddly shaped chamber crammed with jars, boxes, barrels, crates, chests, sacks, casks, bales, bags, and hampers. Many containers remain full, and bales hold black cloth and finished Greater Temple garments. Four psychic predators were hiding on its ceiling. Two were killed, while two remain curled in the shadows.

### Temple Quarters

The party’s resting area and effective command center. Calmer has become notably comfortable there and openly says that he likes being in charge. Falrinth’s illusory projection appeared while the party was drinking and relaxing for the evening.

### Nulb

A muddy river village with smoke lying low over the water, old barges leaning at rotting piers, and tavern patrons still debating which elemental prophet will win a war that ended ten days earlier. Its ignorance of the temple’s changed leadership makes it strategically valuable. The party returns to repair the temple’s supply chain and receives a covert update about Captain Tolub.
