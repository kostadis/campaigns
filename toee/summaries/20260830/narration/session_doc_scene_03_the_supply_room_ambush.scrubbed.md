Broken containers covered the floor of the first chamber. Splintered crates, cracked jars, sacks cut open and scraped clean. Recent footprints crossed through the litter.

The remaining food had been carried away. The Temple’s supplies were low, and nobody had taken charge of what remained. A failing supply chain inside a three-way war. Useful.

“We should probably fix that,” Sequoia said.

The next chamber was wider and badly proportioned, its corners crowded beyond sightlines. Jars stood beside boxes, barrels beside chests, sacks against casks. Bales and hampers filled the gaps. Some had been broken open. Many were still sealed.

Black cloth showed through one split bale. Beneath it lay finished cloaks.

I knew them immediately. Greater Temple equipment. Deep Temple issue.

Zephyr touched the edge of the cloth. “Our equipment?”

Not ours. Not yet. But the route between the Upper Temple and whatever waited below had left material evidence in front of us. Someone had stocked this chamber for agents moving through the complex. Food, garments, cover. Logistics revealed command more reliably than banners did.

The rest of the room gave me nothing. No active magic. No mark I recognized. Too many containers, too many blind angles.

We went farther in.

Sequoia stopped and tilted his head back.

“I look at the ceiling, because the answer is always on the ceiling.”

Four tails were wrapped around cracked stone beams above us.

The creatures clung upside down among the shadows: gaunt bodies, ash-gray hides, four long arms apiece. Black hooks tipped their fingers. Their jaws opened on uneven fangs, and three eyes tracked us from each skull. Violet pulses moved around their heads and disturbed the air.

They saw us seeing them.

They hissed.

“Okay,” Zephyr said.

“I was right.”

Sequoia kept staring upward.

“Hmm.”

Then the fact caught up with him. “No way. I was saying it as a joke, oh my god.”

There was no surprise left for the predators to exploit. The joke had secured the room better than our search.

Zephyr drew his pistol. “Dude, what are these creatures?”

The violet light brightened.

“Bruh.”

“What the freak?” Sequoia said.

The predators dropped.

Zephyr fired first. Both shots missed as the creatures twisted through the descending smoke.

I met the nearest one before its feet touched stone. My body moved into the opening: strike, turn, reorient. Sequoia came in on the same target, Frostbrand cutting hard twice before he slipped beyond the reach of those hooked claws.

Pressure struck my mind.

Cold insistence. Hunger without language. It pushed for the place behind thought where the body forgets its orders.

I held. Zephyr held. Calmer held.

Sequoia went rigid. His arms dropped, his eyes fixed, and he stopped in the middle of his escape.

Calmer looked at him. “I'm very impressed. I am very impressed. May the blessing of the Earth God be with you.”

The words landed cleanly. Earth God. Our cover had an answer for everything now, even gratitude.

Calmer called up Spirit Guardians. Radiance filled the crowded chamber, circling him in a broad killing field. The predators caught inside it slowed, their movements dragging against the light.

“When in doubt, reach for the best.”

Zephyr found the wounded one and shot it through the opening Sequoia and I had made. It fell among the crates.

The others converged on Calmer.

One attacked with every limb it could bring to bear. Claws struck plate, shield, stone. Another joined it and found nothing but metal. Calmer stood at the center of the assault until one hook slipped through, scored him, and broke his hold on the guardians. The radiance vanished.

A third predator caught me across the side. The blow landed without depth. I adjusted my stance.

Sequoia remained trapped inside his own body.

“Or, you know, he could kill me.”

Calmer turned toward him.

“Brother.”

Healing crossed the room and reached Sequoia. Calmer followed it with Word of Radiance, light bursting around the creatures that had surrounded him.

Zephyr shifted his dagger into position. “Well… okay, I'll come with my dagger, finally.”

He reconsidered. “Or actually, no, I will use a spell, maybe.”

Then: “Actually, I will attack it. Spells are kind of useless.”

He left the second predator barely standing.

I took it.

One step inside the hooked arms. One strike to turn the skull, another to finish the movement. It collapsed. I stepped clear.

Sequoia dragged himself free of the psychic hold. His first breath sounded like it hurt.

“Finally made it.”

The surviving predators were still hungry. Still committed. One came at me and missed twice. The other kept near Calmer, searching for another breach in the plate.

Calmer shouted, “Are you okay, Sequoia?”

“I guess…”

“My guess is not an answer. Yes or no.”

“Maybe.”

“Go do whatever you want.”

Calmer did not. “Here, just take another heal.”

“Just don't blame me when your logistics breaks down.”

More healing settled into Sequoia. His shoulders lowered.

“Is that better?”

“Yeah…”

Calmer answered the surrounding claws with another Word of Radiance. Zephyr reached for Chill Touch; spectral force closed around one predator and left it unable to feed strength back into itself.

I remembered another option held in reserve: the focused strike that could lock a body in place, or at least slow it enough to make the next attack certain. I had not needed it. The survivors had read the room before I could use it.

Both withdrew into the same dark corner. Tails curled close. Three eyes apiece watched us from behind stacked casks.

“That's good, though,” Zephyr said.

They hissed. We held the open ground.

“Boo.”

Zephyr pointed toward them. “You know where the boss is, okay? So don't ever try anything again.”

The creatures sneered.

“I speak Undercommon.”

They answered with howls and wet hissing, then folded themselves deeper into the shadows.

Wrong language. Clear message.
