I was a completionist.

“Yeah.”

There were rooms left. That was enough. At the next split I chose west.

The chamber had once been painted. Maybe people had rested there. Now the paint peeled over rot, and the floor had disappeared beneath garbage, dung, bones, and skulls. The smell settled on my tongue. Near the center, bronze bands showed through the refuse.

“Big pile of bodies.”

I looked again.

“Just a bunch of bodies.”

Zephyr studied the bones. “You can use that for necromancy, right?”

“Okay, let's burn everything.”

Good answer.

We had no door to shut. No easy way to contain anything that stood up while we were arranging the fire.

“Leave the door locked.”

There was no door.

Calmer checked what he had prepared and admitted he had no fire spell. A mistake. I had none either.

“No.”

“Do we have a— Meteor, whatever it's called?” Zephyr asked.

“No, we don't.”

Calmer laughed. “Meteor Swarm, no. No, no, we do not have one of those.”

“Really? Not even in, like, the lower temple?”

Apparently our control of the Temple did not yet include dropping the sky on a garbage heap.

“Let's find some Fire Temple guys, no?” Zephyr said. “Salamander.”

He wanted two of us left behind in case the bones moved. Calmer claimed he could stay alone. Master of his domain. New Fire Temple chief. Safe.

Zinnia started to agree.

“No.”

“You know? Okay, I'll stay with him,” Zinnia said. “Yeah, I'll watch what he does.”

Good.

Zephyr and I took the dark corridor because it was unexplored. It brought us back near the old skeleton and a route we knew. The brown marble carried heat from farther south. Something struck stone beyond the walls. The war continued without requiring us.

We could have gone to Vurakhal.

“Go to the captain of the guard,” Zephyr said. “Varek Solain.”

Captain Varek Solain looked better than the last time I had seen him. Less defeated. Still surrounded by a Fire Temple that had lost.

“What's the problem?”

“We need to burn something?”

“You need to burn something, and you have come to the Temple of Fire. For fire. For burning fire.” He waited. “What do you need to burn?”

“Pile of trash. Just a pile of trash.”

“A pile of trash.” He considered it. “Oh, you must mean that area to the north.”

“Dude, no.”

I checked the direction in my head.

“Yeah?”

“Yeah, so just so you know, that's where we throw out all the garbage. I mean, if you're gonna burn all the trash—” Varek stopped. “I guess that makes sense. Bosses, why are you burning all the trash? We just sort of throw it there, and it kind of gets eaten away.”

“Well, there is a necromancer,” Zephyr said. “That is quietly taking control of all the skeletons. And you cannot have that.”

“I guess you're in charge now, right, Zephyr?”

“Dude?”

Varek told us about the necromancer. He had worked for Barkinar, wanted the position Romag received, and disappeared when he lost it.

“But if you've been seeing undead show up, he's around. And I told Alrrem — I told him there was a necromancer, I told him to be careful, I told him. And he didn't listen. And instead of going and finding that necromancer and making an alliance—”

He stopped himself.

“Whatever. The war is over. So be it.”

“Well, you better find him fast,” Zephyr said.

“With what army? I need supplies. I need food, I need troops, I need— I'm sorry, man, I don't have the resources for that right now. I'll get to it, I promise.”

“Well, do we have money and resources to go to?”

“You took all my money and resources, dude!”

Fair.

Zephyr looked at me. “I talked to my friends. Logistics. You're the head of logistics.”

Apparently that had become a real position.

“I'm in charge of getting shit done.”

“What do you need?”

Varek paused. “I need somebody to go back to Nulb and sort out the supply chain issues.”

“Let's— let's go to Nulb.”

Another obligation. Fine.

Varek gave us a small bundle of fire arrows. “Alright — but in the meantime, since you said you needed fire— Here's a few arrows of fire. What you do is, you take a flask of oil, right?”

“Yeah?”

“You throw the flask of oil at the target, and then you shoot with the burning arrow.”

“Okay.”

“Okay,” Zephyr said.

“Now, I want you to know, I have a limited supply of these.”

Zephyr took them. “I'll take the fire.”

Still no salamander.

Then Zephyr gave Fire Temple the northern food depot.

“You will have control of the depot up north here. The food one, yes. Take good care of that. Protected.”

Varek smiled. He wanted the food.

“Food… food is always a good thing. He who controls food controls everything.”

“Okay, don't get too hasty here. Let me control—” Zephyr corrected himself. “We control the food, not you. You're simply guarding it?”

“Okay, alright. But it shows your trust for the Fire Temple.”

“Yes, yes. That's true.”

“And fire shall burn again!”

“Hmm,” Zephyr said. “Okay. Maybe don't.”

“Really? Yeah.”

Zephyr proposed placing an Earth Temple man near the depot.

“We'll get some Earth Temple guy to infiltrate. So, just to keep in check.”

We had assigned food, guards, oversight, and a supply mission while trying to set fire to rubbish.

Logistics.

Back at the Garbage Room, I soaked the heap in oil and nocked one of Varek’s arrows.

“Yep.”

The fire caught. Something horrific rose from inside the refuse and tried to smother it. It had not expected us. We could have left.

“We have to protect the territories, no,” Zephyr said.

Our territory.

Calmer began, “You dare to—” Then he cast Hold Monster.

The creature stopped. Muscles locked. Fury stayed alive in its eyes while the rest of it refused to move.

Zinnia hit it twice. Zephyr found the opening and drove his blade in deep.

My turn.

“I hit—”

Frostbrand struck cleanly. The trapped creature could not turn, block, or pull away.

“Oh, I can roll off crit damage.”

The blade should have done more.

“Oh, I need to still roll another 5— HOLY DICE!”

I stared at the result.

“WHAT THE F!”

Then again, because the first attempt had not covered it.

“What the fu—”

“The di— SHIT!”

I counted twice.

“Effective damage in total on my critical.”

“No, no.”

“36, sorry. You wanna know how many dice I rolled?”

“12!”

“12 of that damage!”

It was insulting.

“You want the total amount of damage I dealt with?”

“I averaged two.”

“Average of bloody two, bro!”

“What the fuck!”

I looked down at Frostbrand.

“You had one job.”

Calmer rang Toll the Dead through the room. “This new spell of mine is very effective.”

The creature strained against Hold Monster. Nothing moved except its eyes. Zephyr stepped in again and finished it.

“Bruh…”

Calmer looked pleased.

We stepped outside. Fire filled the chamber and worked through the refuse, the bones, and whatever remained of the bronze-bound coffer. Smoke rolled along the ceiling but the blaze stayed contained.

“There's no way it moves out and, like, burns itself?” Zephyr asked.

It did not.

“Let's walk away — it's our territory now.”
