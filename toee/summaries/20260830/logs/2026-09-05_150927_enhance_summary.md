# The Sleeper in the Iron Coffin

Date: 2026-08-30

## Summary
The party stood at the threshold of a ruined reception hall, its walls adorned with faded murals and its floors littered with the bones of the long dead. At the center of the room, seated upon a throne and caked in filth, was what appeared to be a decorative statue of a Minotaur — until Sequoia noticed the slow, deliberate rise and fall of its chest. He quietly pulled the door shut before the creature could spot them, and the group huddled together to decide their next move.

Unable to speak Abyssal, the party made themselves understood through sign language and the creature's broken Common, then chose a bold gambit: they would walk in with Calmer dressed as the Earth Cleric and Zephyr announcing that they were the Minotaur's new leadership. The creature, it turned out, harbored a deep and violent hatred for the temple's former masters, and the announcement sent it into a thunderous rage. It charged Zephyr with terrifying speed, goring him savagely and hurling him to the ground, then loomed over the prone rogue with a double-headed axe and murderous intent. Zinnia moved to strike the creature but failed to land a blow, and it was Calmer who stepped forward with a calm and persuasive voice, somehow managing to give the beast pause — though it pointedly gestured at the fallen Zephyr as if sizing him up for a meal.

Battered and lying on the cold stone floor, Zephyr pleaded that they were different, then offered the creature food from the party's packs. The Minotaur, grumbling and suspicious, began to eat. With its hunger momentarily satisfied, it grumbled of black-coated bandits who had come and attacked it, and declared it would guard the reception hall in exchange for regular food and protection from them. It turned its gaze back to Zephyr and said, with surprising casualness, "No hard feelings" — before adding that if the bandits came again and the party failed to protect it, it would find them, kill them, and eat them. The truce was struck, and the party quietly noted they now had a very large, very hungry guard on their payroll. Zephyr had to physically pull Calmer aside to stop the cleric from demanding the Minotaur formally acknowledge a new clerical order with the high lord Master Calmer presiding over it.

As the group moved deeper into the temple, Calmer began to reveal a troubling shift in his thinking. When the subject of feeding the Minotaur arose again, he calmly suggested that the prisoners still held captive somewhere in the temple could solve the problem neatly — two birds, one stone. He reminded his companions that they were now the evil overlords of the temple, and that freeing good people was not exactly in keeping with their new station. When Sequoia challenged him, Calmer airily reflected on his growing disillusionment with the Church of St. Cuthbert, noting that Zinnia's surrender of a magical trident as payment for his return had demonstrated a loyalty he intended to honor in kind. He insisted he was not yet an evil man, but admitted that certain reading material had put a different perspective on the endless prattle of St. Cuthbert — and he had already begun scribbling notes toward a new educational system inspired by the principles of the Earth Temple.

The party pressed on and discovered a series of storerooms that revealed just how well supplied the temple truly was. One room held stacks of lumber and firewood, while a second — its lock no match for Sequoia's skilled hands — contained a remarkable cache of equipment: chain mail, plate armor, backpacks, holy water, lanterns, lockpicks, mirrors, parchment, rope, sacks, spikes, and tinderboxes. Zephyr, as Chief of Staff, resolved to delegate the cataloguing to others, while Calmer surveyed the rotting remains of a food storage area and declared the mess absolutely unacceptable. He proposed that Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades be brought in to clean it up, and Zephyr agreed, proposing that each of the elemental temples be assigned control of one of the supply depots — accidentally counting the Water Temple twice, prompting Sequoia to needle him. Calmer, meanwhile, pulled out a scrap of scroll and began drafting a chaotic new curriculum of St. Cuthbert recast along Earth Temple lines, before anyone could stop him.

The exploration was cut short when a massive, demented ghoul lurched out of the shadows and fixed its hollow gaze on the living warmth of the party. Zinnia immediately recognized the handiwork of a necromancer — the same one responsible for the undead that had plagued the temple before, now raising far more dangerous creatures than previously seen. The battle was brutal. Sequoia struck first from hiding, and Zephyr followed with his pistol. The ghoul sank its teeth into Calmer, and the cleric crumpled, paralyzed by the bite. Sequoia took a raking blow from its claws but shrugged off the paralysis, while Zinnia — having, as a masked wanderer, smelled and tasted worse things in his life — resisted the creature's foul, poisonous stench and delivered a series of devastating blows even while fighting at a disadvantage. Sequoia continued to press the attack with his dagger until Zinnia finally put the ghoul down for good.

When the party searched the corpse, they found something unexpected: a note addressed directly to the four of them. It was written by a necromancer who called the temple his home and the party interlopers and thieves, thanking them with cold sarcasm for the harvest of death they had left in their wake — every corpse they had created was a resource he intended to use against them. The note went on at length to specifically mock Calmer as a disgrace, unqualified to be head of the Earth Temple, his approach to the rituals all wrong. Calmer read it with an expression of mild offense, then folded it carefully and tucked it away. Piecing together what they knew, the party formed a hypothesis: a figure connected to the transition between Barkinar and Romag's leadership had likely been passed over and had disappeared into the temple's depths, nursing a bitter resentment — and the party's trail of bodies had given him more material to work with than ever before.

The group's next discovery came in an oddly shaped chamber where a low stone wall held back the temple's water supply. Zephyr stepped inside to investigate and immediately slipped on the algae-slicked, subtly sloping floor, sliding hard into the far wall. Calmer threw him a rope, and once steadied, Zephyr spotted a large, round, slime-covered object resting at the bottom of the pool. He tied the rope around himself, took a deep breath, and dove — his first attempt nearly dislodging the object further into the murk, but on the second dive he barely seized it and wrenched it free just before his lungs gave out. When the slime was cleared away, the object revealed itself to be a finely crafted magical shield, which Calmer accepted with great satisfaction.

When the party finally reached the area where the prisoners had been held, they found the chains torn apart and the captives gone — not escaped, but taken. Their corpses had been claimed by the necromancer, added to his growing arsenal of the dead. The party resolved to track him down, with Calmer insisting that Zephyr draft a notice to be posted throughout the temple, which he would then sign. They continued their exploration, discovering a refuse-filled chamber that had once been a place of relaxation but was now a heap of stinking garbage, bones, and skulls. Concerned the necromancer would use the remains, the party decided to burn the entire room. Lacking fire of their own, Zephyr and Sequoia headed to the Fire Temple while Zinnia stayed behind with Calmer.

Captain Varek Solain of the Fire Temple, no longer defeated in demeanor, received them and revealed that the necromancer had once served under Barkinar and had disappeared after being passed over for promotion — and that Alrrem had been warned about the threat but had failed to act. Zephyr granted the Fire Temple control over the northern food supply depot, greatly pleasing Varek Solain, who could not resist declaring that fire would burn again. He provided the party with fire arrows and oil to carry out the incineration. When the party returned and set the refuse ablaze, a furious creature emerged from the trash, desperately trying to douse the flames. Calmer stepped forward and held it in place with a powerful incantation, leaving it completely helpless as Zinnia, Zephyr, and Sequoia delivered devastating blows, and Calmer finished it with a burst of necrotic energy. The party stepped outside and let the room burn to the ground.

Pressing deeper, the party entered an oddly shaped chamber crammed with barrels, crates, and bales of black cloth — equipment belonging to the Greater Temple. Before anyone could investigate further, Sequoia looked up at the ceiling and spotted four nightmarish creatures clinging upside down by their prehensile tails: gaunt, four-armed predators with ash-gray hide, hook claws, three alert eyes, and violet psychic energy pulsing around their skulls. The creatures dropped and attacked, unleashing blasts of psychic force that stunned Sequoia and battered the party's minds. Calmer summoned a swirling aura of protective spirits and released bursts of radiant energy, while Zephyr and Zinnia focused their strikes to bring the creatures down one by one. After two of their number were slain, the remaining two retreated hissing into the shadows, and Zephyr's attempt to communicate with them in Undercommon was met only with sneering silence.

An exhausted Dren Halveth arrived shortly after, notepad in hand, to deliver a perfectly deadpan summary of the temple's cascading administrative disasters: the Minotaur had food for only three days, the men had pay for four days, and the dead had started leaving without permission. The party issued their orders — feed usable bodies to the Minotaur, burn the rest, assign the armor depot to the Water Temple and the firewood depot to the Air Temple — and resolved to travel to Nulb the following morning to fix the broken supply chain. Dren was left in charge, with the pointed reminder from Sequoia that betrayal would not be tolerated.

That evening, as the party rested, an illusory image of a hooded man appeared before them. He identified himself through implication as Falrinth, a wizard hiding from the Greater Temple, and warned them about Lucius Graeme — a flattering man with golden eyes and soft hands who arrived bearing gifts and secretly catalogued everything he gave in order to collect favors later. Falrinth revealed he possessed the Orb of Golden Death and had no intention of surrendering it to any of the temple's factions. He warned Calmer to guard his scarab carefully, as powerful enemies like Senshock would recognize it on sight and use it against him. Before fading, Falrinth delivered his most chilling revelation: on the level beneath their feet lay a forgotten room containing a sleeper locked in an iron coffin, and waking it would bring the forces of Verbobonc crashing down upon the temple. He urged them to ask their golden friend what he was so afraid they would find — then signal him. The illusion dissolved, leaving the party with far more questions than answers.

The next morning, the party traveled to Nulb dressed discreetly, choosing not to announce their new status as lords of the temple. The muddy river village was unchanged, its dockworkers still arguing over which elemental prophet would win a war that had already ended beneath their feet. A child from the Rhennee river folk recognized them and ran shouting to announce their return, while a dock hand pressed a folded note into Sequoia's hand from an informant named Skole. The note carried a simple, pointed update: Captain Tolub was still telling people the party was dead. The game, it seemed, was far from over.

I stand guard,” it explained, so long as the food continued and the bandits did not return. It turned its gaze back to Zephyr and said, with surprising casualness, “No hard feelings?” — before adding, “However, if bandit come attack, I find you, I kill you, I eat you.” The truce was struck, and the party quietly noted that they now had a very large, very hungry guard on their payroll. Zephyr had to physically pull Calmer aside to stop the cleric from demanding that the Minotaur formally acknowledge a new clerical order with the high lord Master Calmer presiding over it.

As the group moved deeper into the temple, Calmer began to reveal a troubling shift in his thinking. When the subject of feeding the Minotaur arose again, he calmly suggested that the four prisoners still held captive somewhere in the temple could solve the problem neatly — two birds, one stone. “Don’t forget,” he reminded them, “we are now the evil overlords of this temple. Freeing good people is not exactly something we should be doing.” When Sequoia asked whether Calmer was trying to have his clerical powers taken away, the cleric airily dismissed that as a common misconception, claiming that once a cleric reached a certain level of power, that power became intrinsic.

Calmer openly reflected on his growing disillusionment with the Church of St. Cuthbert. Zinnia’s surrender of a magical trident to Terjon as payment for his return had demonstrated loyalty that Calmer intended to honor in kind. “Loyalty is a two-way street,” he said. “You have demonstrated loyalty, and I will demonstrate loyalty to you. As for St. Cuthbert and I, we have a good working relationship.” He insisted that he was not yet an evil man, but admitted that his recent reading had put “a different perspective on the endless prattle of St. Cuthbert.” He had already begun scribbling notes toward a new educational system inspired by Earth Temple principles. Frostbrand, the party’s sentient sword, also reminded Sequoia that their plan had always been to remain discreet and avoid drawing attention to themselves.

The party pressed on and discovered a series of storerooms that revealed just how well supplied the temple truly was. One room held stacks of lumber and firewood, while a second — its lock no match for Sequoia’s exceptional roll of 33 — contained a remarkable cache of equipment: chain mail, plate armor, backpacks, holy water, lanterns, lockpicks, mirrors, parchment, rope, sacks, spikes, and tinderboxes. Zephyr, acting as Chief of Operations, resolved to delegate the cataloguing: “We’ll get some other guys to do this.” Calmer surveyed the rotting remains of a former food-storage area and declared the mess absolutely unacceptable. He proposed that Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades be brought in to clean it up, and Zephyr agreed that Dren was fond of manual labor.

Zephyr proposed assigning an individual supply room to each elemental temple, accidentally counting the Water Temple twice. Sequoia immediately observed that somebody had not passed school. Calmer responded by pulling out a scrap of scroll and making notes for his chaotic Earth Temple educational system before anyone could stop him.

The exploration was cut short when a massive, demented ghoul lurched out of the shadows and fixed its hollow gaze on the living warmth of the party. Zinnia immediately recognized the handiwork of a necromancer — the same one responsible for the undead that had plagued the temple before, now raising far more dangerous creatures than previously seen. The battle was brutal. Sequoia struck first from hiding for 29 points of damage. Zephyr followed with his pistol, fumbling his first shot with a natural 1 but landing the second for roughly 31 points with his assassin’s bonus.

The ghoul sank its teeth into Calmer for 21 points of damage, and the cleric crumpled, paralyzed by the bite. Sequoia suffered 8 points from its claws but shrugged off the paralysis. Zinnia rolled a natural 20 to resist the creature’s foul, poisonous stench — having, as a masked wanderer, smelled and tasted worse things in his life — and delivered a series of devastating blows even while poisoned and fighting at disadvantage. Sequoia continued to press the attack with his dagger until Zinnia finally put the ghoul down for good.

When the party searched the corpse, they found something unexpected: a note addressed directly to the four of them. It was written by a necromancer who called the temple his home and the party interlopers and thieves, thanking them with cold sarcasm for the harvest of death they had left in their wake. Every corpse they had created was a resource he intended to use against them. “My god, this person’s busy,” Calmer observed as the lengthy note went on to mock him specifically as a disgrace, unqualified to be head of the Earth Temple, and entirely wrong in his approach to its rituals. Calmer read it with mild offense, then carefully folded it and tucked it away.

Piecing together what they knew — with Zinnia rolling a 21 and Zephyr a natural 20 — the party formed a hypothesis. Romag had previously muttered that the undead on the first level no longer reported to him, suggesting that they once reported to someone. Barkinar had led the Earth Temple before being promoted to the upper levels, after which Romag had taken his place. A figure connected to that transition had likely been passed over and disappeared into the temple’s depths, nursing a bitter resentment. The party did not yet know his identity or full motives, but their trail of bodies had given him more material to work with than ever before.

The group’s next discovery came in an oddly shaped chamber where a low stone wall held back the temple’s water supply. Calmer dryly recalled that the last time they believed they had found a water supply, it had turned out to be a creature from hell. Zephyr stepped inside to investigate, rolled a dismal 8, and immediately slipped on the algae-slicked, subtly sloping floor, sliding hard into the far wall. Calmer threw him a rope, and once steadied, Zephyr spotted a large, round, slime-covered object resting at the bottom of the pool.

Zephyr tied the rope around himself, took a deep breath, and dove. His first attempt failed and nearly dislodged the object further into the murk. On the second dive, with a roll of 14, he barely seized it and wrenched it free just before his lungs gave out. Zinnia helped Calmer haul him back to the surface. When the slime was cleared away, the object proved to be a finely crafted Shield +1. Calmer, the only party member equipped to use a shield, accepted it with great satisfaction: “Very well done! That was very well done!”

The Minotaur’s warning about black-cloaked bandits now carried a second implication: some of the attackers might not have been living at all. The Minotaur could eat undead flesh, though it preferred fresh meat. As the party put it, beggars could not be choosers.

When the party finally reached the area where the four prisoners had been held — after acknowledging that they had left them without food for several days — they found the chains torn apart and the captives gone. An Investigation check of 22 revealed that they had not escaped; their corpses had been claimed by the necromancer and added to his growing arsenal of the dead. The necromancer’s activities were becoming increasingly irritating, and the party resolved to track him down.

Zephyr proposed posting notices throughout the temple ordering anyone with information about the necromancer to report to the party. Calmer enthusiastically approved: “I think that’s a fantastic idea, Zephyr. As the Chief of Staff, I think you are responsible for all matters related to security operations. Produce an appropriate note, and I will sign it.” He then reconsidered and asked whether the initiative should properly belong to Zinnia as Chief of Security. Sequoia, meanwhile, was reminded that he was in charge of supplies — or, in his own words, “I’m in charge of getting shit done.” With Zinnia’s approval, Calmer instructed Zephyr to bring him something to sign.

Rather than immediately returning to their offices, the party chose to finish exploring the remaining sections of their domain. Sequoia declared, “I’m a completionist,” and led them east into a refuse-filled chamber that had once been a secluded place of relaxation. It was now heaped with rotting garbage, dung, bones, and skulls, with parts of a bronze-bound coffer visible near the center. Concerned that the necromancer would use the remains, the party decided to burn the entire room.

No one had a suitable fire spell — Calmer admitted this was a mistake, and laughed when Zephyr asked about Meteor Swarm — so Zephyr and Sequoia headed to the Fire Temple while Zinnia stayed behind to keep an eye on Calmer. Taking an unexplored dark corridor on the way, they emerged into familiar territory and went directly to Captain Varek Solain.

Varek Solain, no longer defeated in demeanor, received them and confirmed that the refuse room was where the Fire Temple habitually dumped its garbage. When told that a necromancer was quietly taking control of the skeletons, he finally connected the threat to an old political grievance. The necromancer had served under Barkinar and disappeared after Romag received the promotion he believed should have been his. Varek did not know where the necromancer was hiding — or even whether he remained inside the temple — but insisted that if undead were appearing, “he’s around.” Varek had warned Alrrem about the danger, but Alrrem had ignored him instead of finding the necromancer and making an alliance.

Zephyr pressed Varek to find the necromancer, only for the captain to answer, “With what army? I need supplies. I need food, I need troops,” noting that the party had taken his money and resources. He asked Sequoia, as head of logistics, to send someone to Nulb and repair the supply chain. In the meantime, he provided several fire arrows and explained their use: throw a flask of oil at the target, then shoot it with a burning arrow. He stressed that his supply was limited.

Zephyr then granted the Fire Temple control over the northern food depot. Varek was visibly delighted. “Food is always a good thing,” he said. “He who controls food controls everything.” Zephyr immediately corrected him: “We control the food, not you. You’re simply guarding it.” Varek accepted the distinction, saying it demonstrated trust in the Fire Temple, but could not resist proclaiming, “And fire shall burn again!” Zephyr responded, “Okay. Maybe don’t,” and suggested placing an Earth Temple infiltrator nearby to keep the Fire Temple in check.

When the party returned and set the refuse ablaze, a horrific creature emerged from the trash and furiously tried to douse the flames. The party chose to defend its territory rather than walk away. Calmer cast Hold Monster, successfully paralyzing the creature. Zinnia struck it repeatedly for automatic critical hits, Zephyr dealt 52 points in a devastating sneak attack, and Sequoia rolled twelve damage dice on a critical — only to average poorly and curse Frostbrand for having “one job.” The creature remained paralyzed through its saving throw. Calmer cast Toll the Dead and proudly remarked, “This new spell of mine is very effective.” Zephyr eventually landed another 57-point critical and killed the creature. The party stepped outside and allowed the refuse chamber to burn for several hours.

Continuing onward, they found another room filled with broken containers whose disturbed litter showed recent traffic, probably from people carrying off the remaining foodstuffs. The temple’s food supplies were plainly running low, and nobody was currently managing them. Beyond that lay an oddly shaped chamber packed with jars, boxes, barrels, crates, chests, sacks, casks, bales, bags, and hampers. Some were broken or empty, but many remained full. Bales held black cloth and finished garments. Zinnia’s Insight roll of 24 identified these as supplies and uniforms belonging to the Greater, or Deep, Temple.

The party’s Arcana checks failed to reveal anything further. Sequoia nevertheless looked up, explaining, “The answer is always on the ceiling.” An Investigation result of 21 vindicated him: four creatures hung overhead by their tails, claws poised. “I was right,” Sequoia said in disbelief. “I was saying, as a joke, oh my god.”

The creatures were nightmarish, gaunt, ape-like predators with ash-gray hides, four long muscular arms ending in hooked black claws, powerful jaws filled with uneven fangs, three alert eyes, and thick prehensile tails wrapped around the stonework. Their limbs were unnaturally jointed for climbing and ambushing, and faint violet psychic energy pulsed around their skulls.

Zephyr acted first but missed both pistol shots. Zinnia and Sequoia concentrated their attacks on the same creature, while the predators unleashed psychic blasts against the entire party. Zinnia, Zephyr, and Calmer resisted; Sequoia failed the DC 17 Wisdom save, suffered 17 points of psychic damage, and was stunned. Calmer cast Spirit Guardians, creating a fifteen-foot aura that damaged and slowed several attackers.

Zephyr’s next pistol attack, including sneak-attack damage, killed the already wounded predator. The others swarmed Calmer with as many as five claw attacks apiece, but his armor class of 21 — bolstered by the new Shield +1 — allowed only one blow through. He suffered 8 slashing damage and lost concentration on Spirit Guardians, while another creature missed him five times in succession. Calmer healed Sequoia after the wounded logistics chief pointedly suggested that the cleric could save him, then used Word of Radiance against the enemies clustered around him.

Zinnia slew a second creature, while Calmer continued alternating healing and radiant blasts. “Are you okay, Sequoia?” he called. When Sequoia answered, “I guess,” Calmer replied, “My guess is not an answer, yes or no.” Sequoia finally answered, “Maybe,” and warned, “Just don’t blame me when your logistics breaks down.” Calmer cast another Healing Word, restoring 14 hit points, then struck both nearby predators with another Word of Radiance.

Zephyr used Chill Touch against one survivor, preventing it from regaining hit points, while Zinnia, newly reminded of his Stunning Strike technique, and Sequoia continued the assault. The two remaining predators finally disengaged and fled to a shadowed corner. Zephyr warned them, “You know where the boss is, okay? So, don’t ever… try to… anything again.” No one spoke Deep Speech or Undercommon well enough to negotiate. The creatures merely sneered, howled, hissed, and curled into the darkness.

An exhausted and flustered Dren Halveth arrived shortly afterward, notepad in hand, to deliver a perfectly deadpan summary of the temple’s cascading administrative disasters. Addressing Zephyr, he reported, “I’ve been doing the supply check. The Minotaur has food for 3 days. The men have pay for 4. The dead have started leaving without permission. I cannot solve… I… I can’t solve any one of these. I cannot solve all three while pretending this is a normal command.”

Dren explained that supplies ordinarily came through Nulb, meaning someone would have to travel there and restore the supply chain. The party issued its orders: assign the armor depot to the Water Temple, assign the firewood depot to the Air Temple, burn corpses before the necromancer could claim them, and feed usable bodies to the Minotaur. Dren requested permission to use the Fire Temple’s salamander to help with the burning, and the party agreed. He also reminded them that Barkinar was still waiting for a decision about the Chest of Rao.

The group resolved to travel to Nulb the following morning. Dren was left with broad authority to keep order and the Minotaur fed, though Sequoia warned him, “We trust you, but if you betray that trust, you know…” They retired to their quarters, where Calmer relaxed with evident satisfaction and admitted, “I like being in charge.”

That evening, as the party drank and rested, an illusory image of a hooded man appeared. “Forgive the theatrics,” he began. “I cannot be seen sending a runner, and you cannot be before to be seen. To be seen receiving one.” The figure’s message and the party’s previous knowledge identified him through implication as Falrinth, a wizard hiding from the Greater Temple whom Barkinar had considered important enough to mention.

Falrinth warned them about Lucius Graeme: “The flattering man has already been to you. Golden eyes, soft hands, a great deal of wine. He gave you presents and asked you what you were short of. Lucius Graeme, write the name down. He collects the ones he gives you.” Lucius had already visited the party carrying gifts, and Falrinth advised them not to commit when he returned, but to stall him.

Falrinth then revealed that he possessed the Orb of Golden Death. “The orb this temple frets over, yes! I have it. I’m not giving it to Hedrack, nor to Senshock, nor to any of the holy gentlemen who would like me dead. I’m not giving it to you either, but that is a separate conversation.” When Sequoia asked how he had acquired it, Falrinth answered, “That is a conversation for another year.” Romag had previously described the fully assembled orb as a source of tremendous power capable of summoning monsters and destroying virtually anything, raising the question of why Falrinth possessed it but did not appear to be using it.

Falrinth next turned his attention to Calmer’s scarab, inscribed T-Z-G-Y. “Yes, you should be more, far more careful with it than you have been. Lucius will know it on sight. Senshock will know it on sight. They will use it against you in different ways, and neither will tell you they notice.” He also revealed that Lareth had been his friend and said, “I’m told you handled him with discretion, which I appreciate more than you would expect. But there are other friends, and not all of them are as patient as I am.”

Falrinth explained how to reach him: “A candle in the West will reach me.” Reporting him to Barkinar was another option, but Falrinth predicted that Barkinar would believe the party and simply fail to find the wizard where they claimed he was. Either decision would reveal the party’s choice of allegiance.

As the illusion began to fade, Falrinth delivered his most chilling revelation: “There is a room below, deep, forgotten, on the level beneath your feet. The holy gentleman keeps something in it. A sleeper. My leash is short, and the gates are watching. But you, you have keys I never did. If you ever want Verbobonc and all its banners to come down on this terrible… hell… on this temple, and oh, they would come for this, wake what sleeps in the iron coffin. Ask your golden friend what he’s so afraid you’ll find, then signal me.”

Only after trying to question him did the party realize that the projection was a one-way message whose creator had merely anticipated some of their likely responses. It faded away, leaving the party with the revelation that Falrinth either possessed the Orb of Golden Death or was lying about having it, and that something beneath their feet could provoke a full military response from Verbobonc.

The next morning, the party traveled to Nulb dressed discreetly, choosing not to announce their new status as lords of the temple. Zephyr summarized their philosophy as “Power and subtlety.” Calmer approved: “Good choice. This is why I have made you Chief of Staff.” Zephyr added, “We don’t have to say that we’re powerful, we just know,” prompting Calmer to puff himself up and answer, “We just know. I like it!”

Smoke lay low over the river. The same barges leaned against the same rotting piers, and men at the tavern doors continued arguing over which elemental prophet would win a war that had ended beneath their feet ten days earlier. Nulb had not changed, which was how the party knew nobody had told it.

On the first muddy stretch of the river road, a Rhennee child from the river folk the party had previously aided recognized them and ran shouting, “FAMILY HAS A RETURNED!” As they entered the village, a dock hand pressed a folded note into Sequoia’s palm. It read, “Skole says the captain is still telling people you’re dead. You thought you might enjoy the update.” Captain Tolub — who had previously tried to kill them — remained convinced, or wanted everyone else convinced, that the party was dead. The game, it seemed, was far from over.
