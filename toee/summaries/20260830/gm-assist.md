# The Sleeper in the Iron Coffin

Date: 2026-08-30

## Summary
Prisoners were still being held somewhere in the temple. When the party finally reached the area, they found the chains torn apart and the captives gone — not escaped, but taken. Their corpses had been claimed by the necromancer, added to his growing arsenal of the dead. The party resolved to track him down, with Calmer insisting that Zephyr draft a notice to be posted throughout the temple, which he would then sign. They continued their exploration, discovering a refuse-filled chamber that had once been a place of relaxation but was now a heap of stinking garbage, bones, and skulls. Concerned the necromancer would use the remains, the party decided to burn the entire room. Lacking fire of their own, Zephyr and Sequoia headed to the Fire Temple while Zinnia stayed behind with Calmer.

Captain Varek Solain of the Fire Temple, no longer defeated in demeanor, received them and revealed that the necromancer had once served under Barkinar and had disappeared after being passed over for promotion — and that Alrrem had been warned about the threat but had failed to act. Zephyr granted the Fire Temple control over the northern food supply depot, greatly pleasing Varek Solain, who could not resist declaring that fire would burn again. He provided the party with fire arrows and oil to carry out the incineration. When the party returned and set the refuse ablaze, a furious creature emerged from the trash, desperately trying to douse the flames. Calmer stepped forward and held it in place with a powerful incantation, leaving it completely helpless as Zinnia, Zephyr, and Sequoia delivered devastating blows, and Calmer finished it with a burst of necrotic energy. The party stepped outside and let the room burn to the ground.

Pressing deeper, the party entered an oddly shaped chamber crammed with barrels, crates, and bales of black cloth — equipment belonging to the Greater Temple. Before anyone could investigate further, Sequoia looked up at the ceiling and spotted four nightmarish creatures clinging upside down by their prehensile tails: gaunt, four-armed predators with ash-gray hide, hook claws, three alert eyes, and violet psychic energy pulsing around their skulls. The creatures dropped and attacked, unleashing blasts of psychic force that stunned Sequoia and battered the party's minds. Calmer summoned a swirling aura of protective spirits and released bursts of radiant energy, while Zephyr and Zinnia focused their strikes to bring the creatures down one by one. After two of their number were slain, the remaining two retreated hissing into the shadows, and Zephyr's attempt to communicate with them in Undercommon was met only with sneering silence.

An exhausted Dren Halveth arrived shortly after, notepad in hand, to deliver a perfectly deadpan summary of the temple's cascading administrative disasters: the Minotaur had food for only three days, the men had pay for four days, and the dead had started leaving without permission. The party issued their orders — feed usable bodies to the Minotaur, burn the rest, assign the armor depot to the Water Temple and the firewood depot to the Air Temple — and resolved to travel to Nulb the following morning to fix the broken supply chain. Dren was left in charge, with the pointed reminder from Sequoia that betrayal would not be tolerated.

That evening, as the party rested, an illusory image of a hooded man appeared before them. He identified himself through implication as Falrinth, a wizard hiding from the Greater Temple, and warned them about Lucius Graeme — a flattering man with golden eyes and soft hands who arrived bearing gifts and secretly catalogued everything he gave in order to collect favors later. Falrinth revealed he possessed the Orb of Golden Death and had no intention of surrendering it to any of the temple's factions. He warned Calmer to guard his scarab carefully, as powerful enemies like Senshock would recognize it on sight and use it against him. Before fading, Falrinth delivered his most chilling revelation: on the level beneath their feet lay a forgotten room containing a sleeper locked in an iron coffin, and waking it would bring the forces of Verbobonc crashing down upon the temple. He urged them to ask their golden friend what he was so afraid they would find — then signal him. The illusion dissolved, leaving the party with far more questions than answers.

The next morning, the party traveled to Nulb dressed discreetly, choosing not to announce their new status as lords of the temple. The muddy river village was unchanged, its dockworkers still arguing over which elemental prophet would win a war that had already ended beneath their feet. A child from the Rhennee river folk recognized them and ran shouting to announce their return, while a dock hand pressed a folded note into Sequoia's hand from an informant named Skole. The note carried a simple, pointed update: Captain Tolub was still telling people the party was dead. The game, it seemed, was far from over.

## NPCs
### The Minotaur
Installed in Chapter 33 as guard of the ruined reception hall, in exchange for regular meals and protection from black-coated bandits. Its own tongue is Abyssal, though it understands and speaks broken Common. This session it is a standing logistics problem rather than a presence on stage: Dren Halveth reports it has food for three days, and is left with authority to keep it fed. It warned the party that failure to deliver would result in their deaths.

### The Necromancer
An unseen antagonist who left a mocking note for the party, claiming the temple as his home and vowing to use every corpse they create against them. He specifically mocks Calmer as unqualified to lead the Earth Temple. Varek Solain reveals he was formerly under Barkinar and disappeared after being passed over for promotion.

### Varek Solain
The Captain of the Fire Temple guard. No longer defeated in demeanor, he reveals that a disgruntled necromancer formerly under Barkinar is likely responsible for the undead infestation, and that Alrrem had been warned but failed to act. He provides the party with fire arrows and oil in exchange for control of the food supply depot.

### Alrrem
The former head of the Fire Temple (deceased), mentioned by Varek Solain as having been warned about the necromancer threat but failing to act on it.

### Dren Halveth
An exhausted and flustered administrator who manages the temple's day-to-day logistics. He reports that the Minotaur's food supply is dwindling, the men are unpaid, and the undead have begun wandering off without permission. He is left in charge of the temple while the party travels to Nulb.

### Psychic Predators
Four gaunt, four-armed ape-like creatures with ash-gray hide, three eyes, hook claws, and prehensile tails. They clung to the ceiling of the Greater Temple storeroom and attacked with both slashing claws and violet psychic blasts. Two were slain; two retreated into the shadows.

### Falrinth
A wizard who communicated via an illusory hooded projection. He claims to possess the Orb of Golden Death and refuses to surrender it to any temple faction. He warned the party about Lucius Graeme, cautioned Calmer about his scarab, and revealed the existence of a sleeper in an iron coffin on the level below.

### Lucius Graeme
Described by Falrinth as a flattering man with golden eyes and soft hands who arrives bearing gifts and wine, secretly cataloguing what he gives in order to collect favors and information later.

### Captain Tolub
A captain who previously attempted to kill the party and is currently spreading rumors in Nulb that they are dead, as reported by the informant Skole.

### Skole
An informant in Nulb who passed a covert note to Sequoia via a dock hand, revealing that Captain Tolub is still telling people the party is dead.

Recovered in Chapter 33 from the bottom of the temple's algae-covered water supply pool, and claimed by Calmer. This session it proved its worth by deflecting the multi-armed attacks of the psychic predators in the storeroom.

### Necromancer's Note
A letter addressed directly to the party, found in Chapter 33 on the ghoul's corpse. Written with cold sarcasm, it mocks Calmer as a disgrace unqualified to lead the Earth Temple and vows to use every corpse the party creates as a resource against them. Calmer read it with mild offense, then carefully folded it and tucked it away. It is why the party spends this session hunting its author — and why they burn the refuse chamber rather than leave him the remains.
