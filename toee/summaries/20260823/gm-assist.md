# Chapter 33 The Necromancer's Thank You Note

Date: 2026-08-23

## Summary
The party stood at the threshold of a ruined reception hall, its walls adorned with faded murals depicting grand welcomes that had long since turned to ruin. Battered furniture lined the walls, skeletons of humans and other humanoids lay scattered across the floor, and at the center of the room sat what appeared to be a filth-covered decorative statue of a Minotaur upon a throne. It was Sequoia who noticed first — the creature was not stone at all, but very much alive. He quietly pulled the door shut before it could spot them, and the party huddled together to decide their next move.

Rather than cut the beast down, the group chose a bolder gambit: they would walk in with Calmer dressed as the Earth Cleric and Zephyr announcing himself as the new management. The Minotaur, it turned out, harbored a deep and violent hatred for the temple's former leadership, and the announcement sent it into a rage. It charged Zephyr with terrifying speed, goring him savagely and hurling him to the ground before looming over him with murderous intent. Zinnia moved to strike the creature but failed to land a blow, and it was Calmer who stepped forward with a calm and persuasive voice, somehow managing to give the beast pause. Zephyr, battered and prone on the cold stone floor, then offered the creature food from the party's packs, and the Minotaur — grumbling and suspicious — began to eat.

With its hunger momentarily satisfied, the creature agreed to terms. It would guard the reception hall in exchange for regular food and protection from the black-cloaked bandits that had been harassing it. It declared the room its own, then turned its gaze back to Zephyr and said, with surprising casualness, "No hard feelings." The truce came with a pointed warning, however — if the food stopped coming or the bandits returned, the Minotaur promised to hunt down every last one of them, eat them, and feel no remorse about it. The party accepted the arrangement and quietly noted they now had a very large, very hungry guard on their payroll.

As the group moved deeper into the temple's lower corridors, Calmer began to reveal a troubling shift in his thinking. When the subject of feeding the Minotaur arose again, he calmly suggested that the prisoners still held captive somewhere in the temple could solve the problem neatly — two birds, one stone. Sequoia was visibly unsettled, and even Frostbrand, the party's sentient sword, chimed in to remind Sequoia that their plan had always been to remain discreet and avoid drawing attention to themselves. Calmer, for his part, reflected openly on his disillusionment with the church of St. Cuthbert, explaining that when he had died, his companions arranged his return, with Zinnia surrendering a magical trident to Terjon as payment. That loyalty, he said, had put a very different spin on the religious doctrine he had been raised to follow. He was not yet an evil man, he insisted, but he had begun scribbling notes about a potential new educational system inspired by the principles of the Earth Temple.

The party pressed on, discovering a series of storerooms that revealed just how well-supplied the temple truly was. One room held stacks of lumber and firewood, while a second — its lock no match for Sequoia's skilled hands — contained a remarkable cache of equipment: chainmail and plate armor, holy water, lanterns, lockpicks, mirrors, parchment, ropes, sacks, spikes, and tinderboxes. Calmer surveyed the rotting remains of what had once been a food storage area and declared that Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades would need to be brought in to clean the mess up. He then proposed assigning individual storerooms to each of the four elemental temples as part of the new management structure, pulling out a scrap of scroll to begin making notes before anyone could stop him.

Their exploration was cut short when a massive, demented ghoul lurched out of the shadows of a corridor and fixed its hollow gaze on the living warmth of the party. Zinnia recognized immediately that a necromancer had been at work — the same one responsible for the undead that had plagued the temple before, now raising far more dangerous creatures than before. The battle was brutal. The ghoul sank its teeth into Calmer, and the cleric crumpled, paralyzed by the bite. Sequoia and Zephyr struck from the shadows with ranged attacks, and Zinnia weathered the creature's foul, poisonous stench to deliver a series of devastating blows. Even poisoned and fighting at a disadvantage, Sequoia continued to press the attack with his dagger until Zinnia finally put the ghoul down for good.

When the party searched the corpse, they found something unexpected: a note, addressed directly to them. It was written by a necromancer who called the temple his home and the party interlopers and thieves. He thanked them, with cold sarcasm, for the harvest of death they had left in their wake — every corpse they had created was a resource he intended to use against them. The note went on to specifically mock Calmer's qualifications as the new head of the Earth Temple, declaring his approach to the rituals entirely wrong. Calmer read it aloud with an expression of mild offense, then folded it carefully and tucked it away.

Piecing together what they knew, Zinnia and Zephyr formed a hypothesis about the threat. Barkinar had once led the Earth Temple before being promoted to the upper levels, and Romag had taken his place. They suspected another figure connected to that transition might be the necromancer behind the attacks and might resent the party's occupation of the temple, but his identity and motives remained unconfirmed. Whoever he was, the party's trail of bodies had given him more material to work with. They would need to find him, and soon.

The group's final discovery of the day came in an oddly shaped chamber bisected by a low stone wall holding back a deep pool of cold water — the temple's water supply. Zephyr stepped inside to investigate and immediately slipped on the algae-slicked, sloping floor, sliding hard into the far wall. Calmer threw him a rope, and once steadied, Zephyr spotted a large, round object resting at the bottom of the pool beneath the slime. He tied the rope around himself, took a deep breath, and dove. The first attempt nearly dislodged the object entirely, sending it tumbling further into the murk, but on the second dive Zephyr seized it and wrenched it free just before his lungs gave out. Zinnia helped Calmer haul him back to the surface, and when the slime was wiped away, the object revealed itself to be a finely crafted shield +1. Calmer accepted it with great satisfaction, and as the party gathered themselves to move on, the Minotaur's earlier words drifted back to them — it had mentioned black-cloaked bandits attacking its territory, and now they understood: some of those bandits had not been alive at all.
