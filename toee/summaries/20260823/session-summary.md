# Chapter 33 The Necromancer's Thank You Note

Date: 2026-08-23

## Summary
The party stood at the threshold of a ruined reception hall, its walls adorned with faded murals depicting grand welcomes that had long since turned to ruin. Battered furniture lined the walls, skeletons of humans and other humanoids lay scattered across the floor, and at the center of the room sat what appeared to be a filth-covered decorative statue of a Minotaur upon a throne. No torches remained in the many cressets and wall sconces — the only light was what the party carried in with them. It was Sequoia who noticed first — the creature was not stone at all, but very much alive. He quietly pulled the door shut before it could spot them, and the party huddled together to decide their next move. The Minotaur spoke only Abyssal, a language none of them knew — not even Vurakhal the fire salamander — which complicated any plan to talk their way through.

Rather than cut the beast down, the group chose a bolder gambit: they would walk in with Calmer dressed as the Earth Cleric and Zephyr announcing "We're your boss." The Minotaur, it turned out, harbored a deep and violent hatred for the temple's former leadership, and the announcement sent it into a rage. It charged Zephyr with terrifying speed, goring him savagely for seventeen points of damage and hurling him to the ground — Zephyr rolled a natural 1 on the strength save — before looming over him with murderous intent, double-headed axe in hand. Zinnia moved to strike the creature but failed to land a blow, and it was Calmer who stepped forward with a calm and persuasive voice, rolling a 21 on persuasion and somehow managing to give the beast pause — though it pointedly gestured at the prone Zephyr as if sizing up eating material. Zephyr, battered and prone on the cold stone floor, pleaded "We are different" (matching Calmer's 21 with Zinnia's help), then offered the creature food from the party's packs, and the Minotaur — grumbling and suspicious — began to eat.

With its hunger momentarily satisfied, the creature agreed to terms. It grumbled of "bandits — men in black cloaks" who had come and attacked it, and it would guard the reception hall in exchange for regular food and protection from them. "Well, this is not my room. I stand guard," it declared. "As long as food keep coming. And no more bandit come and attack." Then it turned its gaze back to Zephyr and said, with surprising casualness, "No hard feelings?" The truce came with a pointed warning, however: "If bandit come attack, I find you, I kill you, I eat you." Zephyr promised. The party accepted the arrangement and quietly noted they now had a very large, very hungry guard on their payroll — and Zephyr had to physically pull Calmer aside to stop the cleric from demanding the Minotaur formally acknowledge "a new clerical order with the High Lord Master Calmer."

As the group moved deeper into the temple's lower corridors, Calmer began to reveal a troubling shift in his thinking. When the subject of feeding the Minotaur arose again, he calmly suggested that the four prisoners still held captive somewhere in the temple could solve the problem neatly — two birds, one stone. "Don't forget," he reminded them, "we are now the evil overlords of this temple. Freeing good people is not exactly something we would be doing." When Sequoia asked whether he was trying to get his cleric abilities taken away, Calmer airily explained that this was "a common misconception" — that once a cleric reaches a certain level of power, that power is intrinsic to him. Even Frostbrand, the party's sentient sword, chimed in to remind Sequoia that their plan had always been to remain discreet and avoid drawing attention to themselves. Calmer, for his part, reflected openly on his disillusionment with the church of St. Cuthbert. His companions had arranged his return, with Zinnia surrendering a magical trident to Terjon as payment. "Loyalty is a two-way street," he said, "and you have demonstrated loyalty, and I will demonstrate loyalty to you. As for St. Cuthbert and I, we have a good working relationship." He was not yet an evil man, he insisted, but the reading material did put a different perspective on "the endless prattle of St. Cuthbert," and he had begun scribbling notes about a potential new educational system inspired by the principles of the Earth Temple.

The party pressed on, discovering a series of storerooms that revealed just how well-supplied the temple truly was. One room held stacks of lumber and firewood, while a second — its lock no match for Sequoia's skilled hands and a roll of 33 — contained a remarkable cache of equipment: chainmail and plate armor, backpacks, holy water, lanterns, lockpicks, mirrors, parchment, pouches, ropes, sacks, spikes, and tinderboxes. Zephyr, as Chief of Operations, resolved to delegate: "We'll get some other guys to do this." Calmer surveyed the rotting remains of what had once been a food storage area, declared the mess "absolutely unacceptable," and suggested Dren Halveth and the Broken Blades be brought in to clean it up — Zephyr agreeing that Dren was "fond of manual labor." He then proposed assigning individual storerooms to each of the elemental temples — accidentally counting the Water Temple twice, prompting Sequoia to needle him that "somebody didn't pass school" — before pulling out a scrap of scroll to begin making notes on a chaotic-Earth-Temple educational system before anyone could stop him.

Their exploration was cut short when a massive, demented ghoul lurched out of the shadows of a corridor and fixed its hollow gaze on the living warmth of the party. Zinnia recognized immediately that a necromancer had been at work — the same one responsible for the undead that had plagued the temple before, now raising far more dangerous creatures than before. The battle was brutal. Sequoia struck first from hiding for 29 points of damage, and Zephyr followed with his pistol — his first shot a natural 1, his second landing for roughly 31 points with his assassin's bonus. The ghoul sank its teeth into Calmer for 21 points of damage, and the cleric crumpled, paralyzed by the bite. Sequoia took eight points from its claws but shrugged off the paralysis. Zinnia rolled a natural 20 to weather the creature's foul, poisonous stench — having, as a masked spy, smelled and tasted worse in his life — and delivered a series of devastating blows. Even poisoned and fighting at a disadvantage (the table ruling on the spot that sneak attack does not apply when attacking with disadvantage), Sequoia continued to press the attack with his dagger until Zinnia finally put the ghoul down for good.

When the party searched the corpse, they found something unexpected: a note, addressed directly to the four of them. It was written by a necromancer who called the temple his home and the party interlopers and thieves. He thanked them, with cold sarcasm, for the harvest of death they had left in their wake — every corpse they had created was a resource he intended to use against them. The note went on at length — "my god, this person's busy," Calmer observed — to specifically mock Calmer as a disgrace, unqualified to be head of the Earth Temple, his approach to the rituals all wrong. Calmer read it aloud with an expression of mild offense, then folded it carefully and tucked it away.

Piecing together what they knew — Zinnia rolling a 21 and Zephyr a natural 20 — the party formed a hypothesis about the threat. They remembered Romag muttering that the undead on the first level no longer reported to him, which implied they had once reported to somebody. Barkinar had once led the Earth Temple before being promoted to the upper levels, and Romag had taken his place. They suspected another figure connected to that transition might be the necromancer behind the attacks and might resent the party's occupation of the temple, but his identity and motives remained unconfirmed. Whoever he was, the party's trail of bodies had given him more material to work with. They would need to find him, and soon.

The group's final discovery of the day came in an oddly shaped chamber bisected by a low stone wall holding back a deep pool of cold water — the temple's water supply. Calmer dryly noted that the last time they thought they'd found a water supply, it turned out to be a creature from hell. Zephyr stepped inside to investigate and, rolling a dismal 8 on investigation, immediately slipped on the algae-slicked, subtly sloping floor, sliding hard into the far wall. Calmer threw him a rope, and once steadied, Zephyr spotted a large, round, slime-covered object resting at the bottom of the pool. He tied the rope around himself, took a deep breath, and dove. The first attempt failed and nearly dislodged the object entirely, sending it tumbling further into the murk, but on the second dive Zephyr — with a 13, just barely — seized it and wrenched it free just before his lungs gave out. Zinnia helped Calmer haul him back to the surface, and when the slime was wiped away, the object revealed itself to be a finely crafted shield +1. Calmer accepted it with great satisfaction — "Very well done. That was very well done" — and as the party gathered themselves to move on, the Minotaur's earlier words drifted back to them: it had mentioned black-cloaked bandits attacking its territory, and now they understood — some of those bandits had not been alive at all. The Minotaur, it turned out, could eat the undead, but preferred fresh meat; as the saying goes, beggars can't be pickers.

- Zinnia, rolling insight with advantage (a 1, then a 12), realizes that the necromancer who was raising the dead is still around and active — and "has upped his game and is no longer just raising minor creatures, but far more deadly creatures."
- A large, demented ghoul emerges from the shadows and approaches the party, moaning — deciding "You are living. He is undead. This is an opportunity."
- Sequoia hides around a corner (with a +13 to "all the dex stuff") and strikes the creature with a hidden shortbow shot for 29 points of damage including sneak attack.
- Zephyr fires his pistol at the ghoul — a natural 1 on the first shot, then a 19 to hit, dealing roughly 31 points of damage with his assassin's bonus dice.

## Locations
### Pool Chamber
A dangerous location featuring a sentient acidic pool from which the party recently narrowly escaped, referenced in the session recap. The entity in the water could plant thoughts in their minds — it lured Zephyr in by mental suggestion, and Sequoia made the same mistake twice.

### Brother Eelrich Vane's Chambers
The living quarters of a temple official that the party raided for scrolls, ritual texts, and magical items, referenced in the session recap. The loot included ritual texts on elemental water, a bronze mace, a golden pearl ring, and potions of water breathing; Vane's texts strongly implied he had always known about the pool's living nature.

### Temple Reception Hall
A 40 by 50 foot chamber decorated with murals depicting new arrivals being welcomed "to the delights of the temple." It contains battered furniture — couches, chairs, and stands along the walls — a shallow central pool whose fountains have stopped, and a central throne where a much-dirtied Minotaur resides amidst human and humanoid skeletons and gnawed skulls and bones. None of the many cressets and wall sconces are lit, and no torches remain — the only light is what visitors bring in.

### Temple Storerooms
A series of rooms used for logistics: a large chamber (area 239) storing wooden lumber — stacks of firewood near the entrance, planks and beams, and large lumpy sacks just inside the entry — plus a locked room (opened by Sequoia with a 33) holding a large cache of adventuring gear including chainmail, plate armor, holy water, and various tools. A further supply room lies in ruin, its foodstuffs rotting and containers broken and tossed about. The rooms reveal the temple to be exceptionally well supplied — and remind the party that being the big bad means handling supplies and boring things like equipment.

### Temple Corridor
A dark hallway where the party was ambushed by a powerful ghoul and subsequently discovered a taunting note from a mysterious necromancer. A stairway leading up was also noted nearby, along with the watery remains of a previous battle. The party knows the Greater Temple has forces deployed somewhere on this second level, revealed when they suddenly showed up from all sorts of locations after the recent battle.

### Temple Water Supply
An oddly shaped chamber bisected by a curving four-foot wall of well-dressed, bordered stone that holds back a deep pool of water — high enough to almost flow over the top — fed by trickles from the east, south, and west walls. Overflow gravitates to floor drains. The floors are dangerously slick with a barely visible film of algae and slope subtly downward, serving as the reservoir for the temple's upper levels. A slime-covered shield +1 lay hidden at the bottom of the pool.
