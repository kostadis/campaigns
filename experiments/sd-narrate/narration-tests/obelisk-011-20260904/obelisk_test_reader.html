### Zenvon Forepot The smell of smoke gives us a direction as we climb the rugged ridge on Wyvern Tor’s lower slopes. I move ahead far enough to look down into the ravine. A cave mouth opens at the bottom, about fifty yards away. Twenty yards outside it, a single bugbear sits hunkered beside a boulder. One that I can see. There is a difference. He is within reach of my shortbow. I look at the distance between him and the cave, then withdraw to where Pip, Sister Maela, and Veyra wait. “Okay, guys, so I see a bugbear…” I try to explain where he is. “At the far end of the cave.” That is not quite the location. “Yeah, ravine. At the ravine? Okay, yeah, yeah. So, I was thinking I could use my shortbow and take that guy out.” The shot is possible. Pip wants a more particular description. “Well… To explore that, you need to go close to him, and I think it's a bad idea going close to him.” He looks at me. “You can sneak, right?” “I can sneak, but, well, yeah, yeah, I guess you're right, okay, I can… can go as close as possible. Let me try.” I go back along the ridge and work closer without drawing the bugbear’s attention. From here I can see the milky film over one eye. That eye is blind. He leans on a salvaged pike, making use of it while there is nobody to stab. *Salvaged bike?* Pike. Like a spear. A long, pointed weapon, and at present something to lean against. Pip has a useful question, yes. The guard looks bored. He has the look of somebody who has been given this assignment and would prefer another one. But he is still between us and the entrance, with a weapon long enough to make approaching him inconvenient. I look beyond him for anybody else. The cave gives me nothing. Its mouth is visible, but the darkness inside stops me seeing farther. The cookfire smoke is clearer here, coming from within. There is somebody else in there. How many, I cannot establish. A stone could make him look away. I consider using Mage Hand to disturb his pike, or throw something without showing myself. A noise would let us choose where he looks, at least for a moment. Then the others attempt to move closer, and he notices something. He straightens and looks south. His pike comes forward. He advances roughly ten feet, watching the ground ahead of him. He has not found us, but the bored part of his assignment is over. I put Mage Hand to work on the other side of him. To the north, behind his back, a stone strikes rock. The sound gives him something else to attend to. It does not tell me whether he has forgotten the first disturbance. My idea has been to make more noises after this one. East, then west. Give him enough to think somebody is there, so he calls another bugbear out. Perhaps two. Then we can engage them outside, where we can see them. Pip’s advice from our discussion bears on that also. Quietly killing the sentry is easier than bringing out an unknown number. I may expect one or two. The cave is under no obligation to contain one or two. “You're the one… you know, it's like…” he has told me. “You haven't led us wrong yet, Zenvon.” That is a generous account of things. I have weighed his warning aloud: “I mean, it makes sense, but I'm thinking if we can kill him without… Alerting others. Okay, yeah, I guess, I guess it doesn't matter either way, like, it's…” What I want is a manageable fight. There is no advantage in adding a spell and several noises if the result is the same fight, with more people invited. “I feel using the mage hand is more effort. For the same output.” Now the hand has made its noise, and we still have the distance to solve. Pip is roughly sixty feet from the guard. He cannot cover all that ground and strike before the bugbear has time to react. My bow can reach. Pip needs to get closer. So the distraction has a use after all. Not to call anybody out. To let Pip approach. I want the sentry to stay where he is and turn his back toward Pip. A northern noise should give Pip time to move into the ridge and stay in position. That is what I am trying to arrange. But the bugbear has already heard something to the south, and I cannot decide for him which noise matters more. I watch him with the pike held in front. He looks south, then over his shoulder. The stone has given him a second direction to watch. It has not made him conveniently certain about either one. I study his movements for some indication of what he will do next. Whether he is about to keep coming, turn back, call somebody. There is nothing further I can read from him. One blind eye, one ready pike, and now he is paying attention. “Tricky here.”

### Zenvon Forepot I still have the potion of invisibility. For now, I keep it and peek into the cave. There is a cookfire inside. Enough light reaches the occupants for me to count them, which is useful because I cannot see into the dark beyond it. Four bugbears. An ogre. Then I make out one orc as well. I look for something that makes this arrangement easier to deal with. Nothing presents itself. There are six of them, and four are bugbears. I already know something about fighting four bugbears. Sister Maela pokes her head in to look. She finds no more than I do. Pip takes his turn, and he spends his attention on the occupants. “You know what's interesting about this group? The orc.” The ogre has more of my attention. I wait for Pip to explain. “He's the weakest of all of these characters, and yet he seems to be the only one that has any authority here. Like, normally, an ogre's in charge. So I don't know, but this seems like a very interesting situation.” Authority is useful to know about. But I want to know how he confirms it. An orc among larger creatures does not by itself tell me who gives the orders. “Look, Zenvon. In the military, sometimes most of these creatures respond to whoever's the biggest, so normally it would be the ogre in charge.” Pip directs my attention back inside. “An orc would be bringing them food left, right, and center. But if you look inside, he's the one sitting next to the fire. And everybody's sort of listening to him.” I look again. The orc sits by the cookfire, and the others give him their attention. I have counted the same creatures Pip has. He has found something in how they sit together. “Okay, that's a brilliant observation.” “So, I'm just gonna tell you: that tells me he's the brains of the operation.” “Hmm.” Six enemies is one problem. Six enemies who depend on one of them to organize the others might be a different problem. I keep watching the orc. “So… you know, I don't want to tell you how to do your job.” Pip has already told me what I have missed. I let him continue. “My experience is not with these kinds of groups, but in the army, if you kill the leader in this kind of group—it's kind of a mob, kind of a gang—if you kill the gang boss, everybody else suddenly doesn't want to fight anymore.” “That makes our job interesting.” “I just wanna throw that out there, boss.” He offers it carefully. It is still a possibility, yes. The bugbears and the ogre do not become smaller because we identify the orc. “Or, you know, we could run in there and try to kill them all. Last time we faced 4 bugbears, we had to run for our lives, so I'm not entirely certain that's something we want to do.” That is a fair comparison. We have already tried the four bugbears part. Adding an ogre does not improve it. “Well, you make a point, but that's—” I pause, considering the orc beside the fire. “Pretty valid. So, what do you suggest? Do you suggest to kill the orc?” Knowing whom to attack is useful. Reaching him without having to fight everyone else is the part still missing. “From this far, or do you…” I look between Pip and the cave. “Wanna confront him? Because if we confront him, like, he's gonna…” The others are already listening to him. That makes his authority easy to see.
