### Vukradin

Aldus Hern still has not given us an exact time for dinner, which makes it difficult to arrange an evening around Lord Dagult Neverember, particularly when we have somewhere to be at three in the morning. We can wait for a summons with a time attached. Until then, the lord can wait for us.

“Is it Neverember or Neverwinter? I'm kidding.”

The name catches in my mouth anyway. “Neverember. It is never, like, a fire ember.”

“He's never flaming. He keeps it on the DL,” Soma says.

“Yeah, I think Soma has it exactly right.”

We are in the Common Chord, where I used to play to empty rooms and could be thrown out unpaid. Now it is the hardest ticket in Neverwinter. I look across the patrons, the food, the table commemorating Brewbarry, and try to reconcile the room with the one I remember. There is leather on that table now. A plaque. My name apparently does better business here when I am elsewhere.

“Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor.”

“Great, isn't that Valphine?” Soma asks.

“No.”

“Oh, right.”

“It's a rumor, and I suspect Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see.”

Valphine turns her attention back to us. “I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I?”

“Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming.”

I bring her back to the rumored advisor.

“Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact.”

“That is my guess too, yes.”

“Everyone's related to everyone,” Soma says.

It remains a guess. We have a dinner without a time and an advisor without a name, and putting the two together does not yet give us a sibling. There are practical matters we can settle before we meet either.

“Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?”

“I believe it's in progress,” Soma says.

For a moment I have to sort the money from the goods it is meant to buy. The capital is secured. Getting the imports here is the expensive part.

“But, oh, but the cost of import is very high.”

“Right, because their shipping lanes are still…” Soma pauses. “Shipping portals, whatever, are still hosed.”

And House Margaster controls the available logistics.

“Of course they are.”

I cannot dispute that they deliver on time and on schedule. That much must be acknowledged, even while we sit here trying to work out how Brewbarry can afford to bring softness to people through a house that controls how the softness gets here. Being reliable does not make the import bill smaller.

Valphine considers House Margaster competition. I turn that over briefly, then return to the part of the night for which we actually have a time.

“Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by 3 AM. And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready.”

There is still time before we need to go. Around us, the Common Chord continues charging people to enjoy the place where they once declined to enjoy me.

One of those people is discussing precisely this injustice within earshot. The Chord has become unaffordable, he complains, and it is all due to Vukradin.

He has not noticed that Vukradin is standing nearby.

I listen. He wants to hear music without spending so much money. I want people to hear music without spending so much money. We have reached agreement before I even introduce myself, which is an excellent beginning, provided we can get past the part where I am apparently responsible for his bill.

I approach him and explain that I am planning an independent studio, with a charitable fund to help open it.

“That will speed the opening of a low-cost, nonprofit way to hear Lathander's Death on a regular basis.”

There. Something he can help bring into existence. A place he can actually attend. My studio does not have to remain an idea I carry between other people's expensive rooms; if the people who want it help, then we can have the room, and the music, and an audience that does not have to dress for the privilege.

“And spread the word.”

While he considers the offer, a nearby conversation supplies something rather less pleasant to think about. The river sends a tide through the sewers. We are not being warned personally; people at the bar are discussing it among themselves, and I catch enough to turn back toward my companions.

“Hide in the sewers?”

I had understood that we were preparing for a late-night operation. I would also like to understand whether the floor is going to become a river.

“There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?”

Neverwinter is beside the sea. The sea comes in, the river rises, and the water does not politely stop at the underground entrances. Once I follow that connection, the next question seems obvious.

“How many moons does this planet have?”

“That's an easy question to answer,” Valphine says.

“Just the one.”

“It just rotates—it revolves really fast,” Soma says.

“It has one moon named Selûne,” Valphine tells me.

“Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah.”

There are also the Tears of Selûne, which briefly complicate my understanding of what is pulling on what.

“So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons.”

But the Tears are a cluster of smaller bodies, meteors and asteroids, rather than another moon. I work back through it.

“They're not sufficiently gravitational. They're not really moons.”

“Yeah, they're… they're just comets,” Soma says.

“They're asteroids. So the one moon, then.”

The useful information from the neighboring conversation is two high tides and two low tides each day. I hold on to that.

“There you go. Not one and one: two high and two low.”

“That's murder on trade,” Soma says.

“It should take effect underground, too, right?” Valphine asks.

Yes. That is the problem. The sewer belongs to the same rising water as the river, however much stone is over our heads.

“So it's a 6-hour cycle.”

I begin counting the intervals, briefly halve one that does not need halving, and correct myself aloud.

“High to low is 3 hours. Yeah, no, high to low. High to low is 6 hours, 6… High to low is 6 hours.”

This is why we discuss things before entering the sewer.

“We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide.”

“3 AM,” Soma says.

“We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable.”

“I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely.”

I look at Soma. An excellent capability for Soma.

The local I have approached is a little drunk. He does not have much money, but he hands me ten gold pieces.

For a moment the sewer leaves my thoughts entirely.

“Wow, that's pretty generous, actually.”

“That's a hell of a donation,” Soma says.

It is. He has just been complaining about what it costs to be here, and now he is contributing ten gold toward somewhere less expensive. I keep the coins separate from our ordinary funds.

“That goes in the studio fund, not the regular coffers. Thank you very much for contributing. That's awesome.”

He pauses before the exchange is quite finished.

“Do I get on… Is there some kind of signage for people who contributed?”

“Absolutely. Yes.”

This is reasonable. People should know who helped.

“There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. 10 gold pieces puts you at the,”

“Premier or the Plus, which one?” Soma asks.

“At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet.”

He is pleased with Plus. I am pleased with ten gold, and with the prospect of a program that will have both music and the names of people who made the music possible.

“We need his name. We need to write it down for the program notes, and…”

The duration matters. I clarify it.

“Per year. A year. If you want to be in next year's, you'll have to donate again, but one year is a long time.”

“Yeah, we call this Patreon,” Valphine says.

“It's my Patreon, exactly.”

“This is where it comes from, patronage,” Soma says.

I look around the Common Chord again. There are people here who want music, people who remember music, people paying for an evening in a room that trades on my name. Some of them might want to help build another one.

“Before we go, should I try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?”

Plenty before three.

“Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here.”

I approach five more patrons. The first recognizes me as the actual Vukradin, which is useful, but has already completed his charitable giving for the year.

“Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody.”

He has given to charity. Good. The fact that it is not my charity does not undo that, and I am not going to make a person regret recognizing me because he has already helped somebody else.

“The benefits are all on you. If you can be at the Plus or Premier level, I'm cool. I just like fans.”

He assures me that he is my biggest fan.

“Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?”

“Brewbarry's very large,” Valphine says. “In fact, he's huge in Japan sometimes.”

Brewbarry is there to substantiate the first part of this claim. I glance toward him and accept the limit of the comparison. There is no contribution required to remain a fan.

The other four are receptive. I make sure we record Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, and Ondrelle Kass for the future program credits. Names matter here. These are people helping pay for the studio; when there is finally a program, I want them in it.

Wick is enthusiastic about patronage of the arts. He has no idea who Lathander's Death is, and he does not believe I am Vukradin, but neither uncertainty interferes with his support. He understands that I am putting together a music school.

“School?” Soma asks.

“School, studio—they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people. That's just how it goes.”

I see no difficulty. People come to make music. People learn to make music. We will need somewhere to do both, and Wick wants to help provide it. He does not even require me to be famous.

Sabbatha's attention moves past the studio's prospective founder to Brewbarry. She looks him over and admires his commitment to the Brewbarry look.

I look too. It is a considerable commitment.

“Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?”

“Rippling,” Soma supplies.

“Exactly.”

Sabbatha contributes. I make certain her name is among the credits.

Old Hesp is the last patron I speak with. He recognizes me without needing to be persuaded.

“I remember when you used to play to an empty room.”

“Yeah?”

I look at him more closely. An empty room is how I remember it too, but there must have been someone in it for someone to remember me playing.

“Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back at the end of.”

I do not finish before he confirms it. He remembers the guarantee: if he would not rate the performance ten out of ten, I would not keep his money. He remembers getting it back.

“I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?”

He pauses.

“Now this is a friggin' expensive place to be. Everybody's gotta look cool.”

I follow his attention across the room. The people here have managed the requirement. That does not mean everyone who wants to hear the music can manage it, or afford to discover whether they like it, and I cannot ask him what he thinks now and then refuse to hear the answer because I would rather receive a compliment.

“Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running.”

He tries to describe what this place has lost, the look and the feel of it, the grit. I know the room he means. I remember standing in it.

“We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back.”

“This is too commercial.”

“Yeah, yeah, I agree.”

He blames Petra, though there is respect mixed into the complaint. She has turned the Common Chord into a money-making operation. He points out Brewbarry's table, remembering it before the leather finish, before the shine, before the plaque.

I look at the leather. The old table was available for use without being an attraction. Now people can sit near evidence that Brewbarry once sat here, and elsewhere in the room Sabbatha is admiring how convincingly Brewbarry resembles himself.

Old Hesp remembers when they did not serve this fancy food, either.

He is giving toward the studio while telling me all this. I do not have to convince him that something is missing. He has come back to the Common Chord and found the table commemorated more carefully than the evening he used to have around it.

Twenty gold pieces go into the studio fund tonight. Petra's separate five hundred for my likeness is still pending until the performance two days from now. I keep that promise separate from the money actually in hand, and keep the donor names with the obligation to credit them for a year.

There is room in the studio I am imagining for Wick to learn something, for Old Hesp to come without looking fashionable, for this generous local to hear Lathander's Death regularly. I want to get the doors open. Tonight, people who want to walk through them have helped.

Now we have to reach Lim's deliveries while the water is low.

“Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking.”
