### Vukradin

I remember the crate. I remember that I was following it, and that following it mattered, but the passage ahead no longer seems worth the trouble. The purpose is still there when I reach for it. The interest is gone.

There must be something more useful in another direction.

I take another direction.

The sewer carries on around me, damp underfoot, the confined air sitting against my face. By the time I stop, the others are no longer with me. I look back along the passage. I cannot account for why I chose this one. I can account for the crate, for the pursuit, for having companions, which ought to be enough to establish where a person is going, and yet here I am with none of those things in front of me.

A man stands ahead of a group of other men. He gives me a peculiar look.

“How the hell did you get over here?”

“I don't know.” I look past him, then back. “Where am I?”

He looks at me.

“Because I'm not over there.”

That answers how I come to be here in the broadest possible sense. It does very little for either of us in the narrower one. I try to understand what concerns him, what sort of men I have walked into, whether this is a misunderstanding I can resolve by explaining myself. I get nowhere. He seems confused rather than angry, but confusion is all we have in common so far.

I do know what I was doing before I arrived here. I do not think a detailed account of the crate will improve my request for directions. Being lost is the part that matters.

“Yeah, there you go. I'm lost. I don't know what… I don't know how I got here, I just…”

The words falter in exactly the place where an explanation should go. He accepts it. Whatever else he thinks of me, he believes that I do not know where I am.

“Yeah. Wrong turn. Should have taken that left at Albuquerque.”

He licks his finger.

“Well, this is my territory.”

“This is your territory.”

“Yes, this is my territory. Wow.”

I look at the tunnel again. I had not understood that I was crossing a boundary. A person can hardly respect a boundary he cannot locate.

“Did you want me to go?”

“I'd want to know why you're in my territory.”

“Oh, I'm sorry that I got lost. I'm happy to leave. Could you point me in the direction of Lim's? Cafe, that's… that's where I want to be headed.”

“Lim's Cafe?”

“We're friends.”

This makes things worse.

His attention sharpens. A moment ago I was an unexplained visitor; now the name of someone I know has given him a reason to examine me more closely. I cannot tell what Lim means to him, and I have no useful theory about why friendship should complicate directions to a cafe. Still, we have found a connection. We can work from a connection.

“Are you that singer?”

“Exactly. And Lim's a fan.”

“What are you doing down here, man?”

“I told you, I got lost!”

He looks at me again.

“Vukradin… you're Vukradin, right?”

One of the men beside him is smiling. Really smiling, openly pleased, and I feel the relief of it before I have quite caught up with the change in our situation.

“This is… is this really Vukradin?”

Here, too. Beneath Neverwinter, in a stretch of sewer I cannot name, somebody is happy to see me. I had been trying to explain my position in relation to several tunnels, when all along there was an entirely better way for us to understand each other.

“Do you guys want autographs?”

They would love autographs.

“Sure, we can do some autographs, but then I gotta be going.”

There are still friends to find. I am pleased to meet these men, and I want them to have something to remember the meeting by, but the fact that I have found fans does not mean I have found my way.

“Point me in the right direction.”

“You know what, we're gonna do one better, we're gonna take you there.”

“Alright, sounds good.”

“We don't want you to get lost again, Mr. Vukradin.”

“Lim will be thrilled.”

An escort is excellent. An escort means that the person who understands the tunnels will be responsible for choosing them, which seems a sensible division of abilities. I am ready to proceed on that basis when the leader pauses.

“It's very dangerous down here.”

“Is it?”

His answer begins uncertainly, and I try to make the question more useful.

“Yeah, it's dangerous in your territory? I would think you'd keep it very safe. You seem like a safe individual. I'm sure you keep the place shipshape.”

“Mr. Vukradin, I agree with you. But I have to tell you why I was out here. I was concerned when I saw you, and I'm very glad I recognized you. One of my guys tells me you have a deal with a friend of mine.”

“At the Common Chord?”

“No, no, at the Toe mine.”

“Mountain's Toe Mine. Oh, yeah…”

“Zeleen…”

“Oh, I didn't know you knew him.”

Zeleen Varnaster knows this man. The mine reaches farther than its production would suggest. I came down here following a crate and have somehow arrived at a business acquaintance.

“Yeah, we spent some time in Phandalin, yeah, yeah.”

“Yeah, he said that he had a business relationship with you, I said that he was full of shit.”

“No, no, that's true, that's true. Yeah, they haven't produced very much yet, but they say they're working hard.”

He laughs.

“That's how I feel about it, too.”

He laughs again. I am glad we can agree about this. Working hard is encouraging, but I would also appreciate something produced by the work. The studio in Phandalin needs actual money, and my ambitions do not become less sincere because I would like the mine to contribute some.

“Look, I manage the tunnels here,” he says, still laughing.

“Oh, you're the tunnel manager.”

“Yeah, a tunnel manager.”

“So this is your territory, and you have all the tunnels.”

“Not all the tunnels, but the tunnels in this area.”

That is a useful distinction. Rsolk manages a particular area, and something within it has become dangerous enough that he needs to warn an unexpected visitor. I listen while he explains.

“Two months ago, things started coming up the floodline.”

“Floodline.”

He describes a tunnel that floods regularly. I try to place it in relation to where we are, though the practical workings of this place remain well beyond me.

“I don't know anything about tunnels, man, I'm a musician.”

“I've been having this problem with the tunnels. I've lost men, so we don't go there anymore.”

The laughter is finished. I look at the men behind him. These are people who knew the way down here, people for whom this was familiar ground, and some of them went into a tunnel and did not come back. Knowing the route was not enough.

That is something my friends and I may be able to help with.

“Do you need help with that? My friends and I have some abilities, and we're looking for ways to make money. Maybe we can help you.”

He needs very little persuading. The offer has his attention immediately, and I can see a practical possibility opening between us: he needs a danger removed, we have experience removing dangers, and a payment for useful work could bring the studio closer without anybody having to pretend that finding somebody else's possessions makes them ours.

First, though, I need to understand what took his men.

“One of the guys who escaped said there was some kind of creature they'd never seen before.”

“Oh.”

“It looked like…”

“Creature stuff.”

“It looked like a spider, but it had all the wrong colors.”

That brings back something I do recognize. The strange webbing we encountered before, the wrongness of it, the difficulty of looking at something a spider had made and accepting that it belonged in front of us. Rsolk describes webs that were visibly wrong as well. I cannot identify a creature from a survivor's account alone, but this is more familiar territory than the floodline.

“Yeah, we have experience with that.”

I wish it were experience we had no further use for. Still, it is useful to Rsolk now. He has lost men, and we have faced something resembling what the survivor saw. We can come back with the others and investigate. I do not have to solve the tunnel by myself simply because I have wandered into it by myself.

With the possibility of help established, Rsolk has another request.

“Mr. Vukradin, I know this is too much to ask, but could you get me some tickets to the Common Chord?”

“I can ask.”

Tickets. I am glad he asks, though this is a different problem from spiders and requires its own accurate statement of our abilities.

“we have kind of an arm's length relationship with the Common Chord, but I will ask for you.”

“That's all I could ask for.”

We have not settled a price for clearing the tunnel. We have settled that he wants our help, that I will ask about tickets, and that there is work here worth bringing to my friends. That is enough to begin with. I can already imagine explaining the opportunity to them: unnatural spiders, men lost near a flooding tunnel, someone who knows how to get us there. A possible contribution to the studio, earned by making this place safer.

“Alright, let's do some autographs, and then we gotta be on our way.”

I give the men their autographs. The smiling associate is here in front of me, and for these few moments there is nothing difficult about understanding why we are all standing in this tunnel.

Then Rsolk and his associates escort me back toward familiar ground. I leave with the directions we will need to find him again, following people who know where they are going.
