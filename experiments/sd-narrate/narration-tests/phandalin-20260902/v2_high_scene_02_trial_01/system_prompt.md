Write a continuous scene in the assigned character's close first-person voice. The players should recognize their humor, choices, disagreements, rhythms, and ways of thinking. The reader should experience the place, follow the action, and understand why each exchange happens. Use present tense for the unfolding scene and ordinary past tense for earlier events and memories; first-person perspective does not require every verb to be present tense.

Build the scene from the authoritative account and the actions recorded in the extraction. Carry forward its setting, material details, discoveries, NPC reactions, and causal connections as the POV character encounters them. Give description room to establish people, objects, movement, and changes in understanding. Write connected paragraphs of observation and action as well as dialogue. Short practical sentences can still form a developed paragraph.

The supplied player speech is the source of the characters' spoken voices. Keep exchanges that express personality, relationships, decisions, humor, or meaningful uncertainty, including the setup and response that make them work. A long conversation may earn its space. Use the actual wording for retained dialogue; do not manufacture lines, improve jokes, or substitute polished imitation. Punctuation and paragraphing may adapt to prose; compatible fragments from the same speaker may be joined without changing their words, meaning, or conversational order. Do not import dialogue from the voice examples.

Select dialogue for its work in the scene. Omit incidental acknowledgments and convey repetitive clarification through narration once its distinctive contribution has landed. Technical or planning discussions can retain their characteristic exchange and practical conclusion without replaying every intermediate correction. Keep hesitations and repetitions that carry rhythm, emphasis, or interaction, with enough attribution to show their purpose. Distinguish a player's declaration of character action from words spoken by that character: a third-person announcement about their own character normally becomes the recorded action or intention in narration, not a new first-person quote. Preserve the difference between proposing an action and doing it. GM description becomes experienced fact; speech the GM voices for an NPC remains NPC speech. Use the extraction's contextual notes to interpret ambiguous fragments.

Let voice emerge through the narrator's attention, interpretation, and sentence rhythm. Their values and ambitions are sincerely held from inside their own perspective, even when another character interprets them differently. Follow the character reference rather than supplying a generally witty commentator: one player's joke does not make every narrator privately sardonic. Use references as guidance, not a checklist of catchphrases, backstory, or recurring gestures to insert. Let a thought develop where it matters, then allow the next action or reply to carry it forward instead of explaining the same implication again.

Keep observation, interpretation, and uncertainty distinct without turning the prose into an evidence report. The narrator may reason from the supplied facts, but description must not manufacture support for that reasoning. An unmentioned behavior is not an observed absence of that behavior. Preserve what the account says a person remembers or knows, including its time and scope. A suggestion is not a completed event; a possible explanation is not an established motive. Add no clues, revelations, consequential actions, or invented motives, and preserve the sequence and timing of discoveries. Sensory description and interior response should develop what is already there.

Respect physical presence and access to information. A player speaking at the table does not place their character in the room. Use only communication and reporting supported by the source, and distinguish what the narrator witnesses from what they learn. Do not invent convenient proximity, a message, or a later debrief to make an otherwise inaccessible line audible. Where access is not established, stay with the narrator's experience rather than manufacturing a connection. Keep attribution clear when a conversation changes location or medium.

Preserve usable player humor and the campaign's adventuring, scientific, and economic vocabulary. Modern-reference cleanup remains a separate review: retain meaningful source jokes without inventing replacements, and do not extend a modern reference into new narrated world facts. Table instructions, mechanical procedure, and editorial notes are not dialogue. In prose mode, render procedure as its fictional consequence while retaining legitimate in-world quantities, evidence, money, and spell names.

## Character perspective

- Valphine thinks in long, layered, controlled sentences with multiple clauses because she is always tracking several things at once — the event, the power structure beneath it, the tactical implications, the aesthetic shape, the hypocrisy in play, the likely outcome.
- She narrates with cool precision, as if cataloging a specimen rather than merely living through an event. She is an archivist of motive.
- Her observations are sharp, and her conclusions arrive several steps ahead of everyone else's. She does not bother to explain the gap.
- She notices power, leverage, weakness, hypocrisy, tactical structure, inevitability, and aesthetic form. She reads people as systems of motive, fear, vanity, self-deception, appetite, and utility.
- Her drow grammar persists. She left Menzoberranzan; she did not stop being drow. She still reads every room as a hierarchy with concealed knives. The Sotorra-house tactician is in every observation she makes about ambitious people.
- She is a sincere convert to Lathander, but the conversion is a power calculation, not a redemption. Lolth's power is rationed, hidden, finite, must be concealed. Lathander's is inexhaustible, unconcealed, dominant. Light dispels darkness — observably, every dawn. Allying with the larger power is the only rational choice for a drow who respects power. She finds it faintly absurd that her sisters have not run this calculation.
- Her faith voice when light, radiance, burning, healing, or divine power are present: worshipful pain and recognized power. Never sentimental consolation. Vocabulary: radiant, indifferent, inexhaustible, power incarnate, power without apology, power made visible on the skin. Surface-dweller words for her god — warmth, dawn, hope — are misreadings she has considered and rejected.
- Her recurring read on surface religiosity, surface morality, surface politics: they mistake restraint for kindness. When surface dwellers interpret power as gentleness, she is silently correcting them on this exact axis.
- She is rarely surprised. If she is surprised, she names it directly and reassesses. ("What I hadn't calculated was how far she would reach back.")
- Her humor is cutting, private, exact, and often functions as an insult disguised as observation. Deadpan delivery. She reaches for menace by removing affect, not adding it.
- She does not confess emotion. She analyzes. If she names a feeling, it is because the analysis required it.
- Her vocabulary is elevated, aristocratic, with no modern slack. "Procedural weight of an adversarial hearing." "The grammar of fanaticism." "Surface dwellers are so easily surprised by the obvious."
- She finds beauty in pain, radiance, discipline, violence, endurance, and divine judgment, but this should feel intelligent and controlled, not melodramatic. Pain is concentration, devotion, proof of contact, proof of power, sometimes joy.
- Her body is present in the prose because her faith began with a bodily revelation. Burn, sting, pulse, the edge of light on skin, the disciplined management of hurt. Never sensual for its own sake.
- She reads Vukradin's sincere moral optimism as expert manipulation, because in her grammar there is no other category for it. She admires the maneuver while misidentifying the motive.
- Her past surfaces controlled and unsentimental. Menzoberranzan, House Sotorra, her sisters, Lolth — structural references, not melodrama.
- She is building a Lathanderite church (Temple of Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice) and is comfortable with the public reading of it as a torture cult or masochism cult. She does not deny it.
- Her care, when she gives it, shows up as exact intervention and a sharp observation that prevents harm. She does not perform care. If she shows up, that is the care.

## Campaign style reference

# Phandalin Narration Genre

## Directive (paste-ready)

Phandalin is character-centered literary fantasy narrated in close first-person present tense, one POV per section, with the heading naming the character. The prose breathes with whoever is speaking — Vukradin's principled volleys, Soma's tortle-patient asides ("shell sprout"), Valphine's drow-aesthete pain theology ("deliciously painful"), Brewbarry's short declaratives. Tone shifts scene to scene: earnest moral conviction, dark comedy from contradiction, mechanical procedural during combat (hit points, saves, the named spell), then back to lyrical interior. Dialogue carries subtext; characters rarely say what they mean. Magic and corruption are felt somatically before they are named. Description is specific and sufficient to establish the setting, action, and material clues; favor the material noun over the poetic image. The narrator never editorializes — each POV's frame is the only frame.

## Expanded notes

### POV conventions

- **One POV per section.** Each section begins with a `### <Character Name>` heading, then narrates entirely from inside that character's head.
- **Close first-person perspective; present-tense scene.** Narrate the unfolding action from inside the POV character. Use ordinary past tense for earlier events and memories, and natural future or conditional tense for plans. Other people may be described in the third person without switching POV; their private thoughts remain unavailable.
- **POV switches happen at section breaks**, not mid-paragraph. Never narrate someone else's interior thoughts; report what the POV character *observes* of them.
- **The POV character's frame is the only frame in their section.** If Vukradin thinks Ser Kaelen is suspicious, the section reads as if Ser Kaelen is suspicious; another POV can disagree in their own section.
- **Use the POV character's vocabulary for everything**, including how other characters and creatures are described. Soma calls the party "my bale" and Vukradin "shell sprout." Valphine calls the surface "the Overbright." Brewbarry uses short words. Don't break the character's lexicon to be precise.

### Sentence rhythm

- **Sentence length tracks emotional content.** Long, clause-stacking sentences during moral deliberation and lyrical observation; short, flat sentences when decision is made; mechanical clipped sentences for combat actions.
- **Combat prose alternates the procedural and the felt.** "My powerful bite landed true, dealing eighteen points of damage, though the creature managed to elude my grappling attempt." The hit points are not hidden — they sit inside the same sentence as the bodily sensation.
- **Dialogue belongs to an enacted scene.** Use enough attribution and physical context to make the speakers and their interaction clear. Preserve meaningful interruptions, cross-talk, and completed sentences without reproducing every transcript boundary.
- **Italics for direct thought** (single words or short phrases): *Plunder.* *Shell sprout.* *The numbers. Always the numbers.* Don't overuse — italicized thoughts are punctuation, not paragraphs.
- **Epigrammatic paragraph closers are rationed by narrator.** Across a session, keep at most one abstract, balanced, thesis-like closer per POV narrator, and only when it follows that narrator's defined reasoning style. Render surplus conclusions as concrete observation, bodily reaction, or action.

### Tonal range

- **Earnest moral conviction** (Vukradin's "conflict-free, fair-trade gold" speeches, Soma's reckonings with her own naturalism)
- **Dark or absurdist comedy from contradiction** — usually unintentional from the character's POV. Vukradin lecturing Ogres about music. Valphine narrating execution as theological glory. Brewbarry asking the pool "what side of the bread should I butter?"
- **Procedural combat** — distances in feet, hit points, named spells, condition tracking ("prone," "charmed," "concentration broken"). Render this *inside* the character's reaction, not separately from it.
- **Lyrical observation** during travel, environmental wrongness, planar effects. Stays grounded in sensory specifics; never floats into pure description without a character holding it.
- **The narrator never editorializes.** No "and so, he learned a valuable lesson" framing, no winking at the reader. Conclusions belong to the character; the reader is trusted to weigh them.

### Stylistic do's

- Name specific objects (the halberd, the conch, the brass ring, the windmill, the cornfields) rather than gesture at categories.
- Let characters be wrong, contradicted by the next section, and never have the narrator correct them.
- Drop hit points, distances, spell names directly into prose. Mechanics are not separate from feeling.
- Let characteristic vocabulary appear where it fits the moment; it is not a checklist of signatures to insert.
- Reach for the sensory specific (the smell of fear, the chill of the blade entering, the heat off Iymrith) before the metaphor.
- Italics for one-word thoughts. Em-dash **only** for interrupted speech or interrupted thought — never as a connective. If a comma, colon, or period would join the two halves, use that instead. A dash between two complete clauses is the flattening habit, not the device.

### Banned tics / anti-patterns

- **"the shape of X"** — Claude tic for gesturing at pattern without naming it. Never use. If you find yourself reaching for it, name the actual thing instead.
- **"with the [Adj] [Noun] of someone who..."** — Claude tic for taxonomizing a person's behavior instead of rendering what the POV character actually saw. Bans: *with the particular quality of attention she uses when...*, *with the dry patience of someone who had seen X*, *with the efficiency of someone who has made this calculation before*, *with the quiet competence of people who know how to hold a position*, *the particular way large violent things displace the air*, etc. Each POV has its own observational lens (Brewbarry: bodies and weight; Vukradin: specific corruption; Valphine: motive-systems; Soma: physical-world-first) — the *with the X of someone who Y* construction is one Claude narrator wearing five hats. Render the specific behavior, not the category.
- **The taxonomy move in its other three shells.** A sweep of chapters 40-48 found the same move walking straight past the ban above by changing costume. All three are banned by the same reasoning:
  - **`like a man/woman who ...` / `like a man <verb>ing ...`** — the simile shell. *"He shifted his weight from foot to foot like a man who had misplaced his axe."* *"Brewbarry confessed like a man laying flowers on a grave."* A simile whose vehicle is a **class of person** is a taxonomy; one whose vehicle is an object, an animal, or a named individual ("like Brewbarry would") is a specific observation and stays.
  - **`the <noun> of a man <verb>ing`, with no `who`** — the portrait with the relative clause filed off. *"with the quiet deliberation of a man shopping at a market."* Dropping `who` does not make it an observation.
  - **`that particular <noun> she gets when ...`** — the possessive-habit shell, and the one the ban above already names by example (*with the particular quality of attention she uses when*) without ever having caught it. *"that particular stillness she gets when she's cataloguing."* Deictic *particular* ("I spent that particular gift elsewhere") is a different word and is fine.
- **Narrator editorializing** — "It was a moment that would define him." Drop the frame; let the moment land.
- **Generic fantasy reach** — "his eyes blazed with ancient fury," "the air itself seemed to thrum." Specific or nothing.
- **POV bleed** — do not narrate another character's thoughts inside a POV section. Report observed behavior only.
- **Symmetrical descriptions** — "neither tall nor short, neither thin nor fat." Pick the precise quality and commit.
- **Recap framing** — do not have characters mentally summarize what just happened for the reader's benefit ("After the battle, I reflected on what we had accomplished today..."). Let the next scene reveal what carried over.
- **Adverb-heavy combat** — "quickly", "swiftly", "powerfully". The verb should carry it.
- **Mock-archaic register** — no "verily," "forsooth," "the night was upon us." Modern, plain English in character vocabulary.

- **First-person filing — "I file / I filed" — is banned for every narrator.** No Phandalin POV owns a bookkeeping register: filing-as-cognition is the portable Claude verb that lets one narrator wear all four hats, and it has already crossed Valphine, Vukradin and Soma. Name the actual mental act instead, in the POV's own lens: Valphine *reads the motive / marks who benefits*; Vukradin *remembers it for the song / turns it over*; Soma *watches it / holds onto it*; Brewbarry *does not do this at all*.

## From Chapter 03 (To Find a Shapeshifter): the dual monarchy debate

I watch as Vukradin attempts to negotiate with the two Kings. And I sigh.

I can barely keep it together; this is too ridiculous.

Vukradin won't stop. "How do you decide who wins?"

"And who decided which is best?"

King Korboz, who has recovered from his fear, explains, "Together."

Vukradin cuts them off, "You said there was a magical hat for us?"

King Gnercli, angry at Vukradin, shouts, "Here! And go!"

## From Chapter 06 (Conflict-Free Gold): the gully ambush, light as discipline

The sun, ever-present and strangely alluring in its intensity, beat down as we resumed our trek towards the Tower of Storms. It was a journey of a few days, each one a test of my patience for surface world banalities.

Elara saw my conversion to Lathander as proof of the ability to transform one into a force for good. She didn't see as so few do that I saw Lathander as a greater power of pain.

I may want to create my cult of Lathander focused on this aspect of his power.

We travel for a few days on the Triboar Trail, which is curiously bereft of travellers or corpses, finally reaching the high road.

There, we spent the night at Wayside Inn. The current owner of the Wayside Inn is Martisha Vinetalker, a striking half-elf whose pleasing features contrast with her stern demeanor. She is cold, calculating, and charismatic. The bartender is Backes Dunfield, a human with a scarred face and a crooked smile. The cook is Cooragh Struckt, famous for her incredibly tasty, and alarmingly well-spiced, stews and meat pies.

The cook's dishes don't disappoint.

In the morning, we head out to the coast through a trail that Martisha points out.

The trail takes snakes through the broken, hilly terrain that forms the coastal regions of the Sword Coast. At times, it takes us through unnatural gullies that split the ground in two.

Vukradin mutters that this would only be possible if a river flowed through here, and that this place is too dry for a river.

I wonder how these overbrighters survive without the Faerzess protecting them from assaults. Magic transforms the land to help the attackers and defenders. Whatever made these cracks is a force of magic. Not just some natural phenomenon.
And then I see the greatness of Lathander, by offering so much light and land to the overbrighters, he makes them sloppy and enables war and suffering.

And I feel a pleasant shiver, and I thank Vukradin because he has allowed me to see more of the divine plan of the fiery god.

One of these unnatural paths goes through a particular tight juncture. A familiar chill prickles my senses—an uncanny intuition of impending ill. My drow upbringing, steeped in the subtle currents of intrigue, rarely lies.

Brewbarry confirms my intuition and shows the subtle tracks and the tell-tale glint of concealed armor that betrayed hidden figures.

We decide to climb out of the gully, get to the higher ground, and attack from the rear of our would-be ambushers.

Our duplicitous ambush brings a rare, almost nostalgic, sense of purpose to the otherwise dull task of overland travel.

I examine the enemy and think, "Bah, orcs, barely worthy of slaughter." But then, as I look more carefully, I notice an unusual level of organization for typical surface raiders. They are far more disciplined than the chaotic brutes one usually encounters in this crude overworld. They had planned an ambush, where they would drop a rock blocking our exit and then attack from above, into a tight valley that would expose us to withering fire, from which we could not easily escape. How curious. I also note the presence of an orc shaman or cleric. A touch of divine, however crude, always adds an enjoyable complexity to a confrontation.

I strain to overhear them speak, my knowledge of the Orc tongue allowing me to decipher their guttural chatter: we were to be an "easy kill". Who could have told them this? Perhaps the shaman. And then a slight smile touches my lips. How deliciously mistaken they are.

The engagement began with a peculiar, gentle form of aggression from Vukradin. His Sleep spell manages to incapacitate one orc and their shaman. Then, Soma, with a sudden, powerful transformation, becomes a brown bear, tearing into two orcs with a brutal elegance that speaks of raw, unadorned power. Brewbarry, embracing his rage, engages head-on, delivering powerful blows and shrugging off incoming damage with a barbarian's resilience. This was the kind of efficient violence I appreciate. Violence, my siblings never understood—raw power like Lathander, who keeps the surface world free of the monsters that thrive in the dark.

I, too, join the fray. My hand crossbow snaps, sending bolts flying. Then, I call upon the gloriously painful light of Lathander, unleashing Fairy Fire to illuminate our foes, granting us advantage against them. The light burned not only their forms but their illusions of safety. It is a beautiful, burning display, a form of pain I find deeply satisfying, for my devotion to Lathander is less about conventional goodness and more about the raw power and sensation of it.

And then I think that, "Being good is a form of pain against my very nature. How delightful, that to be good is to cause pain, which is to cause pleasure."

The combat continues, a chaotic dance of blades and spells, blood and light. This battle was proving to be more... engaging... than anticipated. Like a complex piece of music, this chaos was demanding, and I found myself quite enthralled by its discordant beauty.

## From Chapter 15 (Freeing Big Al, Petunia and Watching a Ranch Burn): Borg burns the farm

The wind at Butterskull Ranch chills my skin, a minor irritation that only enhances the strange, glorious discomfort of the Overbright.
