Write a continuous scene in the assigned character's close first-person voice. The players should recognize their humor, choices, disagreements, rhythms, and ways of thinking. The reader should experience the place, follow the action, and understand why each exchange happens. Use present tense for the unfolding scene and ordinary past tense for earlier events and memories; first-person perspective does not require every verb to be present tense.

Build the scene from the authoritative account and the actions recorded in the extraction. Carry forward its setting, material details, discoveries, NPC reactions, and causal connections as the POV character encounters them. Give description room to establish people, objects, movement, and changes in understanding. Write connected paragraphs of observation and action as well as dialogue. Short practical sentences can still form a developed paragraph.

The supplied player speech is the source of the characters' spoken voices. Keep exchanges that express personality, relationships, decisions, humor, or meaningful uncertainty, including the setup and response that make them work. A long conversation may earn its space. Use the actual wording for retained dialogue; do not manufacture lines, improve jokes, or substitute polished imitation. Punctuation and paragraphing may adapt to prose; compatible fragments from the same speaker may be joined without changing their words, meaning, or conversational order. Do not import dialogue from the voice examples.

Select dialogue for its work in the scene. Omit incidental acknowledgments and convey repetitive clarification through narration once its distinctive contribution has landed. Technical or planning discussions can retain their characteristic exchange and practical conclusion without replaying every intermediate correction. Keep hesitations and repetitions that carry rhythm, emphasis, or interaction, with enough attribution to show their purpose. Distinguish a player's declaration of character action from words spoken by that character: a third-person announcement about their own character normally becomes the recorded action or intention in narration, not a new first-person quote. Preserve the difference between proposing an action and doing it. GM description becomes experienced fact; speech the GM voices for an NPC remains NPC speech. Use the extraction's contextual notes to interpret ambiguous fragments.

Let voice emerge through the narrator's attention, interpretation, and sentence rhythm. Their values and ambitions are sincerely held from inside their own perspective, even when another character interprets them differently. Follow the character reference rather than supplying a generally witty commentator: one player's joke does not make every narrator privately sardonic. Use references as guidance, not a checklist of catchphrases, backstory, or recurring gestures to insert. Let a thought develop where it matters, then allow the next action or reply to carry it forward instead of explaining the same implication again.

Keep observation, interpretation, and uncertainty distinct without turning the prose into an evidence report. The narrator may reason from the supplied facts, but description must not manufacture support for that reasoning. An unmentioned behavior is not an observed absence of that behavior. Preserve what the account says a person remembers or knows, including its time and scope. A suggestion is not a completed event; a possible explanation is not an established motive. Add no clues, revelations, consequential actions, or invented motives, and preserve the sequence and timing of discoveries. Sensory description and interior response should develop what is already there.

Respect physical presence and access to information. A player speaking at the table does not place their character in the room. Use only communication and reporting supported by the source, and distinguish what the narrator witnesses from what they learn. Do not invent convenient proximity, a message, or a later debrief to make an otherwise inaccessible line audible. Where access is not established, stay with the narrator's experience rather than manufacturing a connection. Keep attribution clear when a conversation changes location or medium.

Preserve usable player humor and the campaign's adventuring, scientific, and economic vocabulary. Modern-reference cleanup remains a separate review: retain meaningful source jokes without inventing replacements, and do not extend a modern reference into new narrated world facts. Table instructions, mechanical procedure, and editorial notes are not dialogue. In prose mode, render procedure as its fictional consequence while retaining legitimate in-world quantities, evidence, money, and spell names.
