# Narration prompt experiment — complete session

Unedited experimental narrations using gpt-6-astra at medium reasoning. Scene 4 is the accepted earlier test; the other four use the same writing brief. Review findings are in all_scenes_review.md. This is a reading copy, not a replacement for the campaign's production narration.

## Scene 1: Patrons of the Common Chord

### Vukradin

Aldus Hern still has not given us an exact time for dinner, which makes it difficult to arrange an evening around Lord Dagult Neverember, particularly when we have somewhere to be at three in the morning. We can wait for a summons with a time attached. Until then, the lord can wait for us.

“Is it Neverember or Neverwinter? I'm kidding.”

The name catches in my mouth anyway. “Neverember. It is never, like, a fire ember.”

“He's never flaming. He keeps it on the DL,” Soma says.

“Yeah, I think Soma has it exactly right.”

We are in the Common Chord, where I used to play to empty rooms and could be thrown out unpaid. Now it is the hardest ticket in Neverwinter. I look across the patrons, the food, the table commemorating Brewbarry, and try to reconcile the room with the one I remember. There is leather on that table now. A plaque. My name apparently does better business here when I am elsewhere.

“Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor.”

“Great, isn't that Valphine?” Soma asks.

“No.”

“Oh, right.”

“It's a rumor, and I suspect Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see.”

Valphine turns her attention back to us. “I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I?”

“Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming.”

I bring her back to the rumored advisor.

“Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact.”

“That is my guess too, yes.”

“Everyone's related to everyone,” Soma says.

It remains a guess. We have a dinner without a time and an advisor without a name, and putting the two together does not yet give us a sibling. There are practical matters we can settle before we meet either.

“Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?”

“I believe it's in progress,” Soma says.

For a moment I have to sort the money from the goods it is meant to buy. The capital is secured. Getting the imports here is the expensive part.

“But, oh, but the cost of import is very high.”

“Right, because their shipping lanes are still…” Soma pauses. “Shipping portals, whatever, are still hosed.”

And House Margaster controls the available logistics.

“Of course they are.”

I cannot dispute that they deliver on time and on schedule. That much must be acknowledged, even while we sit here trying to work out how Brewbarry can afford to bring softness to people through a house that controls how the softness gets here. Being reliable does not make the import bill smaller.

Valphine considers House Margaster competition. I turn that over briefly, then return to the part of the night for which we actually have a time.

“Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by 3 AM. And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready.”

There is still time before we need to go. Around us, the Common Chord continues charging people to enjoy the place where they once declined to enjoy me.

One of those people is discussing precisely this injustice within earshot. The Chord has become unaffordable, he complains, and it is all due to Vukradin.

He has not noticed that Vukradin is standing nearby.

I listen. He wants to hear music without spending so much money. I want people to hear music without spending so much money. We have reached agreement before I even introduce myself, which is an excellent beginning, provided we can get past the part where I am apparently responsible for his bill.

I approach him and explain that I am planning an independent studio, with a charitable fund to help open it.

“That will speed the opening of a low-cost, nonprofit way to hear Lathander's Death on a regular basis.”

There. Something he can help bring into existence. A place he can actually attend. My studio does not have to remain an idea I carry between other people's expensive rooms; if the people who want it help, then we can have the room, and the music, and an audience that does not have to dress for the privilege.

“And spread the word.”

While he considers the offer, a nearby conversation supplies something rather less pleasant to think about. The river sends a tide through the sewers. We are not being warned personally; people at the bar are discussing it among themselves, and I catch enough to turn back toward my companions.

“Hide in the sewers?”

I had understood that we were preparing for a late-night operation. I would also like to understand whether the floor is going to become a river.

“There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?”

Neverwinter is beside the sea. The sea comes in, the river rises, and the water does not politely stop at the underground entrances. Once I follow that connection, the next question seems obvious.

“How many moons does this planet have?”

“That's an easy question to answer,” Valphine says.

“Just the one.”

“It just rotates—it revolves really fast,” Soma says.

“It has one moon named Selûne,” Valphine tells me.

“Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah.”

There are also the Tears of Selûne, which briefly complicate my understanding of what is pulling on what.

“So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons.”

But the Tears are a cluster of smaller bodies, meteors and asteroids, rather than another moon. I work back through it.

“They're not sufficiently gravitational. They're not really moons.”

“Yeah, they're… they're just comets,” Soma says.

“They're asteroids. So the one moon, then.”

The useful information from the neighboring conversation is two high tides and two low tides each day. I hold on to that.

“There you go. Not one and one: two high and two low.”

“That's murder on trade,” Soma says.

“It should take effect underground, too, right?” Valphine asks.

Yes. That is the problem. The sewer belongs to the same rising water as the river, however much stone is over our heads.

“So it's a 6-hour cycle.”

I begin counting the intervals, briefly halve one that does not need halving, and correct myself aloud.

“High to low is 3 hours. Yeah, no, high to low. High to low is 6 hours, 6… High to low is 6 hours.”

This is why we discuss things before entering the sewer.

“We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide.”

“3 AM,” Soma says.

“We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable.”

“I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely.”

I look at Soma. An excellent capability for Soma.

The local I have approached is a little drunk. He does not have much money, but he hands me ten gold pieces.

For a moment the sewer leaves my thoughts entirely.

“Wow, that's pretty generous, actually.”

“That's a hell of a donation,” Soma says.

It is. He has just been complaining about what it costs to be here, and now he is contributing ten gold toward somewhere less expensive. I keep the coins separate from our ordinary funds.

“That goes in the studio fund, not the regular coffers. Thank you very much for contributing. That's awesome.”

He pauses before the exchange is quite finished.

“Do I get on… Is there some kind of signage for people who contributed?”

“Absolutely. Yes.”

This is reasonable. People should know who helped.

“There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. 10 gold pieces puts you at the,”

“Premier or the Plus, which one?” Soma asks.

“At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet.”

He is pleased with Plus. I am pleased with ten gold, and with the prospect of a program that will have both music and the names of people who made the music possible.

“We need his name. We need to write it down for the program notes, and…”

The duration matters. I clarify it.

“Per year. A year. If you want to be in next year's, you'll have to donate again, but one year is a long time.”

“Yeah, we call this Patreon,” Valphine says.

“It's my Patreon, exactly.”

“This is where it comes from, patronage,” Soma says.

I look around the Common Chord again. There are people here who want music, people who remember music, people paying for an evening in a room that trades on my name. Some of them might want to help build another one.

“Before we go, should I try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?”

Plenty before three.

“Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here.”

I approach five more patrons. The first recognizes me as the actual Vukradin, which is useful, but has already completed his charitable giving for the year.

“Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody.”

He has given to charity. Good. The fact that it is not my charity does not undo that, and I am not going to make a person regret recognizing me because he has already helped somebody else.

“The benefits are all on you. If you can be at the Plus or Premier level, I'm cool. I just like fans.”

He assures me that he is my biggest fan.

“Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?”

“Brewbarry's very large,” Valphine says. “In fact, he's huge in Japan sometimes.”

Brewbarry is there to substantiate the first part of this claim. I glance toward him and accept the limit of the comparison. There is no contribution required to remain a fan.

The other four are receptive. I make sure we record Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, and Ondrelle Kass for the future program credits. Names matter here. These are people helping pay for the studio; when there is finally a program, I want them in it.

Wick is enthusiastic about patronage of the arts. He has no idea who Lathander's Death is, and he does not believe I am Vukradin, but neither uncertainty interferes with his support. He understands that I am putting together a music school.

“School?” Soma asks.

“School, studio—they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people. That's just how it goes.”

I see no difficulty. People come to make music. People learn to make music. We will need somewhere to do both, and Wick wants to help provide it. He does not even require me to be famous.

Sabbatha's attention moves past the studio's prospective founder to Brewbarry. She looks him over and admires his commitment to the Brewbarry look.

I look too. It is a considerable commitment.

“Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?”

“Rippling,” Soma supplies.

“Exactly.”

Sabbatha contributes. I make certain her name is among the credits.

Old Hesp is the last patron I speak with. He recognizes me without needing to be persuaded.

“I remember when you used to play to an empty room.”

“Yeah?”

I look at him more closely. An empty room is how I remember it too, but there must have been someone in it for someone to remember me playing.

“Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back at the end of.”

I do not finish before he confirms it. He remembers the guarantee: if he would not rate the performance ten out of ten, I would not keep his money. He remembers getting it back.

“I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?”

He pauses.

“Now this is a friggin' expensive place to be. Everybody's gotta look cool.”

I follow his attention across the room. The people here have managed the requirement. That does not mean everyone who wants to hear the music can manage it, or afford to discover whether they like it, and I cannot ask him what he thinks now and then refuse to hear the answer because I would rather receive a compliment.

“Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running.”

He tries to describe what this place has lost, the look and the feel of it, the grit. I know the room he means. I remember standing in it.

“We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back.”

“This is too commercial.”

“Yeah, yeah, I agree.”

He blames Petra, though there is respect mixed into the complaint. She has turned the Common Chord into a money-making operation. He points out Brewbarry's table, remembering it before the leather finish, before the shine, before the plaque.

I look at the leather. The old table was available for use without being an attraction. Now people can sit near evidence that Brewbarry once sat here, and elsewhere in the room Sabbatha is admiring how convincingly Brewbarry resembles himself.

Old Hesp remembers when they did not serve this fancy food, either.

He is giving toward the studio while telling me all this. I do not have to convince him that something is missing. He has come back to the Common Chord and found the table commemorated more carefully than the evening he used to have around it.

Twenty gold pieces go into the studio fund tonight. Petra's separate five hundred for my likeness is still pending until the performance two days from now. I keep that promise separate from the money actually in hand, and keep the donor names with the obligation to credit them for a year.

There is room in the studio I am imagining for Wick to learn something, for Old Hesp to come without looking fashionable, for this generous local to hear Lathander's Death regularly. I want to get the doors open. Tonight, people who want to walk through them have helped.

Now we have to reach Lim's deliveries while the water is low.

“Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking.”

## Scene 2: The Ninth Crate Disappears

### Valphine Sotorra

The steps descend toward the sewer grille in shallow, aqueduct-like courses, wet stone offering very little purchase and the darkness below offering considerably more concealment. I watch the approach, the ledge beside the opening, and the two men carrying crates; a delivery has at least three useful parts, the goods, the labor, and the person who knows where the goods are meant to go. We have yet to distinguish the last two.

Vukradin has already offered his services.

“I have a very high stealth, if we need me to…”

Soma regards him. “I didn't know bards got high stealth.”

“And it's just because I have boots of elvenkind.”

“Hell yeah.”

The boots are a useful advantage. They also make him the obvious choice for a task the rest of us might prefer him to undertake in company.

“I mean, you could tag along with a familiar…” Soma says.

She casts *Find Familiar*, and a rat joins our surveillance, small enough to belong beside the wet steps without explanation. Through its eyes and ears she can examine what waits below, provided it remains within a hundred feet of her. A useful extension of our attention, although the sewer is unlikely to respect the limit.

The carriers bring their loads down.

“All 9 of them?” Soma asks.

All nine. Eight accumulate together beside the grille, and on those eight I can see no official Commission Harbor mark. Whatever journey brought them into Neverwinter has omitted the authorities’ preferred opportunities to inspect, record, and collect. The absence is consistent with what we already know; the arrangement interests me more.

“So we want one person to track down the gnome, right? Crate 9,” Vukradin says. “We also want to track the people who dropped them. So we want to do two things.”

“What, you said you got your high stealth.”

“If you want, I can track the gnome. You guys can go do the… the sewers.”

“Well, I'm tracking the crate,” Soma says.

“Which crate?”

“The ninth crate, the one that doesn't go into Lim, because I'm more concerned about my friend's shady business.”

I keep my attention on the men while the assignments circulate. Lim’s eight crates are together; the ninth is being handled separately. Our prospective quarry has not yet appeared, and already its parcel commands more discussion than the men who bring it.

“Okay, so you want me to follow them back into the sewers?” Vukradin asks. “That seems pretty dangerous. One party member in the sewers.”

“I see. Alright, we can send the rat that way, then. You can follow the crate.”

One carrier places the ninth crate on the ledge. He pauses there, looks down into the dark, and then leaves it. His companion continues stacking boxes.

The glance is brief enough to be mistaken for caution. There is wet stone underfoot, after all, and an opening beside him. But he looks into the place from which something might arrive. I mark the distinction.

“The rat's gonna go whichever way leads to the sewer,” Soma says.

Vukradin works through the division again, giving each part its own sentence.

“So, look, the… The crates got dropped off. Lim's gonna come out and take 8 of the crates.”

“Right.”

“And she's not gonna take a 9th crate.”

The isolated box rests on its ledge while the carriers finish. Neither has any apparent intention of waiting beside it.

“Vukradin is gonna stick around and stealthily wait to see who comes to get the ninth crate, and then track it.”

“So everyone other than Vukradin is gonna trail these guys, and Vukradin's gonna trail the crate,” I say. “Is that right?”

“Yes,” Vukradin says.

“Yes,” Soma agrees.

An assignment, finally. Then the carriers reach the point at which they might naturally exchange a glance, a word, some small acknowledgment of having just performed the same work, and carefully do none of these things. They walk away in different directions.

“That nonchalant whistling,” Soma says.

I watch their refusal to look at one another. It takes discipline to suppress every ordinary acknowledgment, but rather better discipline to know which acknowledgments should remain. Their separation advertises an intention to separate.

“So if they split up, that's gonna make them hard to follow,” Vukradin says. “You're gonna have to choose one or split again.”

The man at the ledge is my choice, though I examine the conclusion before trusting it. Handling the exceptional crate does not necessarily make him senior; an instructed servant can perform a special errand without understanding it. At first the evidence gives me only that much. Then I hold his pause against his companion’s uninterrupted stacking, letting the last encouragement Vukradin has left me steady my attention.

One man completes a physical task. The other checks for its continuation.

I cannot establish rank from a glance. I can identify which man expects something to emerge from the sewer.

“I'd follow the guy who paid attention to the ledge,” Soma says. “He knows something. He knows something's coming out of that thing.”

That is sufficient agreement. Brewbarry, Soma, and I will follow him.

“I could handle this myself, guys, you don't trust me?”

Before the question requires an answer, something comes up for the ninth crate.

I am surprised. I had allowed for a collector, including the gnome Vukradin expects; what appears is cat-sized, brass and leather, quick in its movements without displaying any haste. It takes the crate and goes back down. There is no examination of the other eight, no search for a mark, no hesitation over which object belongs to it.

The carrier’s downward glance acquires a more precise meaning. He has been checking for an expected collection, and the thing collecting needs neither greeting nor payment here. Whether he knows who instructs it remains unanswered.

“Okay, Vukradin will pursue stealthily.”

“This is my concern,” Soma says. “Vukradin ends up going into the sewers now, and…”

“I'll be fine.”

“One person in the sewers? Alright.”

The objection is almost exactly the one he raises himself a short while ago. Apparently the appearance of something sufficiently interesting has altered the acceptable number of companions.

“Tell me the odds,” Soma says.

“Only a 1 in 400 chance of disaster. It'll be fine.”

A remarkably specific confidence. His boots can suppress the sound of his approach; I see no reason to assign them authority over everything he may approach.

Nevertheless, the construct is withdrawing, and our carrier is leaving. Vukradin follows the brass-and-leather collector below while the three of us take the street after the man who watches ledges. The grille falls behind us. I have no view of Vukradin’s progress once he descends.

Our man stops at a cook shop.

At the fourth hour, there are few open doors offering food, and this is one of them. He settles to breakfast alone, apparently content with the meal. No companion joins him. He speaks to no waiting contact that I can see. Having left eight crates for Lim and one for a construct, he applies himself to eating.

“Can't be a lot of those,” Soma says of the open shop.

An early meal is a reasonable end to a delivery. It is also an inconvenient one for those hoping the delivery will lead directly to an employer. I watch him eat, allowing neither possibility to improve the evidence.

Vukradin’s voice reaches Soma through her Sending Stone.

“By the way, Soma, you have one of the Sending Stones, and I have the other.”

“Good to know.”

The paired stones give our separated inquiries a means of reaching one another. They do not shorten the streets between us or improve the approach through the grille, but I prefer a distant voice to an unexplained absence.

“So we could have little walkie-talkies over. Yeah.”

“We have walkie-talkies.”

Soma turns her attention back toward the meal.

“He's not eating turtle soup or anything at this hour, is he?”

I cannot identify the contents from here. He continues eating with an equanimity our investigation has done nothing to disturb.

“It's not the Board Laid Bare he's at, of course, he's somewhere else,” she says.

Lim’s establishment might have given the stop an immediate significance. This shop offers only a man at breakfast.

“He's just eating alone. He isn't talking to anybody.”

That is the difficulty. We have chosen the carrier who appears to know more, but knowing more does not oblige him to demonstrate it between mouthfuls. We could approach. We could ask. Either would make our interest part of his morning, and from that moment every subsequent choice might be made for our benefit.

“Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections,” Vukradin reminds Soma through the stone.

Then he refines the ownership of the obligation.

“Or you promised them.”

“Thank you so much.”

I appreciate the correction. Vukradin can impose the full weight of a promise while keeping an exact account of whose neck it rests upon.

“I'm not trying to put her out of business,” Soma says. “I want to see where he goes next. Stopping for a snack doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem kosher.”

We leave him to his breakfast. Brewbarry remains with us, and the man gives us no reason yet to move; below the street, Vukradin still has the more unusual trail.

Then his voice comes again.

“Okay… I don't think that's gonna go very well.”

“Good times,” I say.

“I mean, okay, there's some passive charm effect or something.”

The stone supplies his voice without the tunnel around it. I cannot inspect what he has encountered, cannot see the construct, cannot even judge his position from the sound. His account gives us a sudden disinterest in the route, an overwhelming conviction that continuing along it is boring and not worth his time.

“turn into Brewbarry,” Soma says.

“A passive confusion effect. Alright, awesome.”

Vukradin tries to name what is happening while it is happening. I listen for what he still knows, since a mistaken description is less troublesome than a missing purpose.

“Well, it can't have been cast directly on me.”

Then: “This is, like, an area effect.”

That is his assessment from below. From here I have a carrier eating breakfast and a bard explaining why the passage he enters in pursuit of a construct has ceased to merit pursuit. I have no basis for naming a spell.

What becomes clear is that he remembers why he goes underground. He remembers where he is. The purpose survives; the willingness to follow this particular tunnel does not. He decides it is the wrong way to go and turns away from the construct’s trail.

Through the stone comes his final acknowledgment.

“Okay.”

## Scene 3: Lost Among Unexpected Fans

### Vukradin

I remember the crate. I remember that I am following it. But the passage I mean to take no longer seems worth taking, and although I cannot supply a reason for this, my feet accept it. There are other tunnels. I take one.

For a little while, this feels entirely sufficient.

Then I look around for my friends.

I am alone in a section of sewer I do not recognize, with no clear idea of which turn has brought me here, and the purpose that ought to carry me back still refuses to become interesting. I can recall it. I simply cannot make myself care about pursuing it. That is a very strange distinction to be making underground.

A man stands ahead of several others. He looks past me, then at me.

“How the hell did you get over here?”

“I don't know.” I look back along the tunnel. It supplies no assistance. “Where am I?”

He keeps looking at me. I attempt a more complete explanation.

“Because I'm not over there.”

I cannot tell what these men want, or what they make of me, beyond the evident difficulty my arrival is causing their leader. He does not seem angry. Confused, certainly. We have that in common.

“But I don't know what I'm doing here.”

There is a distinction between being lost and explaining why I am in the sewers at all. I stay on the useful side of it.

“Yeah, there you go. I'm lost. I don't know what… I don't know how I got here, I just…”

His scrutiny eases enough for me to continue.

“Yeah. Wrong turn. Should have taken that left at Albuquerque.”

He licks his finger. I have no idea what this contributes.

“Well, this is my territory.”

“This is your territory.”

“Yes, this is my territory. Wow.”

“Okay. Did you want me to go?”

“I'd want to know why you're in my territory.”

We have reached the same question from several directions, which is more than I can say for my intended destination. Still, there is no reason we cannot resolve this. He wishes to understand why I am here. I wish to be somewhere I understand.

“Oh, I'm sorry that I got lost. I'm happy to leave. Could you point me in the direction of Lim's? Cafe, that's… that's where I want to be headed.”

“Lim's Cafe?”

“Yeah.”

“Huh.”

“We're friends.”

This does not reassure him as much as I expect.

“That's interesting. Why are you…”

He stops. His attention sharpens, and I find myself being examined again, more closely now. Apparently friendship with Lim requires an explanation of its own. I wait for the rest of the question.

Instead, he asks, “Are you that singer?”

“Exactly. And Lim's a fan.”

“What are you doing down here, man?”

“I told you, I got lost!”

“Vukradin… you're Vukradin, right?”

One of the men beside him is smiling. Really smiling, with none of his leader’s uncertainty about whether I ought to be here.

“This is… is this really Vukradin?”

There we are. A face I can understand. I have no idea where I am, but somebody is happy I have arrived, and that gives us considerably more to work with than territory.

“Do you guys want autographs?”

They would love some autographs.

“Sure, we can do some autographs, but then I gotta be going.”

“Sure… Maybe you can…”

“Point me in the right direction.”

“You know what, we're gonna do one better, we're gonna take you there.”

“Alright, sounds good.”

“We don't want you to get lost again, Mr. Vukradin.”

“Yeah, exactly.”

The men behind him are no longer merely a group occupying the passage. They are people waiting for signatures, and the man who demands to know how I got into his territory has offered to conduct me out of it personally. I am pleased. A few more words and we have a workable arrangement.

“Lim will be thrilled.”

Their leader pauses.

“It's very dangerous down here.”

“Is it?”

“Yeah, it's, it's, it's,” he begins.

“Yeah, it's dangerous in your territory?” I look along the passage he is offering to guide me through. “I would think you'd keep it very safe. You seem like a safe individual. I'm sure you keep the place shipshape.”

“Mr. Vukradin, I agree with you.”

Good. We agree on the desirable condition of the tunnels.

“But I have to tell you why I was out here. I was concerned when I saw you, and I'm very glad I recognized you. One of my guys tells me you have a deal with a friend of mine.”

“At the Common Chord?”

“No, no, at the Toe mine.”

“Mountain's Toe Mine. Oh, yeah…”

“Zeleen…”

“Oh, I didn't know you knew him.”

Zeleen Varnaster. Of all the connections I expect to make while lost beneath Neverwinter, our business at Mountain’s Toe Gold Mine is not high on the list. But here it is, connecting this man and his associates to something I actually know.

“Yeah, we spent some time in Phandalin, yeah, yeah.”

“Yeah, he said that he had a business relationship with you, I said that he was full of shit.”

“No, no, that's true, that's true. Yeah, they haven't produced very much yet, but they say they're working hard.”

He laughs.

“That's how I feel about it, too.”

He laughs again. I am glad somebody can appreciate the distinction between a business relationship and a business that produces money. The studio requires the latter, eventually. I remain interested in seeing the mine progress to that stage.

“Look, I manage the tunnels here,” he says, still laughing.

“Oh, you're the tunnel manager.”

“Yeah, a tunnel manager.”

“So this is your territory, and you have all the tunnels.”

“Not all the tunnels, but the tunnels in this area.”

“The tunnels in this area, interesting, okay.”

This makes Rsolk’s concern easier to follow. He has a defined area to manage, I have appeared inside it without knowing how, and he has dangers to account for besides wandering musicians. I let him explain.

“Two months ago, things started coming up the floodline.”

“Floodline.”

He describes a tunnel that floods regularly. I listen, trying to attach the description to the passages around us without pretending I understand the arrangement of water beneath a city.

“I don't know anything about tunnels, man, I'm a musician.”

“I've been having this problem with the tunnels. I've lost men, so we don't go there anymore.”

The laughter is finished.

I look at the men standing behind him. There are people missing from this group because something comes up a flooded tunnel, and the solution available to the survivors is to stop going there. Rsolk has just offered to keep me from wandering into danger. If my friends and I can remove that danger, we ought to discuss it, particularly if there is paid work involved, because helping people and building a music studio should be able to occupy the same conversation.

“Do you need help with that? My friends and I have some abilities, and we're looking for ways to make money. Maybe we can help you.”

He takes to the offer immediately. There is no need to persuade him that the problem deserves attention. He has lost men to it.

“One of the guys who escaped said there was some kind of creature they'd never seen before.”

“Oh.”

“It looked like…”

“Creature stuff.”

This is much more within our abilities than tunnel management. I wait for the description.

“It looked like a spider, but it had all the wrong colors.”

“Okay.”

The webs are wrong too. As Rsolk passes on the survivor’s account, I remember webbing we have encountered before, the visible wrongness of it, the sense that what I am looking at does not belong. A spider is one thing. We have experience with something that resembles a spider and leaves that sort of web behind.

I do not have enough to name what is in his tunnel. I have enough to take the account seriously.

“Yeah, we have experience with that.”

There is the beginning of an actual job here: a place to investigate, a danger somebody needs removed, and people who can tell us how to find it. No payment has been named. I am ready to discuss what clearing the passage might be worth when Rsolk returns to another concern.

“Mr. Vukradin, I know this is too much to ask, but could you get me some tickets to the Common Chord?”

“I can ask.”

I want to help him. He has been very accommodating since we established who I am, and his associate’s smile is still an excellent reason to put a little effort into making this happen. But wanting tickets and being able to provide them are separate matters. I should be precise before my agreement becomes a promise I cannot keep.

“we have kind of an arm's length relationship with the Common Chord, but I will ask for you.”

“That's all I could ask for.”

Done.

“Alright, let's do some autographs, and then we gotta be on our way.”

I sign for them. There is time to do this properly, even underground, even with my route missing and my friends elsewhere. The men who first stand behind Rsolk while he questions me now come forward for their autographs, and I give each signature the attention it deserves.

Then they escort me back through the tunnels. I leave the choice of turns to the people who manage them. Rsolk makes sure I have enough information to find him again and return to the matter of the flooded passage.

As we approach ground I recognize, I can at least place myself again. I have directions, an undertaking to ask about tickets, and a possible way to earn money for the studio by clearing something unnatural out of people’s lives. The pursuit that brings me down here still has that inexplicable lack of pull. I cannot account for it.

I keep close to my guides.

## Scene 4: Denvar’s Room and Unpaid Rent

### Soma

The delivery man eats alone. Valphine, Brewbarry, and I watch him finish an early breakfast at a cook shop. Four in the morning, and he can afford to have somebody else cook for him.

“And dinner at 4 a.m.,” I say.

Breakfast, apparently. He has been working all night. I doubt his stomach cares what we call it.

Valphine’s assessment is that he really is a delivery man. No disguised leader. No mastermind carrying crates to keep his hands busy. So we follow the carrier.

He takes us into the Bluelake District, to a building divided into single rooms. Many tenants under one roof. Little room wasted on any of them.

“High efficiency flat,” I say.

He goes to his room and sleeps. My familiar goes in after him. A rat needs considerably less space than I do.

I look through what the rat can find. Some sign of who pays him would help. A receipt. An ordinary pay stub. A mark on his belongings. At first there is nothing. No factional insignia, no identifying marks. Just a sleeping man and possessions that suggest he is doing better than an ordinary carrier should.

The meal alone might mean nothing. A person can spend too much on breakfast. Taken with his belongings, it begins to look less like one indulgence.

“Yeah, he’s a little overcompensated for his job.”

Still no badge. No convenient declaration of criminal allegiance for the rat to inspect. He appears to have found good work.

“Oh shit, I want that gig.”

Valphine considers the building. “So, is he in a house, or is he in an apartment?”

There are enough rooms here to make the arrangement clear.

“Okay, so he has a landlord,” she says. “Who we could potentially ask questions about him of.”

That is more promising than waiting for the sleeper to turn over and reveal a name stitched into his pillow.

“But it’s middle of the night, huh?”

We wait until morning. By seven, the man who runs the place is available, and our delivery man is still asleep. We can ask about the building without knocking on his door.

“Yeah, no. We can’t spook him indirectly, so we’ll just talk to the landlord.”

I approach with Valphine and Brewbarry beside me. Meril’s staff rests in my hand.

“Alright. We just had some questions. We wanted to know…” I settle on the practical one. “What’s it cost to live here? I think it’s the first one we need to know.”

The landlord looks at me.

“I don’t see many turtles around here.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

He keeps looking at my shell. There is a whole building full of rented rooms behind him. I have asked about one of them. Somehow this requires an examination of my back.

“I thought turtles carried their house on their shoulders, so they didn’t need a place to stay.”

“I see what you think of us. I can’t live in this thing. It merely protects me.”

“Oh!”

A discovery before breakfast. Good for him.

“I actually have a nice cabin out in the woods, out by, oh god, where did we start? Neverwinter.” I correct the useful part. “Well, outside of it. I was not in Neverwinter proper.”

“Oh, well, I learned a new thing. I didn’t know turtles didn’t live in their shells.”

I wait.

“I guess you’re not like… I guess you’re not like turtles.”

“No, no, we have… Very… that’s kind of slanderous to us.”

His attention moves past me at last. He notices Valphine. Then Brewbarry. His interest in my ancestry becomes less comfortable.

“I’m just asking a question.”

“Oh, oh, I’m just asking questions, I see.” I look at him. “Just asking questions guy has entered the chat.”

“Just asking questions.”

“Look.” I still need the rent. “I’ll pretend I didn’t hear some of that. I just… I want to know what it costs to live here, and, you know, what kind of people normally stay in a place like this.”

He has mistaken us for prospective tenants. I give him something to do with that.

“You could say I’m looking for a place for my, you know, my shell sprouts in the future.”

“Look, first of all, we don’t care. We’re an equal-opportunity housing establishment.”

“It’s so progressive.”

“It isn’t about being progressive. Money’s money, man. I don’t care who’s got it.”

There. An answer I can use. Briefly, we have reached the subject of money.

“I ask every question,” he adds, and looks at Valphine. “You’re a drow.”

“Very astute.”

“I thought Drow didn’t like the sunlight.”

“Oh, I love the sunlight.”

Her eyes glow gold above her white armor. He has chosen an interesting person to explain sunlight to.

“I love the sunlight,” she repeats.

“It isn’t our duty to educate him,” I say. “He needs to learn on his own. He needs to put in the work.”

I bring him back again.

“So you just take money and don’t ask questions. You don’t know anything about your tenants?”

“How do you deal with the KYC laws in here?” Valphine asks.

“No problem,” I say.

The terms of the lodging finally emerge. He requires substantial cash up front. This is no place for a day laborer to arrive with the evening’s wages and hope they will cover a bed. Our carrier’s room takes money in advance. The same carrier who finishes his shift with a bought meal and sleeps among belongings better than his work would suggest.

“Yeah, it seems odd. You never do any sort of background check? Never make sure people have the gold they claim they’re gonna have after months?”

“I don’t scry on my customers.”

“I don’t believe that. Given the racism you presume, I’m sure you scry on certain ones of them, don’t you?”

His gaze catches on Brewbarry’s chest. The muscles move. The landlord watches them.

“Jiggling about,” I observe.

“Look, I don’t want any trouble.”

We have managed to frighten him without getting a price. My bale can be efficient when it chooses.

“If you want a place to stay here, I’ll give you a discount.”

“Really, we just want to know about one of your tenants.”

“Alright.”

I describe the man we followed home.

“You mean Denvar?”

“Denvar. Yeah. That’s more than we came with, we got a name.” I try it again. “Denvar!”

The landlord has supplied one useful fact. I should let him supply another before I wear it out.

“Denvar, Colorado.”

Then I return to the matter in front of us.

“Yes, yes, we mean Denvar. He dropped something last night.”

The man upstairs does not need to know that three armed strangers have followed him home. A lost pouch gives us a reason to ask after him. A helpful reason.

“We saw him skulking about in the, the alleyway super late at night, and he dropped a little pouch. We’d like to return it to him.”

“Making a delivery,” Valphine adds.

“Oh.”

Yes. That sounds better than skulking. We are being helpful.

While we talk, the rat’s search produces something useful at last. Delivery chits. They are kept in order, covering roughly seven to ten days. I hold my attention on them. This is what I sent the familiar to find.

Each carries the same instruction.

“9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down.”

I go over it under my breath.

“Third up, one down.”

No. I look again.

“Oh, eight up. Sorry. Okay.”

Nine crates. Eight up. One down. A third-hour delivery. The repetition matters more than my first attempt to read it.

There is a client reference and a house mark. No personal name anywhere. Those are the only identifying details, and they do not yet tell me who stands behind the work. The chits are reconciled through weekly settlements.

“And the… all his deliveries… 100% of his delivery chits are the same. They’re all this one thing. He literally has one job.”

I look through them again. Same quantities. Same instruction.

“Yeah. It is. Wow. Yeah, no, that’s not suspicious.”

The earlier chits have settlement marks. Payment stamps. Somebody has accounted for the work and paid him for it.

“They clearly seem to be related to this one contract.”

Then I reach the newest chit. Seven in the group. Six paid. The seventh has no settlement stamp.

That is better than an insignia. An insignia might tell us who he belongs to, if we recognize it. Unpaid work gives him somewhere to go.

“And so that was the one that he just, presumably delivered. Yeah, so I guess we have reason to follow him once he wakes up, don’t we?”

He has finished the delivery we watched. He has come home and gone to sleep. The payment is still outstanding.

“He needs to be able to afford a place like this. He needs the money pretty quickly.”

The landlord can tell us how quickly. I ask whether Denvar is settled up on his bills.

He owes money today.

“Yeah?”

Today. I hold onto that.

“Yeah, so he’s… he’s motivated.”

No need to wake him. No need to search every house mark in the city while he sleeps above us. If his next errand is to collect his pay, we can follow him to somebody who knows more.

“You know, he’s just sleeping in his room right now, right? Like…”

“He’s got until the end of the day, and he’s a good customer. He’s quiet. He doesn’t bother anybody.”

“I’ll bet.”

“Good guy, huh?” Valphine asks.

“Yeah, totally good guy.”

Quiet. Pays reliably. Does not trouble the other tenants. The landlord knows what he needs to know about Denvar. We need the part that happens before the money reaches him.

“You don’t think he’s a serial killer or anything?”

His eyes move over us again.

This time he takes inventory. My staff. Valphine’s white armor and glowing eyes. Brewbarry’s halberd. He looks back at me.

“Should I be worried?”

“You never know with these types. You never know.”

“I’d be worried about him,” Valphine says.

The landlord has his own unpaid bill to consider now. I leave him to it. We have a name, a sleeping man, a payment due, and an end-of-day deadline. Enough to make waiting useful.

Before we leave, he turns back to me.

“Soma, now that I know turtles are looking for housing, perhaps you could tell me what makes a good turtle room?”

I could give him a very expensive answer. He is listening properly now.

But I think of a hearth. Water close enough that fetching it does not become the work of the day. Somewhere to sit with the weight off my joints.

“Oh, a nice hearth, plenty of water. Being near a body of water is nice.”

## Scene 5: Behind the Thousand Faces

### Brewbarry

A few hours is a long time to wait for a man to wake up. Soma casts *Find Familiar* again. Her rat is back. We keep waiting.

Vukradin comes back to us too. The Sending Stone has kept him told of what we are doing. I am glad to see him in person.

“Yeah, I can tell you about Denvar,” Soma says.

“Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?”

“Yeah, there's were-rats all over, isn't it?”

He tells us about Rsolk. There are spiders in the tunnels. We have enough directions to find them, and clearing them out will pay. I listen. Spiders are something we can do something about. This waiting has no weight to push against.

“We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity.” Vukradin gives it its proper name. “Fair Trade Gold.”

I like this plan. Kill spiders. Get his studio built.

Then Denvar wakes, and we have work here.

We follow him to a stone wall. It is part of a building. He looks around before he touches it. I watch his hands. One stone opens. There is a space behind it.

“It's a dead drop,” Vukradin says.

Denvar takes out a bag and closes the stone again. No one comes out to meet him. No one hands him anything.

“Is it like a two-way doctor's-office window, where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they…” Vukradin looks at the wall. “We'd have to go inspect it.”

We stay back while Denvar has his payment. Vukradin says to let him go. So I let him go.

Soma considers following him again another evening. We do not know how early he leaves to make his drops. We could spend more hours waiting and miss him anyway.

“Denvar might be a dead end.”

The stone is still there. I would rather look at that.

“Valphine, you wanna do your…” Vukradin begins.

“Infinite perception,” Soma supplies.

“You mean, like, on him, or on the…”

“on the wall,” Vukradin says.

“on the wall,” Soma repeats.

Valphine watches Denvar first. I look too. He checks the chit and smiles.

“Payday! I'm going to the casino!” Soma says.

Denvar leaves smiling. Whatever fright or trouble there is in this business, he has been paid for his part. We have the wall.

“Could take a little look, then, at the rock,” Valphine says.

The hiding place opens both ways. That does not tell us enough. Someone could have put the payment in from the street. Someone could have put it in from the room behind the wall. I look at the closed stone. I do not like a hole where both people can hide their hands.

“Oh, okay. Well, let's loop around,” Valphine says.

Vukradin is still looking at the building. “I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house.”

“I mean, we have mold earth, but I know you won't.”

“It's not earth, it's stone.”

The building has plenty of stone. Soma's spell will not answer this.

“Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is,” Valphine says.

“The outside of the house,” Vukradin adds.

“Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence, or something like that,” Soma says.

We go around. Valphine tries to decide whether it is a residence or a shop. Then we reach the part that tells us.

It is a tavern. The House of a Thousand Faces.

I know that name.

When I first come to Neverwinter, I hear about this place. I do not have the money to drink here. I drink at the Common Chord. A man can hear about good things without being allowed to have them.

Now I have money.

“So many taverns,” Vukradin says. “Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?”

They are. It is early morning, but the door is open for business.

“Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts,” Soma says.

“Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?”

I go in.

There are mirrors. I see myself where I expect more room. Then another mirror catches me from the side. Mannequins stand in clothes that belong to an older fashion. They do not turn when I pass. I look at their hands anyway.

The couches are lush. Thick cushions. Good cloth. I can tell before sitting down. There is a fire, and its smoke goes up the chimney where it should. The room gets the warmth without having to breathe the smoke.

I stop a little way inside. I want to look at it all.

Outside, Vukradin is still working on the stone.

“I have an idea for investigating without going inside: the *Knock* spell.”

His voice reaches me through the entrance. I stay close enough to hear. I am inside the House of a Thousand Faces. I do not need to go farther yet.

“So we could open our side, and we can cast Knock on the other side,” he says.

“Sorry, how does that work? You use *Knock* to open the two-way dead drop?” Soma asks.

“Right. We can't physically open the other side of the two-way hole, but I think we could open it magically.”

“If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot.”

He explains that they can look through, or send the rat. Soma likes that.

Valphine has another plan. “I have a different strategy, which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal.”

“Yeah, Vukradin could play,” Soma says.

That makes me look back toward the entrance. Vukradin could play here. There are good places to sit and listen. I have already found several.

“Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas where you could do your music preparation—and by music preparation…”

“There you go,” Valphine says.

“We could cast two spells, or we could have an…” Vukradin begins.

“Or we can get paid!”

“extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time.” He pauses. “I do like to perform, though.”

“Yeah, see, I mean… These are tough choices,” Soma says. Then she settles it. “Let's do both! Porque no los dos.”

“Exactly. Why not?”

I look from the old clothes to the couches. This is a good room for music. But first they want to know what is behind the stone. Vukradin lays out the spell and the rat again.

“It's a simple strategy,” Valphine says.

“Before we go inside. We're all together.”

“Yeah, we want to at least not walk in completely blind,” Soma says.

I am already in. They notice.

“Yeah, he was born in the tavern,” Soma says.

Vukradin knows why I want this one. “Right. It's more of a high-end establishment.”

I keep my feet on this side of the threshold.

“He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment.”

“He's a man of class now, he can buy his way into high society,” Soma says.

I look at the cushions again. I can buy a drink here. That is enough to start with.

“Isn't it obvious? It's the quality of your talent,” Valphine says.

“Not,” Vukradin says.

“It's totally not, you sure.”

“It definitely is not.”

His voice has changed. I turn toward it.

“There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering…” He stops and starts again. “We're being…”

“manipulated,” Soma says.

“to their advantage for some reason.”

I do not like that either. Someone is making Lathander's Death famous. We know it is happening. We do not know whose hands are doing it.

I want people to hear Vukradin. I do not want someone hiding behind him.

They go back to the compartment. Their voices move out of easy hearing. I remain inside, near the entrance. They have the spell and the rat. I have the room people are meant to see.

It is a fine room. Too fine for the street around it. Bluelake is cheaper than this. The couches belong somewhere with more money. So do all these mirrors and the dressed figures standing idle. Even the fire works better here.

I once cannot afford to cross this floor. Now I stand on it, looking for what is wrong.

When the others gather at the establishment again, the rat has been through. Vukradin has opened the inner closure with *Knock*. Soma has sent her familiar beyond it and down into a basement office. No windows. A desk. Papers and supplies.

And a symbol the rat finds there is unmistakable.

The Harpers.

“Harper, so that's… Weird,” Soma says.

I look down at the floor. There is an office below us. The mirrors do not show that.

“The Harpers are… smuggling?”

The drop belongs to them. That much we have. The ninth crate is harder. Arranging it, receiving it, moving it for someone else. We do not yet know which.

“Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling because it's being directly delivered,” Soma says. “So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity.”

I keep the payment in mind. Denvar's hand in the wall. His smile at the chit. There is a person paying him, and we still have not seen that person.

The Harpers are no comfort. We know enough of them for that. The Order of the Gauntlet comes into the talk as a better sort of people. Soma finds that debatable. Valphine questions it too.

“Reformed. They're reformed,” Vukradin says. “Well, they had that whole thing with the barbarians, you know.”

I know.

Their political masters used them. They have rejected those masters now. Vukradin accepts the change.

“Exactly, that's what I'm saying. They've been past that, they're reformed now.” Then he gives them a name for it. “Basically, they're the Reformed order of the Gauntlet.”

“Truth, justice, and the American Way,” Soma says.

I stay by the entrance. We have directions to Rsolk's spiders. We have a Harper office under this tavern. We have no name for whoever is pushing Vukradin's fame.

The fire warms the room. The smoke draws cleanly away. I can afford to be here now, and I am still watching the hands on the mannequins.

“Yep, it's a good place, I need to leave it,” Soma says.
