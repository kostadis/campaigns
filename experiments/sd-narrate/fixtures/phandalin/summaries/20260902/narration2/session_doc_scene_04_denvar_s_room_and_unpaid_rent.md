### Soma

My own feet are still outside the cook shop.

"And dinner at 4 a.m."

Breakfast, then. The man eats it alone. Valphine sees a delivery worker. So do I. No hidden lord emerges from behind the bowl.

"It's just a night shift, so what part of town does he live in?"

We follow him into the Bluelake District.

"Blue Lake, okay."

His building has many small rooms. He takes one and goes to sleep. I stay near enough for the rat.

"I mean, we sent the rat in."

The floor comes close. Dust, a bed leg, cloth above me. Borrowed eyes take getting used to.

"Just see if I can find any…"

"Any odd signs, receipts, pay stubs—anything to get a sense."

I search from the floor upward. His belongings are better than I expect.

"That's something to say, right? Has he got nice clothes? Does he have…"

He does. Add the meal bought before dawn. Add this room.

"Yeah, he's a little overcompensated for his job."

No badge catches the rat's eye.

"No signs, no,"

"No marks, no insignias, nothing, nothing for the rat to spot?"

Nothing obvious. No faction sewn onto a coat. No ordinary pay record lying open. Just a man asleep after a well-paid night's work.

"Oh shit, I want that gig."

I consider the crate waiting underground.

"Aww."

"So, is he in a house, or is he in an apartment?" Valphine asks.

"Okay, so he has a landlord."

"Who we could potentially ask questions about him of."

"But it's middle of the night, huh?"

"High efficiency flat," I say.

Many doors. Little space.

"Yeah."

Someone runs the place. We find him downstairs while our carrier sleeps.

"Yeah, no. We can't spook him indirectly, so we'll just talk to the landlord."

"Alright. We just had some questions. We wanted to know…"

I start with the useful question.

"What's it cost to live here? I think it's the first one we need to know."

The landlord looks at my shell.

"I don't see many turtles around here."

"That's not what I asked."

I wait. He is still looking.

"Yeah, jeez."

"I thought turtles carried their house on their shoulders, so they didn't need a place to stay."

I shift the weight on my sore feet.

"I see what you think of us. I can't live in this thing. It merely protects me."

"Oh!"

"I actually have a nice cabin out in the woods, out by, oh god, where did we start? Neverwinter."

"Well, outside of it. I was not in Neverwinter proper."

"Oh, well, I learned a new thing. I didn't know turtles didn't live in their shells."

"I guess you're not like… I guess you're not like turtles."

"No, no, we have… Very… that's kind of slanderous to us."

His gaze finally gets past me. Valphine. Brewbarry. The size of Brewbarry.

"I'm just asking a question."

"Oh, oh, I'm just asking questions, I see."

"Just asking questions."

"Look."

I put my weight on Meril's Staff.

"I'll pretend I didn't hear some of that. I just… I want to know what it costs to live here, and, you know, what kind of people normally stay in a place like this."

"You could say I'm looking for a place for my, you know, my shell sprouts in the future."

"Look, first of all, we don't care. We're an equal-opportunity housing establishment."

"It's so progressive."

"It isn't about being progressive. Money's money, man. I don't care who's got it."

At last. A clear answer.

"I ask every question."

He turns to Valphine.

"You're a drow."

"Very astute."

"I thought Drow didn't like the sunlight."

"Oh, I love the sunlight."

Her golden eyes remain on him.

"I love the sunlight."

"It isn't our duty to educate him," I say.

"He needs to learn on his own. He needs to put in the work."

I return to the work in front of us.

"So you just take money and don't ask questions. You don't know anything about your tenants?"

"No problem."

He describes the cash he requires up front. Too much for a day laborer to find between jobs. Our sleeping carrier has another reason to keep his good gig.

"Yeah, it seems odd. You never do any sort of background check? Never make sure people have the gold they claim they're gonna have after months?"

"I don't scry on my customers."

"I don't believe that. Given the racism you presume, I'm sure you scry on certain ones of them, don't you?"

He looks at Brewbarry again.

"Jiggling about," I observe.

"Look, I don't want any trouble."

"If you want a place to stay here, I'll give you a discount."

"Really, we just want to know about one of your tenants."

"Alright."

I describe the man we follow home.

"You mean Denvar?"

"Denvar. Yeah. That's more than we came with, we got a name."

"Denvar!"

I settle back to the matter.

"Yes, yes, we mean Denvar."

"He dropped something last night."

The story needs a reason for our interest.

"We saw him skulking about in the, the alleyway super late at night, and he dropped a little pouch. We'd like to return it to him."

"Making a delivery," Valphine adds.

"Oh."

Better. Less skulking.

Upstairs, the rat finds what I send it to find: a run of small delivery chits.

"Yeah, see, this is what the rat was going in looking for."

They cover about a tenday. Each repeats the same job. Nine crates at the third hour. Eight up. One down. A client reference, a house mark, no person's name. Settlement is weekly.

"Third up, one down."

I look again.

"Oh, eight up. Sorry. Okay."

"Yeah, but yeah."

Every slip repeats the same instruction.

"And the… all his deliveries… 100% of his delivery chits are the same. They're all this one thing. He literally has one job."

"Yeah. It is. Wow. Yeah, no, that's not suspicious."

I examine the marks beside the writing.

"The, yeah, the checks on them, whatever."

"And stamp of payment."

The stamps settle the earlier work. They do not identify the person handing over the money.

"But no, nothing identifiable on them other than just…"

"They clearly seem to be related to this one contract."

The newest chit lacks the paid mark. It is the last in the run.

"Okay."

"And so that was the one that he just, presumably delivered. Yeah, so I guess we have reason to follow him once he wakes up, don't we?"

"Yeah…"

I let the rat's gaze rest on the unstamped slip.

"He needs to be able to afford a place like this. He needs the money pretty quickly."

"In fact, we could ask the landlord, you know, is Denvar settled up on his bills?"

The answer is useful. Denvar owes him money today.

"Yeah?"

"Yeah, so he's… he's motivated."

I glance toward the rooms.

"You know, he's just sleeping in his room right now, right? Like…"

"He's got until the end of the day, and he's a good customer. He's quiet. He doesn't bother anybody."

"I'll bet."

"Good guy, huh?" Valphine asks.

"Yeah, totally good guy."

"You don't think he's a serial killer or anything?"

The landlord takes another look at my bale. His eyes stop on my staff, Valphine's white armor, Brewbarry's halberd.

"Should I be worried?"

"You never know with these types. You never know."

"I'd be worried about him," Valphine says.

I leave that where it lands.

Before we go, the landlord returns to the question of my name and my shell.

"Yes, I definitely did."

"Soma, now that I know turtles are looking for housing, perhaps you could tell me what makes a good turtle room?"

He waits for an answer. This time it is a question I can answer.

"Oh, a nice hearth, plenty of water. Being near a body of water is nice."

I shift my grip on the staff. A hearth would do nicely just now. We have a sleeping man to wait for.
