### Valphine Sotorra

The descent offers wet stone, narrow steps, and considerably less vanity than the Common Chord. I place my feet carefully. Cold gathers beneath the edge of my armor.

Vukradin presents his qualifications before we settle beside the sewer grille.

"I have a very high stealth, if we need me to…"

"We need someone to do something stealthily."

"I mean, you could tag along with a familiar…" Soma says.

Vukradin's boots make little sound on the stone. An excellent property in a companion whose preferred instrument of investigation is usually his voice.

"I didn't know bards got high stealth."

"And it's just because I have boots of elvenkind."

"Hell yeah."

Soma turns to her own preparation.

"I cast *Find Familiar*."

A rat takes form. I watch its small nose work. Soma can borrow its eyes and ears while it stays near enough; the limitation matters more than the creature's size.

"Oh, okay. Great."

Below us, carriers lower their crates beside the grille.

"All 9 of them?"

I count the stacked boxes and the separate one. The main delivery bears no Commission Harbor mark. Whatever privileges support this trade, ordinary admission through the harbor is not among them.

"So we want one person to track down the gnome, right? Crate 9."

Vukradin studies the delivery.

"We also want to track the people who dropped them. So we want to do two things."

"What, you said you got your high stealth."

"Yeah."

"If you want, I can track the gnome. You guys can go do the… the sewers."

"Well, I'm tracking the crate," Soma says.

"Which crate?"

"The ninth crate, the one that doesn't go into Lim, because I'm more concerned about my friend's shady business."

"Okay, so you want me to follow them back into the sewers?"

His objection arrives before the plan has quite settled.

"That seems pretty dangerous. One party member in the sewers."

"I see. Alright, we can send the rat that way, then. You can follow the crate."

The carriers finish setting down their burdens. Their departure will shortly make this discussion academic.

"Yeah, no, we knew that," Soma murmurs over the unmarked goods.

One carrier pauses beside the ledge. His gaze drops into the dark below the grille. He waits long enough to betray an expectation, then turns away.

"Like, down at the grates, or the…"

I follow Soma's glance. Yes. Down there.

"The rat's gonna go whichever way leads to the sewer."

"So, look, the… The crates got dropped off. Lim's gonna come out and take 8 of the crates."

"Right."

"And she's not gonna take a 9th crate."

Vukradin settles himself to wait for its collector.

"So everyone other than Vukradin is gonna trail these guys, and Vukradin's gonna trail the crate."

I look from Soma to Vukradin.

"Is that right?"

"Yes."

"Yes."

"They come out of the sewers, they drop the crates, and then they just walk on through town, which way?"

The answer is inconvenient. Different ways. Neither carrier acknowledges the other. Their effort to appear unconnected is more noticeable than a farewell would be.

"That nonchalant whistling," Soma says.

"So if they split up, that's gonna make them hard to follow. You're gonna have to choose one or split again."

At first I can draw little distinction between them beyond their burdens. Then I hold to the last of Vukradin's encouragement, the phrase still clear in my mind, and examine the pause again. One man stacks boxes. The other checks whether someone beneath the street is ready to receive one.

"I'd follow the guy who paid attention to the ledge."

Soma reaches the same useful conclusion.

"He knows something. He knows something's coming out of that thing."

I choose him. Seniority is unnecessary if expectation has already identified the better witness.

"I could handle this myself, guys, you don't trust me?"

Movement interrupts the question.

I expect a person. Instead, brass catches the scant light. A construct, no larger than a cat, climbs into view on quick joints beneath leather fittings. It takes the separate crate and descends without hesitation.

I revise the pursuit. A collector that cannot be frightened, flattered, or made careless requires a different approach.

Vukradin follows it.

"This is my concern. Vukradin ends up going into the sewers now, and…"

"I'll be fine."

"One person in the sewers? Alright."

Soma watches the opening.

"Tell me the odds."

"Only a 1 in 400 chance of disaster. It'll be fine."

I turn toward the carrier who looks below. Brewbarry and Soma come with me. Behind us, Vukradin's boots leave no useful sound.

Our man stops at a cook shop. Very few doors are open at this hour.

"Can't be a lot of those," Soma says.

He settles to his meal alone. I see no greeting exchanged, no other customer joining him. A laborer eating after work is an uninteresting sight, which is precisely why I continue watching.

Vukradin's voice reaches us through Soma's stone.

"By the way, Soma, you have one of the Sending Stones, and I have the other."

"Good to know."

"So we could have little walkie-talkies over. Yeah."

"We have walkie-talkies."

Soma looks back toward the food.

"He's not eating turtle soup or anything at this hour, is he?"

"It's not the Board Laid Bare he's at, of course, he's somewhere else."

I watch his hands. Spoon, bowl, bread. No signal I can identify.

"He's just eating alone. He isn't talking to anybody."

The stone supplies Vukradin's objection before we need to make it ourselves.

"Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections."

"Or you promised them."

"Thank you so much."

Soma keeps her voice low.

"I'm not trying to put her out of business. I want to see where he goes next. Stopping for a snack doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem kosher."

I remain where I can see the door.

The next report from below is less reassuring.

"Okay… I don't think that's gonna go very well."

"Good times."

I attend to the stone.

"I mean, okay, there's some passive charm effect or something."

The report offers no attacker to locate. Vukradin still knows why he enters the tunnels, yet the route after the construct has abruptly ceased to deserve his attention. He is walking away from it.

"A passive confusion effect. Alright, awesome."

His own account does not quite support that diagnosis.

"Well, it can't have been cast directly on me."

"This is, like, an area effect."

He remembers the pursuit. He simply finds pursuing it pointless.

"Okay."

I keep my gaze on the cook-shop door and move closer to Soma's stone.
