### Zenvon Forepot

The smell of smoke gives us a direction as we climb the rugged ridge on Wyvern Tor’s lower slopes. I move ahead far enough to look down into the ravine. A cave mouth opens at the bottom, about fifty yards away. Twenty yards outside it, a single bugbear sits hunkered beside a boulder.

One that I can see. There is a difference.

He is within reach of my shortbow. I look at the distance between him and the cave, then withdraw to where Pip, Sister Maela, and Veyra wait.

“Okay, guys, so I see a bugbear… At the far end of the cave.” That is not quite the location. “Yeah, ravine. At the ravine? Okay, yeah, yeah. So, I was thinking I could use my shortbow and take that guy out.”

The shot is possible. What follows the shot is what we need to settle.

“But he's kind of far, and I think I'll be able to make it. What do you guys want to do? Do you have any better idea to tackle the situation?”

“Well, the first thing we should do is—” Pip pauses. “Check to see if he's doing anything.”

“What do you mean is doing anything?”

“Well, is he the kind of guard that spends his time thinking hard, or is he the kind of guard that—you know, what kind of guard is he?”

I have established that he is a bugbear, alone outside a cave, and close enough to shoot. Pip wants a more particular description.

“Well… To explore that, you need to go close to him, and I think it's a bad idea going close to him.”

He looks at me. “You can sneak, right?”

“I can sneak, but, well, yeah, yeah, I guess you're right. Okay, I can go as close as possible. Let me try.”

I go back along the ridge and work closer without drawing the bugbear’s attention. From here I can see the milky film over one eye. That eye is blind. He leans on a salvaged pike.

*Salvaged bike?*

Pike. Like a spear. A long, pointed weapon, and at present something to lean against.

Pip has a useful question, yes. The guard looks like somebody who has been given this assignment and would prefer another one. But he is still between us and the entrance, with a weapon long enough to make approaching him inconvenient.

I look beyond him for anybody else. The cave gives me nothing. Its mouth is visible, but the darkness inside stops me seeing farther. The cookfire smoke is clearer here, coming from within. There is somebody else in there. How many, I cannot establish.

A stone could make him look away. I consider using Mage Hand to disturb his pike, or throw something without showing myself. A noise would let us choose where he looks, at least for a moment.

Then the others attempt to move closer, and he notices something.

He straightens and looks south. His pike comes forward. He advances roughly ten feet, watching the ground ahead of him. He has not found us, but the bored part of his assignment is over.

I put Mage Hand to work on the other side of him. To the north, behind his back, a stone strikes rock. The sound gives him something else to attend to. It does not tell me whether he has forgotten the first disturbance.

My idea was to make more noises after this one. East, then west. Give him enough to think somebody is there, so he calls another bugbear out. Perhaps two. Then we can engage them outside, where we can see them.

Pip’s advice from our discussion bears on that also. Quietly killing the sentry is easier than bringing out an unknown number. I may expect one or two. The cave is under no obligation to contain one or two.

“You're the one… you know, it's like…” he told me. “You haven't led us wrong yet, Zenvon.”

That is a generous account of things.

I weighed his warning aloud: “I mean, it makes sense, but I'm thinking if we can kill him without… alerting others. Okay, yeah, I guess, I guess it doesn't matter either way, like, it's…”

What I want is a manageable fight. There is no advantage in adding a spell and several noises if the result is the same fight, with more people invited.

“I feel using the mage hand is more effort. For the same output.”

Now the hand has made its noise, and we still have the distance to solve. Pip is roughly sixty feet from the guard. He cannot cover all that ground and strike before the bugbear has time to react. My bow can reach. Pip needs to get closer.

So the distraction has a use after all. Not to call anybody out. To let Pip approach.

I want the sentry to stay where he is and turn his back toward Pip. A northern noise should give Pip time to move into the ridge and stay in position. But the bugbear has already heard something to the south, and I cannot decide for him which noise matters more.

I watch him with the pike held in front. He looks south, then over his shoulder. The stone has given him a second direction to watch. It has not made him conveniently certain about either one.

I study his movements for some indication of what he will do next. Whether he is about to keep coming, turn back, call somebody. There is nothing further I can read from him. One blind eye, one ready pike, and now he is paying attention.

“Tricky here.”
