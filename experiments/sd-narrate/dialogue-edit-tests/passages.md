## Valphine — integrated line edit

The exchange is folded into readable prose while her private reading of obligation remains. The connection through the Sending Stone is explicit.

[Full raw response](valphine/c/response.md) · [All approaches](index.html#valphine)

“He's just eating alone. He isn't talking to anybody.”

We have chosen the carrier who appears to know more, but knowing more does not oblige him to demonstrate it between mouthfuls. We could approach. We could ask. Either would make our interest part of his morning, and from that moment every subsequent choice might be made for our benefit.

“Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections,” Vukradin reminds Soma through the stone, then refines the ownership of the obligation. “Or you promised them.”

“Thank you so much.”

I appreciate the correction. Vukradin can impose the full weight of a promise while keeping an exact account of whose neck it rests upon.

“I'm not trying to put her out of business,” Soma says. “I want to see where he goes next. Stopping for a snack doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem kosher.”

## Vukradin — contextual dialogue edit

The mistaken calculation and correction survive; accidental repetition is reduced. His practical self-commentary and the aside about Soma remain.

[Full raw response](vukradin/b/response.md) · [All approaches](index.html#vukradin)

“So it's a six-hour cycle.”

I begin counting the intervals, briefly halve one that does not need halving, and correct myself aloud.

“High to low is three hours. Yeah, no, high to low… High to low is six hours. Six.”

This is why we discuss things before entering the sewer.

“We want to be in the sewer… at low tide.”

“Three AM,” Soma says.

“We have about three hours, maybe four, before things start to get really uncomfortable.”

“I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely.”

I look at Soma. An excellent capability for Soma.

## Zenvon — integrated line edit

The retrospective tense is natural, his assessment remains, and “For the same output” keeps its separate landing.

[Full raw response](zenvon/c/response.md) · [All approaches](index.html#zenvon)

Pip’s advice from our discussion bears on that also. Quietly killing the sentry is easier than bringing out an unknown number. I may expect one or two. The cave is under no obligation to contain one or two.

“You're the one… you know, it's like…” he told me. “You haven't led us wrong yet, Zenvon.”

That is a generous account of things.

I weighed his warning aloud: “I mean, it makes sense, but I'm thinking if we can kill him without… alerting others. Okay, yeah, I guess, I guess it doesn't matter either way, like, it's…”

What I want is a manageable fight. There is no advantage in adding a spell and several noises if the result is the same fight, with more people invited.

“I feel using the mage hand is more effort. For the same output.”

Now the hand has made its noise, and we still have the distance to solve. Pip is roughly sixty feet from the guard. He cannot cover all that ground and strike before the bugbear has time to react. My bow can reach. Pip needs to get closer.
