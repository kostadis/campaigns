### Valphine Sotorra

The steps descend toward the sewer grille in shallow, aqueduct-like courses, wet stone offering very little purchase and the darkness below offering considerably more concealment. I watch the approach, the ledge beside the opening, and the two men carrying crates; a delivery has at least three useful parts, the goods, the labor, and the person who knows where the goods are meant to go. We have yet to distinguish the last two.

Vukradin has already offered his services.

“I have a very high stealth, if we need me to…”

Soma regards him. “I didn't know bards got high stealth.”

“And it's just because I have boots of elvenkind.”

“Hell yeah.”

The boots are a useful advantage. They also make him the obvious choice for a task the rest of us might prefer him to undertake in company.

“I mean, you could tag along with a familiar…” Soma says.

She casts *Find Familiar*, and a rat joins our surveillance, small enough to belong beside the wet steps without explanation. Through its eyes and ears she can examine what waits below, provided it remains within a hundred feet of her. A useful extension of our attention, although the sewer is unlikely to respect the limit.

The carriers bring their loads down.

“All 9 of them?” Soma asks.

All nine. Eight accumulate together beside the grille, and on those eight I can see no official Commission Harbor mark. Whatever journey brought them into Neverwinter has omitted the authorities’ preferred opportunities to inspect, record, and collect. The absence is consistent with what we already know; the arrangement interests me more.

“So we want one person to track down the gnome, right? Crate 9,” Vukradin says. “We also want to track the people who dropped them. So we want to do two things.”

“What, you said you got your high stealth.”

“If you want, I can track the gnome. You guys can go do the sewers.”

“Well, I'm tracking the crate,” Soma says.

“Which crate?”

“The ninth crate, the one that doesn't go into Lim, because I'm more concerned about my friend's shady business.”

I keep my attention on the men while the assignments circulate. Lim’s eight crates are together; the ninth is being handled separately. Our prospective quarry has not yet appeared, and already its parcel commands more discussion than the men who bring it.

“Okay, so you want me to follow them back into the sewers?” Vukradin asks. “That seems pretty dangerous. One party member in the sewers.”

“I see. Alright, we can send the rat that way, then. You can follow the crate.”

One carrier places the ninth crate on the ledge. He pauses there, looks down into the dark, and then leaves it. His companion continues stacking boxes.

The glance is brief enough to be mistaken for caution. There is wet stone underfoot, after all, and an opening beside him. But he looks into the place from which something might arrive. I mark the distinction.

“The rat's gonna go whichever way leads to the sewer,” Soma says.

Vukradin works through the division again, giving each part its own sentence.

“So, look, the crates got dropped off. Lim's gonna come out and take 8 of the crates.”

“Right.”

“And she's not gonna take a 9th crate.”

The isolated box rests on its ledge while the carriers finish. Neither has any apparent intention of waiting beside it.

“Vukradin is gonna stick around and stealthily wait to see who comes to get the ninth crate, and then track it.”

“So everyone other than Vukradin is gonna trail these guys, and Vukradin's gonna trail the crate,” I say. “Is that right?”

“Yes,” Vukradin says.

“Yes,” Soma agrees.

An assignment, finally. Then the carriers reach the point at which they might naturally exchange a glance, a word, some small acknowledgment of having just performed the same work, and carefully do none of these things. They walk away in different directions.

“That nonchalant whistling,” Soma says.

I watch their refusal to look at one another. It takes discipline to suppress every ordinary acknowledgment, but rather better discipline to know which acknowledgments should remain. Their separation advertises an intention to separate.

“So if they split up, that's gonna make them hard to follow,” Vukradin says. “You're gonna have to choose one or split again.”

The man at the ledge is my choice, though I examine the conclusion before trusting it. Handling the exceptional crate does not necessarily make him senior; an instructed servant can perform a special errand without understanding it. At first the evidence gives me only that much. Then I hold his pause against his companion’s uninterrupted stacking, letting the last encouragement Vukradin has left me steady my attention.

One man completes a physical task. The other checks for its continuation.

I cannot establish rank from a glance. I can identify which man expects something to emerge from the sewer.

“I'd follow the guy who paid attention to the ledge,” Soma says. “He knows something. He knows something's coming out of that thing.”

That is sufficient agreement. Brewbarry, Soma, and I will follow him.

“I could handle this myself, guys. You don't trust me?”

Before the question requires an answer, something comes up for the ninth crate.

I am surprised. I had allowed for a collector, including the gnome Vukradin expects; what appears is cat-sized, brass and leather, quick in its movements without displaying any haste. It takes the crate and goes back down. There is no examination of the other eight, no search for a mark, no hesitation over which object belongs to it.

The carrier’s downward glance acquires a more precise meaning. He has been checking for an expected collection, and the thing collecting needs neither greeting nor payment here. Whether he knows who instructs it remains unanswered.

“Okay, Vukradin will pursue stealthily.”

“This is my concern,” Soma says. “Vukradin ends up going into the sewers now, and…”

“I'll be fine.”

“One person in the sewers? Alright.”

The objection is almost exactly the one he raises himself a short while ago. Apparently the appearance of something sufficiently interesting has altered the acceptable number of companions.

“Tell me the odds,” Soma says.

“Only a 1 in 400 chance of disaster. It'll be fine.”

A remarkably specific confidence. His boots can suppress the sound of his approach; I see no reason to assign them authority over everything he may approach.

Nevertheless, the construct is withdrawing, and our carrier is leaving. Vukradin follows the brass-and-leather collector below while the three of us take the street after the man who watches ledges. The grille falls behind us. I have no view of Vukradin’s progress once he descends.

Our man stops at a cook shop.

At the fourth hour, there are few open doors offering food, and this is one of them. He settles to breakfast alone, apparently content with the meal. No companion joins him. He speaks to no waiting contact that I can see. Having left eight crates for Lim and one for a construct, he applies himself to eating.

“Can't be a lot of those,” Soma says of the open shop.

An early meal is a reasonable end to a delivery. It is also an inconvenient one for those hoping the delivery will lead directly to an employer. I watch him eat, allowing neither possibility to improve the evidence.

Vukradin’s voice reaches Soma through her Sending Stone.

“By the way, Soma, you have one of the Sending Stones, and I have the other.”

“Good to know.”

The paired stones give our separated inquiries a means of reaching one another. They do not shorten the streets between us or improve the approach through the grille, but I prefer a distant voice to an unexplained absence.

“So we could have little walkie-talkies over. Yeah.”

“We have walkie-talkies.”

Soma turns her attention back toward the meal.

“He's not eating turtle soup or anything at this hour, is he?”

I cannot identify the contents from here. He continues eating with an equanimity our investigation has done nothing to disturb.

“It's not the Board Laid Bare he's at, of course. He's somewhere else,” she says.

Lim’s establishment might have given the stop an immediate significance. This shop offers only a man at breakfast.

“He's just eating alone. He isn't talking to anybody.”

That is the difficulty. We have chosen the carrier who appears to know more, but knowing more does not oblige him to demonstrate it between mouthfuls. We could approach. We could ask. Either would make our interest part of his morning, and from that moment every subsequent choice might be made for our benefit.

“Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections,” Vukradin reminds Soma through the stone.

Then he refines the ownership of the obligation.

“Or you promised them.”

“Thank you so much.”

I appreciate the correction. Vukradin can impose the full weight of a promise while keeping an exact account of whose neck it rests upon.

“I'm not trying to put her out of business,” Soma says. “I want to see where he goes next. Stopping for a snack doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem kosher.”

We leave him to his breakfast. Brewbarry remains with us, and the man gives us no reason yet to move; below the street, Vukradin still has the more unusual trail.

Then his voice comes again.

“Okay… I don't think that's gonna go very well.”

“Good times,” I say.

“I mean, okay, there's some passive charm effect or something.”

The stone supplies his voice without the tunnel around it. I cannot inspect what he has encountered, cannot see the construct, cannot even judge his position from the sound. His account gives us a sudden disinterest in the route, an overwhelming conviction that continuing along it is boring and not worth his time.

“Turn into Brewbarry,” Soma says.

“A passive confusion effect. Alright, awesome.”

Vukradin tries to name what is happening while it is happening. I listen for what he still knows, since a mistaken description is less troublesome than a missing purpose.

“Well, it can't have been cast directly on me.”

Then: “This is, like, an area effect.”

That is his assessment from below. From here I have a carrier eating breakfast and a bard explaining why the passage he enters in pursuit of a construct has ceased to merit pursuit. I have no basis for naming a spell.

What becomes clear is that he remembers why he goes underground. He remembers where he is. The purpose survives; the willingness to follow this particular tunnel does not. He decides it is the wrong way to go and turns away from the construct’s trail.

Through the stone comes his final acknowledgment.

“Okay.”
